# Gap research: non-English communities, localisation and regional minors/payments law

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Scope:** fills the gap that R1-R34 contain no language, localisation or regional-law data and that the only country data (Germany 10-16%, Japan 6%, Brazil 4%, comScore 2007) are two decades old.

## Method note

Fourteen WebSearch calls (the hard cap) were used; page fetching (WebFetch/curl) is blocked in this sandbox, so every figure below comes from quoted extracts in search-result snippets rather than from reading the source pages. Where a figure is from search extracts it is cited inline as (Source: URL). Where I rely on knowledge not confirmed by a search in this run it is marked **[prior knowledge, unverified]**. Several questions (Steam language stats for VRChat, Firestorm locale telemetry, the exact Linden Lab localisation-cut timeline, Roblox's per-country payment rails, any retention study of chat translation) returned nothing usable and are explicitly flagged as open.

## Findings

### 1. Current country/language mix

**Second Life.** Semrush (page dated 13 Dec 2025) estimates secondlife.com at 6.40 M visits in Nov 2025 (12:24 avg session, -15% MoM), with traffic 49.6% United States, 7.0% Brazil, 5.6% Colombia, 5.1% UK, 3.8% Germany (Source: https://zh.semrush.com/website/secondlife.com/overview). No Similarweb country table surfaced in search. Read against comScore's March 2007 finding that 61% of active residents were European, 19% North American and 13% Asia-Pacific (Source: https://www.comscore.com/Insights/Press-Releases/2007/05/Second-Life-Growth-Worldwide), the picture is an inversion: SL has become a majority-US, English-first world with a Latin-American (Brazil + Colombia ~12.6%) second tier and a shrunken German presence. Website visits are a proxy, not logged-in concurrency; the Spanish-speaking share (Colombia, plus Spain/Mexico/Argentina not in the top five) is probably under-stated by a top-5 cut. The viewer itself still ships in roughly a dozen UI languages (Wikipedia lists 12) (Source: https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/SecondLife); in 2009 Linden Lab's localisation director described a tier-1/tier-2 model in which the Lab translated tier-1 languages and "the Second Life community did nearly everything" for tier-2, at a time when 64% of actives were non-US (Source: https://gilbane.com/?p=2016).

**VRChat.** Japan is now VRChat's largest web market. Sensor Tower's 2025 data puts Japan at 37% of visits to the official site versus 27% for the US, with Japanese visits rising since July 2025 and passing 23 M in September (Source: https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/vrchat-high-popularity-in-japan-JP). For the mobile app released October 2025, Japan took 25-26% of downloads (25 Oct-30 Nov 2025), second to the US (Source: https://www.moguravr.com/?p=312262). A VRDB summary gives the 2023 Japanese web-traffic share as 12.9% and VRChat's own post claimed "Japan Growth 3.9x" without defining the metric (Source: https://vrdb.app/blog/vrchat-metaverse-growing-japan-2026-v2; https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-key-stats-japan-growth-concurrents/). VRChat does not publish per-country actives; Steam language stats for VRChat were not retrieved (open item).

**OpenSim hypergrid.** Hypergrid Business's October 2025 census counted the equivalent of 147,614 standard regions, 46,244 active users and 492,301 registrations across reporting grids, with top grids by actives BloodMoon (785), AviWorlds (775), Gentle Fire (625), ZetaWorlds (486) and Kitely (426); Kitely Market has shipped to 665 grids; stats exclude most DreamGrid and private school/company grids (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79070). Country mix is not tabulated. AviWorlds is Brazilian-run and historically Portuguese-heavy, and several of the mid-size grids (DorenasWorld, Craft World, FrancoGrid; the closed German Metropolis grid) are single-language European communities **[prior knowledge, unverified]**, so the hypergrid is more linguistically federated than SL: language lives at grid level, not region level.

**Roblox (for contrast).** APAC DAUs grew 76% YoY in Q2 2025, with Japan bookings +50% and India +90% (Source: https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q2/Q2-2025-Earnings-Conference-Call-Transcript.pdf); in Q4 2025 (144 M DAU, +69%) APAC bookings rose 96%, Indonesia +700%, Japan +160%, India +110% (Source: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/roblox-daily-users-jump-69-to-record-144-million-in-q4-2025). A third-party estimate puts APAC at ~28% of DAU by end-2025, up from ~18% in early 2024 (Source: https://rolearn.dev/insights/apac-growth-opportunity-roblox).

### 2. Which non-English SL communities persist, and what they lost

Search returned no 2025 census of SL language communities, so this is a mix of dated evidence and inference. **Brazilian/Portuguese:** the strongest surviving non-English bloc, consistent with Brazil being SL's #2 traffic country. Ajuda SL Brasil, a volunteer Community Gateway founded 2009, was still running self-funded in 2018 "primarily – but not exclusively – serv[ing] Portuguese speaking users" (Source: https://modemworld.me/2018/05/). The commercial channel collapsed earlier: Kaizen Games, the official Brazilian SL partner, "stopped its activities" (Source: https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/second-life-acaba-no-brasil), and the Brazilian viewer localisation was a community project on the SL wiki (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Brazilian_Viewer_Localization_Project). **Japanese:** a decline reported as early as December 2007 ("Japan: depopulating Second Life") (Source: https://globalvoices.org/2007/12/19/japan-depopulating-second-life/); what persists is niche, e.g. a Nihongo Tea Room and native-Japanese-run language-exchange events still active in 2022 (Source: https://community.wanikani.com/t/closed-genki-1-genki-2-study-group-with-native-japanese-volunteers-in-second-life-starting-january-2022/54815/44). **German:** once the largest non-US cohort; now 3.8% of web traffic. Academic work documents German-themed sims as identity anchors (Source: https://kartdok.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/receive/kartdok_mods_00001391), but no current estate list surfaced. Italian, French, Spanish, Turkish and Russian communities were not reachable within budget; anecdotally they survive as group-and-club clusters rather than estates **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.

What they lost: the June 2010 restructuring cut 30% of staff, closed the Singapore and UK offices, "reconfigured" customer support and hit community/customer-advocacy roles hardest (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-07-linden-lab-laying-off-staff-closing-singapore-office.html; https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-10-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2010-and-the.html). That removed Asia-based staff, non-English in-world Linden presence and the paid tier-1 translation pipeline; translation reverted to community volunteers and the Gateway programme became volunteer-run (Ajuda SL Brasil is explicitly "self-funded as a non-profit"). The 2009 interview is the last time the Lab publicly described a funded multi-language programme. No search result confirmed the precise date the Lab ended localised support (open item).

### 3. What VRChat-Japan and Roblox-Japan/India growth depend on

**VRChat/Japan:** an external avatar supply chain, not VRChat's own store. pixiv's BOOTH 3D-model category exceeded 10 billion yen (~US$65-70 M) in annual transaction value in 2025 (Source: https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html), and in September 2026 pixiv took an equity stake in VRChat Inc. with a joint beginner world planned for autumn 2026 (Source: https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/09/09/27973.html). Avatar sales happen "mainly through external digital markets such as BOOTH, or through VRChat's official Avatar Marketplace" (Source: https://biz.moneyforward.com/tax_return/basic/81371/); a single studio's avatars sell at 6,000-7,000 yen (Source: https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Community:STUDIO_JINGO). The Japanese surge dates from around June 2024 and is linked to VTuber culture and the domestic creator economy (Source: https://vrdb.app/blog/vrchat-metaverse-growing-japan-2026-v2). BOOTH accepts konbini and Japanese wallets and VRChat's UI has had a Japanese localisation since 2023 **[prior knowledge, unverified]**. The dependency chain is: Japanese payment rails on a Japanese marketplace -> VRM/Unity avatar pipeline -> VRChat as the stage.

**Roblox/Japan/India:** regional pricing and local cash rails. Roblox launched Regional Pricing for in-experience items in April 2025 (an item at 1,000 Robux in the US may be 700 in Brazil or 500 in India) and early tests raised paying users 17% in Mexico, 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items; https://engadget.com/gaming/the-roblox-economy-is-going-global-as-users-can-now-set-regional-pricing-160047440.html). In Japan, yen-denominated Roblox gift cards sold through Blackhawk Network since November 2023 give cash/konbini users a route to Robux (Source: https://technode.global/prnasia/blackhawk-network-japan-partners-with-roblox-to-launch-roblox-digital-gift-cards-for-japanese-users/). Direct Konbini/Pix/UPI acceptance by Roblox was not confirmed in search (open item). Management cited AI translation tooling as part of international scaling (Source: https://www.aol.com/articles/roblox-rblx-q4-2025-earnings-224200551.html). Local-moderator staffing was not found in any source.

### 4. What regional minors law requires of a social world

**Brazil, ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025), in force 17 March 2026.** Applies to any IT product "intended for or likely to be accessed by minors, regardless of where ... developed, sold, or operated"; obligations: safety-by-default settings, reliable age verification, parental supervision tools, limits on profiling-based ads (Source: https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/). Risk-tiered duties; "age assessment" vs "age verification" with ANPD technical rules (preliminary guidelines 20 March 2026); age-verification data may not be reused; games with loot boxes must age-verify or offer a loot-box-free version to minors; Decree 12,880/2026 adds obligations beyond the statute (Source: https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies). Penalties up to R$50 M or 10% of Brazilian revenue, up to service shut-off; self-declaration checkboxes are insufficient (Source: https://withpersona.com/blog/brazil-digital-eca-age-assurance-regulation). Xsolla reads the law as an outright under-18 loot-box ban even with parental consent; Mayer Brown reads it as verify-or-offer-alternative (Source: https://xsolla.com/blog/protect-your-revenue-and-stay-compliant-in-brazil-with-xsolla-parental-controls).

**Germany.** Two regimes: JuSchG (ratings, and since 2021 §24a "precautionary measures" for platforms used by minors: reporting tools, parental controls, age-aware defaults, enforced by the BzKJ) and the states' JMStV (online content, supervised by KJM) (Source: https://law.ungovr.org/age/de; https://mebis.bycs.de/assets/dsc/posts/ba4bf8c1-062d-4417-9ab5-00e1f872a9ab/institutionen-des-jugendmedienschutzes-in-deutschland.pdf). Online distribution of JMStV-restricted content without approved age verification is an administrative offence, fines up to EUR 500,000 (Source: https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/fan-service-%e3%83%95%e3%82%a1%e3%83%b3%e3%82%b5%e3%83%bc%e3%83%93%e3%82%b9-german-regulators-increasingly-crack-down-on-japanese-anime-video-games). The Sixth Interstate Media Treaty (in force 1 Dec 2025) adds JMStV §§12-12b, a device-level "one-button" youth-protection mandate on operating systems by 1 Dec 2027 at the latest (single secondary source) (Source: https://law.ungovr.org/age/de). The §24a threshold (>1 M German users) and KJM-approved AVS for adult content are **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.

**Japan.** No statutory age-verification mandate for games; self-declaration plus CERO/industry self-regulation (Source: https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2766/2750/2810). The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications published a first report on youth protection online on 2 June 2026 aimed at platforms, carriers and OS vendors (Source: https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/40399-ministry-of-internal-affairs-and-communications-published-report-recommending-measures-for-youth-protection-in-online-environments). The 2008 Youth Internet Environment Act (carrier filtering), the 2012 "kompu gacha" ban and the Payment Services Act registration/deposit duties for prepaid in-world currency are **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.

**Other non-EU/UK/AU/US regimes.** Indonesia's PP Tunas (in force 28 March 2026) requires age verification and moderation tooling with a minimum age of 16; Meta and Google were summoned for non-compliance and Roblox is subject to mandatory age verification for all Indonesian users, with unverified profiles losing communication features (Source: https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/03/31/indonesia-summons-meta-google-for-failing-to-enforce-under16-age-limits-under-new-childprotection-law/214489; https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/). Malaysia began enforcing under-16 account age verification (passport, ID or MyDigital ID) in June 2026 (Source: https://bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2573981). India's DPDP Act defines a child as under 18 and requires verifiable parental consent via ID documents or DigiLocker before processing a child's data (Source: https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-data-protection-rules-2025-children-data-india/). Turkey's parliament passed a bill barring social-network services to under-15s with mandatory age verification, in force six months after gazette publication (Source: https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/04/turkiye-lawmakers-approve-bill-restricting-social-media-access-for-children-under-15/). South Korea has no dedicated minimum-age statute in force but a Network Act amendment with age verification and recommendation limits for adolescents was introduced 1 Oct 2026 (Source: https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/21749-bill-amending-network-act-including-provisions-on-age-verification-and-restriction-of-personalised-recommendation-systems-for-adolescent-users-of-large-scale-social-media-services-bill-no-2221711). 

### 5. Does real-time chat translation measurably raise cross-language retention?

No published retention evidence was found. Roblox's February 2024 launch of real-time text translation in 16 languages on a proprietary model reported only that testing showed "stronger engagement and session quality" and a "very positive impact on time spent, chat engagement, and session quality", and noted that a third of chat time is between users who do not share a language (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/02/roblox-introduces-ai-powered-real-time-chat-translations-in-16-languages; https://corp.roblox.com/?p=20149). No D1/D7/D30 figures, no cohort comparison and no independent evaluation surfaced; the broader search produced patents (latency claims), vendor marketing and forum feature requests only (Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/US10769387). The honest answer is: directional company claims, zero measured retention effect in the public record.

## Design implications for nolife

1. **Treat language as a first-class shard key, not a UI setting.** SL's non-English communities survived only where volunteers built their own gateway (Ajuda SL Brasil) or where a grid was theirs (OpenSim's AviWorlds, DorenasWorld). nolife should let a community own a language-tagged home (grid or estate), with per-language discovery, onboarding and help channels, and should budget paid tier-1 localisation for at least Portuguese, Japanese, German and Spanish rather than relying on community translation that evaporates when the volunteers leave.
2. **Open the avatar pipeline to the markets that already exist.** VRChat-Japan did not win by building a store; it won by being the stage for BOOTH's 10 bn-yen VRM economy. nolife should support VRM/glTF import, allow off-platform marketplaces (BOOTH, Gumroad, Kitely-style) to sell directly, and only later layer an official marketplace.
3. **Price and pay locally from day one.** Roblox's regional-pricing tests (+26% payers in Brazil, +52% Philippines) and yen gift cards show that purchasing-power pricing and cash rails (konbini, Pix, UPI, prepaid cards) are growth levers, not compliance chores. Keep a single global currency but allow creator-set regional prices and local acquisition rails.
4. **Build age assurance as a configurable regional module.** Brazil (17+ risk-tiered, no self-declaration, loot-box rules), Indonesia (16, communication features gated on verification), Malaysia (16), Turkey (15), India (verifiable parental consent under 18), Germany (§24a precautions plus KJM-grade AVS for adult areas) each set different thresholds and proofs. nolife needs: age-signal storage separated from identity, per-region thresholds, feature gating (chat/voice/trading) rather than binary bans, parental-supervision surfaces, and a loot-box-free mode. The Indonesian Roblox precedent (unverified = no comms) is the likely template regulators will copy.
5. **Keep adult content and youth modes architecturally separate** (SL's Adult/Moderate/General split is the right instinct) because German JMStV and Brazilian ECA both treat "likely accessed by minors" as the trigger; an all-ages world with adult sims inside it is the worst compliance position.
6. **Ship chat translation but do not bet retention on it.** Offer per-region translation (text first, voice later) as a social lubricant; instrument D7/D30 for mixed-language groups so nolife can produce the measurement nobody else has published.
7. **Watch the OS-level mandates.** Germany's one-button device filter (by Dec 2027), Japan's MIC platform/OS recommendations and Indonesia's app-store enforcement mean a client that honours OS parental signals (and degrades gracefully) will be table stakes.

# Safety, moderation, identity and regulation for virtual worlds (2026) — brief for "nolife"

Prepared 2026-10-09. Method note: 24 web searches were run (extended mode for dated/niche facts). Direct page reads via WebFetch were blocked by the session's egress proxy (403 on CONNECT to every host, including roblox.com, esafety.gov.au, ec.europa.eu, ofcom-related sites, sec.gov, congress.gov, wikipedia.org), so primary sources were read through search-engine excerpts of those pages rather than full-text fetches. Facts below are therefore cited to the primary URL where the excerpt came from that page, and marked "(secondary)" where only a law-firm/press summary was available. Figures flagged "contested" differ between sources.

## Findings

### 1. Child safety and age assurance — the 2025–2026 enforcement wave

**United Kingdom (Online Safety Act).** The age-check duty for services with pornographic or other primary-priority content applied from 25 July 2025; Ofcom requires checks to be "highly effective" (technically accurate, robust, reliable, fair) and can fine up to £18m or 10% of global turnover (Source: https://natlawreview.com/article/you-must-be-tall-click-online-safety-act-and-age-appropriate-access; https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/The-UKs-Online-Safety-Act-explained-what-you-need-to-know). Enforcement so far targets adult sites, not virtual worlds: AVS Group £1m + £50k (Dec 2025, 18 sites, 72-hour compliance order with daily penalties); Kick Online Entertainment £800k + £30k and 8579 LLC £1.35m (Feb 2026); Youngtek £500k + £100k (May 2026, secondary) (Source: https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/; https://xident.io/blog/age-assurance-evidence-ofcom-information-request-second-fine-2026/). Every substantive fine was paired with a second fine for failing to answer Ofcom information requests — evidencing compliance is itself a duty (Source: https://xident.io/blog/age-assurance-evidence-ofcom-information-request-second-fine-2026/). Ofcom's one-year statutory report (July 2026) says all top-10 and most top-100 porn sites now have checks, but it has 23 open investigations into 88 adult services and says "tougher action is needed" (Source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/16/ofcom-warns-that-tougher-action-is-needed-on-age-assurance-following-publication-102nc4m). The Act applies to any user-to-user service accessible in the UK, which squarely includes virtual worlds (Source: https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/The-UKs-Online-Safety-Act-explained-what-you-need-to-know).

**European Union (DSA Article 28 + age-verification app).** On 14 July 2025 the Commission published guidelines on protection of minors and a prototype age-verification app (Source: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_25_1820; https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/newsletter/insafe-weekly-update-22-july-2025). The guidelines apply to all platforms minors can access, not only VLOPs; treat self-declaration as unreliable; expect minors' accounts to be private by default with limits on who can contact them, geolocation off, and no engagement-maximising notifications (Source: https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-longawaited-eu-guidelines-on-article-281-dsa-what-online-platforms-must-know; https://2b-advice.com/en/2025/08/13/eu-age-verification-data-protection-compliant-age-verification-under-the-digital-services-act/). The app is a "prove you are over 18 without revealing anything else" credential expected to plug into the EU Digital Identity Wallet; guidelines are not binding but are the Commission's supervisory yardstick, with DSA fines up to 6% of global turnover (secondary) (Source: https://2b-advice.com/en/2025/08/13/eu-age-verification-data-protection-compliant-age-verification-under-the-digital-services-act/).

**Australia (under-16 account ban).** From 10 December 2025 ten services — Facebook, Instagram, Kick, Reddit, Snapchat, Threads, TikTok, Twitch, X, YouTube — must take "reasonable steps" to prevent under-16s holding accounts. eSafety explicitly lists Roblox, Discord, Steam/Steam Chat, LEGO Play, GitHub, Messenger, WhatsApp, Pinterest and YouTube Kids as NOT meeting the "age-restricted social media platform" definition, because services whose sole or primary purpose is online gaming (or messaging) are excluded (Source: https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs, FAQ updated 1 April 2026). eSafety has warned the list "is not static" and earlier floated adding Roblox (Source: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-11-05/what-social-media-apps-are-getting-banned-in-australia/105973258). Interpretation: a virtual world whose primary purpose is socialising rather than gaming is at real risk of designation; a world that frames itself as a game is currently outside.

**United States (COPPA 2.0 / KOSA / KIDS Act).** KOSA and COPPA 2.0 passed the Senate 91–3 in 2024 but never reached a House vote (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Online_Safety_Act). On 29 June 2026 the House passed H.R. 7757 (KIDS Act) 267–117, consolidating 14 bills including KOSA and COPPA 2.0 but dropping KOSA's "duty of care" and adding broad state-law preemption; the Senate Commerce Committee cleared S. 1748 (KOSA) unanimously on 5 August 2026 and a floor vote is pending (Source: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/house-advances-bipartisan-kids-online-safety-bill-but-senate-showdown-looms; https://www.techpolicy.press/congresss-bipartisan-child-online-safety-coalition-is-unraveling/). Nothing is law as of 9 October 2026; state AG enforcement is the live risk instead.

**Roblox as the bellwether (lawsuits → mandated age checks → settlements).** Louisiana (Aug 2025), Kentucky (Oct 2025) and Texas (Nov 2025) AGs sued Roblox for deceptive safety claims and enabling grooming; Texas argued under-13 chat limits were bypassable by creating an alternate account claiming to be 13+ (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/07/texas-ag-sues-roblox-accusing-it-of-prioritizing-pixel-pedophiles-over-child-safety/; https://www.wafb.com/2025/08/14/attorney-general-files-child-protection-lawsuit-against-roblox/). Roblox then announced (Nov 2025) and enforced (Dec 2025 in Australia, New Zealand, Netherlands; globally from January 2026) a facial age estimation requirement to access chat, via Persona, sorting users into six bands (under 9, 9–12, 13–15, 16–17, 18–20, 21+); by default users can chat only with adjacent bands; under-9 chat is off without parental consent; images are said to be deleted after processing; "tens of millions" had completed the check (company claim) (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/01/roblox-age-checks-required-to-chat; https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/11/roblox-requires-age-checks-limits-minor-and-adult-chat; https://www.biometricupdate.com/202601/roblox-rolls-out-facial-age-estimation-for-chat-access-globally). Settlements followed: West Virginia $11.1m, Alabama $12.2m, Nevada $12.5m (April 2026, $35.8m total); Mississippi (June 2026, $5m liquidated damages); South Dakota ~$10m over four years (July 2026); Kansas $9.8m over three years plus $10m if non-compliant (Sept 2026); Tennessee, Texas, Iowa, Florida and Nebraska suits still pending as of April 2026 (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-21/roblox-settles-with-states-for-35-8-million-over-child-safety; https://www.ag.ks.gov/Home/Components/News/News/365/1292; https://southdakotasearchlight.com/2026/07/13/10-million-settlement-over-online-gaming-child-exploitation-will-fund-safety-after-school-efforts/). Settlement terms consistently bind Roblox to verify ages and limit adult→under-16 contact.

**Discord.** UK and Australia checks in 2025; "teen-by-default" global model announced Feb 2026, delayed from March to H2 2026 after privacy backlash, rollout reportedly began 22 Sept 2026 with >90% of users age-bucketed automatically by behavioural signals and on-device facial estimation only for the remainder (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/discord-to-roll-out-age-verification-next-month-for-full-access-to-its-platform/; https://thenextweb.com/news/discord-global-age-checks-teen-default; https://discord.com/safety/how-discord-is-building-safer-experiences-for-teens). The cautionary tale: a third-party support vendor compromise (58 hours, 22–24 Sept 2025, disclosed 3 Oct 2025) exposed ~70,000 government-ID images uploaded for age appeals; attackers claimed 2.1m IDs/1.5 TB, which Discord disputes (contested) (Source: https://cyberinsider.com/discord-confirms-70000-government-ids-exposed-in-third-party-breach/).

### 2. VR harassment: a ten-year evidence trail

- 2016: Jordan Belamire's account of being groped in QuiVr is the canonical first public case (not re-verified by search this session; treat as medium confidence).
- Late 2021 / Jan 2022: Horizon Worlds beta tester and a 43-year-old British woman in Horizon Venues reported groping by groups of male avatars; Meta's initial response was that the user had not used the block tool (Source: https://siliconrepublic.com/?p=962203).
- 4 Feb 2022: Meta shipped "Personal Boundary" — an always-on ~4 ft (2 ft radius) no-go cylinder that halts movement without haptic feedback; Meta said it was mandatory "to set behavioral norms for a new medium" (Source: https://ispr.info/2022/02/08/meta-establishes-four-foot-personal-boundary-to-deter-vr-groping/; https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/metas-solution-to-vr-creepers-a-four-foot-zone-of-personal-virtual-space).
- Jan 2024: UK police opened what was reported as the first investigation into the "virtual rape" of a girl under 16 in a VR room; officers said current law was not built for it and prosecution looked non-viable; NPCC's Ian Critchley called the metaverse "a gateway for predators" (Source: https://lbc.co.uk/news/police-investigate-rape-metaverse; https://grahamcluley.com/sexual-assault-in-the-metaverse-investigated-by-british-police). No charge has been reported.
- 9 Sept 2025: former Meta researchers Jason Sattizahn and Cayce Savage told the Senate Judiciary subcommittee that Meta shut down research showing under-13s in Horizon Worlds were exposed to sexual content; Meta called it "selective misrepresentation"; Fairplay filed a COPPA complaint with the FTC (Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/109419-whistleblowers-tell-us-senate-meta-ignored-child-safety.html). Meta had lowered the Quest minimum age from 13 to 10 (2023) and opened Horizon Worlds to parent-approved preteens with voice chat off by default (2024) (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-request-children-access-horizon-worlds/).
- March 2026: Meta announced Horizon Worlds would leave the Quest store at end of March and be removed from VR on 15 June 2026, surviving only as a mobile app (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/18/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-vr.html).

### 3. Moderation at scale

Roblox does not disclose trust-and-safety spend separately; its combined "infrastructure and trust & safety" line was $1,153.5m in 2025 vs $915.4m in 2024 (+26%), with most of the increase being data-centre cost; the 10-K says AI automation "led to a decrease in safety moderation and customer support costs" (company characterisation) (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/roblox-stock-earnings-safety.html). Roblox filed 24,522 NCMEC reports in 2024 (0.12% of 20.3m total NCMEC reports); its open-sourced Sentinel system (running since late 2024) contributed ~1,200 NCMEC reports in H1 2025 and 35% of detected grooming cases were caught proactively before any user report (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/08/open-sourcing-roblox-sentinel-preemptive-risk-detection; https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_news/2025/Aug/15/Roblox-Response-8-15-25.pdf). Roblox claims 145 safety features shipped in 2025 (Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2025/11/07/texas-is-third-state-to-sue-roblox-as-controversy-over-child-safety-grows/).

VRChat uses a behavioural Trust Rank ladder (Visitor → New User → User → Known → Trusted, plus a visible "Nuisance" flag) that gates content upload and reporting rights; rank is earned by time, content, friendships and is explicitly not raised by AFK idling, low-effort uploads or mass-friending (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system; https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Trust_Ranks). VRChat's optional 18+ verification (Persona, government ID + liveness selfie) began Dec 2024, reached all VRC+ subscribers 28 Jan 2025, and a UK content-gating change forces the "Sexually Suggestive" filter on for un-assured users (Source: https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Assurance; https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202512).

Second Life's baseline: General/Moderate/Adult region ratings, Adult content confined to Zindra and designated private regions, access requiring an 18+ affirmation plus payment-on-file or third-party age verification, with Linden Lab's own FAQ admitting "no such system is truly infallible" (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Linden_Lab_Official:Adult-oriented_content_controls_FAQ; https://lindenlab.com/legal/second-life-terms-and-conditions). Its 2007 police-blotter approach to abuse reports generally resulted in warnings (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-03-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2007.html).

### 4. Identity: pseudonymity vs verification

Meta's Oculus-requires-Facebook policy (2020) was reversed: announced Oct 2021, Meta accounts shipped Aug 2022 with a "Meta Horizon profile" whose public name can be a real name, username or anything else; Facebook/Instagram linking became optional (Source: https://androidcentral.com/gaming/virtual-reality/meta-finally-kills-the-oculus-quest-2-facebook-account-requirement; https://www.roadtovr.com/?p=105092). Second Life moved from fixed "first + surname" avatar names to usernames plus freely changeable display names in 2010 (well-known; not re-verified this session, medium confidence). Across Roblox, VRChat and Discord the 2025–26 settlement is: pseudonymous public identity, but a privately verified or estimated age band attached to the account, with vendors (Persona) and deletion-after-processing claims as the privacy backstop (Sources above). The Discord breach shows the appeals path (humans reviewing uploaded IDs via an outsourced vendor) is the weakest link.

### 5. Biometric and eye-tracking data

Meta's Quest Pro eye-tracking notice states raw eye images are processed on-device and deleted, but "abstracted gaze data" can be sent to Meta servers to animate avatars when the user grants an app access, can appear in crash logs, and Meta retains interaction metadata such as tracking quality (Source: https://www.meta.com/legal/quest/eye-tracking-privacy-notice/). Illinois BIPA (statutory damages $1,000–$5,000 per violation, private right of action) lists retina/iris scans and face geometry but no court has ruled whether gaze tracking qualifies; Colorado HB 24-1130 (effective 1 July 2025) requires consent, a public retention/deletion policy and bans sale of biometric identifiers, AG-enforced only (Source: https://fpf.org/blog/old-laws-new-tech-as-courts-wrestle-with-tough-questions-under-us-biometric-laws-immersive-tech-raises-new-challenges/; https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/us-biometric-laws-and-pending-legislation-tracker.html).

### 6. AI-generated content and likeness

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026; the Commission's final guidelines were published 20 July 2026 and ~190 organisations had signed the voluntary Code of Practice by end-July 2026. Providers must mark AI output in machine-readable form using a layered approach (no single watermark is deemed sufficient); deployers must label deepfakes perceptibly — a watermark buried in metadata or a T&C disclosure does not satisfy the duty; artistic/satirical content gets lighter disclosure (Source: https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content; https://www.techpolicy.press/the-eus-ai-transparency-code-of-practice-explained/; https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-first-draft-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content). The NO FAKES Act of 2026 (S. 4591 / H.R. 8915, introduced 20 May 2026) would create a federal digital-replica right for every individual with DMCA-style notice-and-counter-notice; the Senate Judiciary Committee advanced it by voice vote in June 2026 (date reported as 18 or 22 June, contested) and it was placed on the calendar 2 July 2026; not yet passed by either chamber (Source: https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8915; https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s4591; https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name). The TAKE IT DOWN Act's notice-and-removal obligations for non-consensual intimate imagery are already in effect, which SAG-AFTRA calls "only the beginning" (Source: https://www.sagaftra.org/take-it-down-act-notice-and-removal-obligations-now-effective). California AB 2602 requires explicit consent for performer digital replicas (Source: https://variety.com/2024/politics/news/sag-aftra-limit-ai-digital-replica-california-bill-1236120379/).

### 7. Crypto/NFT economies

Valve added "applications built on blockchain technology that issue or allow exchange of cryptocurrencies or NFTs" to Steam's prohibited list on 15 October 2021, framed as an extension of its rule against items with real-world value; Epic took the opposite stance (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/steam-is-now-banning-all-games-that-engage-in-cryptocurrency-or-nft-trading; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_game). The SEC's first NFT actions (Impact Theory, 28 Aug 2023, $500k penalty; Stoner Cats, 13 Sept 2023, $1m penalty) rested on marketing that promised price appreciation and required destruction of unsold tokens and removal of royalty code (Source: https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/the-secs-second-nft-enforcement-action-sec-v-stoner-cats-2-llc; https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/intellectual-property-news/2023/sec-settlement-with-stoner-cats-may-signal-agencys-intent-to-assert-its-authority). Under Chair Atkins (2025) the SEC's interpretive position is that digital collectibles are generally not securities, but the 2023 settlements stand and marketing-driven profit expectations still trigger Howey (Source: https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-chair-nfts-not-securities-explained; https://astraea.law/insights/nft-regulation-2025-sec-enforcement-retreat). Net effect: token worlds lost the largest PC distribution channel in 2021 and spent 2023–25 under securities uncertainty; the regulatory risk has eased in the US in 2025 but distribution bans and player hostility have not.

### 8. Gambling, loot boxes, skins

Linden Lab banned chance-based wagering paying out in L$ or anything convertible on 25 July 2007 (skill contests and novelty-only payouts allowed), after inviting the FBI in and in the shadow of UIGEA 2006; Linden's Ginsu Yoon: "It's not always clear to us whether a 3-D simulation of a casino is the same thing as a casino"; casino operators had been among the largest tier-fee payers and cancelled land (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2007/07/25/second-life-bans-gambling-following-fbi-investigation/; https://virtuallyblind.com/2007/08/10/gambling-clarified-enforced-tos-unchanged/; https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-03-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2007.html). Belgium's Gaming Commission ruled paid loot boxes illegal gambling in April 2018 (EA stopped selling FIFA Points there in 2019); an Antwerp court in Jan 2025 held Top War loot boxes illegal and found Apple facilitated unlawful gambling by hosting it (Source: https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/). The Dutch Council of State reversed the €500k/week FIFA fine on 9 March 2022, holding loot boxes inside a game of skill are not automatically games of chance (Source: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=eecf1cdb-e924-4d50-bd90-3f1e75dcb44d; https://www.akd.eu/insights/understanding-the-dutch-lootbox-judgment-are-all-lootboxes-now-illegal-in-the-netherlands-). The UK chose 11 industry principles (Ukie, 18 July 2023, 12-month implementation: parental consent for under-18 loot-box purchases, odds disclosure); a longitudinal App Store study found poor compliance (Source: https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12115820/). Brazil bans loot-box sales to under-18s from March 2026 (secondary) (Source: https://blog.promise.legal/lootbox-regulation-2026-game-studios/). Skins: Washington State ordered Valve on 5 Oct 2016 to stop CS:GO skins being used on gambling sites; Valve had sent 40+ cease-and-desist letters to API abusers but denied facilitating gambling (Source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/counter-strike-global-offensive/washington-gambling-commission-demand-valve-end-skin-gambling). DCMS's Sept 2025 rapid evidence review counted 50+ skins-gambling sites reachable from the UK with 6.9m global visits in Feb 2025, mostly without age verification (Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/a-rapid-evidence-review-of-skins-gambling/a-rapid-evidence-review-of-skins-gambling). Lesson: any transferable, cash-out-able item plus any chance mechanic equals gambling exposure somewhere.

### 9. Accessibility

The European Accessibility Act (Directive 2019/882) applies to products and services placed on the market from 28 June 2025, including e-commerce, websites and mobile apps, with WCAG-style perceivable/operable/understandable/robust requirements, a micro-enterprise exemption (<10 staff, <€2m), a documented "disproportionate burden" defence, and a transition to 28 June 2030 for pre-existing products used in service delivery (Source: https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-accessibility-act-time-start-implementation-projects-now; https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/a-new-milestone-for-accessibility-the-european-accessibility-act-applies-tomorrow/). Games are not a named annex category, but a world's storefront, payments, account and support flows are e-commerce and are in scope (interpretation, medium confidence).

## Patterns / root causes

1. **Regulation follows a harm-then-headline loop, and the platform pays twice.** Every platform in this brief shipped the control after the lawsuit, the whistleblower or the police probe (Roblox chat age-gating after three AG suits; Meta's boundary after press reports; Linden's gambling ban after FBI visits). Retrofitted controls cost settlements ($54m+ for Roblox by Sept 2026) and still leave pending suits.
2. **Self-declared age is now legally worthless.** The EU guidelines, Ofcom, Texas's complaint and the Roblox settlements all converge: a date-of-birth field is not age assurance. The accepted pattern is a private age band (estimation or credential) bound to a pseudonymous public identity.
3. **Age verification creates its own breach surface.** Discord's 70k IDs leaked through the human appeals vendor, not the automated estimator. Centralised ID storage is the liability; on-device estimation and wallet-based credentials (EU app) are the direction of travel.
4. **Enforcement punishes un-evidenced compliance as much as non-compliance.** Ofcom's paired information-request fines and the settlement liquidated-damages clauses mean logging, audit trails and reporting are product requirements.
5. **Definitions decide which regime you fall under.** Australia's "primary purpose" test exempts Roblox and Discord today; the Dutch "game of skill" test saved FIFA; Belgium's "tradeable item" test condemned it; Howey turns on marketing language. Product framing and economy design are legal levers.
6. **Embodied harassment is real harm the law cannot yet prosecute.** UK police concluded in 2024 that a virtual assault was non-viable to charge; platform-level prevention (boundaries, mute, instance control, trust gating) is the only effective layer.
7. **Moderation economics drive AI-first pipelines.** Roblox credits automation for lower per-unit moderation cost while total safety+infra spend rose 26%; proactive detection (35% of grooming cases) is where results come from, not report queues.
8. **Real-world-value items are the common root of gambling, securities and distribution bans.** SL 2007, Steam 2021, Belgium 2018/2025, Washington 2016 and the SEC 2023 all trace to cash-out-able items.

## Design implications for nolife

1. Ship age-banding at account creation on day one: pseudonymous display identity; private age band from on-device facial estimation or an EU-wallet/credential proof; never store ID images; appeals handled without uploading documents where possible; default adjacent-band-only chat like Roblox's six bands.
2. Minors-by-default settings per DSA guidelines: private profiles, no stranger DMs/voice, geolocation off, no engagement notifications, adult regions hard-gated behind a "highly effective" check (Ofcom standard), with region ratings inherited from SL (General/Moderate/Adult) but enforced by credential, not checkbox.
3. Always-on embodied safety primitives: personal boundary (default on, user-adjustable radius), one-gesture mute/block/vanish, instance-level kick/lock, and session recording-on-report (short rolling buffer) so VR assaults are evidentiable.
4. Behaviour-earned trust ranks (VRChat model) governing upload, scripting, voice and proximity rights; visible nuisance flag; no rank from idle time or mass-friending.
5. Proactive detection pipeline with measurable NCMEC/report metrics published quarterly; budget trust & safety as a disclosed line item; design for Ofcom/DSC information requests (audit logs, retention policy, response SLAs).
6. Economy rules: no chance mechanics paying out anything transferable or cash-out-able (SL 2007 rule); odds disclosure and under-18 purchase consent for any random reward; no blockchain tokens or NFTs — they forfeit Steam distribution and invite Howey analysis.
7. Biometric minimisation: eye/face/hand tracking processed on-device; only abstracted animation data leaves the headset; explicit consent, public retention/deletion policy, no sale (Colorado/BIPA-proof); no ad targeting from gaze.
8. AI content governance: machine-readable provenance on all AI-generated assets and avatars plus a perceptible in-world "AI" label; consent registry and takedown/counter-notice flow for likeness replicas (NO FAKES/TAKE IT DOWN ready); block uploading real-person likenesses without verified consent.
9. Framing and jurisdiction: define nolife's primary purpose (creation/play vs social) deliberately with Australia's test in mind; geo-fence adult content and economy features by jurisdiction; EAA-compliant storefront, account and support UX from launch.
10. Parent/guardian tooling for 10–15 bands (approve worlds, contacts, spend) because the Roblox settlements make these contractual expectations, not nice-to-haves.

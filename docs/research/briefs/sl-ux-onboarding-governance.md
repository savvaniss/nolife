# Second Life: UX, onboarding, retention, governance and public perception

**Brief for the "nolife" successor-world design. Live-search revision, 2026-10-09.**

## Method note

This revision re-grounds the earlier prior-knowledge draft (`sl-ux-onboarding-governance.prior.md`) in live web search. **20 of 20 WebSearch calls were used** (19 standard, 1 extended). Page fetching (WebFetch/curl) is blocked in this sandbox, so every "live" fact below rests on the quoted extracts the search engine returned, not on the full source page; where an extract itself was quoting a secondary source, that is said. Facts I could not search are kept only where load-bearing and are tagged **(prior knowledge, unsearched)**. Two claims in the prior draft were corrected by search: Linden's much-repeated 2007 retention figure was **10% at 90 days**, not 30 days; and the Openspace/Homestead pricing sequence ran $75 to $95 to $125 (phased), not a climb-down from $125 to $95. The 2010s "Basic mode was withdrawn" claim and the "400,000 sign-ups per month" claim could not be confirmed and are downgraded. Current (2025-2026) status is covered only by third-party grid trackers; Linden Lab publishes no official user numbers today.

## Findings

### 1. The funnel: sign-ups, actives and retention

- The "Residents" counter counted every account ever created. In October 2006 Linden's own table showed **235,311 residents logged in during the last 30 days** against a headline residents figure then approaching one million (Source: https://www.raphkoster.com/2006/10/03/second-life-posts-weekly-uniques/). By early 2009 the counter read **16,785,351** (Source: https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/PCWorld/story?id=6688909); by 10 August 2025 it read about **68.98 million** (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/).
- **Retention, as stated by Linden Lab (January 2007):** "new-user retention to 90 days is still about 10%", "more or less unchanged over the last 12 months" (Massively, via Source: https://andrewchen.com/interesting-graph-on-second-life-usage/ ; also https://nevillehobson.com/2007/01/29/second-lifes-accelerating-growth/). A 2007 secondary analysis ("The phony economics of Second Life", quoted at Source: https://netzpolitik.org/2007/die-wirtschaftskraft-von-second-life/) found that about **15% of the October 2006 sign-up cohort logged in again after their first 30 days**, which reduced ~2 million "residents" to roughly a quarter million real users.
- **Wired, July 2007** (Frank Rose, "How Madison Avenue Is Wasting Millions on a Deserted Second Life"): avatars had passed 7 million, but Linden said distinct individuals were closer to 4 million; only about **1 million had logged on in the previous 30 days**; "more than 85 percent of the avatars created have been abandoned"; a little over 100,000 Americans per week were reachable by US marketers (Source: https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56 ; https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/second-life-massive-marketing-self-deception ; https://facweb.ischool.illinois.edu/sigia-l/2007-July/020715.html). Rose's companion essay "Lonely Planet" is dated 1 August 2007 (Source: https://frankrose.com/?p=1438).
- Forrester's Brian Haven put peak concurrency in mid-2007 at **30,000-40,000** (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2).
- **Linden Lab's own 2008-2009 metrics:** nearly **400 million user hours in 2008** (+61% on 2007); a record 112 million hours in Q4 2008; peak concurrency **76,000** in Q4 2008; 1.76 billion m2 of resident-owned land; user-to-user transactions of about **US$361 million** in 2008, +54% (Source: https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/PCWorld/story?id=6688909). The **all-time peak concurrency was 88,000** (2009); Q3 2009 peak was "more than 77,000", the drop attributed by Linden to its new policy restricting bots; "monthly repeat logins" grew 23% September 2008 to September 2009 (Source: https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/352/c8/302580/1.html ; https://www.engadget.com/2009-08-13-linden-lab-publishes-second-life-second-quarter-metrics.html).
- **Mid-2010s:** CEO Ebbe Altberg cited roughly **900,000 monthly active users** around SL's 12th year (2015); later summaries describe a plateau around **500,000 MAU** with flat logins and transactions despite steady sign-ups (Source: https://www.computerworld.com/article/1343403/linden-lab-holds-to-grand-plans-for-second-life.html ; https://www.engadget.com/tag/oldbie). A 2019 estimate put "regular users" at about 600,000 (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2019/09/18/why-second-life-still-has-600000-regular-users-after-16-years/). The "400,000 sign-ups per month" figure attributed to Rod Humble (2013) was not found in any extract (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **First-hour loss.** Altberg spent a June 2014 TPV developer meeting on retention and the new-user experience ("lots of different types of users... make it easier for some users without losing the power for other users") and his first in-world chat identified "the not very intuitive viewer interface with the steep learning curve and high client performance requirements" as what stops people logging on a second time (Source: https://modemworld.me/2014/06/29/ebbe-altberg-on-user-retention-now-and-in-the-future/ ; https://modemworld.me/lab-chat-1-ebbe-altberg-concerning-second-life/). Rosedale's repeated statements that most newcomers were lost in the first hour before meeting anyone are (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **2025-2026 status (third-party trackers only):** 2025 peak concurrency **49,798 on 24 March 2025**; no peak above 48,000 after January; mid-December 2025 daily median about 32,000-35,000 and daily minimum 25,000-28,000; **26,663 regions on 10 August 2025, of which about 9,033 Linden-owned**, with Linden-owned regions down about 800 during 2025 and private estates also declining (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/12/15/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-mid-december-2025-update/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/06/23/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-1st-to-21st-june-2025/). Ownership change to the Oberwager-led group (2020) and Rosedale's return as adviser (January 2022) were not reachable (prior knowledge, unsearched).

### 2. Why newcomers left: viewer, hardware, orientation, empty world

- **Viewer complexity and hardware** were named by Linden's own CEO as the primary second-login blocker (see above). Social Media Today's 2007 summary of the Wired piece listed "the technology limitations of Linden Labs' servers", "a kludgy user interface" and "the excessive time it takes to find and get to anything" (Source: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/second-life-massive-marketing-self-deception).
- **Viewer 2 and Basic mode.** Viewer 2.6 (beta, 2011) introduced "Basic mode", a simplified interface that was the default for first-time installs while existing users kept "Advanced" (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Release_Notes/Second_Life_Beta_Viewer/2.6). Critics noted it removed avatar customisation, offering only 24 preset avatars, and argued it was aimed at "a different market share" rather than newbies; voice was added to Basic mode in May 2011 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2011/04/11 ; https://modemworld.me/2011/05/24/). Its later withdrawal is (prior knowledge, unsearched). Viewer 2's public beta date (23 Feb 2010) is (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **Third-party viewer dominance.** Firestorm took the #1 spot among viewers by total user time in 2012; Linden metrics cited in 2015 showed **542,967 unique Firestorm users across 9.9 million sessions in a 30-day period**; in June 2025 Linden put Firestorm on the official viewer download page (Source: https://modemworld.me/2012/09/05/firestorms-2-and-1-celebrating-the-highs-and-lows-of-a-tpv/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/firestorm-by-far-most-popular-on-hypergrid/ ; https://modemworld.me/2025/06/19/). No published majority-share percentage was found.
- **Empty world, measured.** At 2008 peak, 76,000 concurrent avatars over roughly 25,000-30,000 regions is under three avatars per 256 m region; in 2025, a 32,000 median over 26,663 regions is about 1.2 per region (derived from the figures cited above). The 2007 Wired traffic scores make the point for brands: Sears on IBM's Innovation Island scored **281**, Coca-Cola's Virtual Thirst pavilion **27**, while the most-visited places were free-money and adult areas (Source: https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56). The LA Times (July 2007) described "a blank schedule of events" at Sun's site and "a deserted Dell Island" (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2). Orientation Island/Help Island/Welcome Island/Learning Island cycling and the per-region avatar cap are (prior knowledge, unsearched).

### 3. Griefing, CopyBot, gambling and banks

- **CopyBot (November 2006)** came out of libsecondlife, a reverse-engineering project started with Linden's approval; its release made it trivial to strip the permission check and copy almost any object or avatar appearance marked no-copy, though scripts (server-side) were safe. Shop owners closed stores in protest and a "general strike" was called; designer Veronica Brown (about US$60,000 revenue in 2006) was the public face; Linden's CEO promised "a lot of attribution" rather than DRM (Source: https://blog.granneman.com/?p=1042 ; https://www.few.vu.nl/~eliens/media/note-secondlife.html ; https://ryanschultz.com/tag/controversies/). The 14 Nov 2006 ToS-violation declaration is (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **Gambling ban (summer 2007)** erased about **5% of the in-world economy** by one account (Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2007/10/15/223526/financial-woes-in-second-life). The exact 25 July 2007 date is (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **Ginko Financial** froze withdrawals in early August 2007 after a run, converting deposits into "Ginko Perpetual Bonds"; it owed about **L$200 million (~US$750,000)** and had offered 40%+ annual interest (Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2007/10/15/223526/financial-woes-in-second-life ; https://lists.artdesign.unsw.edu.au/pipermail/empyre/2007-August/msg00100.html).
- **Banking ban:** from **22 January 2008** no in-world object could offer interest or return on investment without proof of government registration or a financial charter; Linden said it would not act as a bank regulator and that L$ carry no redemption guarantee; bank runs followed the announcement (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2008/01/08/virtual-banking-banned-in-second-life ; https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/17927/second-life-cracks-down-on-unregulated-banks).
- Grey-goo attacks, the Anshe Chung CNET interview (20 Dec 2006), political griefing, and the 2007 age-play/age-verification episode are (prior knowledge, unsearched).

### 4. Adult content and Zindra (2009)

- **12 March 2009:** Linden announced it would "geographically separate" adult content so it was accessible "only to those who ought to (and who desire to) access it"; estate owners with adult content had to flag, not move (Source: https://www.theregister.co.uk/2009/03/13/sadville_adult_continent/ ; https://gwynethllewelyn.net/2009/03/12/second-lifes-new-red-light-district/4/ ; https://archive.techdirt.com/articles/20090313/1242454109.shtml). Techdirt's headline captured the business framing: "Linden Labs thinks it's the sex that's keeping businesses out of Second Life".
- **15 June 2009:** Viewer 1.23 ("Adult enabled") shipped with the new Adult rating and age verification by documents or payment status, and the continent built as "Ursula" opened as **Zindra** (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2009-06-16-second-life-moves-to-1-23-opens-adult-continent-allows-more-co.html ; https://modemworld.me/2009/06/16/zindra-unveiled/).
- **Scale and migration:** the initial map had **157 regions**, expanded 3-10 July 2009 due to demand; about **850 move tickets** were submitted between 29 June and 24 July 2009; remaining Zindra land was auctioned from 8 September 2009; publicly promoted adult content had to be on Zindra or private Adult regions after **15 September 2009** (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Zindra ; https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Adult_content).

### 5. Corporate and government presence 2006-2008, and the exodus

- **Reuters** announced its bureau in early October 2006 (Adam Pasick as "Adam Reuters", later joined by Eric Krangel as "Eric Reuters"), with a Reuters News Centre HUD; it closed in **November 2008** after The Register noted Pasick "hasn't filed a story in months" (Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna15289096 ; https://thebaron.info/news/article/2008/11/21/reuters-closes-virtual-bureau).
- **Embassies:** the Maldives opened the first virtual embassy on Diplomacy Island days before Sweden; the Swedish Institute's **Second House of Sweden opened 30 May 2007**, a replica of the Washington embassy, at a cost of 400,000 kronor (about US$57,000), with "no political function" (Source: https://www.thelocal.se/20070522/7379 ; https://www.thelocal.se/20070529/7441 ; https://scandasia.com/3335-welcome-to-the-virtual-swedish-embassy/).
- **IBM:** on **27 September 2007** Italian IBM unions picketed IBM's SL campus over a cancelled EUR1,000 bonus, with about **1,850 avatars from 30 countries**; an agreement followed in October and IBM Italy CEO Andrea Pontremoli resigned about 20 days later (Source: https://www.theregister.co.uk/2007/09/26/ibm_italy_strike_second_life/ ; https://salon.com/2007/09/18/virtual_labor_economics ; https://www.commondreams.org/views/2009/01/16/social-movements-20). IBM's ~US$10 million 2006 commitment is (prior knowledge, unsearched).
- **Backlash:** Wired's July 2007 cover story opens with Coca-Cola's Michael Donnelly finding American Apparel, Reebok and Scion sites with "nobody else around" ("I felt like I was in The Shining"); Social Media Today: marketers "marching like lemmings off the Second Life cliff" (Source: https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56 ; https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/second-life-massive-marketing-self-deception). TechCrunch, 16 July 2007: "Marketers realizing that Second Life ain't worth the trouble" (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2007/07/16/marketers-realizing-that-second-life-aint-worth-the-trouble/amp/). American Apparel's 2006 opening/2007 closure, the CSI: NY and The Office episodes (Oct 2007) and the April 2008 House hearing are (prior knowledge, unsearched).

### 6. Governance

- **Openspace/Homestead pricing (Oct-Nov 2008).** Linden announced roughly a **66% rise** (about US$50/month) on Openspace regions; after outcry, M Linden's 5 November 2008 revision converted non-compliant Openspaces to "Homesteads" at **US$75 to $95 on 5 January 2009, then $95 to $125 in July 2009**, with avatar and prim limits; forum reports said estate owners were leaving and Openspace counts fell sharply (Source: https://modemworld.me/2008/10/31/ ; https://gwynethllewelyn.net/2008/11/11/wrong-answer-mr-m-linden/4/ ; https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/354/3d/291223/1.html).
- **2010 restructuring.** On **9 June 2010** Linden Lab cut about **30% of staff** (headcount reported above 300, so roughly 100), merged product and engineering, and promised a browser-based viewer; CEO Mark Kingdon stepped down on **24 June 2010**, Rosedale became interim CEO and CFO Bob Komin added COO (Source: https://phys.org/news/2010-06-life-creator-linden-lab-staff.html ; https://www.theregister.com/2010/06/09/linden_labs_lays_off_30_per_cent_of_staff/ ; https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-24-linden-lab-ceo-steps-down.html ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/linden-lab-ceo-resigns-shortly-after-layoffs).
- **Education discount.** In late 2010 (THE Journal report, 3 Nov 2010) Linden said the 50% education/non-profit discount would end **1 January 2011**, effectively doubling region costs, alongside the Teen Grid shutdown; institutions could lock the old rate with 6-24 month prepaid contracts; EDUCAUSE recorded the "instant boost" this gave OpenSim; Linden **restored the 50% discount in 2013**, to which a University of Richmond director replied "it's too late for many of us. Linden Lab blew it" (Source: https://thejournal.com/Articles/2010/11/03/Linden-Lab-To-End-Second-Life-Educational-Discounts.aspx ; https://er.educause.edu/articles/2011/4/second-life-is-dead-long-live-second-life ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/08/educators-wary-of-latest-linden-offer/ ; https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/wiredcampus/academics-discuss-mass-migration-from-second-life).
- **2013 ToS.** In **August 2013** Linden revised Section 2.3 to a licence covering sublicensing and derivative works in all media; **Renderosity and CG Textures banned use of their assets in SL** ("Second Life's new TOS conflicts with our Renderosity license"); an in-world protest meeting was held 29 September 2013; Linden said its intent "may have been misconstrued" but left the text unchanged until **July 2014**, when it limited rights to use "inworld or otherwise on the Service" and made sale/sublicensing require an affirmative user action (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms ; https://modemworld.me/2013/09/30/tos-in-world-meeting-september-29th-a-personal-perspective/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/blogs/entry/1294-updates-to-section-23-of-the-terms-of-service/ ; https://modemworld.me/2014/07/16/lab-updates-section-2-3-of-their-terms-of-service-will-it-calm-doubts/).
- **Resident self-governance: the CDS.** Founded **21 September 2004** as the Neualtenburg Project (founder Ulrika Zugzwang argued a constitution had to precede an elected body); after a founders' split the city continued as Neufreistadt and renamed itself the Confederation of Democratic Simulators by referendum when more regions joined. Government: a three-person Executive, a **Representative Assembly of five** drawn from several parties, and a **Scientific Council of five** headed by a Dean; land ownership equals citizenship with the vote; it calls itself "the oldest democracy in Second Life" and its land store lists six regions today (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Confederation_of_Democratic_Simulators ; https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/103/d9/22948/1.html ; https://steamlands.fandom.com/wiki/Neufreistadt ; https://marketplace.secondlife.com/stores/130883 ; https://whyy.org/segments/lessons-from-second-life-democracy-in-the-metaverse). Linden's "hands-off" posture, the Community Standards "Big Six" and Bragg v. Linden (2007) are (prior knowledge, unsearched).

### 7. Community events, education, disability and what SL did well

- **SL Birthday.** For **SL9B (2012)** Linden announced the theme and withdrew from participation at the same time; volunteers built a central celebration in about a month, "organised by residents for residents, with no direct involvement from Linden Lab", on regions donated by Dream Seeker Estates (ten, then twenty) (Source: https://modemworld.me/2012/04/17/sl9b-end-of-an-era/ ; https://modemworld.me/2012/06/17/sl9b-let-the-festivities-begin/). Linden later resumed running it: **SL20B (2023)** had 321 exhibits and daily Lab Gab programming; **SL22B ran 20 June to 20 July 2025**, theme "Myths and Legends" (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/blogs/entry/13615-sl20b-is-open-celebrate-two-decades-of-innovation-on-second-life's-20th-birthday/ ; https://modemworld.me/2025/03/13/sl22b-theme-exhibitor-applications-now-open/).
- **Disability.** Virtual Ability (Alice Krueger, "Gentle Heron", MS, wheelchair user) formed in 2007, incorporated as a 501(c)(3) in January 2008, and **co-won the first Linden Prize in 2009 with Studio Wikitecture, US$10,000 each**; members include blind, deaf, stroke, amputee and homebound people who "skydive, fish, mountain climb" via avatars (Source: https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Virtual_mobility_for_disabled_wins_Second_Life_prize_999.html ; https://virtualability.org/history/ ; https://www.kunc.org/health/2013-07-08/coloradans-with-disabilities-connect-in-second-life-for-social-health-perks).
- **Education** scale (150+ universities per NMC), Relay for Life totals, Burning Life and Boellstorff's ethnography are (prior knowledge, unsearched); the 2010-2013 discount episode above is the verified governance story.
- **What SL did uniquely well, on the evidence:** a real creator economy (US$361M user-to-user in 2008), an organised labour action that moved a real employer, a functioning resident democracy running for 21 years, and a disability community that an outside funder recognised with the platform's first prize. All were built by residents on land they paid for.

## Patterns / root causes

1. **The funnel never moved.** Linden itself said 90-day retention was "about 10%" and "unchanged" in 2006-2007; Wired found 85% of avatars abandoned; eight years later the active base plateaued at 500,000-900,000 against tens of millions of sign-ups. Every orientation redesign addressed the same loss and none changed it.
2. **Emptiness was structural.** 76,000 concurrent avatars over ~30,000 regions in 2008, and 32,000 over 26,663 in 2025, is a ghost town by arithmetic; brand sites with traffic scores of 27 were the predictable result.
3. **One interface for two audiences.** Linden's CEO named viewer complexity and hardware as the second-login blocker; Basic mode was derided by incumbents for having 24 avatars; the community's answer was Firestorm, which Linden eventually put on its own download page.
4. **Governance by decree, then retreat.** Openspace (2008), education pricing (2010), the ToS (2013) and Zindra (2009) all followed the same arc: unilateral announcement, outcry, partial reversal 6-36 months later, trust lost permanently ("Linden Lab blew it").
5. **Finance without a regulator.** Gambling, Ginko and banks showed that a hands-off platform ends up acting as a regulator anyway, late and by ban.
6. **Content rights are trust infrastructure.** CopyBot (2006) and the 2013 ToS both triggered creator strikes because permissions and licences were the only thing protecting livelihoods.
7. **The durable things were resident-owned institutions**: CDS, Virtual Ability, SL9B, the Italian IBM strike.

## Design implications for nolife

- Publish first-hour, 7-day, 30-day and 90-day retention from day one; define first-hour success as "met a person or joined a group", not "made an avatar".
- Replace fixed region sharding with density management: newcomers always land where people and a host are; never show an emptier world than the population allows.
- Ship two interfaces over one world (consumer and creator/pro) and do not let incumbents veto the newcomer client; expect a third-party client ecosystem and bless it early.
- Thin client first: low hardware floor, instant first render, streaming only what is in view.
- Purpose without quests: scheduled hosted activities, apprenticeship routes into music, building, education and disability communities.
- Constitutional change control: published charter, notice periods and a resident assembly with bounded but real powers over mainland rules, pricing and ToS, so Openspace/education/2013-style shocks cannot happen by blog post.
- Creator-first content licence from launch, narrowly scoped to operating the service; server-side permissions and provenance so that a CopyBot cannot collapse the economy.
- Maturity zoning and age assurance designed in from the start, with adult communities given first-class segregated space rather than a forced 2009-style migration.
- Financial rules up front: no unregulated interest-bearing products, clear L$-equivalent redemption terms.
- Price guarantees for institutional communities (education, non-profit, disability); they are the reputation of the world.
- Sell brands outcomes (hosts, events, measured traffic), never land.

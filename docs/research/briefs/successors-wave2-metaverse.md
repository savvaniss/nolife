# The 2016-2026 metaverse wave: postmortem brief for "nolife"

Scope: Horizon Worlds, AltspaceVR / Microsoft Mesh, Decentraland, The Sandbox, Voxels, Somnium Space, Otherside, Spatial, Niantic, VRChat, Rec Room, plus the NVIDIA Omniverse and Apple Vision Pro hardware bets. Compiled 2026-10-09. Method note: 17 web searches completed (extended mode); direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox's DNS, and the shared search budget ran out before a final batch of confirmations, so a few items below are marked as thin or single-sourced. Company claims and independent measurements are labelled where they differ.

## Findings

### 1. The hype envelope (2021-2022)

Facebook renamed itself Meta on 28 October 2021, explicitly betting the company on the metaverse as "the successor to the mobile internet" (Source: https://opb.org/article/2021/10/28/facebook-rebrands-as-meta-to-emphasize-metaverse-vision). Sell-side and consulting forecasts followed: Citi's April 2022 "Metaverse and Money" report put the 2030 addressable market at $8-13 trillion with up to five billion users, while noting it would need a ">1,000x" improvement in computational efficiency (Source: https://fortune.com/2022/04/01/citi-Metaverse-economy-13-trillion-2030). McKinsey's June 2022 report projected ~$5 trillion of value by 2030, $2.6 trillion of it e-commerce, based on a survey of 3,400 consumers and executives (Source: https://cointelegraph.com/news/metaverse-could-be-worth-5-trillion-by-2030-mckinsey-report). These were forecasts, not measurements; nothing in the 2023-2026 record approaches them.

Virtual land was the speculative instrument. Average Decentraland parcel prices peaked at $37,238 in February 2022 and fell to $5,163 by 1 August 2022 per WeMeta data (Source: https://cointelegraph.com/markets/metaverse-housing-bubble-bursting-virtual-land-prices-crash-85-amid-waning-interest); Dune Analytics data cited by Benzinga shows a different series ($4,683 in Feb 2022 to $1,878 in Dec 2022), so the absolute level is contested but the ~60-85% collapse is not (Source: https://benzinga.com/z/30275813). Monthly trading volume across metaverse land platforms fell 96% from January to ~$2 million in November 2022 (Source: https://www.theblock.co/post/197062/ncrooks@theblock.co).

### 2. Meta Horizon Worlds: missed targets, pivots to teens, then to mobile, then a near-shutdown

Horizon Worlds opened to US/Canada adults in December 2021 (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_Worlds). Meta's last public figure was 300,000 monthly users in February 2022 (Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2022/10/17/metas-horizon-worlds-has-somehow-lost-100000-players-in-eight-months/). In October 2022 the Wall Street Journal, citing internal documents, reported under 200,000 MAU against an end-2022 target of 500,000 that had already been cut to 280,000; most users did not return after their first month; usage had declined steadily since spring; and only 9% of worlds were ever visited by 50 or more people, most never visited at all (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/15/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-losing-users-falling-short-of-goals.html; https://musically.com/2022/10/17/metas-horizon-worlds-200k-active-users/). In February 2023 Fortune reported the target had been reset to 500,000 for H1 2023 and 1 million by end-2023 while actual usage was "just above 200,000", and that Meta's remedy was opening the platform to teens (Source: https://fortune.com/2023/02/07/metas-flagship-metaverse-is-floundering-and-it-wants-teens-to-rescue-it; https://gizmodo.com/meta-metaverse-facebook-horizon-worlds-vr-1850082068).

From November 2024 Meta began an explicit push to make mobile the main entry point; in early 2025 developers could ship mobile-only games, and the titles Meta showcased in 2025 (Grow A Garden, Steal A Brainrot, Yeet Yourself) were short-session, Roblox-style games aimed at younger players; a Meta product manager framed the goal as "a billion people connected" which "couldn't rely on VR headset sales alone" (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/inside-horizon-worlds-mobile-fueled-comeback-in-2025/). On 19 February 2026 Reality Labs content VP Samantha Ryan announced Meta was "explicitly separating our Quest VR platform from our Worlds platform and shifting the focus of Worlds to be almost exclusively mobile" (Source: https://www.prismnews.com/news/meta-shifts-horizon-worlds-to-mobile-and-separates-it-from-quest-vr). On 17-18 March 2026 Meta told its community Horizon Worlds would be delisted from the Quest store on 31 March and shut down in VR on 15 June; within 24-48 hours CTO Andrew Bosworth reversed it ("we will keep Horizon Worlds working in VR for existing games to support the fans who've reached out"), but only legacy Unity-built worlds stay in VR, worlds built on the newer Horizon Engine are flatscreen-only, no new VR content is planned, and the app will not be promoted on the store (Source: https://euronews.com/next/2026/03/20/meta-u-turns-on-horizon-worlds-vr-shutdown-after-user-backlash; https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/03/20/meta-reverses-decision-to-end-horizon-worlds-vr-metaverse/).

Reality Labs financials (Meta's own segment reporting, so these are audited company figures): Q4 2025 operating loss $6.02 billion on $955 million revenue, the segment's largest quarterly loss since reporting began in Q4 2020 (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/28/metas-reality-labs-posts-6point02-billion-loss-in-fourth-quarter.html). Q1 2026 loss $4,028 million on $402 million revenue; Q2 2026 loss $4,619 million on $431 million revenue; H1 2026 loss $8,647 million (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm). Media tallies put cumulative losses at over $80 billion by end-2025 and roughly $88 billion by mid-2026; Meta does not publish a cumulative figure (Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113304-meta-reality-labs-division-lost-46-billion-q2.html). On 13 January 2026 Meta cut roughly 10% of Reality Labs (reports range 1,000-1,500 jobs), closed first-party VR studios Twisted Pixel, Sanzaru and Armature, hit Horizon Worlds teams, and framed the move as redirecting investment from VR to AI and Ray-Ban Meta glasses (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/13/meta-lays-off-vr-employees-underscoring-zuckerbergs-pivot-to-ai.html; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/extended-reality/meta-shutters-three-vr-studios-as-part-of-reality-labs-layoffs). CNBC described the result as fears of a "VR winter" (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/24/metas-reality-labs-cuts-sparked-fears-of-a-vr-winter.html).

### 3. AltspaceVR and Microsoft Mesh: consumer social VR abandoned twice, then enterprise 3D shrunk into Teams

AltspaceVR announced it was shutting for lack of funds in summer 2017 and was rescued by Microsoft about two months after the planned closure (Source: https://roadtovr.com/microsoft-social-vr-xr-interface-layoffs/). In January 2023, alongside 10,000 company-wide layoffs that also hit HoloLens 2, IVAS and the entire Mixed Reality Toolkit team, Microsoft announced AltspaceVR would close on 10 March 2023 and that focus would shift to Microsoft Mesh, a workplace product (Source: https://uploadvr.com/microsoft-kills-altspace/; https://gamesbeat.com/microsoft-shutters-altspacevr-amid-company-wide-layoffs/). Users were told to export photos, friend lists and event data before the date (Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/microsoft-shuts-down-metaverse-altspacevr).

Mesh, announced in 2021 and supporting Unity-built environments with up to 330 participants, was itself retired: the Mesh Toolkit on 24 June 2025, and the Mesh PC/Quest apps, mesh.cloud.microsoft and the Teams "Immersive space (3D)" view on 1 December 2025, replaced by "immersive events" inside Teams that support 16 participants in standard sessions and require a Teams Enterprise licence to host (Source: https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftteamsblog/immersive-events-in-microsoft-teams-now-generally-available/4468628; https://www.uctoday.com/immersive-workplace-xr-tech/microsoft-ends-mesh-and-ushers-in-immersive-events-for-teams-what-you-need-to-know/). The Register tied this to Microsoft ending HoloLens hardware development and its Army contract in 2024 (Source: https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/12/02/microsoft-mops-up-mesh-after-another-metaverse-misfire/2385405).

### 4. Decentraland: the 38-DAU episode and the measurement gap

On 7 October 2022 CoinDesk, using DappRadar data, reported Decentraland had 38 "daily active users" and The Sandbox 522, against a ~$1.3 billion token ecosystem (Source: https://www.coindesk.com/web3/2022/10/07/its-lonely-in-the-metaverse-decentralands-38-daily-active-users-in-a-13b-ecosystem). DappRadar's metric counts unique wallets that transact with the platform's smart contracts, so logging in, chatting or attending an event does not register (Source: https://blockworks.com/news/metaverse-platforms-set-the-record-straight-about-daily-active-users). Decentraland's creative director Sam Hamilton countered with ~8,000 average daily users and 56,697 MAU for September 2022, figures that are self-reported and measured as "enters and moves out of the initial parcel" (Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/this-billion-dollar-metaverse-claims-it-has-7000-daily-users-not-38/; https://decentraland.org/blog/announcements/how-many-dau-does-decentraland-have). DappRadar said it would recalculate (Source: https://www.coindesk.com/web3/2022/10/11/dappradar-says-its-recalculating-decentraland-user-data) and later published ~650 DAU (Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/dappradar-says-decentraland-650-daily-094641218.html). The Foundation's own blog concedes "there is no official, public tool that tracks the exact DAU". Both numbers are "true" for different definitions; neither is independently verified. Land floor price was 0.64 ETH in May 2023 (Source: https://cointelegraph.com/markets/metaverse-housing-bubble-bursting-virtual-land-prices-crash-85-amid-waning-interest).

2025-2026 state (thin): no layoffs were found in the searches run. Executive Director Yemel Jardi announced in May 2026 that he would step down to an advisory role, with Esteban Ordano as interim ED; the Foundation is funded by a 10-year irrevocable vesting contract running to February 2030, which means it can persist at low usage until then (Source: https://forum.decentraland.org/t/transitioning-leadership-at-the-decentraland-foundation/25264). Third-party MAU claims for 2025 ("~300,000") come from price-prediction sites and should be treated as unverified (Source: https://coincub.com/price-prediction/decentraland/).

### 5. The Sandbox: $350M of land sold, then half the company gone

The Sandbox sold more than $350 million in land parcels in 2021 and raised about $300 million over eight years (Source: https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/08/28/the-sandbox-cuts-50-staff-restructures-as-animoca-brands-take-control). In 2022 on-chain DAU was ~500 while the company claimed ~200,000 MAU (Source: https://medium.com/@observer1/empty-metaverses-decentraland-and-the-sandbox-only-has-less-than-1-000-daily-active-users-baa73a0e0468). In August 2025, per The Big Whale via CoinDesk, it cut more than half of ~250 staff, closed offices in Argentina, Uruguay, South Korea, Thailand, Turkey and (expected) Lyon, sidelined co-founders Arthur Madrid and Sebastien Borget under Animoca CEO Yat Siu with Robby Yung as CEO, and was reported to have DAU "dwindled to a few hundred", some alleged to be bots in South America (Animoca disputed the bot claim and said DAU swings with Alpha Seasons); the treasury was estimated at $100-300 million and the new revenue idea was a memecoin launch hub on Base (Source: https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/08/28/the-sandbox-cuts-50-staff-restructures-as-animoca-brands-take-control; https://cryptonews.com/news/the-sandbox-founders-exit-as-animoca-assumes-full-control-in-major-overhaul/). Company-side 2025 figures (two Alpha Seasons, 144,000 players, 7.9 million quests) appear only in low-quality aggregator posts (Source: https://www.bydfi.com/en/cointalk/sandbox-sand-price-2026-analysis).

### 6. Otherside, Voxels, Somnium: land-first worlds that never became places

Yuga Labs' Otherdeed mint (late April 2022) sold 55,000 parcels at 305 ApeCoin (~$5,800) each for ~$317 million and burned an additional ~$172 million in Ethereum gas; buyers had to pass KYC (Source: https://www.theblock.co/linked/144549/otherside-land-nfts-sell-out-in-hours-as-yuga-labs-rakes-in-317-million; https://blockonomi.com/yuga-labs-otherside-nfts-317-million-sale-200-million-gas-fee-etherscan-bombs/). Yuga laid off staff in October 2023 (keeping >120 employees) (Source: https://www.dlnews.com/articles/people-culture/bored-apes-creator-yuga-labs-announces-layoffs/), and in April 2024 returning CEO Greg Solano said "Yuga lost its way" and refocused on Otherside (Source: https://cointelegraph.com/news/yuga-labs-restructures-layoffs-new-exec). In May 2025 it sold the CryptoPunks IP to the NODE Foundation (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoPunks). No search surfaced a shipped, populated Otherside world as of 2025-2026; treat its status as unresolved.

Cryptovoxels rebranded to Voxels in May 2022 to reach a wider audience, deliberately keeps land scarcer than rivals, and has no token (its COLR token is described as dead) (Source: https://medium.com/@cryptovoxels/cryptovoxels-is-voxels-174b39c60923; https://hypenoon.com/cryptovoxels/). Somnium Space's land peaks were 2021-2022 (a 28-parcel estate for ~$444,000; ~$1.5 million in one sale week) (Source: https://dappradar.com/blog/virtual-estate-in-somnium-space-sells-for-444-470); an undated OpenSea snapshot shows ~1,034 unique owners and a ~$125 floor (Source: https://opensea.io/collection/somnium-space). No reliable 2024-2025 usage data was found for either.

### 7. Spatial and Niantic: pivot out of "metaverse" entirely

Spatial began as an AR collaboration workspace, pivoted in 2021 to NFT showrooms, announced in December 2021 it would be "the metaverse for cultural events" with $25 million new funding ($50 million total) (Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211214005169/en/Spatial-Unveils-New-Market-Focus-To-Pioneer-A-Free-And-Open-Metaverse-With-25-Million-In-New-Funding), and per a November 2023 Wired piece cited by Wikipedia "pivots again, this time to games"; it now describes itself as a Unity/C# UGC gaming platform publishing to web, mobile and VR (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_(platform); https://spatial.io/about). Niantic, the "real-world metaverse" company, sold its games division (Pokémon GO, Pikmin Bloom, Monster Hunter Now, Campfire, Wayfarer) to Scopely for $3.5 billion on 12 March 2025 (~$3.85 billion including $350 million distributed cash), keeping Ingress and Peridot in a new geospatial-AI company, Niantic Spatial, funded with ~$250 million ($200 million Niantic, $50 million Scopely) (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/12/pokemon-go-maker-niantic-is-selling-its-games-division-to-scopely-for-3-5b; https://shacknews.com/article/143439/niantic-sells-pokemon-go-scopely).

### 8. VRChat: the survivor, and why it is fragile

Growth came from hardware and community, not marketing: the Quest 2 launch pushed VRChat to a then-record 24,000 concurrent users in late 2020, more than half in VR (Source: https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-record-concurrent-users-traffic-quest-2/). On 25 July 2022 VRChat announced Easy Anti-Cheat, which disabled all client mods including accessibility tools such as VRC-CC closed captions; a Canny ticket demanding reversal passed 22,000 votes in 24 hours, Steam recent reviews dropped to "Mostly Negative" with over 5,000 negative reviews since 1 July, and on 26 July the company said it was reprioritising its roadmap around the feedback, starting with accessibility (Source: https://www.dazeddigital.com/science-tech/article/56662/1/why-thousands-of-users-are-abandoning-the-worlds-most-popular-vr-game; https://caniplaythat.com/2022/08/09/vrchat-eac-update-backlash-accessibility-worries-promises/).

VRChat launched its Creator Economy with paid subscriptions (payments via Tilia) on 5 December 2023 (Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231205640487/en/VRChat-Launches-Creator-Economy-via-Paid-Subscriptions). In June 2024 it laid off ~30% of staff; CEO Graham Gaylor said the company overhired during the pandemic boom, that the user base had shrunk year over year, and that it lacked a management team; departing staff got 12 weeks severance, six months healthcare and a reduced store fee if they sold in the Creator Economy (Source: https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-lays-off-30-of-company/; https://www.shacknews.com/article/140244/vrchat-layoffs-2024). In May 2025 it opened an avatar marketplace paid in VRChat Credits; the revenue split is not public beyond "creators keep the largest cut" (Source: https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/20/vrchat-virual-reality-avatar-marketplace-creator-economy/).

Concurrency kept rising after the layoffs: 136,567 on 1 January 2025 (third-party API tracker, +30% YoY), 148,886 on 1 January 2026 (announced by community lead Tupper) with normal weekend peaks of 120-125k, and a corrected 158,192 at a Japanese-language concert in February 2026 (Source: https://www.roadtovr.com/vrchat-new-record-new-years-eve-2025-2026/; https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-key-stats-japan-growth-concurrents/). SteamDB recorded a Steam-only peak of 79,593 on 15 February 2026, nine years after release (Source: https://steamcommunity.com/groups/SteamDB/announcements/detail/7285229827084255392). Japan's share of vrchat.com visits rose from 12.9% in 2023 to ~27.5% in December 2025 per Similarweb (Source: https://vrdb.app/blog/vrchat-metaverse-growing-japan-2026-v2). Much of the avatar economy lives off-platform: pixiv's Booth reported 2024 3D-model transaction volume above 5.8 billion yen, up ~187% YoY (covers all 3D models, not only VRChat) (Source: https://creator-economy.org/vrchat-creator-economy-worlds-avatars-real-money/). Caveat: VRChat publishes no regular user figures; most numbers are staff posts or third-party trackers.

### 9. Rec Room: the UGC-everywhere strategy and the margin that killed it

Rec Room cut 16% of staff in March 2025 and about half (141 positions) in August 2025 (Source: https://roadtovr.com/rec-room-layoff-half-staff-august-2025/; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/developer-rec-room-lays-off-about-half-its-staff). Co-founder Cameron Brown's explanation: the company tried to be a universal creation platform across VR, PC, console and mobile; top PC/VR creators drove growth, but mobile and console creation tools under-delivered and the "Maker AI" bridge "just frustrated our more impactful creators"; the company was "too small" for the vision and "too big" to pivot (Source: https://www.geekwire.com/2025/rec-room-lays-off-half-its-staff-in-major-shakeup-at-seattle-gaming-startup/). The unit economics were explicit: UGC revenue grew 70% YoY, but Rec Room kept ~30 cents per dollar on UGC items after platform fees and creator cuts versus ~70 cents on first-party items, while millions of low-quality creations added moderation and infrastructure cost (Source: https://roadtovr.com/rec-room-layoff-half-staff-august-2025/). On 31 March 2026 the company, once valued at $3.5 billion and claiming 150 million lifetime players, announced it was shutting down: new accounts blocked immediately, token sales ended 1 May, final creator payout and go-dark at 12 p.m. PT on 1 June 2026, saying it could no longer count on raising more money (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/social-gaming-platform-rec-room-once-valued-at-3-5b-is-shutting-down/; https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/03/rec-room-shuts-down-after-decade-and-150-million-players/).

### 10. Hardware bets: Vision Pro and Omniverse

Apple has never disclosed Vision Pro sales. IDC, via the Financial Times, estimated ~390,000 units shipped in 2024; The Information reported in October 2024 that production had been cut to ~1,000 units/day (half of peak) with components already built for 500-600k units; manufacturing partner Luxshare reportedly halted production in early 2025; one estimate puts the 2025 holiday quarter at ~45,000 units; Sensor Tower data shows Apple cut Vision Pro digital ad spend by more than 95% in 2025 (Source: https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/technology/apple-cuts-vision-pro-headset-prodcution/; https://www.pymnts.com/apple/2026/apple-retreats-on-vision-pro-as-consumer-demand-falls-short/; https://hypebeast.com/2026/1/apple-vision-pro-faces-cuts-as-spatial-bet-stalls). All figures are third-party estimates.

NVIDIA's Omniverse is the one "metaverse" asset that grew, by dropping the word. In 2022 it was pitched as the "industrial metaverse" with Siemens (Source: https://futurumgroup.com/insights/nvidia-siemens-partner-to-target-the-industrial-metaverse/); at CES January 2025 it was re-framed as a "digital twin operating system" beside the Cosmos physical-AI models, and by October 2025 the Mega blueprint for factory-scale digital twins listed Belden, Caterpillar, Foxconn, Lucid, Toyota, TSMC and Wistron as users (Source: https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-Expands-Omniverse-With-Generative-Physical-AI/default.aspx; https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-US-Manufacturing-and-Robotics-Leaders-Drive-Americas-Reindustrialization-With-Physical-AI/default.aspx). Adoption claims are press-release level.

## Patterns / root causes

1. Headset-gated distribution capped the audience. Every VR-first world hit the same ceiling: Horizon's 200k MAU, AltspaceVR's closure, Rec Room's dependence on PC/VR creators, and Meta's own admission that "a billion people" cannot come from headset sales. Even Apple's premium entry shipped ~390k units in its launch year and then went quiet. The survivors (VRChat, Horizon-mobile, Spatial, Roblox-likes) are cross-platform with VR as an option, not a requirement.

2. Land-first economics inverted the order of operations. Decentraland, Sandbox, Otherside, Somnium and Voxels sold scarce real estate before there was anything to do on it. Land prices moved with crypto, not with usage, and when speculation left (96% volume decline in 2022) nothing remained to retain visitors. The 38-vs-8,000 DAU fight shows that no one, including the platforms, could prove anyone was there.

3. Measurement opacity was a symptom and a cause. Meta's figures leaked; Decentraland's were self-defined; VRChat publishes none; Sandbox's were disputed as bots. Platforms that could not measure retention could not manage it, and the absence of credible numbers let hype run until it collapsed.

4. Empty worlds compound. Horizon's "9% of worlds visited by 50+ people" and "most users don't return after month one" describe a discovery and density failure: user-generated supply grew faster than the audience, so the median experience was being alone in someone's unfinished room.

5. UGC margins are thin unless the platform owns distribution and moderation cheaply. Rec Room's 30-cents-vs-70-cents disclosure is the clearest number in this brief: a creator economy that pays creators and platform fees leaves the host with a fraction of first-party margin while adding moderation and infra load. VRChat survives partly because much of its economy (Booth, Patreon-style subscriptions) runs off-platform at no cost to it.

6. Corporate owners cut consumer social VR first. AltspaceVR (Microsoft), Mesh (Microsoft), Horizon VR (Meta), Supernatural/first-party studios (Meta): when budgets tightened, enterprise pivots and AI absorbed the money. Communities built on these platforms lost their homes with 60-90 days' notice.

7. Trust shocks hurt more than feature gaps. VRChat's EAC episode (22k votes in 24 hours, review-bombing) and Meta's 48-hour Horizon reversal show that for a social world the community's sense of ownership is the product; unilateral platform changes are existential events.

8. What survived: cross-platform social worlds with strong creator cultures (VRChat, especially in Japan), casual mobile UGC game platforms (Horizon-mobile is copying Roblox), and industrial simulation (Omniverse). What died: VR-only, land-token, and enterprise-meeting metaverses.

## Design implications for nolife

1. Ship PC/mobile/web first with VR as a first-class but optional client; never gate identity, inventory or social graph on a headset. Target the VRChat/Roblox shape, not the Horizon-2021 shape.
2. Do not sell land before there is population. Allocate space by activity and let it be reclaimed when idle; if scarcity is needed for status, make it earned (reputation, tenure) rather than purchased up front.
3. Publish a public, auditable activity metric (DAU defined as "entered, moved, interacted with another user for N minutes"), with a third-party-readable API. Being measurable is a competitive advantage after the 38-DAU era.
4. Solve density by default: instance-merge low-population spaces, route new users to live events and populated hubs, and treat "empty world" as a bug with an SLO (e.g., no newcomer sees fewer than X people in their first session).
5. Design the economy for the host's margin from day one: low-cost moderation (automated plus community), creator take-rate that still leaves the platform >50% on UGC transactions, and first-party anchor content so the platform is not wholly dependent on UGC margin. Learn from Rec Room's 30-cent problem.
6. Make the platform community-governed in practice, not only in branding: a public change-review process for client-affecting updates (the EAC lesson), export/portability guarantees for avatars, worlds and friend lists (the AltspaceVR lesson), and a published sunset covenant (minimum 12 months notice, data export, open-sourcing of world formats).
7. Build for Japan and VTuber/avatar culture explicitly: localisation, avatar-tooling pipelines compatible with Booth/VRM assets, event capacity for 100k+ concurrent attendees (VRChat's records are concert-driven).
8. Keep hardware-bet exposure minimal: do not depend on any single vendor's headset roadmap (Quest line, Vision Pro) given 2026 Reality Labs cuts and Vision Pro's production halt.
9. Use "industrial" techniques (OpenUSD-style interchange, digital-twin tooling) for content import so the world is not locked to one engine; Horizon's Unity-to-Horizon-Engine switch is what stranded its VR content.

# Gap research: consumer law and tax rules bearing on a closed currency, stipends and cash-out

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: R10 (closed currency with published spread), R12 (subscription with stipend), R7 (published all-in take), plus creator cash-out.

## Method note

Fourteen WebSearch calls (the hard cap) were used; page fetching (WebFetch/curl) is blocked in this sandbox, so every statement below rests on the quoted extracts the search engine returned, not on full-text reading of the cited pages. Where the extracts were silent (notably the Star Stable outcome, Fortnite/Roblox actual EU storefront UI, and Tilia's DAC7 posture) the gap is stated explicitly and anything filled in from memory is labelled "prior knowledge". Results reach roughly June 2026 (US litigation) and May 2026 (DFA); anything later is unverified.

## Findings

### 1. Digital Fairness Act (DFA): not yet published

- No search result showed a published Commission proposal. The latest dated commentary (May 2026) says the 2026 Commission Work Programme schedules the DFA for Q4 2026 and that "a concrete draft has not yet been presented"; earlier material said Q3 2026 or "fall 2026". (Source: https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html ; https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act)
- What is known comes from the July-October 2025 consultation and its factual summary: 1,733 of 3,341 respondents backed new binding EU rules on dark patterns, addictive design, video-game features, unfair personalisation and influencer marketing. Within the games section there was "strong support" for (a) displaying real-world prices of items bought with virtual currency, (b) odds disclosure for loot boxes, and (c) letting users switch off features including virtual currencies. (Source: https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102m222/digital-fairness-act-european-commission-publishes-responses-to-consultation ; https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/07/eu-consults-on-digital-fairness-act-big-changes-ahead-for-consumer-facing-platforms)
- The Commission's consumer-law head reportedly singled out in-game virtual currencies and loot boxes as fairness concerns likely inside DFA scope; minors' protection is flagged as a focus, including bans on targeted advertising and personalised pricing to minors and exploration of age verification via the EU digital identity wallet. (Source: https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/interface/2025/the-dmcca/eu-consumer-law-reform-is-coming-but-when-will-it-happen-and-what-will-it-cover ; https://electronlibre.info/?p=103953)
- "Pay-to-progress" did not appear in any extract; the nearest DFA themes are addictive design and pressure tactics. A March 2026 joint call by 200 signatories confirms the file was still pre-proposal then. (Source: https://coface-eu.org/joint-call-for-an-ambitious-digital-fairness-act-to-better-protect-people-online/)

Bottom line: treat the DFA as a Q4 2026 proposal with a 2-3 year path to application; the consultation signals price display, virtual-currency opt-out and minors' protections as its likely shape.

### 2. What applies today: CPC Key Principles and the Star Stable action

- On 21 March 2025 the CPC Network and the Commission published "Key Principles on In-game Virtual Currencies". They are non-binding but are the authorities' stated interpretation of existing law (UCPD, CRD, UCTD) and will guide national enforcement. (Source: https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr ; https://mondaq.com/uk/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1606580/new-european-consumer-protection-guidelines-for-virtual-currencies-in-video-games)
- Core requirements as summarised: show the real-money equivalent of any paid virtual currency and of any item priced in it; do not mix multiple currencies or force multi-step exchanges; avoid "odd pricing" and bundle-only sales so players can buy the exact amount they need; give pre-contractual information; respect the 14-day withdrawal right (or obtain valid waiver); plain-language terms; design that respects vulnerabilities (no pressure timers aimed at children). Soft currency earned only through gameplay and cryptocurrencies are out of scope; one summary says that even gameplay-earned balances of a paid currency should carry a monetary reference. (Source: https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/global/regulatory-spotlight-virtual-currency-vs-consumer-law-part-2 ; https://www.reedsmith.com/es/perspectives/2025/03/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper)
- Star Stable Entertainment AB (Sweden) was the first enforcement target, same day. The CPC found that both buying Star Coins with real money and spending them in-game are subject to EU consumer law; objections included bundle-only sales with no 1:1 conversion, time-limited pressure offers aimed at children, unclear purchase information and influencer-related practices. The company had one month to respond with commitments. No search result reported the outcome; the Spanish ministry page says only that "several measures" were adopted, so status after March 2025 is unverified. (Source: https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry ; https://portal-cec.consumo.gob.es/en/comunicacion/noticias/2025/european-commission-and-national-authorities-act-protect-children)
- BEUC's "Game OVER" complaint (September 2024, 22 members, 17 countries) targeted Activision Blizzard, EA, Epic, Mojang, Roblox, Supercell and Ubisoft. Primary ask: an outright ban on paid in-game currencies; fallbacks: restrict for minors, mandatory fiat-price disclosure, transactions off by default. (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/12/bad-credits-consumer-group-files-eu-suit-over-manipulative-payments-in-games-like-fortnite-and-minecraft-calls-for-a-ban ; https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/european-consumer-organisation-challenges-in-game-monetisation-landscape-as-unfair)
- How Roblox, Fortnite and Second Life display prices in the EU: searches found no Epic or Roblox statement on CPC compliance and no evidence of fiat equivalents beside item prices. Euro prices exist only at the V-Bucks pack level (e.g. 1,000 V-Bucks = EUR 8.99 after the October 2023 alignment); Roblox items remain priced in Robux, and its terms say Robux have "no real-world value", with EU/EEA regional terms added March 2026 whose content the extracts did not show. (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/upcoming-fortnite-pricing-alignment-in-czech-republic-denmark-eurozone-countries-united-states-and-more-in-october-2023 ; https://conductatlas.com/platform/roblox/roblox-terms-of-use/virtual-content-no-real-world-value-and-no-enforceable-legal-rights/) Second Life: L$ is bought on the LindeX at a floating market rate; Tilia applies VAT by declared country to USD-denominated Lab products (land, fees, name changes) but historically not to LindeX trades because "Linden Lab only facilitates the transaction". Prior knowledge, unverified: the SL Marketplace shows L$ prices with an approximate USD figure, and the viewer shows a USD estimate on buy-L$ dialogs; this is exactly the "published spread" pattern R10 proposes. (Source: https://secondlife.com/corporate/vat ; https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/341/ac/273938/1.html)

### 3. Tax reporting on the cash-out rail: DAC7 and 1099-K

- US: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act restored the Form 1099-K threshold to more than USD 20,000 AND more than 200 transactions per payee per year, repealing the USD 600 rule retroactively to 2022; IRS Fact Sheet 2025-08 (23 Oct 2025) confirms. Sources differ on whether the section is 70432 or 70433. The Act also raised the 1099-NEC/MISC threshold to USD 2,000 from 2026. Income is taxable whether or not a form issues. (Source: https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000 ; https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html)
- EU: DAC7 (Directive 2021/514, in force 1 Jan 2023) obliges platform operators, including non-EU operators facilitating EU-resident sellers, to collect and report per seller: name, primary address, date of birth, TIN (and VAT number if any), payout financial-account identifier, quarterly consideration, and fees/taxes withheld; due diligence by 31 Dec, filing by 31 Jan. The only exclusion is for goods sellers with under 30 sales and under EUR 2,000 per platform per year; personal services (which is how a virtual-item or scripting economy would most likely be characterised) have no floor, so the first euro is reportable. (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview ; https://stripe.com/gb/resources/more/dac7-what-businesses-in-germany-need-to-know ; https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/en_gb/todas-gestiones/impuestos-tasas/declaraciones-informativas/modelo-238-decl_____informacion-parte-operadores-plataformas/preguntas-frecuentes/dac-7-informacion-vendedores.html)
- Tilia/Second Life today: Linden Lab's Required Tax Documentation FAQ says the IRS requires W-9 (US) or W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E (non-US) above transaction-volume triggers; the trigger counts LindeX sell orders per person (not per avatar); a 1099-K is filed at 200+ transactions and >USD 20,000; without a form on file, backup withholding applies (28% in the 2013 post; the current statutory rate is 24%, prior knowledge). Requests may originate from "Tilia LLC". No Linden Lab or Tilia statement on DAC7 was found; whether Tilia reports EU creators is unknown. (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Linden_Lab_Official:Required_Tax_Documentation_FAQ ; https://community.secondlife.com/blogs/entry/1094-required-tax-documentation/)

### 4. US App Store Accountability Acts

- Texas SB 2420: enjoined 23 Dec 2025 (Judge Pitman, W.D. Tex., First Amendment); Fifth Circuit granted an administrative stay, then in May 2026 stayed the injunction, reasoning that app listings "propose commercial transactions" (intermediate scrutiny) and that universal injunctions exceeded equitable authority under Trump v. CASA. CCIA's emergency application to the Supreme Court was denied, leaving the law in force while the appeal proceeds. Apple reportedly began enforcing in Texas on 4 June 2026. (Source: https://www.pearlcohen.com/fifth-circuit-stays-injunctions-against-texas-app-store-accountability-act/ ; https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law ; https://aiweekly.co/alerts/apple-enforces-texas-age-verification-in-app-store)
- Utah SB 142: HB 498 (18 Mar 2026) delayed effect to 6 May 2027 and removed AG enforcement (private right of action remains); CCIA's suit was voluntarily dismissed 21 Apr 2026. Louisiana HB 977 delayed to 1 Jul 2027. California AB 1043 (Digital Age Assurance Act, signed 13 Oct 2025, operative 1 Jan 2027) places the duty on OS providers to collect a birth date at account setup and supply an age-bracket signal to developers on request; app stores are not themselves required to signal; developers must use the signal; AG penalties cited as USD 2,500/7,500 per child. Alabama has also enacted a version. (Source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/challenge-to-utah-s-app-store-6173927/ ; https://www.alstonprivacy.com/california-enacts-digital-age-verification-law/ ; https://median.co/blog/new-age-verification-laws-2026)
- Age signals developers will actually receive: Texas-style statutes require stores to bucket users into statutory age categories (under 13, 13-15, 16-17, 18+ in the Texas text, prior knowledge) and obtain verifiable parental consent before minors download or make in-app purchases; developers must assign an age rating to each app and each in-app purchase and notify stores of significant changes. Apple's Declared Age Range API returns a declared age range (and parental-consent state) rather than a birth date; Google's Play Age Signals API is in beta and returns live data for Texas users. California's signal is an age bracket only. (Source: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/810754 ; https://betanews.com/article/google-play-age-signals-api-global-rollout/ ; https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102kddh/texas-law-requires-age-verification-for-app-stores-and-developers/)

### 5. Currency designs and their regulatory surface

| Design | EU CPC/DFA exposure | Tax rail exposure | Economy preserved? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single paid premium currency, bundle-only (Robux/V-Bucks pattern) | Highest: price display, bundle, odd-pricing, withdrawal and minors issues; BEUC ban target | Same as any | Yes |
| Closed currency with published fixed/floating spread and fiat shown at every price (SL-with-dual-pricing) | Medium: compliant with CPC if fiat shown, exact-amount purchase allowed, no second currency | DAC7/1099-K on cash-out | Yes |
| No premium currency; direct fiat pricing with platform fee (R7 take) | Lowest; each micro-sale is a fiat distance contract (withdrawal, invoicing, VAT) | DAC7/1099-K on every payout; marketplace-facilitator VAT at scale | Yes, but tips and micro-payments become card-cost-bound |
| Fiat-pegged stored-value balance (1 unit = USD 0.01, displayed as money) | Low: it is money, not "virtual currency"; must still show price and allow exact top-up | Becomes e-money/money-transmission (Tilia's licensing model) | Yes |

Note the CPC carve-out: a currency obtainable only through gameplay is out of scope. A stipend (R12) delivered in the same currency that is also sold for cash does not qualify for that carve-out; one summary even says gameplay-earned balances of a paid currency must carry a monetary reference.

## Design implications for nolife

1. R10 (closed currency, published spread) is defensible under today's CPC principles only if every price in-world and on the marketplace is rendered with its fiat equivalent at the displayed rate, buyers can purchase the exact amount they need (no bundle tiers, no odd pricing), and there is exactly one currency with no intermediate exchange step. A floating LindeX-style rate complicates "real-money price at purchase"; a fixed or daily-fixed rate plus published spread (R10) displays truthfully.
2. The safest EU posture is to make the unit a fiat-pegged stored-value balance presented as money (cents), not a "coin". This exits the premium-currency debate (BEUC ban ask, possible DFA restriction) at the cost of a licensed money-services partner (the Tilia model). No second currency, no loot mechanics.
3. R12 stipends: a stipend in the paid currency is not "soft currency" under the CPC carve-out. Display stipend balances with fiat value, and avoid time-pressure or FOMO mechanics around spending it, particularly for any user flagged as a minor.
4. R7 published all-in take: already aligned with CPC pre-contractual information duties and with the DAC7 requirement to report fees withheld per seller; design the ledger so per-creator quarterly gross consideration, fees and payout account are first-class fields from day one.
5. Cash-out rail: collect W-9/W-8 at onboarding to the payout rail (not at the USD 20,000 trigger) and the DAC7 set (name, address, DOB, TIN, VAT number, payout account) from any EU-resident creator before the first payout, because services have no DAC7 floor. Budget for 24% backup withholding on US payees without a TIN and for filing by 31 January in at least one EU member state (register as a non-EU platform operator) if the operator sits outside the EU.
6. Minors and app stores: ship a native-client age-bracket path that consumes the Apple Declared Age Range / Google Play Age Signals / California OS signal, rate the in-app currency purchase as an in-app purchase requiring parental consent for under-18 accounts in Texas (in force) and plan for Utah/Louisiana (2027) and California (Jan 2027). Make minor-bracket accounts non-spending by default (BEUC's fallback ask) without losing social functionality.
7. Track the DFA proposal (expected Q4 2026): its text will fix whether virtual-currency opt-out, fiat display and minors' spending limits become binding and whether a stored-value design is in scope.

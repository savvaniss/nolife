# Failure taxonomy: why Second Life stalled, why its successors died, and what "nolife" must do differently

Lead analyst synthesis, revised 2026-10-09 after three critiques (completeness, steelman, causal) and four gap briefs. Built only from `scratchpad/research/`, `scratchpad/analysis/`, `scratchpad/verify/` and `scratchpad/critique/`. No web search was used for this document.

## Evidence quality

All 14 core topics were re-checked by a search-backed checker (12 searches each); six were re-researched live and their `ledger2` files supersede the first-pass ledgers. The four gap briefs had 14 searches each and 5-search fact checks; the critics used 8, 8 and 0. Page fetches were blocked throughout, so every "verified" fact rests on search extracts, and the no-search checkers' verdicts are hypotheses. Base rates quoted from the steelman (D90 retention, OpenSim's July 2026 record, Project Zero's cost, SLua's beta) are its search extracts with no captured URL.

Figures most at risk: the $78M payout and creator-income counts (one secondhand town-hall summary); 2025-26 region counts (uncorroborated); Sansar's cost (never found); Quantic Foundry's "62.7%" (of 1,799 respondents, not 1.75M gamers); pixel-streaming cost (the first draft's $0.5-1/hour was too low; a 2025 modemworld extract puts Project Zero at ~$1.75/hour, unsearched); Avakin's financials (Preqin: GBP -11.17M EBITDA, not a "loss"); the 2026 Zindra expansion and adult hub (unsupported); the Semrush country split (unsearched); IMVU's and Entropia's self-reported figures; Linden's ~200 staff (one May 2026 profile). "(unsearched)" marks prior knowledge only.

### What this analysis does not claim

Second Life is not a failed business: profitable by 2005, owner-funded, keeping ~10% of a ~US$650M/yr economy, MAU up 24% in 2025, and healthier by the critics' own financial standard than Roblox, VRChat or Zepeto. Peak concurrency has not "halved": the 2009 peak was bot-inflated; against the 2020 human high of 60,068, 2025-26 peaks of 45-50K are a ~20% decline over 16 years. SL's 7-10% D90 retention is at the industry base rate, so the funnel did not cause the plateau; nor is the land tax shown to (OpenSim: same architecture, land 5-13x cheaper, one-twelfth the users). The cheapest hypothesis, that the audience for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out is ~0.5-1M people and SL already serves most of it, fits every demand shock (2006, 2020, 2025 each reached a ~500-650K plateau faster) and cannot be refuted here; the requirements are therefore sized for a Linden-scale world that can grow. Not researched: build cost and funding climate, music licensing, engines, the loneliness literature, bot share, creator interviews, OpenSim prices.

## 1. Why Second Life did not evolve

Second Life is not dead: ~600,000-620,000 MAU (company count, Dec 2025), peak concurrency 48,802 on 1 April 2026 (third-party estimate 1.15-1.45M unique monthly logins) (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/12/19/second-life-now-at-620000-monthly-active-users/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/05/11/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-update-10th-may-2026/). The all-time peak of 88,220 (29 March 2009) included an unquantified bot share and Engadget tied the slide that followed to Linden's bot crackdown, so 2009 and 2025 readings are not comparable (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2009-12-25-second-life-user-concurrency-spends-year-in-slow-decline.html ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2024/09/25/second-life-maximum-daily-concurrency-peaks-january-2020-to-september-2020/). The region map has been flat at ~27,000 for a decade, and the retention statement went from "about 10% at 90 days" (January 2007) to "only around 7% of those coming in are actively being retained" (September 2025) (Source: https://andrewchen.com/interesting-graph-on-second-life-usage/ ; https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/). The MAU series has no stated definition at any point and is read only as a plateau.

Eight root causes reduce to four with a mechanism, one amplifier, one missed cause and one unexamined cohort; a trait is a debt only where a chain to lost users or revenue can be stated (1.9 gives the rule).

### 1.1 Content without budgets: the compatibility ratchet (mechanism holds in narrow form)

A region is 256 m square on one simulator process, capped at 100 avatars, with a Land Impact budget last changed in November 2016 (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Land ; https://modemworld.me/2016/11/03/lab-reveals-li-prim-allowance-changes-in-second-life-in-full). Linden said in 2008 the region size would not change "because of its far-reaching effects on the platform" (Source: https://list-archives.secondlife.com/sldev/2008-October/012153.html). The 100-avatar cap is not the debt: Roblox, Fortnite and VRChat run tens to ~100-200 per instance. The debt is that no asset carries a per-tier budget, so the hardware floor rises with the catalogue; Linden said in January 2025 that ~50% of users lack high-end machines (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/01/02/). That chain is the one SL root cause with no counter-example in the corpus.

Mesh shipped in 2011 (unsearched); EEP, announced 2017, released 20 April 2020 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2020/04/27/2020-viewer-release-summaries-week-17/). PBR went grid-wide on 28 November 2023, a decade after game engines, though SL migrates fast for a persistent world: ~80% of Firestorm users were on a PBR-capable build within ~18 months (Source: https://modemworld.me/2023/11/28/ ; https://www.firestormviewer.org/some-statistics-firestorm-versions-whos-running-what/). Every upgrade was additive: prims, mesh, legacy materials and PBR coexist; PBR terrain launched only on private regions (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Mirrors ; https://releasenotes.secondlife.com/viewer/7.1.8.9375512768.html). The first draft's "mirrors render everything twice" is withdrawn: mirrors are opt-in, off at every preset, and planar reflection renders twice in every engine. The client: 542,967 unique Firestorm users in 30 days (2015); in June 2025 the Lab put Firestorm on its own download page (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/firestorm-by-far-most-popular-on-hypergrid/ ; https://modemworld.me/2025/06/19/). This is an asset; the residual debt is that nobody owned the newcomer interface.

The 2020 AWS "uplift" changed neither caps nor frame budget (Source: https://modemworld.me/2020/11/19/ll-confirms-second-life-regions-now-all-on-aws/). glTF import arrived in 2025, but in July 2026 the Lab was still asking creators what they want from glTF (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/07/03/2026-week-27-sl-ccug-meeting-summary-updating-sl-mesh/). SLua, Linden's Luau fork, went from alpha (March 2025) to main-grid beta in December 2025, additive to LSL (steelman extracts: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Lua_Alpha ; https://modemworld.me/2026/03/18/). None of this touched the cause: no per-asset budget and no conversion policy.

### 1.2 Revenue model: land tier as cost of entry, not cause of the plateau

Land was "the one resource that is limited, and the main source of revenue for Linden" (Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna15163036). A full private region cost US$1,000 setup plus US$295/month in 2008-2015 and costs US$349 plus US$199/month from 15 June 2026 (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/140885-how-much-does-a-full-region-from-ll-actually-cost/ ; https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/). A flat per-region fee penalises emptiness (what R16 wants), not success; "taxed what should have compounded" is withdrawn.

The natural experiment. OpenSimulator runs the identical simulator and viewer with regions at US$15-40/month (2015-era lists; no 2026 list found) or free, and the hypergrid reached 48,376 actives in May 2026 (49,690 in July per the steelman), one-twelfth of SL after 17 years (Source: https://www.kitely.com/virtual-world-news/2015/06/01/60-off-large-worlds-and-dedicated-memory-guarantee/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/digiworldz/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/). Real tier roughly halved 2008-2025 while estates fell: a demand shift, not a price effect (Source: https://secondlife.com/land/private-pricing ; https://secondlife.com/corporate/pricing). Private estates fell from ~26,600 (2008, single ambiguous source) to below 19,000 by July 2014 and ~17,600 (2026, uncorroborated), so ~80% of the "34% decline" is 2008-2014, dominated by the Openspace repricing booked under 1.5; since mid-2014 estates fell ~7%, and the 2025 drop was mostly Linden retiring first-generation Linden Homes (Source: https://webtribunal.net/blog/second-life-facts/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2014/07/28/private-estates-drops-below-19-000-regions-in-second-life/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/11/04/second-life-region-statistics-early-november-2025-update/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/04/08/second-life-grid-shows-positive-region-growth-since-start-of-2026/).

What it does explain: builders' cost of entry and fee creep once growth ended: Marketplace commission 5% to 10% on 2 December 2019, LindeX fees up in 2023 and reportedly 10% to 11% in 2026, Premium to $12.99/month in July 2026 (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2019/11/25/editorial-second-life-users-are-less-than-happy-about-linden-lab-doubling-commission-rates-on-the-sl-marketplace-from-5-to-10/). Users estimate the all-in take at 20-31% against the "90/10" marketing (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/). "GDP" is gross flow: US$567M in 2009 produced US$55M of resident earnings; ~US$650M produced ~US$78M in 2023 (single secondhand source), flat in real terms since 2017 (Source: https://venturebeat.com/business/second-lifes-economy-grows-65-to-567m ; https://modemworld.me/2024/12/20/second-life-1-3b-to-build-1-1b-paid-to-creators/). Anshe Chung's fortune was land arbitrage alongside a content business (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshe_Chung). Tier cuts in 2016, 2018, 2023 and 2026 shifted cost from land to subscriptions and spreads (Source: https://modemworld.me/2018/06/20/second-life-major-private-region-pricing-restructure-announced/). Causality runs plateau then fees.

### 1.3 Funnel, density and demographics: a constant cannot explain a change

Linden's CEO in 2014 named "the not very intuitive viewer interface" and "high client performance requirements" as the second-login blocker (Source: https://modemworld.me/2014/06/29/ebbe-altberg-on-user-retention-now-and-in-the-future/). Wired (July 2007): more than 85% of avatars abandoned (Source: https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56). Unity's glossary puts the gaming average at ~10% D90 and GameAnalytics says three-quarters of mobile games miss 3% at D28 (steelman extracts), so SL's 7-10% D90 is average to good. Linden (September 2025): mobile plus Project Zero reach ~10x the trial volume with ~7% retained, i.e. ~7x the old retained inflow, consistent with MAU +24% in 2025, while DAU "has gone down" (Oberwager, Nov 2024) (Source: https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/ ; https://nwn.blogs.com/nwn/2024/11/linden-lab-sl-future-philip-rosedale-brad-oberwager.html). Removing the access barrier moved the ceiling a little. Density: 76,000 peak avatars over ~30,000 regions in 2008 is under three per region; 32,000 over 26,663 in 2025 is ~1.2 (Source: https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/PCWorld/story?id=6688909 ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/). Both divide bot-inclusive concurrency by all regions, including ~17,600 private estates that are empty when owners are away. Public-hub density was never measured; Horizon, with no land, was as empty.

Demographics. 2008 Linden data: 41% female, 39% North America, 32% Western Europe (Source: https://www.yupingliu.com/wordpress/tag/statistics/). A 2025 self-selected survey found mean ages ~45 (men) and ~38.6 (women), weighted ~42.8 (Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00287 ; not re-searched). The Teen Grid inference is withdrawn (16-17-year-olds moved to the main grid). An audience generating ~US$1,080 of gross flow per MAU per year (Roblox: US$53-61 bookings per DAU) is a monetisation asset. Roblox's 18+ DAU grew 32% YoY in Q2 2026 (secondary source) with a 42% DevEx bonus on age-verified adult spend (Source: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/roblox-q2-2026-revenue-soars-36-to-15b-despite-bookings-miss ; https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42).

What Linden tried. Viewer 2 "Basic mode" (2011) offered 24 preset avatars and was derided; its retention effect was never published (Source: https://modemworld.me/2011/04/11). The Unity mobile app, in open beta since June 2024, is "still in beta" at 2.5 stars on 1M+ downloads (Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lindenlab.secondlife&hl=en_US). Firestorm Zero (streamed, March 2025) closed by June 2025 after a provider-forced migration; Project Zero ended 21 April 2026 after 14 months, reframed as an experiment; no source states why (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/ ; https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/).

### 1.4 Purpose: supplied by groups with a schedule, and never measured (restated to be testable)

"Purpose vacuum" as first stated was unfalsifiable: IMVU (~7M MAU claimed, no land, no quests), Avakin (1.6M MAU, no UGC) and SL survive on "meet people and dress up"; Rec Room and PS Home had loops and died (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/25/imvu-35-million-funding/ ; https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/75555/avakin-life-surpasses-500-million-downloads-cementing-12-year-legacy-as-a-global-social-powerhouse/). Restated: retention concentrates in users attached to a group with a recurring time and place, and SL supplied neither schedule nor roles. The causal effect of group membership on retention is held by Bungie, Square Enix and Linden and published by none, so this is a hypothesis to measure (R11), not a finding.

Venues that worked were staffed, against unstaffed corporate builds like "a shop in the High Street filled with brochures but never having any staff" (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2). What lasted was resident-built: the Confederation of Democratic Simulators (since 2004, ~80 citizens), Virtual Ability (1,000+ members), Cafe Musique's 60+ shows a week, volunteer-run SL9B, BURN2 (community-run since 2010) and the Anglican Cathedral, which survived its founder's exit (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Confederation_of_Democratic_Simulators ; https://virtualability.org/about-us ; https://secondlife.aditi.lindenlab.com/destination/aism ; https://modemworld.me/2012/06/17/sl9b-let-the-festivities-begin/ ; https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/ ; https://episcopal.cafe/transitions_at_the_anglican_cathedral_in_second_life/). No independent attendance data exists for any of them.

Adult and romantic roleplay, omitted from the first draft, is plausibly SL's largest resident-built purpose and economy; the only hard shares are old. In November 2009, 1,325 private and 348 mainland regions were Adult-flagged, 5.69% of 29,409 (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Adult_content); Au's 2008 book put ~18% of land in "mature activity" and 13.6% of residents regularly having virtual sex (Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/book-review-the-making-of-second-life). Zindra is "hundreds of regions" (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com:443/wiki/Zindra); 2026 reports of 92 added regions and an adult hub are unsupported. No Lab figure for the adult share of L$ flow exists. The loops removed by ban, gambling (25 July 2007, ~5% of the economy by one account) and banks (22 January 2008, after Ginko's ~L$200M collapse), were not load-bearing: the economy grew 65% in 2009 (Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2007/10/15/223526/financial-woes-in-second-life ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginko_Financial ; https://venturebeat.com/business/second-lifes-economy-grows-65-to-567m). Linden supplied places, never roles, schedules or scarcity, and never measured or defended its largest purpose engine.

### 1.5 Governance and trust: an amplifier, not a cause

Decree, outcry and partial retreat are industry practice (Epic, Roblox, Blizzard, Meta); SL's distinction is the absence of notice and grandfathering, and no churn series is attached to any episode. Openspace tier (Oct 2008): $75 to $125 with no grandfathering, later revised to Homesteads (Source: https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/356/24/291220/1.html). Zindra (15 June 2009): ~850 forced move tickets (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Zindra). The education discount ended 1 January 2011 and was restored 24 July 2013 ("Linden Lab blew it") (Source: https://thejournal.com/Articles/2010/11/03/Linden-Lab-To-End-Second-Life-Educational-Discounts.aspx ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/08/educators-wary-of-latest-linden-offer/). The August 2013 ToS made Renderosity and CG Textures ban their assets in SL until July 2014 (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms ; https://modemworld.me/2014/07/16/lab-updates-section-2-3-of-their-terms-of-service-will-it-calm-doubts/). CopyBot (November 2006) triggered a creator strike (Source: https://blog.granneman.com/?p=1042). Metrics were opaque: no official MAU/DAU series, a 2023 "750K" irreconcilable with 2024's "500K" (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-10-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2009.html). The one measurable loss is the education exodus (section 5).

### 1.6 The VR sibling bet without continuity (first draft's 1.6 and 1.7 merged)

Sansar (teased 2014, beta 2017, layoffs 2019-20, sold to Wookey 24 March 2020) "wasn't making enough money" (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2020/04/linden-lab-sells-sansar-to-wookey/ ; https://ryanschultz.com/2020/02/11/linden-lab-lays-off-40-staff-shuts-down-the-sansar-project/). Average concurrency was in the tens, peak ~220; the cost was never found. The counterfactual fails on dates: concurrency declined from mid-2009, five years before Sansar, and 2020-2026 (no sibling, full focus) shows the same flat trajectory. What it shows is the mechanism in the successor table: a sibling with no inventory, identity or social-graph continuity, on hardware the audience did not own, cannot inherit a community. SL's own VR exposure was a week: an Oculus viewer shipped 1 July 2016 and was withdrawn within days as not meeting "our standards for quality" (Source: https://modemworld.me/2016/07/08/second-life-oculus-rift-support-suspended/). ~1.77% of Steam users had a headset in January 2026; Quest shipments fell 16% through Q3 2025 (Source: https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/ ; https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/02/apple_vision_pro_meta_quest_sales_drop/). The real mobile blocker was 1.1's content base, not timing. Leadership churn (a 30% layoff and Kingdon's exit in June 2010, Humble 2010, Altberg 2014, the 2020 sale to Waterfield and Oberwager, Tilia sold 2024) brackets Sansar on both sides (Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/linden-lab-ceo-resigns-shortly-after-layoffs ; https://modemworld.me/2020/07/09/linden-lab-announces-it-is-to-be-acquired/ ; https://modemworld.me/2024/04/26/summary-of-tilia-acquisition-lab-gab-sl-round-table-news/).

### 1.7 The missing cause: the 2010 switch to cash-flow management (unsearched; checker finding)

Both checkers locate the start of the flat decade at the June 2010 restructuring, when Linden began managing cash flow; it has since run on ~160 staff and a ~10% take (Oberwager, Dec 2024), ~200 staff and minus Patch Linden per one May 2026 profile (unverified) (Source: https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=88910 ; https://yespress.io/linden-lab). The 2010 cut closed the Singapore and UK offices and hit community roles hardest, ending funded localisation (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-07-linden-lab-laying-off-staff-closing-singapore-office.html). Additive-only engineering, fee reshuffles and a decade-late mobile client fit a company that chose to stay small and profitable; nolife's funding posture is therefore a design decision (R30), and the subsidy-collapse pattern in 2.2 is its mirror image.

### 1.8 Non-English communities: a quarter of the base, unserved since 2010

In March 2007 comScore put 61% of active residents in Europe, 19% in North America and 13% in Asia-Pacific, Germany alone ~16% (Source: https://www.comscore.com/Insights/Press-Releases/2007/05/Second-Life-Growth-Worldwide). A Semrush web-traffic estimate for November 2025 (a proxy, unsearched) puts the US at 49.6%, Brazil 7.0%, Colombia 5.6%, UK 5.1%, Germany 3.8% (Source: https://zh.semrush.com/website/secondlife.com/overview). After 2010 funded localisation ended: in 2009 the Lab translated tier-1 languages while "the Second Life community did nearly everything" for tier-2 (Source: https://gilbane.com/?p=2016); Kaizen Games, the official Brazilian partner, stopped (Source: https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/second-life-acaba-no-brasil); Ajuda SL Brasil, a volunteer gateway, was still self-funded in 2018 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2018/05/); Japan was "depopulating" by December 2007 (Source: https://globalvoices.org/2007/12/19/japan-depopulating-second-life/). Japan is VRChat's largest web market (37% of visits vs 27% US), built on BOOTH's ~10.4bn-yen 3D-model trade and pixiv's 2026 stake in VRChat (Source: https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/vrchat-high-popularity-in-japan-JP ; https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html ; https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/09/09/27973.html); Roblox's APAC DAU grew 76% YoY in Q2 2025 and regional pricing lifted paying users 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines (Source: https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q2/Q2-2025-Earnings-Conference-Call-Transcript.pdf ; https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items). No published study shows chat translation moving retention; Roblox reports only "stronger engagement" (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/02/roblox-introduces-ai-powered-real-time-chat-translations-in-16-languages).

### 1.9 What Second Life got right, and the rule for moat versus debt

A real economy with cash-out: ~US$1.1B paid to creators since 2003, ~US$650M/yr of transactions, 21,152 earning creators in 2023 (single source), a currency stable 2008-2017, profitability by 2005 on US$25M raised (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/ ; https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46605 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Second_Life). A licensed payments rail (Tilia, 2019) sold with a five-year partnership (Source: https://modemworld.me/2024/04/26/summary-of-tilia-acquisition-lab-gab-sl-round-table-news/). Latent demand: a 50% rise in regular users in 2020 (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2020/05/24/linden-lab-ceo-ebbe-altberg-second-life-has-seen-a-50-increase-in-regular-monthly-users-because-of-the-pandemic/). And nobody replaced it: the only worlds covering all four pillars (persistent homes, in-world creation, adult users, economy) are Resonite (~170-200 Steam concurrent) and the OpenSim hypergrid (~45-48K monthly actives) (Source: https://steambase.io/games/resonite ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/). OpenSim is the cleanest test of portability as a retention feature: export, self-hosting and cheap land, but no economy, no audience and no stability (OSgrid wiped its database in 2025 and was offline three weeks in 2026) (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). Educators moved for cost and control; creators and consumers did not follow.

The rule: a trait is a moat where it retains the people present and a debt where it blocks those not yet present. Backward compatibility, the land tier and the volunteer client are both: inventories and relationships are a switching cost no competitor has offered to carry, and the same unbudgeted inventories set the hardware floor. The design problem is continuity of identity, inventory and social graph (the moat) with budgets and conversion on content (the debt); R2, R3 and R18 are written to that rule.

## 2. Why the successors failed

### 2.2 Recurring patterns, re-ranked with a denominator

A pattern is kept only if it appears in failures and in fewer than a third of the survivors above; each world is counted once.

1. Subsidy collapse (strongest; absent from the first draft). VC- or parent-funded worlds ended when the funding posture changed: Sansar, High Fidelity, AltspaceVR, Horizon-VR, Rec Room (runway to 2029, no path to profitability), Spatial, Lively, Home, Hathora's customers (2026) (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174). Survivors are cheap to keep alive: SL (owner-funded on a 10% take), OpenSim (volunteer), Resonite (Patreon), Active Worlds, VRChat after its layoffs.

2. Headset-gated audience (frequent; caps rather than kills). Blue Mars, High Fidelity, Sansar, vTime, AltspaceVR, Horizon; Rosedale put daily VR users ~100x below plan (Source: https://modemworld.me/2019/04/11/high-fidelity-changes-direction-the-reality-of-vr-worlds-today-tomorrow/).

3. Land or tokens sold before population (frequent, severe; no survivor did it). Decentraland, Sandbox, Otherside, Somnium, Voxels; metaverse land volume fell 96% in 2022 (Source: https://www.theblock.co/post/197062/ncrooks@theblock.co).

4. UGC margin and hosting cost (frequent, severe). Rec Room kept 30c of each UGC dollar; Spatial cited hosting cost; VRChat cut 30% of staff at record usage; Avakin ran a GBP -11M EBITDA (unverified) (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/ ; https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-lays-off-30-of-company/). Roblox keeps ~75% of spend and still lost ~$1B GAAP in 2024 (arithmetic in section 5).

5. Trust shocks (moderate frequency; severe for small platforms). VRChat's EAC update drew 22,000 votes in 24 hours; NeosVR died of a founder split over crypto; SL's ToS; Meta's 48-hour reversal (Source: https://www.dazeddigital.com/science-tech/article/56662/1/why-thousands-of-users-are-abandoning-the-worlds-most-popular-vr-game ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeosVR).

6. Safety scandal (rare; no death in the corpus is attributed to one). Habbo 2012 compounded an existing contraction; Roblox's $35.8M-plus settlements did not dent its position (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2012/06/12/trouble-at-habbo-hotel-balderton-dumps-13-stake-in-owner-sulake-over-dodgy-child-content/ ; https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-21/roblox-settles-with-states-for-35-8-million-over-child-safety).

Shared category traits (present in most survivors; not causes): the "purpose vacuum" as originally stated (SL, IMVU, Avakin); density failure (SL, OpenSim, Horizon; Sony's "niche community" verdict on Home and Horizon's "most users don't return" are one observation) (Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/insertcoin/2014/09/27/sony-finally-shutting-down-playstation-home-on-ps3/ ; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/15/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-losing-users-falling-short-of-goals.html); measurement opacity (Decentraland's 38 vs 775 vs 8,000; VRChat and Linden publish nothing) (Source: https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-final-word-on-decentralands-numbers); paywall at entry (FFXIV, Hytale, Entropia and Pax Dei are paywalled survivors) (Source: https://massivelyop.com/?p=566277); fixed costs at low density (Ever, Jane died over $500-600/month of servers; Active Worlds lives on less) (Source: https://massivelyop.com/2021/01/11/kickstarted-jane-austen-mmorpg-ever-jane-closed-its-doors-over-the-holidays/). "The operator kills the world" is an outcome, not a cause.

## 3. Where life-simulation and a-life did not give purpose, and what AI changes now

A-life made agents interesting and never made a place necessary: Creatures, Black & White and Spore produced creatures and no reason to stay (Source: https://www.howwegettonext.com/the-brain-in-the-machine/ ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/spore-my-view-of-the-elephant). The Sims Online removed the god-view control Sims players were buying; 97,000 subscribers at six months against a 1M target (Source: https://www.loosewireblog.com/?p=2349). Animal Crossing's scripted villagers and real clock delivered "place" and "return" (31.18M units, unsearched); Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld sell emergent stories through legible systems (Source: https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/RimWorld). Inside SL, a-life worked only as a scarcity economy: Ozimals bunnies hibernated when a creditor closed the food server in May 2017 (food-independent pets survived); KittyCatS breeders describe a trait-inflation boom-bust (Source: https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/435130-how-much-have-you-made-via-kittycats-plantpets-dfs-other-breedables/).

Since 2023 agents have memory, reflection and planning (Smallville; arXiv 2411.10109, not re-searched); Project Sid ran up to 1,000 agents in Minecraft for four-hour runs the authors call "preliminary" (Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00114 ; https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377). Shipped AI-NPC games are small; Inworld pivoted in 2025 to infrastructure (Source: https://inworld.ai/blog/new-ai-infrastructure-scaling-games-media-characters). Generative worlds (Roblox Cube; Genie 3) are asset or video generators, not persistent multi-user state (Source: https://corp.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/03/introducing-roblox-cube ; https://bdtechtalks.com/2025/08/07/deepmind-genie-3/). No vendor publishes a cost per NPC-hour and prices turn over within a year (Source: https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/inworld-ai/ ; https://docs.inworld.ai/models).

Risks. Engagement with AI characters is deep but solitary and legally exposed: Character.AI banned open-ended chat for under-18s from November 2025 and settled the Setzer wrongful-death case in January 2026; Italy fined Replika EUR 5M (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots ; https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/01/google-and-character-ai-agree-to-settle-lawsuit-linked-to-teen-suicide). Hostility concentrates among the social players nolife targets: 63% of 1,799 Quantic Foundry respondents chose the most negative option; 52% of GDC 2026 developers say generative AI hurts the industry (Source: https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/ ; https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-games-disclosing-generative-ai-use-are-up-800-this-year/). SAG-AFTRA's NLRB charge over Fortnite's AI Darth Vader has no reported outcome (Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/actors-union-files-labor-charge-over-ai-darth-vader-in-fortnite). EU AI Act Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026: a plain-language notice or persistent badge, never a generic "assistant" label, and agents must say on whose behalf they act (Source: https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/). Bots were 51% of web traffic in 2024 (Source: https://www.imperva.com/resources/resource-library/reports/2025-bad-bot-report/).

Conclusion. Nothing in the corpus shows agent-filled density retaining humans; AI changes what an empty room looks like, not whether people have a reason to be in it, so agents are a disclosed, capped stopgap (R25), not a purpose mechanism.

## 4. What the winners did right

Caveat: selected on outcome. Rec Room met every criterion below in 2021 and died; Roblox's DAU fell from 152M to 123M with a ~$1B 2024 GAAP loss; VRChat has had 30% layoffs and publishes no metrics; Zepeto's numbers stop in 2022 (EBITDA about -US$42.5M, unsearched). Each principle is at most necessary.

Roblox. Free instant entry, an engagement-weighted payout ($923M DevEx in 2024, >$1.5B in 2025), platform-owned moderation ($915M in 2024, $1,153M in 2025) and continuous re-pricing (42% uplift for verified adult spend from June 2026) (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000009/ex991-q42025shareholder.htm ; https://app.edgar.tools/companies/RBLX). Q2 2026: DAU 123M (fourth sequential decline), bookings $1.6B, net loss $185M, Q3 guidance down, attributed to engagement shifting to lower-monetising evergreen titles and a recommender change favouring retention, the trade-off R8 makes explicit (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm ; https://www.shacknews.com/article/150208/roblox-daus-grew-10-percent-yoy).

Fortnite/UEFN. 40% of net revenue into an engagement pool, rewritten on 1 November 2025 to count only purchasing players with a 75% bounty for new or lapsed players, plus in-island sales at ~74% of cash; $1B cumulative paid and 47% of play hours in creator islands by June 2026 (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items ; https://gamesbeat.com/epic-games-has-paid-out-1-billion-to-creators-since-launching-unreal-editor-for-fortnite-in-2023/). Its 12-15M concerts ran in ~100-player instances; Horizon's $50M fund also paid on retention and failed.

Minecraft. Self-hosted servers under a 2014 EULA plus a curated Marketplace paying a "majority" of post-store revenue (Source: https://fortune.com/2017/09/05/minecraft-marketplace-microsoft-million).

VRChat. Avatar identity culture (Japan its growth engine; 158,192 concurrent at a 2026 concert), behaviour-earned trust ranks gating upload and voice, per-device performance ranks, a creator economy at ~50% of spend, and an off-platform economy (BOOTH) it does not pay for (Source: https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-key-stats-japan-growth-concurrents/ ; https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system ; https://creators.vrchat.com/economy/welcome-to-the-ce).

GTA RP (FiveM/NoPixel). Whitelisting, 32-200 seats, paid queue priority ($25-200/month on comparable servers); 221,062 Steam peak in May 2026; Rockstar absorbed Cfx.re in August 2023 (Source: https://steamcharts.com/app/2676230 ; https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/08/11/cfx-re-joins-rockstar-deal-fivem-redm-roleplay). Thousands of FiveM servers are empty and Pax Dei's seat-capped plots sit at a few hundred concurrent.

FFXIV. Engineered scarcity of shared land (lottery since 2022, 45-day auto-demolition with 80% refund, suspended repeatedly) made housing a status object and spawned an unplanned venue scene (Source: https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-updates-lottery-empyreum-patch-6-1 ; https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/ffxiv-house-demolition-suspension-reinstated-temporarily).

Habbo. Never-reissued "rares", room decoration as status, and an 18+ "Origins" relaunch in June 2024 that inherited its community by preserving the 2005 client (Source: https://www.habbo.com/community/article/22292/update-state-habbo-economy ; https://80.lv/articles/the-original-2005-habbo-hotel-opens-its-doors-later-today).

Zepeto. Avatar-first, mobile-first: 2M creators, 20M MAU by March 2022; usage collapsed when Korean schools reopened (Source: https://m.kedglobal.com/newsView/ked202203040009).

Discord. The social graph every world rents (200M+ MAU; Social SDK with voice open to all developers since August 2025) (Source: https://discord.com/press-releases/social-sdk-ingame-communications); a 70,000-ID vendor breach is on record (Source: https://therecord.media/discord-government-docs-exposed-breach).

Meta's Horizon Engine (2025: replaces Unity, claims "well over 100" concurrent users and scaling from cloud rendering to phones) is the nearest live precedent for tiered rendering; its claims are unverified and the switch stranded Unity-built content (Source: https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-horizon-engine-at-a-glance ; https://roadtovr.com/meta-horizon-worlds-engine-upgrade-concurrent-users-horizon-studio/).

What none offers: one persistent self with portable inventory, owned land, in-world creation, adult cash-out, and 18+ spaces designed in rather than retrofitted.

## 5. Constraints a successor must satisfy

Regulation and safety. The UK Online Safety Act's "highly effective" age-check duty applied from 25 July 2025 (fines to GBP 18M or 10% of turnover) (Source: https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/); the DSA Article 28 guidelines expect verification for adult content, minors private by default and an annual risk review (Source: https://cms.law/en/fra/news-information/protecting-minors-in-the-digital-space); Australia's under-16 ban exempts Roblox and Discord only as "primary purpose" games (Source: https://www.esafety.gov.au/node/2109). The Roblox settlements set the template: six age bands with adjacent-band chat by default, adult-to-under-16 contact only via verified connections, liquidated damages (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/01/roblox-age-checks-required-to-chat ; https://www.sdpb.org/crime-courts/2026-07-13/roblox-settlement-yields-tighter-restrictions-over-8-million-for-south-dakota). Verification creates its own breach surface (Discord's 70,000 IDs via a support vendor) (Source: https://therecord.media/discord-government-docs-exposed-breach). Embodied harassment is unprosecutable (UK police, 2024), so platform controls are the only layer (Source: https://lbc.co.uk/news/police-investigate-rape-metaverse). The NO FAKES Act advanced in June 2026; BIPA and Colorado treat biometrics as consent-only (Source: https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s4591 ; https://fpf.org/blog/old-laws-new-tech-as-courts-wrestle-with-tough-questions-under-us-biometric-laws-immersive-tech-raises-new-challenges/). Any cash-out-able item plus chance is gambling somewhere; blockchain tokens forfeit Steam distribution and invite Howey analysis (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2007/07/25/second-life-bans-gambling-following-fbi-investigation/ ; https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102inw4/the-cats-out-of-the-bag-secs-theory-impacts-nft-industry). The European Accessibility Act covers storefront and account flows from 28 June 2025 (Source: https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-accessibility-act-time-start-implementation-projects-now).

Regional minors law beyond UK/EU/AU/US. Brazil's ECA Digital (in force 17 March 2026) applies to any service "likely to be accessed by minors" wherever operated, rejects self-declaration, requires parental tools and loot-box age-gating, fines to R$50M (Source: https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/ ; https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies). Indonesia's PP Tunas (enforceable March 2026, minimum age 16) is the template regulators will copy: Roblox must verify every Indonesian user and unverified accounts lose communication (Source: https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/ ; https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/03/31/indonesia-summons-meta-google-for-failing-to-enforce-under16-age-limits-under-new-childprotection-law/214489). Germany layers JuSchG §24a precautions and KJM-approved verification for adult content (single source) (Source: https://law.ungovr.org/age/de). Japan has no statutory mandate (Source: https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2766/2750/2810). Malaysia (16), India (verifiable parental consent under 18) and Turkey (15) add thresholds (Source: https://bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2573981 ; https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-data-protection-rules-2025-children-data-india/ ; https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/04/turkiye-lawmakers-approve-bill-restricting-social-media-access-for-children-under-15/).

Adult content and the payment rail. No Linden statement on the OSA was found; SL's Adult gate is still a self-declared birth date (since 2012), residents in 2026 say "there is no official age verification in SL", estate owners can only require payment info on file, and Ofcom has fined a small adult service GBP 50,000 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2012/07/10/ll-revises-sl-age-verification/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/533810-age-verification-in-2026/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/knowledgebase/english/accessing-adult-land-and-content-r236/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/518772-new-uk-censorship-requirements/ ; https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/21/ofcom_osa_fine_undress/). VRChat verifies 18+ via Persona since January 2025 and bans pornography platform-wide (Source: https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Assurance ; https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Verification), so a gated Adult district with a real economy has no studied precedent. In July 2025 Valve removed adult titles after acquirers cited Mastercard Rule 5.12.7, itch.io deindexed all NSFW content and DLsite lost Visa/Mastercard; Mastercard denied evaluating any game and the UK government would not intervene (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story ; https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/07/24/itch-io-delists-all-adult-nsfw-content-due-to-pressure-from-payment-processors-and-collective-shout/ ; https://automaton-media.com/en/news/dlsite-disables-visa-mastercard-payment-after-attempt-to-outsmart-credit-card-companies/ ; https://www.thepinknews.com/2025/10/08/visa-and-mastercard-censorship-uk-government). SL and Tilia were not named, plausibly because the merchant is "virtual currency" (unsearched). Visa's VIRP makes adult content Tier 1 (MCC 5967); Mastercard's AN 5196 requires uploader verification, pre-publication review and complaint handling (Source: https://www.legitscript.com/regulatory-and-card-brand-compliance/adult-escort-and-dating-merchants-are-your-practices-in-line-with-virp-updates/ ; https://verifymy.io/resources/regulation/card-brand-requirements). Stripe prohibits adult content; Adyen restricts it with approval (Source: https://conductatlas.com/platform/stripe/stripe-restricted-businesses-list/adult-content-prohibition-and-restriction/ ; https://adyen.com/legal/list-restricted-prohibited). Tumblr's 2018 ban cost ~29% of page views in three months (Source: https://thenextweb.com/insider/2019/03/14/tumblr-traffic-dropped-by-nearly-100m-the-month-after-it-banned-porn/); OnlyFans reversed its 2021 ban within a week once scheme controls were agreed (Source: https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6148524); itch.io's deindex-but-sell model preserved creator income.

Consumer law on the currency, and tax on the rail. The CPC Network's March 2025 "Key Principles on In-game Virtual Currencies" (soft law) require fiat prices on paid currency and on items priced in it, exact-amount purchase without bundles, one currency and the 14-day withdrawal right; a stipend paid in a currency also sold for cash gets no gameplay-only carve-out (Source: https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr ; https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/global/regulatory-spotlight-virtual-currency-vs-consumer-law-part-2). Star Stable was the first target (bundle-only coins, time pressure on children); outcome not found (Source: https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry). BEUC's 2024 complaint asks for a ban on paid in-game currencies, with fiat display and transactions-off-by-default as fallbacks (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/12/bad-credits-consumer-group-files-eu-suit-over-manipulative-payments-in-games-like-fortnite-and-minecraft-calls-for-a-ban). The Digital Fairness Act is unpublished, expected November 2026; its consultation showed strong support for fiat display, loot-box odds and a virtual-currency opt-out (Source: https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html ; https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102m222/digital-fairness-act-european-commission-publishes-responses-to-consultation). SL's model (floating LindeX rate with a USD figure shown, unsearched) is the closest fit (Source: https://secondlife.com/corporate/vat). Tax: DAC7 obliges platform operators, including non-EU ones, to collect identity, TIN and payout-account data from EU-resident sellers and report quarterly consideration, with no floor for services (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview); the US 1099-K threshold reverted to >$20,000 and >200 transactions, the trigger Linden already uses (Source: https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000 ; https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Linden_Lab_Official:Required_Tax_Documentation_FAQ). App stores: Texas's App Store Accountability Act is in force pending appeal; Utah and Louisiana slip to 2027; California's AB 1043 puts an age-bracket signal on OS providers from 2027; developers receive declared age ranges, not birth dates (Source: https://www.pearlcohen.com/fifth-circuit-stays-injunctions-against-texas-app-store-accountability-act/ ; https://www.alstonprivacy.com/california-enacts-digital-age-verification-law/ ; https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/810754).

Communities, accessibility and educators. ## 6. Design requirements for nolife

Each requirement names the cause it discharges and a test combining a launch check with an outcome metric.

R1. Elastic cells of 50-150 simulated avatars under a presence layer; published SLOs of 100-150 per cell and 500-1,000 per place. Cause: 1.1, 2.2 #2. Test: 150-avatar cell load test with voice at launch; p95 cell frame time within SLO at monthly peak, quarterly.

R2. Per-tier asset budgets (triangles, textures, bones, draw distance) with auto-generated fallbacks, enforced server-side across Reference (RTX 4060-class), Lite (Switch 2, Quest 3, mid Android) and Compat (WebGL2, low Android) tiers; over-budget content is impostored or hidden. Cause: 1.1, 1.9, section 5. Test: pipeline down-converts or rejects on all tiers; ≥60 fps on Lite devices at 100-avatar presence, monthly.

R3. Versioned formats (glTF 2.0 + VRM 1.0 runtime, OpenUSD interchange) with a published compatibility policy: conversion is platform-funded and retirement needs a converter plus 24 months' notice. Cause: 1.1, 1.9. Test: policy published; zero assets unconverted after a retirement date.

R4. The platform ships the newcomer client continuously on a documented protocol; third-party clients are expected and blessed. Cause: 1.1. Test: protocol and third-party policy public; first-session completion on the first-party client ≥ any third-party client, annually.

R5. Sandboxed scripting (Luau or WASM) with per-owner CPU, memory and network budgets that throttle the worst offender. Cause: 1.1. Test: a runaway script degrades only its owner's budget in a 150-avatar cell; script-caused frame overruns <1% of cell-hours.

R6. Persistence is charged from day one as rent (home, estate, inventory above a trial allowance); activity is priced near cost; the revenue mix is published annually. Cause: 1.2, 1.7, 2.2 #1, section 5 arithmetic. Test: price list with rent, allowance and activity rates, no free world-hosting tier; rent plus subscriptions ≥50% of platform revenue at month 24.

R7. One published fee stack: own billing on web and PC (app stores as top-ups only); all-in take on creator sales ≤15% round trip, 0% on resale; the platform's required margin stated beside it. Cause: 1.2, 1.5, 2.2 #4. Test: fee page and web billing live; effective take within 2 points of published (annual audit); store-routed money-in ≤25% by month 12.

R8. A creator pool of 20-30% of platform net revenue, weighted by returning purchasing users with anti-farming rules, 10-20% reserved for venues, formula published. Cause: 1.4, 2.2 #4, section 4. Test: formula and payout schedule published; ≥40% of DAU-hours in creator-run places by month 12, reviewed semi-annually.

R9. One closed currency, transferable between users but not off-platform, at a published fixed or daily-fixed rate with fiat shown at every price, exact-amount purchase, no bundles, no chance mechanics paying anything cash-out-able; stipends carry fiat display. Cause: section 5 consumer law, 2.2 #3. Test: every price shows fiat, one currency, no loot boxes; zero CPC-type findings; DFA text reviewed within 90 days of publication.

R10. Cash-out on a licensed, partnered rail settling the platform currency under KYC, with tax data (W-9/W-8, DAC7) collected at payout onboarding and an adult-specialist acquirer pre-qualified in reserve. Cause: 1.9, section 5. Test: rail contract names its adult-content policy, tax data captured before first payout; payout failure <1%, no 1099-K or DAC7 misses.

R11. First hour = first group: every new account lands in a hosted, scheduled cohort; success is "met a person or joined a group"; measured by randomised holdout. Cause: 1.3, 1.4 (never measured). Test: cohort scheduling and holdout live; D30 of treated vs holdout published at month 6, kept only if the lift exceeds the industry D30 base.

R12. Density management with a measured SLO: public hubs target one avatar per 0.1 ha at peak; thin instances merge, empty districts close, newcomers route to live events. Cause: 1.3. Test: merge, close and routing live; median people visible in a newcomer's first session ≥10, SLO re-set after six months.

R13. Publish first-hour, D7, D30, D90, DAU/MAU, peak concurrency and bot share on a fixed cadence with a documented "active" definition and a third-party-readable API. Cause: 1.5, 2.2 shared trait. Test: metrics page and API live; quarterly publication without gaps, third-party reproduction of concurrency within 5%.

R14. Desktop and web (WebGPU, WebGL2 fallback) at launch, native mobile within 12 months; VR optional, never required for identity or inventory; pixel streaming only as a time-capped funnel at a published cost per hour. Cause: 1.6, 1.7, 2.2 #2. Test: two clients at launch and a mobile date; mobile ≥30% of DAU by month 24, streaming cost per retained user published.

R15. A civic calendar on day one (a weekly rhythm and one annual scarce ritual on a region that exists only then), first-party events as synchronised ~100-avatar instances, and roles (host, mentor, DJ, moderator, builder) as first-class objects with slots, badges, stipends and a named deputy for any role owning land or a calendar. Cause: 1.4. Test: calendar with named owners, role and deputy fields in groups; ≥25% of MAU attend a scheduled event monthly, ≥80% of venues survive a leader's 90-day absence.

R16. Scarce shared public land by lottery with full-price tickets and a 45-day use-it-or-lose-it rule with refunds and a published pause policy; private homes are rented under R6 and never auto-demolished. Cause: 1.2, section 4 FFXIV. Test: rules published; public-parcel occupancy ≥70% at peak.

R17. Curated, seat-capped roleplay estates with whitelisting, access and ban lists, roles as ranks, damage-enabled parcels and paid queue priority under published rules (cosmetics and priority yes; pay-to-win and loot boxes no). Cause: 1.4, section 5 census, section 4 GTA RP. Test: estate tools and rules live; ≥50 whitelisted estates with weekly events by month 12.

R18. Continuity for migrating communities: a charter import (group, roles, members, ban list, rating, calendar and land in one transaction), OAR/IAR import, a 12-24-month chat and event bridge with SL, and glTF/VRM export of own-created inventory. Cause: 1.6, 1.9. Test: charter and OAR import demonstrated with a named community; ≥10 named SL institutions migrated with membership by month 18.

R19. Constitutional change control: a published charter, 12 months' notice and grandfathering for persistence price rises, public change-review for client-affecting updates, and an elected advisory assembly whose published vote must be answered in writing (no world has shipped a binding council). Cause: 1.5. Test: charter and assembly rules published; zero retroactive price changes, assembly responses on record.

R20. Portability, sunset and vendor covenant: self-hostable cells, escrowed server images, export of own-created content, 12 months' notice before any shutdown, cells deployable on two hosting providers, swappable voice, and the engine choice documented with its exit cost. Cause: 2.2 #1, 1.9 (OSgrid's wipe shows self-hosting needs escrow). Test: escrow, export tools and a failover demo at launch; annual restore test and failover drill pass.

R21. Age assurance as a configurable regional module: on-device estimation or wallet credential (no stored ID images), per-region thresholds, feature gating rather than binary bans, parental surfaces, adjacent-band chat and private-by-default for minors, OS age signals for app clients, and Adult districts gated by an Ofcom-grade check that also unlocks Adult-tagged commerce. Cause: section 5. Test: UK, EU, Brazil, Indonesia and Texas profiles configured; zero regulator findings, assurance completion rate published.

R22. Adult content as a measured, controlled first-class purpose: maturity-tagged spend and cash-out instrumented from day one; uploader verification, consent records and pre-publication review for Adult-tagged goods; consumer purchases coded as platform currency, never an adult MCC; a deindex-but-sell switch; exclusion from general cash-out held only as a processor-triggered contingency. Cause: 1.4, section 5 rails. Test: controls and switch demonstrated, rail policy on file; adult share of spend published, no processor action.

R23. Always-on embodied safety primitives (personal boundary default-on, one-gesture mute/block/vanish, instance kick/lock, evidence buffer on report, earned trust ranks gating upload, voice and proximity) with incident-based KPIs (reports per 1,000 user-hours, time-to-action) published quarterly and audit logs for information requests. Cause: section 5. Test: primitives present, KPI definitions published; median time-to-action <24h, quarterly report on schedule.

R24. Platform-hosted, escrowed life-support for value-bearing scripted objects (breedables, pets, vendor-dependent HUDs) with a designed end-of-life conversion to a food-independent state. Cause: 2.2 #5, section 3. Test: escrow API and conversion exist; zero value-bearing objects bricked by vendor exit.

R25. Every AI inhabitant is disclosed (badge, notice, on whose behalf it acts), has a purpose, is capped at a published fraction of visible occupants and withdraws as humans arrive; no companion loops for minors; voices licensed or synthetic with provenance; cognition tiered (scripted, on-device, frontier) with a published cost per agent-hour. Cause: section 3 (density only, not purpose), section 5 AI Act. Test: badge, cap and cost model implemented; human D30 in agent-filled places ≥ comparable human-only places (else the cap is lowered); agent cost ≤10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget.

R26. Onboarding produces a furnished home and a dressed avatar before any scripting panel, with a first-party open-licensed avatar body and a consumer interface separate from the creator interface. Cause: 1.3 (transfer from non-UGC worlds assumed). Test: two interfaces, home and avatar in the first session; first-session completion ≥60%.

R27. Accessibility as a shipped client: a first-party text client (Radegast successor) covering chat, inventory, teleport, object list and voiced GPS; speech-to-text and text-to-speech of chat (XAG 119); keyboard-only navigation; motion-sickness and seated options; funded maintenance. Cause: section 5 census. Test: text client ships at launch with an XAG 119 and GAG checklist; a Virtual Ability-scale community (≥1,000 members) operating by month 12.

R28. Education tier: institutional SSO, bulk student accounts, private regions with OAR-equivalent export, General-rated enforcement, a class-region price under US$50/month and a written persistence commitment. Cause: 1.5, section 5 census. Test: tier page and SSO live; ≥20 accredited institutions by month 18.

R29. Language as a shard key: language-tagged community homes with per-language discovery and onboarding; paid localisation for at least Portuguese, Spanish, Japanese and German; local prices and cash rails; chat translation with its retention effect measured; an open avatar pipeline (VRM/glTF import, off-platform marketplaces). Cause: 1.8, 1.1. Test: four localised flows, two local rails and VRM import live; non-US DAU ≥40% and ≥30% of avatar items off-platform by month 24.

R30. Funding posture written down: a published runway plan to self-funding at a Linden-scale audience (≤600K MAU) with fixed costs sized to survive at 100K MAU, no sibling product, and growth features funded only after rent and subscriptions cover costs. Cause: 1.7, 2.2 #1. Test: cost and runway plan published with a headcount cap; operating costs covered by recurring revenue by month 36.

## Additional findings relevant to designing a successor (prior knowledge, not independently sourced in this run)

1. OpenSimulator (2007-present) is an open-source reimplementation of the Second Life server protocol with "Hypergrid" teleports between independently hosted grids. It shows (a) the SL client/server protocol is reproducible, (b) inter-grid identity and asset transfer were solved at a prototype level 15 years ago, and (c) fragmentation without a shared economy produced many small, empty grids. A successor should learn from Hypergrid's identity/asset portability while keeping a single economy.
2. Linden Lab's "SLua" scripting (Luau-based) has been in alpha/beta on the test grid since spring 2025 and is additive: LSL is not being deprecated. This repeats the backward-compatibility ratchet the brief identifies.
3. Avatar render-cost limits ("jelly dolls", 2016) were introduced because a UGC world has no asset budget; a successor should design per-avatar and per-parcel GPU/CPU budgets into the content format from day one rather than bolting them on.
4. Linden Lab introduced formal "Scripted Agent" (bot) registration and later let parcel owners block bots; bots are a non-trivial fraction of login-screen concurrency, so any successor's public metrics should separate humans from agents.
5. LSL has executed on the Mono VM since 2008; the runtime was modernized once but the language and the per-frame "scripts lose first" scheduler were not. The lesson is that a VM swap alone does not fix a frame-budget model.
6. Historical note the brief gets right by implication: Linden Lab's 2008 Openspace repricing and the 2010 layoff were both triggered by land-revenue economics, so land (region rental) was the primary revenue line long before the creator-economy framing; a successor needs a revenue model that does not depend on selling fixed-size server slices.

## Overall assessment

- Nothing in the brief is implausible on its face; the dates and headline figures are broadly consistent with the checker's background knowledge of each company's history.
- The brief's weakest evidence is concentrated in claim 7 (Habbo headcount/recovery from forum + fan-wiki sources) and claim 9 (the Club NEVERDIE year, and the provenance/date of the MindArk deposit figures). Claim 6's "low hundreds" concurrency should be attributed to a named observer.
- Several figures the brief presents flatly are company self-reports (IMVU MAU/products, MindArk volumes, Entropia user count) and should carry that label wherever quoted.

## Additional findings relevant to designing a successor (background knowledge; not verified in this run)

1. The actual successors at scale are not in the brief: Roblox (tens of millions DAU, creator payouts near US$1B/yr) and VRChat (six-figure peak concurrency on New Year's events, desktop + VR, creator economy added 2023, 30% layoff in 2024). Any "nolife" plan should benchmark against these, not only against 2000s-2010s failures.
2. Second Life itself remains the control case: still ~US$600M+ annual user-to-user transactions, hundreds of thousands of monthly users, and a 2024-2025 mobile viewer. Its survival on land fees + Linden-dollar exchange contradicts the brief's suggestion that land/sponsor revenue is inherently fragile; the distinction is land rented by *users* vs. sponsored by *brands*.
3. The blockchain-world wave (Decentraland, The Sandbox, 2021-2023) repeated the purpose-vacuum pattern with DAU in the low thousands despite billion-dollar token valuations; the lesson about concurrency vs. registrations generalises to token holders vs. active users.
4. Meta's Horizon Worlds repeated the hardware-bet error at a far larger budget (Reality Labs cumulative losses > US$60B 2020-2024) and then ported to mobile/web; this reinforces the brief's "ship for hardware the audience has" implication.
5. Several shutdowns in the brief ended with assets lost (vTime, PS Home). The brief's "sunset plan" recommendation has a live precedent: PS Home's fan resurrection and There.com's 2025 viral return both show that preserving client/server binaries and content export is what makes a community revivable.

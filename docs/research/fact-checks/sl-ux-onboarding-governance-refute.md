## Additional findings the researcher missed (verifier recollection; unverified)

1. Linden Lab's own successor attempt, **Sansar** (announced 2014, opened 2017, sold to Wookey in 2020), failed despite far better graphics and VR support: it launched with no user-owned economy, no persistent social institutions and almost no resident content, which is direct evidence that graphics alone do not create retention. The brief's design section does not learn from Sansar.
2. **High Fidelity** (Rosedale, 2013-2019) similarly pivoted away from a social virtual world to spatial audio after failing to reach sustained concurrency, despite open-source and distributed hosting. Two post-SL attempts by SL's own founders failed on community, not technology.
3. Linden launched an official **mobile viewer** (iOS/Android, 2024) and a **PBR/glTF rendering pipeline** (2023-2024), i.e. the "thin client" and "modern graphics" items in the brief's design implications are things the incumbent is already doing; a successor needs to differentiate on something else.
4. **Tilia**, Linden's money-services subsidiary (licensed money transmitter), is the regulatory backbone of the cash-out economy; any successor with creator cash-out needs an equivalent regulated payments entity from day one, which the brief does not mention.
5. Age assurance in SL changed materially in **2024-2025** (Linden moved toward mandatory age verification for Adult access under UK/other regulation); this should be verified as it affects the "maturity zoning built in" recommendation.
6. Recent Linden-published scale (2022-2024 interviews/press): roughly 200,000 daily users and ~750,000-900,000 monthly actives, with ~350,000 new sign-ups a month, implying the first-hour funnel problem is unchanged two decades on.

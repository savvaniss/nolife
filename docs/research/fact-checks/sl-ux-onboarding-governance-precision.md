## Precision issues found without network access

1. Claim 7's "under two avatars per region" does not follow from the numbers in the same sentence (see table).
2. Claim 0's "remained the standard retention figure cited through the decade" is unsupported by anything in the brief and conflicts with the brief's own Section 1, which cites different Humble/Altberg-era funnel figures.
3. Claim 5's "any visible object" overstates CopyBot: it copied appearance (geometry, textures), not scripts or inventory. This matters for the brief's design implication about provenance: CopyBot defeated visual-asset protection, not script/IP protection.
4. Claim 2's "roughly 100 people" is a derivation (30% of ~300+), not a reported figure; it should be labelled as such.
5. Claim 8's "partially reinstated" needs the exclusion named or the qualifier removed.

## Additional findings relevant to a successor world (unverified in session)

- **Linden Lab already built and abandoned a successor.** Sansar (announced 2014, open beta July 2017, VR-first, experience-centric rather than one contiguous world) never carried over SL's users, content or economy, lost most of its team in the 2019-2020 layoffs, and was sold to Wookey Project Corp in March 2020. Any "nolife" plan must explain why it will not repeat Sansar: it launched without the creator economy, without inventory/avatar continuity, and with a VR hardware barrier that was worse than SL's GPU barrier.
- **Real-money economies trigger money-transmitter regulation.** Linden spun out Tilia (2019) and obtained US money-transmitter licences specifically to keep running the LindeX and cash-outs. A successor with a convertible currency must budget for this from day one; it is a governance and compliance workstream, not a feature.
- **Philip Rosedale's own post-mortems (High Fidelity, 2019-2022)** concluded that general-purpose social VR did not have a mass market yet and that SL's survival came from its economy and existing communities, which is the opposite of the "better graphics will fix it" thesis. High Fidelity pivoted away from social VR in 2019.
- **OpenSimulator/Hypergrid** is the de-facto open, federated alternative built on SL's protocols; it is where the 2010-2011 education exodus went and shows that a federated land model can run at much lower cost, at the price of fragmentation and no shared economy.
- **The 2008 Openspace/Homestead pricing revolt** (not in the claim list but in the brief) is the clearest precedent that pricing changes announced by blog post produce legitimacy crises; it strengthens the brief's "change-control with notice periods" recommendation.
- **Linden stopped publishing detailed economic/concurrency statistics around 2010-2011**, so any post-2011 retention or population figure is either a company statement in an interview or an independent estimate (Grid Survey for regions; third-party concurrency trackers). The researcher's open question 1 is unlikely to be answerable from primary sources.

## Additional findings relevant to designing a successor (from checker's knowledge, not fetched)

- The actual successors that worked after this wave were not "SL clones": Roblox (tens of
  millions of daily users, creator payouts in the high hundreds of millions of dollars per
  year) and VRChat (user-built worlds via a Unity SDK, VR optional, roughly half its users
  on flat screens). Both validate the brief's "purpose loop + optional VR + creator economy"
  conclusions and should be the benchmark, not Sansar or High Fidelity.
- High Fidelity's open-source codebase was forked by its community (Vircadia, later Overte)
  after the company withdrew its apps, and Sansar was kept alive by Wookey; open source and
  transferable server software are the proven "sunset insurance" the brief recommends.
- IMVU's VCOIN (Nov 2020) was the first transferable token to receive an SEC no-action
  letter; any successor with a withdrawable currency inherits that regulatory template,
  and Entropia's PED (10:1 USD) shows the resulting economy is net-extractive from players.
- Meta's Horizon Worlds absorbed multi-billion-dollar annual Reality Labs losses and still
  pivoted to mobile in 2024-25; it is the most recent confirmation of the "wrong hardware
  bet" pattern and should be added to the pattern list.
- Habbo's 2024 relaunch of a 2005-era client ("Habbo Hotel: Origins") revived engagement,
  evidence that nostalgia and preserved community identity are a reusable asset (same
  lesson as There.com's 2025 return surge and PS Home's fan revival).
- Second Life itself (the incumbent the successor must beat) is missing from the brief:
  it still runs with hundreds of thousands of monthly users, a user-to-user economy in the
  hundreds of millions of dollars per year, and shipped an official mobile viewer in 2024.

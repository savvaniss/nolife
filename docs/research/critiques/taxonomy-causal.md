# Causal-reasoning review of `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md`

Methodologist critique, 2026-10-09. No web search was used. Sources: the taxonomy, the 14 first-pass ledgers, the six `ledger2-*` rewrites, and the `*-searchverify.json` verdicts in `scratchpad/analysis/`. Where the steelman (`taxonomy-steelman.md`) or completeness (`taxonomy-completeness.md`) critiques already make a point, I cite them and do not repeat the argument; this document is about the inference structure, not the facts.

## 0. The test applied

For every root cause (section 1), every lesson in the successor table (2.1), every pattern (2.2) and every requirement (section 6) I asked four questions:

1. **Counter-example.** Within the corpus itself, is there a platform with the same trait that survived, or without the trait that died? (The corpus names ~35 platforms, so this is answerable without new research.)
2. **Mechanism.** Does the text state a chain from trait to outcome that could in principle be observed (trait -> intermediate variable -> churn/closure), or only co-occurrence?
3. **Traceability.** Does the requirement follow from a cause the taxonomy actually established, or from a borrowed constant (another platform's number) or a ledger's design opinion?
4. **Testability.** Is there an acceptance criterion at launch (a feature or number you can check) *and* an outcome metric that would tell you the requirement worked?

Verdict codes used in the tables: **C** = causal claim with stated mechanism and no in-corpus counter-example; **M** = mechanism stated but counter-example exists (so at most a contributing factor); **X** = correlation only, or post-hoc assignment; **T** = tautology or definitional (the "cause" restates the outcome).

## 1. Summary verdict

The taxonomy is a well-sourced chronicle with an under-specified causal layer. Of the eight SL root causes, two have an observable mechanism that survives the corpus's own counter-examples (1.1 in its narrow per-asset-budget form; 1.6/1.7 in their merged form). Three are constants or outcomes being used to explain a trend (1.3, 1.5, 1.8). One is unfalsifiable as written (1.4). Of the 19 successor "lessons", eleven are contradicted by a platform elsewhere in the same corpus, four are post-mortem behaviours or tautologies rather than causes, and two generalise in a direction that, read literally, predicts nolife itself will fail (Sansar: "a sibling product cannot inherit a community"; Dual Universe: "a single shard worked technically and died commercially" versus R1). The 11 patterns in 2.2 are ranked by frequency over a sample with no survivor control set and with single worlds counted under up to four patterns, so the ranking is not a ranking. Of the 34 requirements, 23 are launch-checkable but only 6 name an outcome metric; 9 contain undefined terms; 7 cite the failure case's own features as evidence; 5 pairs contradict each other; and the numerical constants (35-45%, 45 days, $1.5-3/DAU, 12 months, <10%) are borrowed from unrelated platforms rather than derived from any cause.

None of this says the taxonomy is wrong. It says the document does not yet distinguish "SL had trait X and stalled" from "trait X caused the stall", and the requirements inherit that gap.

### 2.2 Duplicated causes

- **1.6 and 1.7** are one cause (the VR-first sibling bet with no inventory/social-graph continuity). The taxonomy charges it twice, once as "distraction" and once as "hardware timing".
- **1.2 and 1.5** both rest on the 2008 Openspace repricing: 1.2 uses the resulting estate decline as evidence of the land tax; 1.5 uses the same episode as evidence of governance by decree. One event, two causes.
- **1.3 and 1.8** are the same funnel observed at two points in time; 1.8 adds no mechanism.
- **1.4 (purpose vacuum) and 2.2 #6 (density failure)** are, per `ledger-community-purpose`'s own summary, the same problem ("at bottom, a density and role problem rather than a content problem"). The taxonomy lists them as separate patterns and counts SL under both.
- **1.7 and 1.1**: the stated obstacle to mobile (unbudgeted 20-year content) is 1.1's mechanism.

Net: eight causes reduce to about four independent ones (per-asset budgets/compatibility ratchet; land-tier revenue model; funnel/density; VR sibling bet), plus governance as a trust *amplifier* with no demonstrated churn effect.

### 2.3 The missing cause

`ledger-sl-economy` and `ledger-sl-history` both point at a cause the taxonomy never names: **the 2010 switch from growth investment to cash-flow management** under a 30% layoff, followed by private ownership funding ~160 staff from a ~10% take. Every "additive, never a migration" decision in 1.1, the fee reshuffles in 1.2, the mobile delay in 1.7 and the Sansar bet in 1.6 are consistent with a company that chose to stay small and profitable. If that is the cause, the requirements that follow (R1-R34, a product far larger than Linden Lab) do not address it; they presuppose a funding posture the taxonomy never argues for (completeness §2.6: no brief estimates build cost or funding climate).

## 4. Patterns (section 2.2): the ranking has no denominator

1. **No control set.** The 11 patterns are coded only over worlds selected for having failed or stalled. Not one survivor (IMVU, Avakin, Active Worlds, Entropia, OpenSim, VRChat, SL) is coded against the same list. Coding the survivors from the corpus's own facts gives: SL and IMVU under #1 (purpose vacuum); SL and OpenSim under #6 (density); VRChat and Linden under #7 (measurement opacity: the two most durable survivors publish no metrics); Entropia and Pax Dei/FFXIV/Hytale under #10 (paywall); Habbo and Roblox under #9 (safety scandal). Five of eleven "failure patterns" are present in the survivor column.
2. **Non-independent counts.** Sansar is counted under #1, #2, #3 and #11; Blue Mars and High Fidelity under #1, #3 and #11; Horizon under #1, #2, #3, #6; AltspaceVR under #1, #2, #3. "Most frequent" therefore measures how many labels a well-documented failure attracts, not how often a trait precedes death.
3. **Severity labels are unsupported.** #9 "safety scandal (rare, catastrophic)": zero deaths in the corpus are attributed to one (Habbo survived; Roblox survived). #10 "paywall (fatal)": the two paywalled cases cited (TSO, There 2012) died of other things named in their own rows, and three paywalled survivors exist. #2 "operator kills the world (very frequent, fatal)" is **tautological**: a commercial product ends when its operator ends it; the list is the set of closures, not a cause of them. The causal content is in *why* the operator chose to (margin, parent priorities, hardware bet), which are patterns #3, #5, #11.
4. **Overlapping patterns.** #2/#11 (operator closure vs pivot chains and fixed costs), #4/#7 (land speculation vs measurement opacity: same two worlds), #3/#6 (headset gating produces density failure), #1/#6 (see 2.2 above), #5/#11 (UGC margin vs fixed costs at low density). Eleven patterns collapse to roughly six: hardware-gated audience; land-before-population; margin/fixed cost; density; trust shock; safety.
5. **The strongest pattern in the corpus is absent.** Every ledger's additional findings say the same thing in different words: VC- or corporate-subsidised worlds died when funding stopped (Rec Room, Horizon, AltspaceVR, HiFi, Sansar, Sandbox) and the survivors are cheap to keep alive (SL, OpenSim, Resonite on Patreon, Active Worlds, VRChat post-layoff). `ledger-successors-wave2` lists it as a design implication ("fund for a long runway at low usage rather than hype-scale burn"). It is not a pattern in 2.2 and no requirement addresses it; R34 ("fund the core world from the core economy") assumes an economy that exists before the world does.

## 6. Three structural problems, stated once

1. **Outcome-conditioned coding.** Traits were read off dead platforms and called causes; the same traits were read off live platforms and called strengths (section 1.9, section 4). Until the survivors are coded on the same variables as the failures, no pattern in 2.2 and no "lesson" in 2.1 can be called more than a description. The corpus already contains enough survivors to do this without new research.
2. **Mechanisms stop one step short of the outcome.** Nearly every cause stops at an intermediate state (empty regions, a decree, a VR bet, an old audience) and never attaches a churn, retention or revenue series to it. The two ledgers that looked for the link (community-purpose on group membership; sl-ux on orientation redesigns) report that it was never measured. The requirements then specify the intermediate state and call it done.
3. **Constants are imported, not derived.** The numbers in section 6 come from whichever platform currently has a number. A requirement derived from a cause would read "X because the mechanism in 1.n implies a threshold near Y"; none does. The practical consequence is that the requirement set is a union of every winner's current configuration plus every failure's negation, which is why it describes a product larger than any survivor (steelman §6).

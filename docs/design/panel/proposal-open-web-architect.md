# nolife: an open-protocol successor to Second Life

Design proposal from the open-standards angle (glTF/OpenUSD/VRM/ActivityPub; OpenSimulator/Hypergrid and avatar-portability work). Prepared October 2026. Evidence: `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (requirements R1-R30) and the briefs on legacy interop, alt survivors, networking and scale, community census, business models, safety and identity, graphics, SL economy, community purpose, adult economy and rails, virtual-currency law, AI agents and non-English communities. No web search was used; every cited fact comes from those files with its original URL.

## 1. Thesis and premise

**nolife is a protocol with a reference implementation and a chartered economy, not a product.** The name is read three ways, each a design commitment. First, the world has *no life of its own*: it ships no quests, lore or story, because purpose is supplied by groups with a schedule and a place, never by the operator (taxonomy 1.4). Second, there is *no Second Life*: no single company whose funding posture, ToS revision or sale can end the world, because subsidy collapse is the strongest successor-killer in the corpus (taxonomy 2.2 #1) and operators kill communities more often than members do. Third, it reclaims the insult thrown at people who live online: the life you have here is owned by nobody but you, so identity, inventory and social graph are portable at the protocol level rather than features of one grid.

The premise is the comparison the taxonomy makes central. OpenSimulator runs SL's simulator and viewer with land 5-13x cheaper, full OAR/IAR export and self-hosting, and after seventeen years has ~45-48K monthly actives, one-twelfth of SL, with no economy, no audience and no stability: OSgrid wiped its database in 2025 and was offline three weeks in 2026 (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). Second Life has the economy (~US$650M/yr of flow, ~US$78M/yr cashed out, 21,152 earning creators in 2023) and no portability: every export path reduces to "creator of every part" and the 2012 Export permission bit was never adopted (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2012/09/hypergrid-permissions-need-viewer-change/). Portability without an economy is a hobby; an economy without portability is a hostage. nolife's bet is that the two coexist if the economy is *federated under a charter*: anyone may run a shard, but only shards that sign and keep the Charter get the currency rail, the directory and the safety network.

The protocol suite has eight separately versioned specifications: **Passport** (portable pseudonymous identity with a private age-band credential), **Vault** (content-addressed inventory in glTF 2.0/VRM 1.0 with signed provenance and permission bits; OpenUSD for scenes), **Cell** and **Presence** (simulation and crowd wire protocols), **Crossing** (shard-to-shard teleport and federation, the successor to Hypergrid), **Charter** (groups as institutions: roles, treasury, calendar, land, ban list, succession), **Till** (one fiat-pegged closed currency and its ledger), **Warden** (age credentials, trust ranks, signed abuse reports, shared hash lists) and **NAR** (the nolife archive, a superset of OAR/IAR). Reference server and clients are Apache-2.0. Two organisations hold it up: the **nolife Foundation**, a non-profit on the KDE e.V./AOUSD model owning the specifications, trademark, conformance suite and reference code, with a partly elected board (section 9); and **Commons**, a commercial operator running the flagship shards, the Bazaar marketplace and the Till rail through a licensed money-services partner, bound by the Charter like any other operator. If Commons dies, any chartered operator can keep every shard alive from escrowed images and exportable NARs (R20).

The trade-off is stated up front. An open protocol gives up three things a closed platform keeps: technical DRM on the asset stream (any client can replay what it receives, as CopyBot proved in 2006), a monopoly take rate, and unilateral moderation. The first was never real in SL either, the second is what R7 forbids, and the third is replaced by a chartered network that can defederate. Everything here is sized for a Linden-scale audience: self-funding at or below 600K MAU, fixed costs that survive at 100K (R30).

## 2. Who comes first and why

The wedge is the population that already knows how to pack an archive and has already lost content once: the OpenSim hypergrid and the institution-holders of Second Life. They are small, older, long-tenured and organised around communities rather than games (legacy-interop brief, pattern 5), and they are the only people for whom "your group, land, inventory and calendar move as one object" is decisive.

**Cohort 1, OpenSim grids as chartered shards (launch minus 6 months).** Kitely (regions from US$14.95-39.95/month, idle regions suspended and reloaded on teleport, the de facto hypergrid store), DigiWorldz (US$8-30/month regions) and AviWorlds (Brazilian-run, Portuguese-heavy) are invited to run the reference server beside their OpenSim stacks as Chartered shards (Source: https://www.kitely.com/virtual-world-news/2015/06/01/60-off-large-worlds-and-dedicated-memory-guarantee/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/digiworldz/). Day one they get NAR import of every OAR and IAR they hold, the LSL-to-Luau transpiler, Till (the economy the hypergrid never had) and Kitely Market plugged into Vault. OSgrid's 2025 wipe is the pitch: nolife's persistence is verifiable (open source, escrowed images, exportable archives) rather than promised (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2025/02/osgrid-wiping-its-database-on-march-21-you-have-five-weeks-to-save-your-stuff).

**Cohort 2, named Second Life institutions (launch minus 3 to plus 12 months).** The census brief lists the decision-makers who can each bring hundreds to thousands: Virtual Ability (1,000-1,300 members, own estate, text-first Cape Able), the VWBPE committee, Second Pride's board, Fantasy Faire's Relay for Life team, the 1920s Berlin Project, the Crack Den collective, Luskwood, Cafe Musique, First UCC SL and UUCSL (Source: https://virtualability.org/about-us ; https://secondlife.com/destination/vwbpe ; https://ryanschultz.com/2019/05/30/the-1920s-berlin-project-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-in-second-life/). Each gets a Charter import (group, roles, opt-in members, ban list, rating, calendar and land in one transaction, R18), a rebuild grant (12 months of estate rent and 40 funded builder-hours), the Reader text client for Virtual Ability's blind members (R27) and an 18-month chat-and-event bridge to their SL group so the audience is not asked to choose on day one. Whole communities move on institutional continuity and a credible persistence guarantee (census brief); both are protocol features here.

**Cohort 3, the diaspora of closed platforms (rolling).** Spatial deleted Free and Pro creator worlds on 27 July 2026; Rec Room announced shutdown for 1 June 2026; Ready Player Me's public creator closed 31 January 2026 with exported GLBs still loading (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/ ; https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/ ; https://genies.com/blog/ready-player-me-shutdown). A glTF/VRM importer plus a hosted shard offer is the cheapest acquisition channel in the corpus.

**Cohort 4, VRM creators and educators (launch plus 6-18 months).** VRChat-Japan grew on BOOTH's ~10.4bn-yen 3D-model trade, not on VRChat's store (Source: https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html); nolife imports VRM 1.0 natively and lets BOOTH, Gumroad and Kitely Market sell straight into Vault, so Japanese creators dual-home rather than migrate (R29). Educators left SL when the 2010 discount removal doubled their costs, for grids with control and backups; a 2014 study found cost and persistence decisive (Source: https://hypergridbusiness.com/?p=35085 ; https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/49578/). They get the Education tier at under US$50 per class region with institutional SSO and NAR export at grant end (R28).

Recruitment is by named-leader outreach, not paid acquisition: at 1-2% payer rates, mobile CPIs of US$1-4 imply US$75-300 per payer, which no social world has recovered (Source: https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267).

## 3. The purpose engine

The platform supplies the *machinery* of purpose and none of its content. Every mechanism is a Charter feature, portable across shards, so a community that moves takes its institutions with it.

**Roles are objects (R15).** A Charter defines 2-10 roles with abilities, as SL groups do (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Group), plus three things SL never had: a schedule slot, a stipend line in the treasury, and a named deputy for any role owning land or a calendar. Host, mentor, DJ, moderator, builder, performer and steward are first-party templates with badges. The test: 80% of venues survive a leader's 90-day absence, as the Anglican Cathedral survived its founder's exit (Source: https://episcopal.cafe/transitions_at_the_anglican_cathedral_in_second_life/).

**Institutions have treasuries and successors.** A Charter holds a Till balance, owns land and objects, publishes a calendar to the federated directory and carries a succession rule (named deputies, then a member vote). Charters federate notices over ActivityPub, so a venue's programme reaches Fediverse followers without the platform paying for reach.

**Scarcity is written down (R16).** Public districts on Commons shards are the only scarce land: parcels go by lottery with full-price tickets refunded to losers, a 45-day use-it-or-lose-it rule with an 80% refund and a published pause policy, FFXIV's scheme that turned housing into a status object and spawned a venue scene (Source: https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-updates-lottery-empyreum-patch-6-1). Private estates are rented and never auto-demolished. Items can be declared never-reissued at mint and the Bazaar enforces it, Habbo's "rares" mechanic (Source: https://www.habbo.com/community/article/22292/update-state-habbo-economy).

**Events are synchronised instances.** First-party events run as ~100-avatar cells synchronised across a place (Fortnite's concerts ran in ~100-player instances), with a declared event mode for 2,000-5,000 impostored attendees (section 7). The civic calendar has a weekly rhythm (market day, open stage, Assembly) and one annual ritual, **Founding Week**, a district that exists seven days a year and is burned at the end, modelled on BURN2's single week (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/).

**Governance is elected and answered (R19).** An Assembly of 15 is elected annually by Passports with 90 days' tenure; turnout is published against EVE's CSM benchmark of 12-17% of eligible accounts (Source: https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/csm-7-the-results). Its published votes must be answered in writing by the Foundation and Commons within 30 days, and it elects three Foundation board seats.

**Status is earned and portable.** Trust rank (section 9) gates upload, voice and proximity; role badges, Charter offices and verified catalogues show on the Passport. None of it can be bought.

**The first hour (R11, R26).** A new Passport lands in a hosted cohort of 8-12 with a paid host, scheduled every 30 minutes per language, Virtual Ability's mentor model (Source: https://virtualability.org/history/). Before the cohort leaves the porch, the newcomer has a furnished Home parcel and a dressed avatar on the open-licensed Commons body; the cohort then attends one live event. Success is "met a person or joined a Charter", measured against a randomised holdout; the mechanic is kept only if its D30 lift exceeds the industry base.

**The first year.** Month 1: Home, cohort, one Charter joined. Month 3: trust rank unlocks upload and voice; first Bazaar purchase or sale. Month 6: eligible for a public-land lottery ticket and a Charter role. Month 12: eligible to stand for Assembly, found a Charter with a treasury, or run a whitelisted roleplay estate (R17): seat caps, access and ban lists, roles as ranks, damage-enabled parcels, paid queue priority under published rules (cosmetics and priority yes; pay-to-win and loot boxes no), the GTA RP pattern where whitelisting and 32-200 seats are the product (Source: https://www.sportskeeda.com/gta/how-join-nopixel-gta-5-rp-server-a-step-by-step-guide).

## 4. World structure

**Vocabulary.** A **shard** is one operator's cluster (Commons, a chartered grid, a university, a hobbyist). A **place** is a named contiguous area made of **cells**; a cell is the unit of simulation and holds 50-150 interacting avatars (R1). A **district** is a discovery grouping with a language tag and a maturity rating. Crossing makes a teleport between shards look like one within a shard.

**Land model.** Three kinds, priced in section 11: **Homes** (1/16 of a cell, included with a Resident subscription or US$3.99/month), **Estates** (one cell-equivalent of 65,536 m² with a content weight budget, US$39/month, suspended when idle and reloaded on arrival in the Kitely manner, never auto-demolished) and **Public districts** (Foundation-owned, lottery-allocated, 45-day rule). Estates may be sub-let only at or below the published rent, removing the land-baron spread that produced SL's first millionaire (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshe_Chung). Chartered shards set their own prices, shown in the directory beside Commons prices.

**Persistence.** Each cell's state is a document store plus an append-only event log with per-cell and per-owner write budgets in the Roblox DataStore manner (60 + 10 × players requests per minute per server is the shipped precedent) (Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits). Owners snapshot and roll back; any owner exports a NAR at any time; assets are content-addressed so a NAR restored elsewhere resolves the same hashes from Vault.

**Discovery.** A federated, machine-readable directory (the Hypergrid Business census and SL's Destination Guide merged) lists places with live human occupancy, bot share, language, rating and the next three events. Ranking is by humans present now and events in the next hour, never by paid placement, which exists only in a labelled Bazaar row.

**Density management (R12).** Target: one avatar per 0.1 ha in public hubs at peak, roughly 40x SL's grid-wide average of fewer than two avatars per region (community-purpose brief, finding 1). Thin instances of a place merge when both are below 20% capacity; public districts below 10% occupancy for 30 days fold back into wilderness; newcomers and idle residents are routed to the nearest live event; "join where my people are" follows friends across shards. Private estates are exempt and are suspended rather than closed.

**How empty-world is avoided.** Four levers: public places opened only when occupancy warrants; a calendar that concentrates people in time; disclosed agents that fill to a liveliness floor and withdraw as humans arrive (section 8); and a directory that shows live humans so nobody teleports into a beautiful empty build. The metric is median people visible in a newcomer's first session, target ≥10, published quarterly (R13).

## 5. Creation and creator economy

**Tools.** In-world creation is first-class because maker cultures grew inside the world (Resonite, Dreams, SL) while worlds that made Unity the real editor (ChilloutVR, Sinespace, Spatial) never grew an economy (alt-survivors brief). The reference clients ship a prim-and-mesh editor, a glTF PBR material editor, a Luau script editor with live reload and a USD stage importer for Blender, Maya and Houdini; SL creators asking in July 2026 for glTF hierarchies with proper origins and USD support get exactly that (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/07/03/2026-week-27-sl-ccug-meeting-summary-updating-sl-mesh/).

**Ownership and licence.** A creator signs every asset with their Passport key; the Vault record carries a **provenance envelope** (creator, date, licence, Copy/Modify/Transfer and the OpenSim-style **Export** bit, maturity tag, per-tier budgets). The content licence is one plain-language page: the platform may display and transmit the asset within the federation, with sub-licensing only on a defined affirmative action (listing in the Bazaar), because the 2013 ToS episode made Renderosity and CG Textures ban their assets from SL (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms). Every conformant client honours provenance; it is a legal and reputational control, not a technical one. Own-created content exports as glTF/VRM/USD with its envelope at any time; Export-flagged purchases export too; non-exportable purchases stay in Vault and travel with the Passport across every chartered shard, the continuity SL never offered.

**Marketplace and take rate (R7).** The Bazaar is an open protocol, not a captive store: Kitely Market, BOOTH or Gumroad can list and deliver into Vault, reversing SL's 2009 absorption of Xstreet and OnRez (Source: https://lindenlab.wordpress.com/2009/01/20/xstreet-sl-and-onrez-to-join-linden-lab/). The published fee stack: 10% commission on primary Bazaar sales (0% for off-platform stores delivering through Vault), 0% on resale, 2% cash-out plus the partner's payout fee: 12% all-in on a Bazaar sale cashed to a bank, inside R7's 15% ceiling, published beside the required margin. SL's "90/10" is in practice 20-31% once Marketplace, LindeX and cash-out fees stack (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/). Minimum price is US$0.25, against VRChat's 600-credit (~US$5) floor (Source: https://creators.vrchat.com/economy/guidelines).

**Creator and venue pool (R8).** 25% of Commons net platform revenue goes to a monthly pool weighted by returning purchasing users per Charter and creator, with Epic's November 2025 anti-farming rules (purchasers only, per-island retention) adopted wholesale, and 15% reserved for Charters that host scheduled events, measured by distinct returning members (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items). The pool is a share of platform revenue, not gross spend, because a Fortnite-style 40% of gross cannot be funded at Linden scale (taxonomy section 5).

**Cash-out (R10).** Till is settled by a licensed money-services partner (the Tilia/Thunes pattern) with KYC at payout onboarding; W-9/W-8 and the DAC7 data set are collected before the first payout because services have no DAC7 floor (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview). Payout threshold US$25, weekly.

**Life-support escrow (R24).** Scripted objects that phone home (breedables, pets, vendor HUDs) register their service with a platform-hosted, escrowed life-support API; if the vendor exits, the service keeps running and the objects convert to a designed food-independent state, so no Ozimals-style hibernation recurs (Source: https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html).

**Moderation of UGC.** Every upload is scanned at the receiving shard (shared illegal-content hash list, Adult-tagging classifier, budget check); Adult-tagged goods require uploader verification, consent records for likeness-based content and pre-publication review, the controls Visa's VIRP and Mastercard's AN 5196 expect of UGC platforms (Source: https://verifymy.io/resources/regulation/card-brand-requirements). A deindex-but-sell switch hides Adult listings from browse within hours while direct links keep working, itch.io's survivable middle path (Source: https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/76389-itch-io-follows-steam-in-removing-adult-games-amid-pressure-from-payment-processors). Likeness replicas of real people need verified consent.

## 6. Graphics and client strategy

**Fidelity target.** Tier A is the UE 5.8 look: Lumen or MegaLights, Nanite for static geometry, Substrate materials, ML upscaling, at 60 fps (16.6 ms) at 1440p with DLSS/FSR/XeSS Quality on an RTX 4060/5060-class GPU, the modal Steam card being an 8-12 GB xx60/xx70 (Source: https://mixed-news.com/en/steam-september-2026-hardware-survey-rtx-5070-32gb-ram/). Tier B is Lumen Lite-class GI or probes at 60 fps and 1080p on Switch 2, Quest 3, M-series Macs and mid Android, Epic's own 2026 target (Source: https://www.unrealengine.com/news/unreal-engine-5-8-is-now-available). Tier C is WebGL2 or low Android at 30 fps with no dynamic GI, a 200-500 m draw distance and VRChat-Quest-style avatar caps.

**Engines and clients.** The protocol is engine-neutral: runtime content is glTF 2.0 with KHR PBR extensions and VRM 1.0 for humanoids, scenes are OpenUSD, so any engine can be a client (R3, R4). Three first-party clients ship: **Lantern**, the reference desktop client on UE 5.8 for Tiers A and B (the UE6 migration, reported for late 2027, is budgeted); **Ember**, a zero-install WebGPU browser client on Babylon.js with WebGL2 fallback for Tiers B and C, since WebGPU has shipped in Chrome, Safari 26 and desktop Firefox but Linux Firefox, Exynos phones and old Android still need the fallback (Source: https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status); and **Reader**, the text client (section 9). Lantern's engine exit is documented (R20): Godot 4.7 (Jolt physics, D3D12/Vulkan, OpenXR 1.1, no Nanite/Lumen equivalent) is the open fallback for Tier B and Ember covers Tier C, so losing Unreal costs Tier A, not the world. Third-party clients are expected; Firestorm, not Linden's viewer, carried SL's users (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/firestorm-by-far-most-popular-on-hypergrid/).

**Budgets (R2).** Every Vault asset carries per-tier budgets in its envelope and the receiving shard generates fallbacks at upload: meshoptimizer LOD chains and impostor atlases, KTX2/Basis texture transcoding, skeleton pruning. Avatar ranks follow VRChat's ladder: Excellent ≤7,500 triangles, Good ≤10,000, Medium ≤20,000 (the Quest ceiling), Poor ≤70,000, Very Poor above; Tier B shows Medium and better, Tier C Good and better, Tier A everything with a user-set minimum (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202115). Texture memory per avatar is 40 MB (B/C) and 200 MB (A); 256 bones; 8 materials (B/C), 32 (A). Content weight replaces Land Impact: 2M triangles streamed per cell in B, 10M in A, 500K in C. Over-budget content is impostored or hidden, never rendered twice, so the hardware floor cannot rise with the catalogue, SL's one root cause with no counter-example (taxonomy 1.1).

**Devices.** Desktop and web at launch, native mobile (Ember wrapped, then native Tier B) within 12 months, VR an optional view of Lantern never required for identity or inventory (R14), since 1.77% of Steam users had a headset in January 2026 and Quest shipments fell 16% (Source: https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/). Pixel streaming is a time-capped funnel only (20 hours per Passport per month, cost published), because Firestorm Zero and Project Zero both closed and raw cost is ~US$1/user-hour against a US$19.99 consumer ceiling (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/ ; https://thegabmeister.com/p/unreal-pixel-stream-aws/).

**How UGC stays beautiful.** Budgets enforced at the format level, an open-licensed Commons body and material library that set a quality floor, and Tier A features (MegaLights, Substrate, optional path tracing) reserved for content that passes budget, so the best-looking places are also the cheapest to run. 3D Gaussian splats are place-capture backdrops with proxy collision and per-tier caps (≤500K on mobile), with commercial training licences secured first; neural texture compression is a 2027 upgrade once SM 6.10 leaves preview.

## 7. Architecture

**Two layers (R1).** The **Presence** layer carries position, pose, chat and voice at 5-10 Hz through interest-managed relays and can show 1,000-5,000 avatars in one place. The **Cell** layer simulates physics, scripts and vehicles for 50-150 interacting avatars; a place is up to ten cells joined by an authoritative entity store, the Star Citizen replication-layer shape, which in August 2026 is still "quasi-dynamic" at ~500 players per shard after a decade (Source: https://starcitizen.tools/Comm-Link:Letter_from_the_Chairman_-_2026-08-27). Published SLOs: 100-150 per cell with voice, 500-1,000 per place, 2,000-5,000 only in declared event mode with impostor avatars, pre-mixed audio and simplified interaction, the ScavLab envelope of 4,144 real players (Source: https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/tennis-for-two-thousand-the-story-behind-improbables-mass-event-in-scavlab/). No 10,000 promise: that is Improbable's 2022 marketing, not observed load.

**Networking.** Cell and Presence run over QUIC (WebTransport in browsers) with a WebSocket fallback; per-client bandwidth is capped at 50-200 kbps; avatars beyond ~30 m are impostors. Voice is a self-hostable WebRTC SFU behind an abstraction that can swap to ODIN (EUR 0.29 per peak concurrent user per month) or the Discord Social SDK (free since August 2025), because Hathora, Dolby.io Communications and Firestorm Zero's provider all vanished inside 18 months (Source: https://odin.4players.io/pricing ; https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/8/discord-social-sdk-launches-to-all-devs-with-in-game-voice-chat-and-cross-platform-play/ ; https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174). Crossing moves a Passport between shards with its Vault references, Charter memberships and block list; the destination fetches assets by hash. Assets live in S3-compatible, content-addressed object storage so any host can serve them; snapshots are hourly, retained 30 days.

**Scripting (R5).** Scripts are Luau compiled to WASM in a component-model sandbox with per-owner CPU, memory and network budgets; the scheduler throttles the worst offender rather than starving all scripts as SL does. Luau is chosen because Roblox runs it and Linden's own SLua is a Luau fork in main-grid beta since December 2025, so one language serves migrants from both (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Lua_Alpha). An `ll` shim implements the LSL function surface on top.

**Deployment and vendor covenant (R20).** Cell servers are containers with one manifest for Edgegap, Amazon GameLift Servers, Nitrado GameFabric or bare metal; an annual failover drill moves a Commons shard between two providers. Images are escrowed with the Foundation as the public repository plus signed releases.

**Cost per CCU.** At US$0.055-0.07 per vCPU-hour, a 2-4 vCPU cell for 100 avatars costs US$0.14-0.28/hour compute and US$0.09-0.23/hour egress at 20-50 kbps per client, about US$0.002-0.005 per avatar-hour before voice and persistence (Source: https://edgegap.com/pricing ; https://aws.amazon.com/gamelift/servers/pricing/). A 10,000-CCU federation runs at US$50-100/hour of server cost; the real bill is trust and safety, budgeted at US$1.50-3.00 per DAU per month against Roblox's automated floor of ~US$0.92 (Source: https://app.edgar.tools/companies/RBLX).

**Scaling plan.** Year 1: 5,000 peak CCU; year 3: 25,000 across the federation; year 5: 60,000, above SL's 2026 peak of 48,802 (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/05/11/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-update-10th-may-2026/). Capacity scales by adding cells and relays; nothing has a global lock, and chartered shards bring their own hosting.

## 8. AI and the living world

Agents are protocol participants, not a feature: an agent is a Passport with the `agent` flag set, a declared operator and purpose, and a persistent badge in every client, which is what EU AI Act Article 50 requires from 2 August 2026 (a plain notice, never a generic "assistant" label, and on whose behalf it acts) (Source: https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/). Nothing in the corpus shows agent-filled density retaining humans, so agents are a disclosed, capped stopgap, not a purpose mechanism (taxonomy section 3; R25).

**What they do.** Five sanctioned roles: *greeter* (assists a human cohort host, never replaces one); *shopkeeper twin* (an opt-in, interview-seeded stand-in tending a creator's shop while they are offline, with full logs; such agents replicated their subjects' survey answers at 85% of self-consistency in a 1,052-person study) (Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1); *chronicler* (writes the shard's weekly record from public events); *translator* (per language district, with its retention effect measured because nobody has published one) (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/02/roblox-introduces-ai-powered-real-time-chat-translations-in-16-languages); and *steward's assistant* (triages abuse reports and drafts actions; a human confirms every sanction). A RimWorld-style storyteller per district paces ambient events on a real clock and seasons, the Animal Crossing lesson that routine beats agent intelligence (alife brief).

**Guardrails.** Agents are capped at 20% of visible occupants in a public place and withdraw as humans arrive; no romantic or open-ended companion loops for minors (Character.AI banned under-18 open chat in November 2025; Italy fined Replika EUR 5M) (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots); voices are synthetic with provenance or licensed; no replicas of real performers without bargained terms. Generative mesh tools (Roblox Cube-class) feed Vault as tooling with AI provenance marked; they do not replace the authoritative simulation.

**Costs.** Cognition is tiered: scripted routines (free), a ~4B-parameter on-device or edge model for ambient talk (the NVIDIA ACE pattern), frontier calls only for memorable interactions, with a published cost per agent-hour. Agent spend is capped at 10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget, and human D30 in agent-filled places must match human-only places or the cap is lowered. Because 62.7% of surveyed gamers feel very negatively about generative AI, a chartered shard may run with agents off and the directory shows which do (Source: https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/).

## 9. Identity, safety, governance, regulation

**Passport.** A pseudonymous identity: a passkey/WebAuthn key pair anchored at a home shard, a freely changeable display name, no real-name requirement, the settlement every surviving platform reached (Source: https://androidcentral.com/gaming/virtual-reality/meta-finally-kills-the-oculus-quest-2-facebook-account-requirement). A Passport can move home shard with its Vault, Charters and block list; the exit right is the ultimate appeal.

**Age assurance (R21).** A private age band is a verifiable credential from Foundation-approved providers (on-device facial estimation, EU Digital Identity Wallet proof, operator checks), stored as a band, never an image; appeals never upload documents to a human vendor, the path through which Discord leaked 70,000 IDs (Source: https://cyberinsider.com/discord-confirms-70000-government-ids-exposed-in-third-party-breach/). Bands are 13-15, 16-17, 18+ and 18+ Adult-verified (an Ofcom-grade "highly effective" check) (Source: https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/). Commons shards admit 16+; chartered education shards may admit 13+ under institutional consent. Defaults follow the Roblox settlement template: adjacent-band chat, minors private, no stranger voice, geolocation off, adult-to-minor contact only through verified connections (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/01/roblox-age-checks-required-to-chat). Regional profiles are configuration: UK (OSA), EU (DSA Article 28), Brazil (ECA Digital, no self-declaration, loot-box-free mode), Indonesia (16, unverified accounts lose communication), Texas and California app-store signals (Source: https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/ ; https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/).

**Embodied safety (R23).** Always-on primitives in every conformant client: a default-on personal boundary with user-set radius, one-gesture mute, block and vanish, instance kick and lock for hosts, and a 60-second rolling evidence buffer attached to any report, because UK police concluded in 2024 that a virtual assault was not viable to charge (Source: https://lbc.co.uk/news/police-investigate-rape-metaverse). Trust ranks on the VRChat ladder (Visitor to Trusted plus a visible Nuisance flag) gate upload, scripting, voice and proximity and cannot be earned by idling or mass-friending (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system); other shards may discount a home shard's rank.

**Moderation in a federation.** The hardest problem for an open protocol. Each shard moderates its own places and publishes reports per 1,000 user-hours and median time-to-action (target under 24 hours). Warden federates signed abuse reports that follow the offender's Passport, a shared hash list of removed illegal content, and an open-source Sentinel-class classifier (Roblox open-sourced its own, which caught 35% of grooming cases proactively) (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/08/open-sourcing-roblox-sentinel-preemptive-risk-detection). The sanctions ladder: shard action, then a Warden flag visible to all shards, then loss of Charter (no Till, directory or Crossing to Commons), then defederation; an unsigned Open shard gets identity federation only. Audit logs and retention policies are built for information requests, since Ofcom fines as much for not answering as for not complying.

**Adult content (R22).** Adult is a first-class, measured purpose, confined to Adult-chartered shards and districts gated by the Adult-verified credential; spend and cash-out are maturity-tagged from day one so the economy can be defended with numbers SL never had. The Till rail stays at one remove: consumers buy platform currency, never an adult MCC, plausibly why SL and Tilia were not named in the 2025 processor wave while Steam, itch.io and DLsite were (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story). An adult-specialist acquirer is pre-qualified; exclusion of Adult goods from cash-out is held only against a specific processor demand, since Tumblr's blanket exit cost ~29% of traffic in three months (Source: https://thenextweb.com/insider/2019/03/14/tumblr-traffic-dropped-by-nearly-100m-the-month-after-it-banned-porn/).

**Community self-rule.** Charters set their own rules inside their land (access and ban lists, roles as ranks, ratings), so Gor, noir roleplay and a congregation coexist on one federation. Appeals go shard, then Foundation ombudsman for Charter breaches, then the Assembly's published review.

**Constitutional change control (R19).** The Charter requires 12 months' notice and grandfathering for persistence price rises, public change-review for client-affecting updates, and a written answer to every Assembly vote. The Foundation board has nine seats: three Assembly-elected, two from chartered shard operators, two from verified creators (one vote each), two appointed by Commons. The Charter's never-list: blockchain tokens or NFTs (Steam bans them; profit-expectation marketing invites Howey analysis), chance mechanics paying anything transferable (SL's 2007 rule), and a sibling product (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/steam-is-now-banning-all-games-that-engage-in-cryptocurrency-or-nft-trading). NeosVR died of a founder dispute over crypto; a constitutional exclusion plus forkable code is the insurance (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeosVR).

**Accessibility (R27).** Reader is a funded first-party text client, the Radegast successor: chat, IMs, notices, inventory, teleport, object list, a voiced GPS, speech-to-text captions on voice, text-to-speech of chat (XAG 119) and keyboard-only navigation, because Radegast has had no release since February 2022 and blind residents depend on it (Source: https://applevis.com/comment/73862 ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/119). Storefront, account and support flows meet the European Accessibility Act from launch. Eye, face and hand tracking are processed on-device, only abstracted animation data leaves the device, and there is no sale or gaze-based targeting (BIPA and Colorado HB 24-1130 proof).

## 10. Legacy support

The rule from the legacy brief governs everything: rights, not formats, are the barrier, so the programme is built around *who may export*, with formats the easy part.

**Second Life.** There is no official export, so the path runs through what residents can lawfully take: Firestorm and Kokua export own-created objects as Collada with own-created textures (Source: https://modemworld.me/2013/08/24/). The **Ferry** importer does six things. (1) *Creator claim*: a resident proves control of an SL account with a one-time code in their SL profile or Marketplace store; Ferry signs the imported catalogue with provenance pointing at the claimed identity, so reputation moves with the creator. (2) *Geometry*: Collada and glTF mesh import directly; prims and sculpts convert to mesh with OpenSimulator's BSD-licensed PrimMesher lineage. (3) *Avatars*: the Bento 133-bone skeleton maps to the VRM 1.0 humanoid with a `nolife-bento` extension preserving the wing, tail and face bones VRM lacks; Bakes-on-Mesh layers composite to textures at import; .anim and BVH become glTF animations. (4) *Scripts*: creators import their own LSL source through the transpiler and `ll` shim; calls that depend on SL-hosted services are flagged for rewrite. (5) *Charter import* (R18) with the leader's attestation. (6) *Environment*: EEP and Windlight settings export to USD sky assets.

What cannot be imported is the resident's *look*, because 2026 avatars are third-party mesh bodies sold no-mod, no-transfer (Maitreya, Legacy, LeLutka). Two answers: a first-party, open-licensed **Commons body** (Bento-compatible, VRM-rigged, Bakes-on-Mesh-equivalent) so migrants are not naked (R26), and a **Body Vendor Programme** offering SL body makers a 0%-commission first year and a verified-vendor badge for re-issuing their bodies with the Export bit to customers who prove purchase by Marketplace order record, the redelivery mechanism Kitely used to rescue stranded OSgrid buyers (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686).

**Dual presence (R18).** For 18 months a Charter can run a relay mirroring group chat and calendar notices between its SL group and its nolife Charter, with Discord as the neutral third channel, so venues keep their SL audience while building a new one. Linden's CCUG is itself moving toward glTF and asking about USD, so the formats converge whatever the Lab decides.

**OpenSimulator.** NAR is a superset of OAR and IAR: `load oar` and `load iar` work unchanged, including terrain, parcels, object inventories, scripts and referenced assets. The transpiler covers the OpenSim LSL dialect and common OSSL functions. Hypergrid's identity model (home grid, foreign agent) is the direct ancestor of Crossing, so a grid running the reference server beside OpenSim sees both worlds in one directory. Phase 2 adds **Hypergate**, a bridge shard speaking the OpenSim viewer protocol to Firestorm and the nolife protocol to the federation, limited to appearance, chat and teleport; it is the first thing cut if it slips.

**VRChat and VRM.** VRM 1.0 imports natively and VRM 0.x migrates on import; it is the only widely adopted open humanoid profile (Source: https://vrm.dev/vrm1/). Avatars get a performance rank on import. Worlds export from Unity as glTF/USD through standard exporters; Udon scripts are re-made in Luau with a migration guide, because VRChat ships no world export. BOOTH is a supported off-platform store: a licence-bearing VRM listed there imports with provenance.

**Roblox-style scripting migration.** Luau is the runtime, so Roblox creators keep their language. A `rbx-compat` library implements a documented subset (Instance tree, events and signals, a DataStore-shaped API with nolife's budgets) so common idioms port; it is a migration aid, not binary compatibility.

**Resonite, Overte, Sansar, Spatial, Dreams.** glTF/GLB import covers Resonite exports and Spatial's Unity-built scenes; Sansar and Dreams content is re-made; Ready Player Me GLBs import as humanoids auto-rigged to the Commons skeleton.

**The counter-lesson.** Habbo Origins inherited its community by preserving the 2005 client; Club Penguin Rewritten reached ~11M users by preserving the thing itself (Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/cops-arrest-3-people-for-running-club-penguin-rewritten-beloved-by-millions/). Sansar inherited nothing because it carried no inventory, identity or social graph (taxonomy 1.6). nolife carries all three, and for the hypergrid the world itself.

**What is re-made.** Third-party no-mod content whose vendor does not opt in; scripts outside the LSL/OSSL/Udon subsets; Unity-specific shaders (approximated as PBR); particle systems (partial); and every SL region build, which has no export path and is covered by rebuild grants for named institutions.

## 11. Business model and go-to-market

**Money flows.** Residents buy Till at par (US$1.00 buys 100 units displayed as US$1.00; a 3% top-up fee covers card cost) and spend it in-world and in the Bazaar; creators and Charters cash out through the licensed partner at 2%. Shard operators pay for federation services; Commons charges rent and subscriptions. Every price shows its fiat value, purchase is exact-amount with no bundles, there is one currency, stipends carry fiat display, and no chance mechanic pays anything transferable, which the CPC Network's 2025 principles require and the Digital Fairness Act will likely codify (R9) (Source: https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr). A fiat-pegged stored value presented as money exits the premium-currency debate at the cost of a licensed partner.

**Price list (R6).** Resident subscription US$9.99/month or US$99/year: a Home parcel, 50 GB of Vault, a US$3 monthly stipend, creator tools, priority cohort hosting. Home without subscription US$3.99/month. Estate US$39/month (65,536 m², 20K-weight budget, 2,000 included avatar-hours, then US$0.004 per avatar-hour); SL charges US$199-209 for the equivalent (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/). Chartered shard fee US$15/month plus 1% of Till volume settled on it. Education tier US$39/month per class region (R28). A free Passport gets 5 GB of Vault and no land: no free world-hosting tier, the pattern that ended Spatial. Pixel streaming at cost, 20 hours/month cap.

**Unit economics at year 3 (300K MAU, ~75K DAU, 15-25K peak CCU).** Revenue: 15,000 estates × US$39 × 12 = US$7.0M; 40,000 unsubscribed Homes × US$3.99 × 12 = US$1.9M; 45,000 Residents × US$99 = US$4.5M; Till fees on US$60M in and US$40M out = US$2.6M; Bazaar commission on US$50M primary = US$5.0M; charter fees US$0.6M; education and enterprise US$0.4M. Total ≈ US$22M, rent and subscriptions 61% (R6). Costs: infrastructure plus trust and safety at US$1.50-3.00 per DAU per month = US$1.4-2.7M; 70 staff at US$150K loaded = US$10.5M; Foundation grant US$1.5M; creator and venue pool at 25% of net platform revenue ≈ US$4.5M; payment processing covered by the top-up fee. Total ≈ US$19-20M. Break-even near 300K MAU; survival at 100K by cutting to 45 staff and pausing the Tier A roadmap (R30). At 600K MAU the same structure gives ~US$50M, the taxonomy's own reconciliation: a Linden-sized business, not a Roblox-sized one.

**Funding posture (R30).** No land pre-sale and no token: land sold before population is a pattern no survivor followed (taxonomy 2.2 #3). Capital is US$30M in two tranches from investors who sign the Charter (no sibling product, no crypto, headcount cap 80), plus community funding in the Resonite and Hytale manner: Foundation memberships, a capped Founders' subscription (three years of Resident prepaid at US$199, limited to 25,000) and education-consortium pre-commitments. SL became profitable in 2005 on US$25M raised; that is the comparator, not Improbable's half-billion. Recurring revenue covers operating cost by month 36; growth features are funded only after rent and subscriptions cover costs.

**Why an open platform still funds itself.** Because people pay for persistence (rent), convenience (subscription), settlement (Till fees) and trust (Bazaar commission, charter fees), not for the protocol. Open source replaces the moat of lock-in with the moat of being the chartered, safe, settled part of a federation; Minecraft's self-hosted servers plus a curated Marketplace paying >US$500M cumulatively is the precedent (Source: https://corq.studio/insights/minecraft-marketplace-inside-the-platforms-500-million-creator-economy/).

**Go-to-market sequencing.** (1) OpenSim grids and the ten named SL institutions in a closed federation; (2) the diaspora offer to Spatial, Rec Room and Sansar creators; (3) the education consortium; (4) VRM creators via BOOTH and Gumroad with Japanese localisation; (5) public web launch on Ember with the cohort funnel; (6) native mobile with regional pricing and local rails (Pix, konbini, prepaid cards), since Roblox's regional pricing lifted paying users 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items). Paid localisation for Portuguese, Spanish, Japanese and German is in the year-1 budget (R29); SL's funded localisation ended with the 2010 cuts (Source: https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-07-linden-lab-laying-off-staff-closing-singapore-office.html).

## 12. Roadmap sketch

**Phase 0, specification and reference server (Q4 2026 to Q2 2027).** Publish Passport, Vault, Cell/Presence, Crossing, Charter and NAR 0.x; ship the Apache-2.0 reference server with NAR import, the LSL-to-Luau transpiler and the Commons body; Lantern alpha on UE 5.8 (Tier B first), Ember alpha on WebGPU, Reader alpha; conformance suite 0.1. Milestone: a Kitely OAR and a Virtual Ability charter import demonstrated end to end.

Three OpenSim grids and ten SL institutions chartered. Milestones: 150-avatar cell load test with voice passed; ≥10 named institutions migrated with membership (R18 test); first Assembly election with published turnout.

**Phase 2, public launch (Q2 to Q4 2028).** Ember public with WebGL2 fallback; Lantern Tier A; native mobile wrapper; VRM 1.0 import and BOOTH integration; Education tier with SSO; Hypergate bridge (first cut candidate); Japanese, Portuguese, Spanish and German localisation. Targets: 100K MAU, first-session completion ≥60%, ≥25% of MAU at a scheduled event monthly, D30 cohort lift published.

## 14. Biggest risks and what you would cut first

**Risk 1, federation weakens safety.** Defederation is a blunt sanction and a rogue Open shard can host what the Charter forbids while still speaking the identity protocol. Mitigation: Till, the directory and Crossing to Commons are chartered-only, so the economy and audience stay inside the safe perimeter, and Warden flags follow Passports. Residual risk accepted: an unchartered shard with bad content will exist and the press will call it "nolife".

**Risk 2, open assets invite protocol-level copying.** Any client can replay the stream. The 2012 Export-bit article said the same of OpenSim and noted most stolen content originates inside SL anyway. Mitigation is legal and reputational (provenance, Bazaar enforcement, vendor badges), not technical, and body vendors may decline the programme. If they do, the Commons body carries the launch.

**Risk 3, the two-sided cold start.** Content without audience and audience without content. The wedge is deliberately small and institutional for this reason, and the hypergrid brings a world rather than a catalogue. If month-18 migration is below ten institutions, the plan narrows to the hypergrid and education and defers the public launch.

**Risk 4, engine and vendor dependencies.** Unreal's licence terms and UE6 timing could strand Lantern; a processor could move against adult content; a hosting vendor could pivot as Hathora did. Each has a documented exit (Godot/Ember, pre-qualified acquirer, two-provider manifest), and each exit costs fidelity or margin, not survival.

**Risk 5, governance capture or founder split.** A Foundation with elected seats can be captured by a bloc; a commercial operator can drift from the Charter. Mitigation: the code is forkable and the data exportable, so the community's exit is real, which disciplines both bodies; the Charter's never-list is constitutional.

**Risk 6, the economics are thin.** Break-even near 300K MAU on a 10-12% effective take leaves little room; a shortfall in estate uptake (the line Linden itself is shifting away from) would push the plan toward subscriptions and Bazaar commission, which the Charter caps.

**What gets cut first, in order.** (1) Hypergate, the Firestorm-compatible bridge shard: valuable but a second protocol implementation. (2) The pixel-streaming funnel. (3) Frontier-model agent cognition, leaving scripted and small-model agents. (4) Tier A extras: path tracing, 3DGS capture, neural texture compression. (5) The `rbx-compat` library. (6) The first-party VR view of Lantern, left to third-party clients. (7) Native Tier B mobile, keeping Ember wrapped on phones past month 12, which would breach R14's test and is therefore last. Never cut: NAR import, Charter import, Reader, Till on a licensed rail, the Warden age credential, and the escrow-and-export covenant, the promises that make a community move.

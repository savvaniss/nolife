# nolife: a design proposal with the creator economy first

Author role: platform economist. Date: 2026-10-09. Evidence base: `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (requirements R1-R30) and the research briefs `sl-economy-creators`, `business-models`, `gap-virtual-currency-consumer-law-and-tax`, `gap-adult-economy-and-payment-rails`, `winners-lessons`, `legacy-interop`, `gap-sl-community-census-and-migration-needs`, `alt-survivors`, `graphics-tech-2026`, `networking-scale-tech`, `community-purpose`, `safety-regulation-identity`, `alife-and-ai-npcs`, `gap-non-english-communities-and-regional-law`. Figures without a citation are my planning numbers; cited figures carry the taxonomy's caveats (company claims, single sources, search extracts). No web search was used.

## 1. Thesis and premise

What nolife is. A persistent, adult-first virtual world in which one self, one inventory and one social graph persist across every place; residents build the world with in-world and external tools; and the economy pays out in real money through a licensed rail. It is the successor to Second Life in the sense the taxonomy defines in 1.9: it carries the moat (continuity of identity, inventory and relationships) and sheds the debt (unbudgeted content, land rent as the main revenue line, an unowned newcomer experience, a self-declared age gate).

The name. "No life" is what people who live in these worlds have been called for twenty years. The name reclaims the insult and states the economic promise: nobody is charged for being alive here. You pay rent on the bytes you keep and a small, published slice of what you sell; you are never charged for visitors, for growing, or for success. The second meaning is a design constraint for section 8: purpose comes from no artificial life. People, roles and a calendar supply it; agents are disclosed furniture.

The economic premise. Second Life's economy is real: ~US$650M/yr of transactions, ~US$78M paid to creators in 2023, ~US$1.1B lifetime, 21,152 creators earning real money, 6,446 above US$1,000 and 139 above US$100,000 (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/ ; https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46605). What is broken is alignment. Linden's income scales with regions rented, not with people or trade; the marketed "90/10" is 20-31% all-in once Marketplace commission (5% to 10% on 2 December 2019), LindeX fees and cash-out fees stack (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/ ; https://ryanschultz.com/2019/11/25/editorial-second-life-users-are-less-than-happy-about-linden-lab-doubling-commission-rates-on-the-sl-marketplace-from-5-to-10/); every fee move since 2015 has been a reshuffle rather than a model that grows with the economy (taxonomy 1.2); and the first Second Life millionaire was a landlord arbitraging tier, not a maker (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshe_Chung). The successors did not fix this. Rec Room kept 30 cents of each UGC dollar after store fees and shut down with runway in the bank (Source: https://blog.recroom.com/posts/2025/8/28/rec-room-finances-and-ugc-revenue); Roblox creators net ~25% of spend (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/fortnite/); Spatial deleted creator worlds because hosting cost was uncovered (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/).

nolife's answer is four money mechanisms, specified in sections 5 and 11. (1) Persistence is rent, priced near cost plus a stated margin, and rent never scales with visitors (R6). (2) One published fee stack: at most 15% all-in on a creator's sale round-tripped to a bank account, 0% on resale and tips, with the platform's required margin printed beside it (R7). (3) A Commons Dividend of 25% of platform net revenue paid to places and venues by returning purchasing users, so creators' income grows with the platform's income rather than with gross spend (R8). (4) A closed, fiat-pegged unit with fiat shown at every price, cashed out on a licensed rail with tax data collected on day one, and adult commerce instrumented so the rail survives a processor campaign (R9, R10, R22). Everything else exists to feed those four: the wedge communities bring the first purchasing users; the purpose engine keeps them returning; the world structure makes density, and therefore pool points, visible; the client tiers stop the hardware floor rising with the catalogue; and governance stops the operator doing what killed trust before.

Sizing. The taxonomy's cheapest hypothesis is that the audience for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out is ~0.5-1M people and SL already serves most of it. I accept it as the planning case. nolife is designed to be profitable at 300K MAU, to survive at 100K MAU on a 30-person team and a reserve, and to grow past Linden scale only with growth features paid from surplus (R30). Second Life was profitable in 2005 on US$25M raised and Hytale funded two years of development from pre-purchases (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hytale). That is the funding posture, not Reality Labs' US$19.2B loss (Source: https://www.shacknews.com/article/147618/facebook-meta-reality-labs-fy25-losses).

## 2. Who comes first and why

Five wedges, ordered by the money and the people they bring. The principle is the taxonomy's own: whole communities move on institutional continuity and a credible persistence guarantee; individuals move on economics.

Wedge 1, Second Life's earning creators (the supply side). 6,446 cleared US$1,000 in 2023 and 139 cleared US$100,000 (Source: https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46605). Their catalogue is a sunk cost in SL only because nothing can be exported. The offer, "Founding Merchant" (first 1,000 verified creators enrolled before public launch): commission 5% instead of 10% for 12 months; a Re-delivery bounty of 10 NC (US$0.10) per catalogue item claimed by a verified former customer (section 10), capped at US$5,000 per merchant; conversion of their Collada/glTF/llmesh catalogue to budgeted glTF at platform cost (R3); same-week cash-out in their country. Day one: a storefront, an in-world vendor API, the fee page, the pool.

Wedge 2, Second Life's institutions. A named outreach list, each a single decision-maker who brings hundreds to thousands: Virtual Ability (1,000-1,300 members), the VWBPE committee (annual, 19-21 March 2026), Second Pride (20 years), Fantasy Faire/RFL (US$358,000 raised by 2019), the 1920s Berlin Project (100+ tenants), the Crack Den, Luskwood (40,000+ customers by 2013), Cafe Musique (60+ shows a week), First UCC SL and UUCSL (Source: https://virtualability.org/about-us ; https://secondlife.com/destination/vwbpe ; https://modemworld.me/2025/04/17/fantasy-faire-2025-your-almost-complete-unofficial-guide/ ; https://ryanschultz.com/2019/05/30/the-1920s-berlin-project-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-in-second-life/ ; https://secondlife.aditi.lindenlab.com/destination/aism). Day one they get the charter import (R18): group, roles, member opt-in list, ban list, maturity rating, calendar and a rented cell created in one transaction with the leader as owner and a named deputy; a venue kit (stream URL, host and DJ roles, a 0% tip jar, charity splits); a 12-month rent holiday on the first cell; and the venue share of the pool from their first scheduled event.

Wedge 3, OpenSim grid operators and residents: ~45,000 monthly actives across the hypergrid, where OSgrid wiped its database in 2025 and was offline for weeks in 2026 (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/01/opensim-land-grows-as-traffic-slows-slightly/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). They already pack OARs. Offer: OAR/IAR import on day one, an operator programme in which a grid becomes a language-tagged District with its own estate controls, and the economy they never had.

Wedge 4, the 2026 diaspora. Rec Room closed on 1 June 2026 with creators who had crossed US$1M a quarter; Spatial deleted Free and Pro worlds on 27 July 2026; Ready Player Me and Union Avatars are gone (Source: https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/ ; https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/ ; https://genies.com/blog/ready-player-me-shutdown). Offer: glTF/VRM import, Founding Merchant terms, and the written persistence covenant of section 9, which is what each of them just lost.

Wedge 5, VRChat-Japan's avatar economy, approached as a stage rather than a store. BOOTH's 3D-model category exceeded 10 billion yen in 2025 and pixiv took a stake in VRChat in September 2026 (Source: https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html ; https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/09/09/27973.html). Offer: VRM 1.0 import, an open delivery API so BOOTH-style stores deliver into nolife at 0% platform take, Japanese localisation and konbini rails in phase 2 (R29).

How they are recruited: not with paid installs. Mobile CPIs of US$1-4 imply US$75-300 per payer at 1-2% payer rates, which no social world has recovered (Source: https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267). Acquisition is leader outreach, the charter-import demo, a 12-24-month dual-presence bridge so venues keep their SL audience while building a new one, and the creator referral bounty of section 5. The test is R18's: ten named SL institutions migrated with membership by month 18.

## 3. The purpose engine

The taxonomy's testable restatement of "purpose vacuum": retention concentrates in people attached to a group with a recurring time and place; SL supplied places and never roles, schedules or scarcity (1.4). nolife's purpose engine is an economy of roles, a calendar and legible scarcity, and the money design pays for all three.

Roles are paid objects (R15). A role (host, mentor, DJ, moderator, builder, performer, greeter, chronicler) is a first-class record on a group: a slot with a schedule, a badge, abilities, a stipend line and a named deputy. Stipends are paid two ways: from the group treasury, and from the Commons Dividend's venue share (15% of the pool), which is paid to the treasury and split by the role roster automatically. A venue that runs a staffed two-hour slot with returning purchasing attendees earns pool points; its host and DJ are paid from them. This converts the staffed-shop finding, that staffed venues lasted and unstaffed corporate builds died like "a shop in the High Street filled with brochures but never having any staff", into a payroll (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2).

Institutions. The group substrate is SL's, because every durable community was built on it: 2-10 roles, a 500-entry ban list, land ownership, a fixed maturity rating (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Group). nolife adds a treasury (a group-owned balance with multi-signature payouts), a calendar object, a charter (rules, succession, deputy) and legal-entity attachment so 501(c)(3)s and companies receive payouts as organisations.

Scarcity, stated as policy. Three scarce things: (1) public lots in civic Wards, allocated by lottery with full-price tickets and a 45-day use-it-or-lose-it rule with an 80% refund and a published pause policy, copied from FFXIV, where scarcity made housing a status object and spawned an unplanned venue scene (R16) (Source: https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-updates-lottery-empyreum-patch-6-1 ; https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/ffxiv-house-demolition-suspension-reinstated-temporarily); (2) festival items that are never reissued, Habbo's rares (Source: https://www.habbo.com/community/article/22292/update-state-habbo-economy); (3) seats on curated roleplay estates with whitelists and paid queue priority under published rules, which is GTA RP's product at US$25-200 a month on comparable servers (R17) (Source: https://ascenddev.tebex.io/category/priority-roles). Private homes are abundant and never demolished.

Calendar. On day one: a weekly rhythm per language shard (Market Day, Open Stage, Assembly Hour) and one annual ritual, "the Week", on a Ward that exists only then, modelled on BURN2 and on SL20B's 60 regions and 425+ performers (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/ ; https://lindenlab.com/press-release/original-metaverse-second-life-celebrates-20th-birthday). First-party events run as synchronised ~100-avatar instances with cross-instance chat. Every event has a one-tap clip button, because rituals spread as postable artefacts.

Governance and status. An elected Assembly (section 9) whose turnout is published, EVE's steady 12-17% being the benchmark (Source: https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/heres-your-csm-20). Status is earned and visible: role badges, trust rank, Ward tenure, never-reissued items, Chronicle entries.

The first hour (R11, R26). Minutes 0-5: account, on-device age band, language, a dressed avatar from the first-party open body plus three starter outfits from the Starter Catalogue, where creators earn pool points for each equipped starter item, so the first thing a newcomer wears is a creator's product. Minutes 5-10: a furnished free room within the trial allowance. Minute 10: the newcomer joins the next scheduled cohort (one starts every ten minutes per language), hosted by a paid Mentor, and ends the hour inside a group event drawn from the calendar. Success is "met a person or joined a group"; a randomised holdout measures D30 and the cohort system survives only if it beats the industry base rate, which SL's 7-10% D90 already matches (taxonomy 1.3).

The first year. Week 1: offered a volunteer greeter role with a badge. Month 1: first purchase from the monthly allowance. Month 3: eligible for a Ward lottery ticket and to rent a parcel. Month 6: first Assembly vote. Month 12: the Week.

## 4. World structure

Places, not acreage. Four kinds of place, each with its own economics:

- Homes: subscription plots in neighbourhoods that open only when occupancy warrants; abundant; never demolished; archived to inventory only when a subscription has lapsed 90 days.
- Estates: rented cells and districts run by groups with full estate controls (access and ban lists, ratings, damage flags, whitelists, queue priority). This is where SL's serious communities always lived, because mainland lacked estate controls (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/472286-difference-between-mainland-region-and-private-region/).
- Wards: platform-run civic districts, scarce, lotteried (R16), language-tagged (R29), where the calendar's public events happen.
- Commons: event instances, from synchronised 100-avatar instances to a 2,000-5,000 presence mode, that exist only for the event.

Land and rent. The unit is a cell (256 m square, one simulation cell, 100-150 simulated avatars), subdivisible to parcels. Rent is for the persistent state a place holds (object budget and bytes) and includes a visitor-hour allowance; beyond it, compute is metered at published cost (section 5.4). An empty cell is the cheapest thing on the grid; a crowded one pays for its own compute from the pool share it earns. Sub-letting is allowed, but because the platform's rent is public and granular down to a quarter cell at US$15, a landlord's margin is a service margin for community management, not the tier arbitrage behind SL's first fortune.

Persistence. Every place is documents plus an event log with snapshots; owners can roll back 30 days; estates export an OAR-equivalent at any time (R20).

Discovery is the calendar, then people, then the map. The newcomer's default view is "happening now, in your language, with people you know"; the map is third. Places rank by distinct returning humans, never by paid placement; the only paid discovery is a capped new-creator shelf at a flat fee, not an auction, so a ten-year-old marketplace cannot calcify around incumbents the way SL's did (sl-economy-creators pattern 3).

Density and the empty-world rule (R12). Public density SLO: one avatar per 0.1 ha at peak in Wards and Commons, roughly 40x SL's grid-wide average of under two avatars per region (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/). Mechanisms: thin instances merge; a Ward below occupancy for 30 days closes and its lots return to the lottery; neighbourhoods open on demand; newcomers are routed to live events; estates are never instanced away or repriced, so care communities keep their homes. The median number of people visible in a newcomer's first session (target at least 10) is published quarterly.

## 5. Creation and creator economy

The rule for the fee page: the platform charges for bytes kept and takes a small slice of sales; it never charges for people or growth.

### 5.1 Tools, from hobbyist to studio

In-world: a prim-and-mesh builder with snapping, a PBR-only materials editor, per-place terrain and lighting editors, and Luau scripting in a sandbox with per-owner CPU, memory and network budgets that throttle the worst offender first (R5). Luau is the base of SLua, so SL scripters' skills carry over (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Lua_Alpha). External pipeline: glTF 2.0 and VRM 1.0 for runtime assets, OpenUSD for scenes, MetaHuman-compatible rigs for Reference-tier humans now that the licence allows any engine (R3) (Source: https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/).

The ladder: Maker (anyone; sell up to US$600 a year with light KYC) to Merchant (verified adult, tax data on file, storefront, vendor API, analytics) to Studio (legal entity, seats, team splits enforced by the ledger, an SLA, escrowed life-support for scripted goods). Two ledger primitives make the ladder real. Splits: any sale divided among up to ten contributors by percentage, enforced at settlement. Component Royalties: a creator attaches a royalty, capped at 10%, to an asset others may build on; the platform takes nothing extra. That is how a one-person body maker becomes a platform for a hundred clothing makers without SL's no-mod lock-in.

### 5.2 Ownership and portability

Every asset carries signed licence metadata: creator, terms, export flag, maturity tag. Creators set the Export bit, the OpenSim idea SL never shipped, and every client honours it (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2012/09/hypergrid-permissions-need-viewer-change/). Users own licences recorded in a Content Passport readable off-platform. The platform's content licence is plain-language and limited to operating the service; any platform reuse requires a defined affirmative opt-in, because the 2013 ToS episode is institutional memory (Source: https://modemworld.me/2014/07/16/lab-updates-section-2-3-of-their-terms-of-service-will-it-calm-doubts/). The marketplace is an open protocol: any store can list, deliver and settle through the API at the same 10%, or at 0% for items paid for off-platform, which reverses the 2009 Xstreet/OnRez lock-in (Source: https://lindenlab.wordpress.com/2009/01/20/xstreet-sl-and-onrez-to-join-linden-lab/). Own-created content exports in glTF/VRM with its signed licence.

### 5.3 The unit

One closed currency, the credit (NC), fixed at 100 NC = US$1.00, with local fiat shown beside every price at a daily-fixed rate; exact-amount purchase from US$1; no bundles, no odd pricing, no second currency, no chance mechanic that pays anything transferable (R9). That is the posture the CPC Network's March 2025 Key Principles require (Source: https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr). Credits transfer between residents and leave the platform only through cash-out. If the Digital Fairness Act, expected Q4 2026, restricts paid virtual currencies, the engineered fallback is to present balances as stored value in cents under the licensed partner's e-money licence (Source: https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html).

### 5.4 The fee page (R7)

One versioned page, with the platform's required margin printed beside it.

Money in. Web and desktop (card, bank, PayPal, Pix, konbini, UPI, prepaid cards): US$1.00 = 100 NC; processing (~3%) absorbed by the platform. App-store top-ups: the store's fee is shown as a line and the buyer receives fewer credits per dollar; app stores are top-ups only, capped at 25% of money-in by month 12. Subscriptions carry a 300 NC monthly allowance shown with its fiat value.

Selling. Primary sale: 10% commission, identical on the web marketplace, in-world vendors and API delivery. Resale between residents: 0%, with an optional creator-set resale royalty up to 10%. Tips, donations and charity splits with no item delivered: 0%. Estate tickets, queue priority and estate subscriptions: 10%; cosmetics and priority allowed, pay-to-win and loot boxes banned, which are Rockstar's RP rules (Source: https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/fivem/server-monetisation/). Off-platform-paid imports: 0%, owner-only and non-transferable unless the creator registers them.

Cash-out. Verified adult with tax data on file; minimum 2,500 NC (US$25) domestic and 10,000 NC international; fee 2.5%; payout cost passed through at cost and shown (US$0.25 ACH, EUR 0.50 SEPA, ~US$2 international); weekly cycles.

Worked example: a US$10 web sale leaves the creator 900 NC; cashed out as part of a US$100 payout that is US$87.75 less US$0.25, an all-in take of 12.5%; at the US$25 minimum it is 13.2%, and the international minimum keeps every case at or under 15%. Against SL's user-estimated 20-31%, VRChat's ~50% creator share and Roblox's ~25%, this is the lowest published creator take of any live world with cash-out (Source: https://creators.vrchat.com/economy/payout ; https://gamesbeat.com/fortnite/).

Rent (persistence, R6), no setup fee, 12 months' notice and grandfathering on any rise (R19):

| Unit | Budget | Visitor-hours included | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parcel S (quarter cell) | 4,000 objects, 4 GB | 2,500/month | US$15/month |
| Parcel M (half cell) | 10,000 objects, 10 GB | 5,000/month | US$29/month |
| Cell (256 m square, 100-150 simulated avatars) | 30,000 objects, 30 GB | 10,000/month | US$59/month |
| District (4 cells, shared estate controls, 1,000-avatar presence) | 120,000 objects, 120 GB | 40,000/month | US$219/month |

Education and registered non-profits pay half, so a class cell is US$29.50, under R28's US$50. Beyond the allowance, compute is US$0.004 per avatar-hour, which is the verified hosting rate with no markup: US$0.055-0.069 per vCPU-hour gives US$0.002-0.005 per avatar-hour (Source: https://edgegap.com/pricing ; https://aws.amazon.com/gamelift/servers/pricing/). Printed cost basis: a cell costs the platform about US$18 compute, US$4 storage and US$6 operations a month; the balance is margin. Compare SL's US$349 setup plus US$199/month from 15 June 2026 and Kitely's US$14.95-39.95 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/ ; https://www.kitely.com/virtual-world-news/2015/06/01/60-off-large-worlds-and-dedicated-memory-guarantee/). nolife sits between them because the taxonomy's arithmetic shows rent is load-bearing, and because free world hosting is what Spatial and Sansar could not sustain.

### 5.5 The Commons Dividend (R8)

25% of platform net revenue (gross revenue less payment processing, refunds and sales taxes), computed and published quarterly with the formula and a per-place leaderboard. 85% goes to places by returning purchasing users (RPU): a resident who has made at least one purchase in the last 90 days and visited the place on two or more distinct days that month, weighted by time share, capped at three places per resident per month; age-verified residents weight 1.0, unverified 0.5. A 75% bonus for six months on the pool contribution of new or six-month-lapsed residents a creator brings by referral link, which is Epic's November 2025 rewrite (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items). 15% goes to venues and institutions by distinct returning members attending scheduled events with staffed roles, paid to group treasuries and split by the role roster. Anti-farming: purchasers only; one pool identity per verified person; places whose visitor mix trips the bot detector lose eligibility pending review; bot share is published (R13). Why a share of net revenue and not Epic's 40% of gross spend: at Linden scale a pool of ~US$10M on ~US$43M of revenue is affordable; 40% of gross spend would exceed the platform's entire margin (taxonomy section 5).

### 5.6 Why this aligns growth

Platform revenue rises with subscribers, sales and rented bytes. The pool rises with platform revenue. Rent does not rise with visitors. A creator who makes a place popular pays the same rent, pays compute at cost out of pool earnings, and takes a larger share of a larger pool. A holder of empty land gets nothing from the pool and pays rent. That is the inverse of SL, where the empty sim and the busy sim paid the same tier and nothing paid the venue; and of Rec Room, where the store tax left the platform too little to keep going.

### 5.7 Moderation of UGC

Every upload passes automated review before it is visible: per-tier geometry and texture budgets, CSAM hashing, likeness and trademark screens, script static analysis. Adult-tagged goods additionally require uploader verification, consent records for any likeness and pre-publication human review, which are the Mastercard AN 5196 and Visa VIRP controls built before anyone demands them (R22) (Source: https://verifymy.io/resources/regulation/card-brand-requirements). Trust ranks gate upload volume and scripting (R23). A notice and counter-notice flow has a published turnaround; repeat infringers lose Merchant status. Creators may set per-region prices, since Roblox's regional pricing lifted paying users 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items).

## 6. Graphics and client strategy

Fidelity target: the best 2026 look at the modal machine, not the ceiling. Unreal Engine 5.8-class rendering (Lumen Lite, MegaLights production-ready, Nanite for static meshes, Substrate materials, ML upscaling through DLSS, FSR and XeSS) on an RTX 4060/5060-class GPU at 1080p-1440p, because the modal Steam GPU is an RTX 5070 and the long tail is far weaker (Source: https://www.unrealengine.com/news/unreal-engine-5-8-is-now-available ; https://mixed-news.com/en/steam-september-2026-hardware-survey-rtx-5070-32gb-ram/). Linden said in January 2025 that ~50% of SL users lack high-end machines (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/01/02/); the lower tiers exist for them.

Three tiers, enforced server-side (R2):

| Tier | Devices | Frame budget | Avatar budget | Lighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reference | RTX 4060-class PC, M-series Mac | 16.6 ms at 1080p-1440p with upscaling | 150K triangles, 2K textures, 256 bones | Lumen/MegaLights, optional path tracing |
| Lite | Switch 2, Quest 3, mid Android, WebGPU browser | 16.6 ms | 32K triangles, 1K textures, 128 bones | Lumen Lite or baked probes |
| Compat | WebGL2, low Android | 33 ms | 10K triangles, 512 textures, 75 bones | Baked only; 500-stud-equivalent draw distance |

Every asset carries a per-tier budget and auto-generated fallbacks (LODs, impostors, transcoded textures, capped ranks); over-budget content is impostored or hidden, VRChat's rank-blocking model, whose Quest ceiling is 20,000 triangles (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202115). The Compat draw distance is Roblox's 2026 low-end floor (Source: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rdc26-what-we-announced/4865880). Nothing is ever rendered twice.

How UGC stays beautiful: PBR-only materials; lighting owned by the place, with presets creators choose from and a custom path for Merchants; a first-party open-licensed avatar body with a MetaHuman-compatible rig at Reference and a stylised rig at Lite and Compat (R26); Gaussian-splat capture as a backdrop format for scanned venues with proxy collision, budgeted per tier at 500K splats on mobile (Source: https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/03/free-plugin-nanogs-puts-nanite-style-gaussian-splatting-in-unreal-engine/). Neural texture compression is a 2027-28 upgrade once SM 6.10 leaves preview; today the SDK says do not ship (Source: https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC).

Clients and devices (R4, R14). At launch: a first-party desktop client on Unreal 5.8 (Reference and Lite), a WebGPU web client with WebGL2 fallback (Lite and Compat: visiting, chatting, buying and the full economy, no building), and a first-party text client (R27). Native iOS and Android within 12 months. VR is an optional view of the desktop client, never required for identity or inventory; ~1.77% of Steam users have a headset (Source: https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/). Consoles in year 3 through the Lite tier. The protocol is documented and third-party clients are blessed. Pixel streaming is only a time-capped funnel, 10 free hours in the first month on Compat devices and 20 hours in Resident Plus, at a published ~US$1.75 per hour, the Project Zero cost, capped at 5% of the infrastructure budget; Linden ended Project Zero on 21 April 2026 after 14 months (Source: https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/). The engine exit cost is documented under R20: content is engine-neutral and the Lite tier is specified so a Godot client could replace it in 12-18 months.

## 7. Architecture

Two-layer world (R1). A presence layer (position, pose, voice and chat at 5-10 Hz, interest-managed through relays and an SFU) that can show 1,000-5,000 avatars in one place, and a simulation layer (physics, scripts, vehicles) sharded into elastic cells of 50-150 interacting avatars with an authoritative entity store, so a cell crash is a reconnect rather than a lost place. That is the replication-layer shape Star Citizen shipped, without promising its 500-player shards or its still "quasi-dynamic" meshing (Source: https://starcitizen.tools/Comm-Link:Letter_from_the_Chairman_-_2026-08-27). Published SLOs: 100-150 per cell with voice; 500-1,000 per contiguous place; 2,000-5,000 in declared event mode with impostors and pre-mixed audio. No 10,000 claims; Improbable's observed peaks were 4,144 and 4,500 at scripted events (Source: https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/tennis-for-two-thousand-the-story-behind-improbables-mass-event-in-scavlab/).

Persistence: documents plus an event log, per-cell and per-creator write budgets on Roblox's 60 + 10 x players requests-per-minute model, snapshots, 30-day rollback for owners, OAR-equivalent export (Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits). Scripting: Luau sandbox with per-owner budgets and worst-offender throttling (R5), the opposite of SL's "scripts starve first".

Multi-vendor by construction (R20): containerised cell servers with one manifest deployable on Edgegap, GameLift Servers or Nitrado GameFabric and on bare metal; voice behind an abstraction that can swap ODIN, the Discord Social SDK or a self-hosted SFU; escrowed server images with a named escrow agent from launch; an annual failover drill. Hathora's 2026 exit and Firestorm Zero's forced migration are the reason (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174 ; https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/).

Cost per CCU. A 2-4 vCPU cell holding 100 avatars is US$0.14-0.28 an hour of compute plus US$0.09-0.23 of bandwidth, US$0.002-0.005 per avatar-hour; voice is about EUR 0.01 an hour for a 30-hour-a-month user at EUR 0.29 per peak user per month (Source: https://edgegap.com/pricing ; https://odin.4players.io/pricing). All-in infrastructure target: at most US$0.02 per avatar-hour; 10,000 CCU is about US$150-200 an hour, roughly US$1.5M a year. Infrastructure plus trust and safety is budgeted at US$2 per DAU per month at launch, falling toward Roblox's ~US$0.8, whose line was US$1,153M in 2025 (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm).

Scaling plan: 5,000 CCU at launch after a 150-avatar cell load test with voice; 25,000 by month 24; 60,000 by month 48; cells added per region (US, EU, Brazil, Japan) as language shards fill.

## 8. AI and the living world

Rule (R25): every agent is disclosed with a badge and a plain-language notice of whose behalf it acts on, which EU AI Act Article 50 requires from 2 August 2026 (Source: https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/). Each has a purpose, is capped at 20% of visible occupants in any public place, and withdraws as humans arrive. No companion loops for minors and no open-ended romantic agents anywhere in year one, given Character.AI's minors ban and the Setzer settlement (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots). Voices are licensed or synthetic with provenance; no replicas of real performers without bargained terms.

What agents do, in the economist's framing: they are staff creators can hire at published rates. A scripted-tier shopkeeper or greeter costs 2 NC an hour; a small-model ambient agent (edge 4B-class) 40 NC an hour; frontier-model interactions are metered per turn and available only to Merchants. A creator's opt-in twin tends the shop and greets visitors while they are offline, with full logs. Platform-run agents: the Chronicler, which writes the world's record of events; translation, text first, with its retention effect measured because nobody has published one (R29); moderation triage and asset review, where proactive detection produces results, as Roblox's Sentinel shows (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/08/open-sourcing-roblox-sentinel-preemptive-risk-detection). Generative tooling such as Cube-style mesh generation is a creator tool with provenance labels, not world content (Source: https://corp.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/03/introducing-roblox-cube).

Costs and guardrails: tiered cognition with a published cost per agent-hour; total agent spend at most 10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget; human D30 in agent-filled places must be at least that of comparable human-only places or the cap is lowered. Agents are a density stopgap, not purpose: the corpus shows nothing in which agent-filled density retained humans (taxonomy section 3), and 63% of 1,799 Quantic Foundry respondents chose the most negative option on generative AI (Source: https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/).

## 9. Identity, safety, governance, regulation

Who is admitted. The open world is 18+. Age is a private band bound to a pseudonymous display name, established by on-device estimation or a wallet credential (the EU app, OS age signals on app clients), with no stored ID images; appeals avoid document upload where possible, because Discord's 70,000-ID breach came through the appeals vendor (Source: https://cyberinsider.com/discord-confirms-70000-government-ids-exposed-in-third-party-breach/). Minors exist only in Campus: institution-run, SSO-provisioned, General-rated, isolated, with no open economy and no cash-out (R28). This takes the minors' commerce rules out of the economy entirely: Brazil's ECA Digital, in force 17 March 2026, Indonesia's PP Tunas and Texas's app-store consent law all bite on services minors use (Source: https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/ ; https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/). It also keeps nolife outside Australia's age-restricted definition as a creation-and-play platform with no under-16 accounts (Source: https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs). The regional module (R21) still ships: per-region thresholds, feature gating rather than bans, parental surfaces for Campus, profiles for UK, EU, Brazil, Indonesia and Texas, assurance completion rates published.

Adult districts (R22). Gated by an Ofcom-grade check, which the Online Safety Act has required since 25 July 2025 while SL still runs a self-declared birth date (Source: https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/533810-age-verification-in-2026/). The same check unlocks Adult-tagged commerce. Consumer purchases are coded as virtual currency, never an adult MCC, which is plausibly why SL and Tilia were not named in the 2025 processor campaign that hit Steam, itch.io and DLsite (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story). Adult-tagged spend and cash-out are instrumented from day one; a deindex-but-sell switch, itch.io's model, hides Adult listings from discovery within hours; an adult-specialist acquirer is pre-qualified; exclusion of adult goods from general cash-out is held only as a processor-triggered contingency, because Tumblr's exit cost ~29% of traffic and OnlyFans reversed within a week once controls existed (Source: https://thenextweb.com/insider/2019/03/14/tumblr-traffic-dropped-by-nearly-100m-the-month-after-it-banned-porn/ ; https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6148524).

Safety primitives (R23): personal boundary on by default; one-gesture mute, block and vanish; instance kick and lock; a rolling evidence buffer attached to reports, since UK police found embodied assault unprosecutable (Source: https://lbc.co.uk/news/police-investigate-rape-metaverse); trust ranks earned by behaviour gating upload, voice and proximity, VRChat's ladder (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system). KPIs published quarterly: reports per 1,000 user-hours, median time-to-action under 24 hours, NCMEC reports, audit logs for information requests, because Ofcom fines un-evidenced compliance as readily as non-compliance.

Community self-rule. Estates set and enforce their own rules within the charter: access and ban lists, ratings, roles as ranks, consent mechanics for avatar-to-avatar actions. Moderator is a paid role.

Constitutional change control (R19). A published charter with never-clauses: no crypto tokens or NFTs, which forfeit Steam and invite Howey analysis (Source: https://www.techradar.com/news/steam-is-now-banning-all-games-that-engage-in-cryptocurrency-or-nft-trading); no loot boxes or chance mechanics paying transferable items, SL's 2007 rule (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2007/07/25/second-life-bans-gambling-following-fbi-investigation/); no retroactive rent rises, 12 months' notice and grandfathering; no sale of biometric data; no undisclosed agents; 12 months' notice before any shutdown. The Assembly: 15 seats elected annually by verified residents, turnout published, every vote answered in writing within 30 days, client-affecting changes through public review. The NeosVR split over crypto features is why this is written down before launch (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeosVR).

Appeals. Tier 1: platform review within 72 hours. Tier 2: a Resident Review Panel drawn by lot from trusted-rank verified residents, advisory on content and binding on community-rule disputes; legal matters stay with the platform. Outcomes and reversal rates are published.

Tax and consumer law. W-9/W-8 and the DAC7 data set are collected at payout onboarding, before the first payout, because services have no DAC7 floor; 1099-K at the restored >US$20,000 and >200 transactions trigger; 24% backup withholding budgeted (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview ; https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000). Storefront and account flows meet the European Accessibility Act (Source: https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-accessibility-act-time-start-implementation-projects-now).

## 10. Legacy support

The interop brief's rule: rights, not formats, are the barrier. A successor cannot import an SL resident's look; it can import what they made and what their institution is. nolife does both and adds a third path for what they bought.

Imported at platform cost (R3, R18): the charter import (group, roles, member opt-in list, ban list, rating, calendar, land, one transaction); OAR/IAR import (terrain, parcels, objects with inventory and scripts, assets), with prims and sculpts converted to budgeted glTF, llmesh and Collada re-imported from source, textures transcoded per tier; an LSL-to-Luau transpiler with a compatibility library for the common functions, where scripts that cannot convert run in a flagged legacy mode with a tighter budget until rewritten, and SLua experience transfers directly; VRM 1.0 and glTF import for VRChat, Resonite, Viverse and Spatial content; a delivery API so BOOTH-style stores and Kitely Market deliver into nolife.

Re-made rather than imported: no-transfer third-party content a resident did not create, which is most of an SL avatar's look. The Re-delivery Programme solves this economically. A creator who joins as a Merchant uploads their catalogue and marks it re-deliverable; a migrating resident proves prior purchase (SL transaction history or Marketplace order export) and claims the item free or at a creator-set price; the platform pays the creator 10 NC per claim from the pool's acquisition line. Kitely re-delivered Kitely Market purchases to stranded OSgrid users on the same principle (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). Mesh body and head vendors are the priority list, because the avatar look is the switching cost.

Dual presence: a 12-24-month opt-in chat and event bridge with SL run through residents' own accounts (relay bots in group chat, calendar mirroring), with the legal position that it is the resident's own client under SL's third-party viewer policy, and Discord mirroring as the fallback if Linden objects.

What each community gets. OpenSim grids become Districts with their own estate controls and an economy. Resonite and VRChat residents get VRM identity continuity in a world with land and cash-out. Educators get OAR export, SSO and a written persistence commitment, the two things that decided their 2010-2013 exodus (Source: https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/49578/). Virtual Ability gets the text client and a never-repriced estate. Live music gets the venue kit. Roleplay gets estate-grade tools (R17). Breedables and vendor-dependent HUDs get platform-hosted, escrowed life-support with a designed end-of-life conversion to a food-independent state (R24), so Ozimals' hibernation cannot recur (Source: https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html).

## 11. Business model and go-to-market

Money flows, end to end. (1) Residents buy credits through the platform's own billing on web and desktop, with app stores as top-ups, and buy subscriptions and rent. (2) Credits flow to creators (90% of a sale), to venues (tips 100%, tickets 90%), to group treasuries and between residents (resale 100%). (3) Platform revenue is rent, subscriptions, commissions, cash-out fees, metered compute and streaming hours. (4) 25% of net revenue returns to creators and venues as the Commons Dividend. (5) Creators cash out through the licensed rail at 2.5% plus payout cost, with tax data on file. Credits are conserved: at Linden scale roughly US$83 per MAU per year enters the credit economy (US$76 bought plus US$7 of allowances), US$17 returns as pool, and US$24 of commission, US$75 of cash-out and the cash-out fee leave it.

Prices. Free account: avatar, 250-item/1 GB inventory, a free room for 90 days, full access to public places, Maker selling rights. Resident: US$9.99 a month or US$99 a year, with a home plot (1,000 objects, 1 GB), 20 GB inventory, a 300 NC monthly allowance (US$3, so 30% of the subscription flows straight to creators) and one lottery ticket a month. Resident Plus: US$19.99 a month, with a larger home (4,000 objects), two extra plots, priority queue for first-party events and 20 streaming hours. Rent, commissions and cash-out are in section 5.4. Subscriptions and creator items carry regional pricing; local rails arrive in phase 2.

Unit economics, planning model. Inputs: 25% DAU/MAU; new money in US$120 per MAU per year; primary sales US$240 per MAU per year (velocity ~3 on the US$83 injected); cash-out US$75 per MAU, below SL's ~US$130; infrastructure plus trust and safety US$2 falling to US$1.2 per DAU per month; staff at US$150K loaded.

Rent plus subscriptions is 61% of revenue at 300K and 600K MAU, meeting R6's test, and the creator-facing take stays at 12.5% because rent and subscriptions carry the platform, which is the taxonomy's own conclusion. Survival mode at 100K MAU is 30 staff, a loss of about US$1M a year, covered by a US$5M reserve held from the raise (R30). Sensitivity: the weakest inputs are primary sales per MAU and the 20% subscription rate; halving both at 600K MAU cuts revenue to ~US$27M and the result to roughly break-even, which is why headcount is capped and the pool is a percentage rather than a promise.

Funding. US$40M in two rounds: US$12M seed for 18 months to launch and US$28M Series A to month 36, with no sibling product and the headcount cap and runway plan published. At launch, a Founders' Charter sale: up to 25,000 three-year prepaid Resident subscriptions with rent credit at US$399 (about US$10M), non-transferable, consumed monthly, refundable pro rata and held in a segregated account. That is pre-purchase as Hytale did it, not land sold before population, which the taxonomy ranks as the pattern no survivor used (2.2 #3). Target: operating costs covered by recurring revenue by month 36 at ~300K MAU.

Sequencing. Founding Merchants and the ten institutions in closed alpha (months 0-9); public launch with web and desktop clients and cash-out in the US, EU, UK and Brazil (month 12); Japanese and German shards with local rails (months 18-24); native mobile (month 24); the education tier (month 20); growth spending only from surplus.

The pitch to a creator, in numbers. On US$100,000 of annual sales an SL creator at a 20-31% all-in stack nets US$69-80K; a nolife Merchant nets ~US$87.5K plus a pool share that at Linden scale distributes ~US$10M a year across perhaps 8,000 earning places and venues, a median that is small and a top decile that is meaningful, published quarterly so everyone can see whether the long tail is growing. Roblox's June 2026 move to a 42% DevEx uplift on verified adult spend shows the youth platforms now pricing for the audience nolife starts with (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42).

## 14. Biggest risks and what I would cut first

Risks, ranked.

1. The audience ceiling is real and SL keeps it. If the market is ~0.5-1M and Linden cuts fees or finally ships an export bit, nolife's wedge is only the pool and the rail. Mitigation: costs sized to 100K MAU; the pitch is continuity plus economics, not features.
2. Migration fails on rights, not formats. If mesh body vendors do not join the Re-delivery Programme, migrants arrive without their look. Mitigation: vendor-first outreach, the open body, bounties. This is the single most important supply-side deal.
3. Processor action on adult commerce. Mitigation is in section 9; the residual risk is a scheme-level rule reaching virtual-currency merchants, at which point the adult-specialist acquirer and the deindex switch are the plan, and Tumblr's 29% is the cost of the alternative.
4. Pool farming and recommender drift. Epic and Roblox each re-engineered formulas within two years, and Roblox's 2026 bookings miss came from engagement shifting to lower-monetising titles after a retention-favouring recommender change (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm). Mitigation: purchasers only, verified weighting, quarterly formula review with the Assembly.
5. Consumer-law shift. If the Digital Fairness Act bans paid currencies, the stored-value fallback is already engineered; exact-amount purchase and fiat display are there from day one.
6. Engine dependence. Unreal licensing for a UGC platform that competes with UEFN may be constrained. Mitigation: a custom licence negotiated before phase 1, engine-neutral content, the Godot path specified.
7. Density. If Wards do not reach the SLO, the first hour fails regardless of the cohort system. Mitigation: fewer Wards, faster closure, harder routing.
8. Rent elasticity. If US$59 cells do not sell to 4% of MAU, revenue falls short. Mitigation: prices can fall but never rise retroactively, and subscriptions carry the homes.

What I would cut first, in order: the VR view and consoles; pixel streaming entirely; creator-hired agents, keeping moderation and translation; the native mobile builder, keeping mobile visiting and buying; OpenUSD scene import, keeping glTF; splat capture; self-hosted cells, keeping escrow and export; the second localisation pair, keeping Portuguese and Spanish and deferring Japanese and German.

What I would never cut: the licensed rail and tax onboarding, the fee page, rent-for-bytes rather than traffic, the Commons Dividend, the charter import, the text client, the age module, the Assembly and the never-clauses. Those are the four money mechanisms and the four trust mechanisms, and the taxonomy's evidence is that these worlds die of subsidy collapse, store taxes and operator trust shocks, not of missing features.

# nolife: a design proposal from the go-to-market and survival angle

Author stance: studio executive, two shipped free-to-play live-service titles, one venture raise. Date: 2026-10-09. Evidence base: `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (R1-R30) and fifteen briefs in `research/`. Every number below is either cited to a brief's source or marked as my own arithmetic.

## 1. Thesis and premise

"nolife" is the insult thrown at the people who have spent twenty years in Second Life, and we take it as the name because those people are the only proven market. The taxonomy's cheapest hypothesis is that the audience for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out is 0.5-1M people and Second Life already serves most of it: ~600-620K MAU, 48,802 peak concurrency, a region map flat at ~27,000 for a decade (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/12/19/second-life-now-at-620000-monthly-active-users/ ; https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/05/11/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-update-10th-may-2026/). Every successor that tried to invent a new audience died of subsidy collapse; every successor that tried to inherit this one without carrying its identity, inventory and institutions (Sansar, High Fidelity) failed to inherit anything. nolife is the first successor built to carry the institutions.

The name also states the three rules the business runs on. No life: the world ships with no life of its own. It has no quests, no storyline, no a-life pets and no undisclosed AI residents; purpose is supplied by communities with a schedule, roles and scarcity, and the platform's job is to make institutions portable, cheap to keep and impossible to kill by decree (R11, R15, R17, R19). No life-support: the company is sized to live on rent, subscriptions and a published take from month 36, with fixed costs that survive a 100K-MAU outcome (R30). Rec Room raised $294M, had 150M lifetime players and runway to 2029 and still announced it "can't make the business work" (Source: https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/); Second Life was profitable in 2005 on $25M raised (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/). We copy the second. No coin: there is no branded currency, no token and no land sale. The wallet is a fiat-pegged stored-value balance shown as money, which exits the virtual-currency fight the EU is about to have (R9) and keeps the cash-out rail one step removed from content (R10, R22).

What nolife is, in one paragraph: a persistent world of simulated cells (100-150 avatars each) under a presence layer (500-1,000 per place), rendered at Unreal-5.8-class fidelity on a mid-range PC and at Switch-2 class on phones and in the browser, where every asset carries a per-tier budget so the hardware floor never ratchets upward (R1, R2). Communities arrive as institutions through a Charter import that moves a group, its roles, ban list, calendar, rating and land in one transaction (R18). Persistence is rented from day one; activity is priced near cost; the fee stack is one page; the creator-facing round-trip take is at most 12.5%; and 25% of platform net revenue is returned to the places people keep coming back to (R6, R7, R8). The company publishes its metrics, its charter and its runway, because measurement opacity, decree governance and funding posture are the three things the graveyard has in common (R13, R19, R20).

The premise is unglamorous: win 150-250K of Second Life's and OpenSim's most organised residents in three years by being the world their institutions can move to without dying, then grow from that base through the markets Linden abandoned in 2010 (Brazil, Spanish-speaking Latin America, Japan's avatar economy, educators, the disability community) and the adult economy nobody has ever measured or defended. The full vision (VR, AI inhabitants, scanned real places, federation) ships only after recurring revenue covers the payroll.

## 2. Who comes first and why

Paid acquisition is off the table. Mobile CPIs of $1-4 at a 1-2% payer rate imply $75-300 per payer, which no social world has ever recovered; Roblox, Fortnite and VRChat all grew organically (Source: https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267). The wedge is therefore institutions that bring their members, in this order.

Wave 0, founding charters (private alpha, 15-25 institutions). These are the named leaders in the census brief, each a single decision-maker who can bring hundreds to thousands: Virtual Ability (1,000-1,300 members, own estate, text-first Cape Able), the VWBPE committee and its educator network, Cafe Musique (60+ live shows a week) and the live-music venue scene, the 1920s Berlin Project (100+ tenants, weekly period events), the Crack Den (three sims of text roleplay), Second Pride, Fantasy Faire's Relay for Life team ($358,000 raised by 2019), Luskwood and the furry hub sims, First UCC SL and the UU congregation, and two or three OpenSim grid operators (Kitely, DigiWorldz) as self-hosting partners (Source: https://virtualability.org/about-us ; https://secondlife.aditi.lindenlab.com/destination/aism ; https://ryanschultz.com/2019/05/30/the-1920s-berlin-project-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-in-second-life/ ; https://thecrackden.com/roleplay/ ; https://modemworld.me/2020/04/25/fantasy-faire-2020-three-reasons-to-relay/). What they get on day one: their Charter imported with the leader as owner; an estate or ward allocation at founding-rate rent frozen for 36 months; OAR/IAR import of what they made; a text client that works with a screen reader; parcel-level stream URLs, host and DJ roles and 0%-commission tips for the venues; whitelists, ban lists, ranks and damage parcels for the roleplayers; SSO and class-cells under $50/month for the educators (R18, R27, R28, R17). What they give: a weekly scheduled event on the civic calendar and a named deputy for every role that owns land or a calendar (R15).

Wave 1, the OpenSim diaspora (public beta). The hypergrid has ~45,000 monthly actives who already know how to pack an OAR, pay $8-40 a month for regions, and lost content when OSgrid wiped its database in 2025 and went dark for weeks in 2026 (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/01/opensim-land-grows-as-traffic-slows-slightly/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2025/02/osgrid-wiping-its-database-on-march-21-you-have-five-weeks-to-save-your-stuff ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). They come for what OpenSim lacks (an economy, an audience, stability) and keep what it gave them (export, escrow, self-hosting, cheap land). Recruitment is an OAR import tool, a Kitely-market redelivery deal and a published persistence covenant.

Wave 2, Second Life residents who are not institution members (launch). The channel is the institutions themselves plus a 12-24-month chat and event bridge so a venue keeps its SL audience while it builds a nolife one (R18). The message is price and rights, not graphics: a full cell at $99/month against Linden's $349 setup plus $199/month from June 2026, a published 12.5% round trip against the 20-31% residents estimate they pay, and an Export flag Linden never shipped (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/). The adult and romantic roleplay communities are in this wave deliberately, with a gated Adult ward that exists at launch rather than being bolted on in a 2009-style forced migration (R22).

Wave 3, refugees and the abandoned markets (months 12-24). Spatial deleted Free and Pro creator worlds on 27 July 2026 and Rec Room went dark on 1 June 2026; a glTF importer and a migration offer are a near-free channel (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/). Brazil and Colombia are already 12.6% of SL web traffic with no funded support since Kaizen Games stopped and the 2010 layoffs closed the Singapore office (Source: https://zh.semrush.com/website/secondlife.com/overview ; https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-07-linden-lab-laying-off-staff-closing-singapore-office.html). Portuguese and Spanish are launch languages with paid community managers; Japanese and German follow in year two with VRM import and off-platform marketplace support, because VRChat-Japan was built on BOOTH's ~10.4bn-yen avatar trade, not on VRChat's store (Source: https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html) (R29).

What is not in the wedge: teenagers (regulatory surface, wrong audience), VR-first users (1.77% of Steam), brands, and crypto. Each is a documented way to die.

## 3. The purpose engine

The taxonomy's restated finding is that retention concentrates in users attached to a group with a recurring time and place, and that Second Life supplied neither schedule nor roles (section 1.4). nolife's purpose engine is five mechanisms, all operated by communities, all measurable.

Roles as objects. Host, Mentor, DJ, Warden (moderator), Builder, Performer, Teacher and Judge are first-class objects on a group: each has a schedule slot, a visible badge, an optional stipend from the group's treasury or the Commons Fund, and a Deputy field that is mandatory for any role owning land or a calendar (R15). Succession is the point: the Anglican Cathedral survived its founder's exit because a team existed; most venues do not (Source: https://episcopal.cafe/transitions_at_the_anglican_cathedral_in_second_life/).

The Almanac. A civic calendar ships on day one with a weekly rhythm owned by named communities (Market Day, Open Stage, Assembly, a service, a class) and one annual scarce ritual, Fairground, held on cells that exist only for that week, modelled on BURN2 and Fantasy Faire (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/). First-party events run as synchronised ~100-avatar instances with cross-instance chat, which is how Fortnite's concerts reached millions (Source: https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/fortnites-remix-the-finale-concert-draws-over-14m-players-breaking-records). Target: 25% of MAU attend a scheduled event monthly.

Scarcity. Public wards are scarce and allocated by lottery with a full-price ticket, a 45-day use-it-or-lose-it rule with an 80% refund and a published pause policy, copied from FFXIV, where a 3% chance of winning a plot created an unplanned venue scene (R16) (Source: https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-updates-lottery-empyreum-patch-6-1). Private homes and estates are rented and never auto-demolished. Status objects are earned and never re-issued: Fairground badges, founding-charter marks, lottery-won ward addresses.

Curated estates. Roleplay and combat communities get seat-capped estates with written applications, whitelists, access and ban lists, roles as in-fiction ranks, damage-enabled parcels and paid queue priority under Rockstar's rules: cosmetics and priority yes, pay-to-win and loot boxes no (R17). FiveM's 221,062 Steam peak and $25-200/month priority tiers show this is the strongest retention and monetisation loop in the genre (Source: https://steamcharts.com/app/2676230 ; https://ascenddev.tebex.io/category/priority-roles).

Self-rule. Groups own land, treasuries, calendars and ban lists; wards vote on their own bylaws; the Council (section 9) is elected at month 12.

The first hour. Every new account lands in Arrivals, a hosted cohort of 8-16 newcomers with a human Host on a schedule, not an empty island (R11). Before the cohort ends the newcomer has a dressed avatar from the open-licensed first-party body, a furnished Studio (an instanced 64 m² flat in a shared block, free), has met at least one person by name and has been offered three groups whose next event is within 48 hours (R26). The scripting panel is not visible in the consumer interface. Success is "met a person or joined a group", measured against a randomised holdout that lands in a self-guided hub; the cohort is kept only if the D30 lift beats the industry base, and the result is published at month 6 (R11, R13).

The first year. Month 1: a Studio, two groups, a ward lottery ticket if subscribed. Month 3: a role (a shift as Host, a slot as DJ, a mentoring appointment) because people stay where they are needed. Month 6: Fairground, the first scarce thing they can only get by having been there. Month 12: a Council vote and, for a few, a Charter of their own.

## 4. World structure

Places are the unit of meaning; cells are the unit of simulation. A place is one or more contiguous cells sharing a presence layer; a cell is 100-150 fully simulated avatars with voice (R1). There are four kinds of place.

Studios: free, instanced micro-homes in shared blocks, visible only to invited friends; they cost a document, not a server, so the trial allowance is cheap to honour (R6). Wards: public neighbourhoods on the Mainland-equivalent, scarce by design, allocated by lottery (R16), each with a bylaw, a Warden rota and a density SLO. Estates: private, rented, seat-capped, with full estate tools (R17). Commons: first-party hubs and event grounds that exist only while occupied.

Persistence is charged as rent above the trial allowance (one Studio, 2,000 inventory items, 500 MB), priced by area and content budget, never by "one server": 512 m² at $5/month, 2,048 m² at $15, a quarter-cell at $35, a full cell (65,536 m², full script and avatar budget) at $99, a four-cell Estate with whitelist tools at $299, a class-cell at $45 with a written persistence commitment, and 50% off for registered non-profits (R6, R28). Idle cells hibernate and reload on teleport, Kitely's pattern (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/06/kitely-cuts-prices-60-for-large-regions), so a cell's hosting cost is $10-35 a month depending on occupancy (my arithmetic on Edgegap's $0.069 per vCPU-hour) and rent is the margin line that funds trust and safety.

Discovery is by calendar and by people, not by map: the default view is "what is happening in the next two hours" and "where are the people I know", ranked by live occupancy and group affinity, with a paid "sponsored row" capped at one slot and no pay-to-rank in organic results. Language is a shard key: Portuguese, Spanish, Japanese and German communities own language-tagged wards with their own discovery and onboarding (R29).

Empty-world is treated as a bug with an SLO. Tide, the density manager, targets one avatar per 0.1 ha in public wards at peak, merges thin instances, closes empty Commons, routes newcomers to live events and never shows a newcomer fewer than ten people in their first session (R12). Horizon's record that only 9% of worlds ever had 50 visitors is what happens without this (Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/15/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-losing-users-falling-short-of-goals.html). Estates are exempt from merging; a private place is allowed to be empty.

## 5. Creation and creator economy

Tools. Creation happens in-world first (prims, parametric builders, a node-based behaviour editor), because the survivors with creator cultures built in-world and the Unity-as-editor platforms never grew an economy (Source: https://voicesofvr.com/?p=14108). Imports are glTF 2.0 with VRM 1.0 for humanoids at runtime and OpenUSD for scenes, plus OAR/IAR and an LSL-compatible runtime (section 10) (R3). Scripting is Luau in a sandbox with per-owner CPU, memory and network budgets that throttle the worst offender (R5).

Ownership and the licence. The content licence is one page, scoped to operating the service, with a defined affirmative action before any platform re-use, written for people who remember the 2013 ToS and the Renderosity ban (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms). A creator-set Portable flag is honoured by every client; signed provenance travels with every asset.

Money. There is no coin. Prices are shown as money ($1.20), the wallet is a stored-value balance held by the licensed partner, purchase is exact-amount with no bundles, no odd pricing and no chance mechanics that pay anything cash-out-able (R9). The fee page, The Stack, has five lines and a sixth stating the platform's required margin (R7): money-in on web and PC 0% (we absorb card cost); app-store top-ups pass the store fee through and are capped at 25% of money-in by design; primary sales commission 10% in-world and on the marketplace; tips 0%; cash-out 2.5% plus the rail's fixed fee; resale 0%. Worst-case round trip on a primary sale is 12.5%, inside R7's 15%. Compare Rec Room, which kept 30 cents of a UGC dollar after store fees and died of it (Source: https://blog.recroom.com/posts/2025/8/28/rec-room-finances-and-ugc-revenue), and Roblox creators at ~25% of spend (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/fortnite/). Our creators keep 87.5% because rent and subscriptions carry the platform, which is the taxonomy's reconciliation.

The Commons Fund. 25% of platform net revenue (after rail and store costs) is distributed monthly, weighted by distinct returning purchasing users per place with anti-farming rules copied from Epic's November 2025 rewrite (purchasers only, per-place retention, a 75% bounty on new and lapsed users for six months) (R8) (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items). 15% of the Fund is reserved for venues and groups measured by returning members, because the Roblox and Epic pools reward maps and not the people who make a place worth returning to. The formula, the payout schedule and the distribution of creator earnings are published quarterly, including the median, so everyone can see whether the long tail is growing.

Cash-out. Payout runs on a licensed, partnered rail that settles the balance under KYC; W-9/W-8 and the DAC7 data set are collected at payout onboarding because services have no DAC7 floor, 1099-K at the US trigger, 24% backup withholding budgeted (R10) (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview ; https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000). Minimum payout $25, weekly. Regional prices are creator-set with purchasing-power defaults (Roblox's tests lifted payers 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines) and local rails (Pix, konbini, prepaid cards) are top-up channels (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items).

Portability. Own-created content exports as glTF/VRM/USD with licence metadata at any time; off-platform marketplaces (BOOTH, Gumroad, Kitely Market) may deliver directly; the official web marketplace follows at month 6 as an open protocol third-party stores can plug into, reversing the 2009 Xstreet lock-in.

Moderation of UGC. Every upload passes automated review (geometry, texture, script static analysis, maturity classification) before it is visible; upload rights, voice and proximity are gated by earned trust rank, VRChat's model (R23) (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system). Adult-tagged goods require uploader verification, consent records for likeness-based content and pre-publication review, the controls Visa's VIRP and Mastercard's AN 5196 expect, built before anyone asks (R22) (Source: https://verifymy.io/resources/regulation/card-brand-requirements). Value-bearing scripted objects (breedables, vendor HUDs) must register with Keepalive, a platform-hosted escrowed life-support service with a designed conversion to a food-independent state, so no Ozimals hibernation can recur (R24) (Source: https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html).

## 6. Graphics and client strategy

Fidelity target: the Unreal 5.8 look on the modal Steam machine, which in September 2026 is an RTX 5070 with the 4060 and 3060 close behind (Source: https://www.digitalcitizen.life/nvidia-rtx-5070-most-popular-gpu-steam-hardware-survey-september-2026/), and a Switch-2-class look on phones and in the browser, because Linden said in January 2025 that half its users lack high-end machines and Blue Mars shrank its funnel by rendering above its audience (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/01/02/).

Three tiers, enforced server-side (R2). Reference: RTX 4060-class and up, 1440p at 60 fps with DLSS/FSR/XeSS upscaling, Lumen or MegaLights, Substrate materials, a 16.6 ms frame budget of which avatars get 6 ms. Lite: Switch 2, Quest 3, M-series Mac, mid Android and the WebGPU browser client; Lumen Lite-class GI, probe lighting, 60 fps at 100-avatar presence, 16.6 ms with avatars at 5 ms. Compat: WebGL2 and low Android, no dynamic GI, 128 m draw distance, 30 fps. Epic's 5.8 investment in Lumen Lite, production MegaLights and MetaHuman crowds is exactly the "make UE5 run on Switch 2" path we need (Source: https://www.unrealengine.com/news/unreal-engine-5-8-is-now-available).

Budgets per asset. Avatars: Reference 70,000 triangles, 8 materials, 48 MB of textures, 256 bones; Lite 20,000 triangles, 2 materials, 12 MB (VRChat's Quest ceiling) (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202115); Compat 7,500 triangles, 1 material. Cells: a published budget per tier for static triangles, texture memory, lights and particles. Every upload gets auto-generated LODs, impostors beyond 30 m and transcoded textures per tier; over-budget content is impostored or hidden on the tier it breaks, never rendered twice. This is the one Second Life root cause with no counter-example, and the only thing that stops the hardware floor rising with the catalogue (R2, R3).

Engine. Native clients (Windows, macOS, later Android/iOS and consoles) are Unreal 5.8, which gives us the lite path, MetaHuman-compatible rigs now that the licence allows any engine (Source: https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/), and pixel-streaming infrastructure if we ever want it. The web client is Babylon.js on WebGPU with a WebGL2 fallback, because WebGPU has shipped in Chrome, Safari 26 and desktop Firefox but Linux Firefox, Exynos phones and old Android still need the fallback (Source: https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status). Content is engine-neutral (glTF, VRM, USD) so a Godot or Babylon native client can serve Lite and Compat if Unreal's terms change; the engine's exit cost is documented (R20). The trade-off I accept: Unreal's royalty on gross revenue is wrong-shaped for a platform whose revenue is rent, so the first commercial act is a negotiated custom licence; if Epic will not do it, Lite and Compat ship on the Babylon stack and Reference waits.

Devices and clients. Launch: desktop (two tiers) and web (zero-install visiting and socialising). Native mobile within 12 months of launch; VR as an optional view of the flat-screen world, never required for identity or inventory; pixel streaming only as a time-capped funnel at a published cost per hour, because Project Zero cost ~$1.75 per streamed hour and died in 14 months (R14) (Source: https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/). Third-party clients are expected on a documented protocol, and the first-party newcomer client is owned by a named team with first-session completion as its KPI, because nobody owned Second Life's newcomer interface and Firestorm's 542,967 monthly users are the proof (R4) (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/firestorm-by-far-most-popular-on-hypergrid/). Avatars are first-party and open-licensed; no rented avatar platform, because Ready Player Me was acquired and shut on 31 January 2026 (Source: https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/netflix-acquires-ready-player-me-games-avatar-creation-1236612915/). Gaussian-splat place capture is a year-three backdrop feature, not a launch one.

## 7. Architecture

Two layers. The presence layer carries position, pose, voice and chat at 5-10 Hz through interest-managed relays and an SFU, and can show 1,000-5,000 avatars in a place as impostors. The simulation layer (physics, scripts, vehicles, interaction) is sharded into cells of 50-150 avatars with an authoritative entity store, so a cell crash is a reconnect and not a lost place. Published SLOs: 100-150 per cell, 500-1,000 per contiguous place, 2,000-5,000 in a declared event mode with pre-mixed audio and simplified interaction (R1). Nothing above that is promised: Star Citizen reached ~500 per shard after a decade and Improbable's observed peaks were 4,144 and 4,500 at scripted events (Source: https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/star-citizen-500-player-server-update/ ; https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/tennis-for-two-thousand-the-story-behind-improbables-mass-event-in-scavlab/).

Persistence is documents plus an event log, with Roblox-style per-cell and per-creator write budgets, snapshots and rollback for land owners, and nightly escrowed backups, because OSgrid shows what losing assets does to trust (R20).

Cost per CCU. Compute at $0.055-0.07 per vCPU-hour (GameLift's c6i.large at $0.109/hour, Edgegap at $0.069) puts a 100-avatar cell at $0.14-0.28 per hour and client egress at $0.09-0.23 per hour, i.e. $0.002-0.005 per avatar-hour before voice; voice via ODIN is €0.29 per peak concurrent user per month, about €0.01 per hour for a 30-hour user (Source: https://aws.amazon.com/gamelift/servers/pricing/ ; https://edgegap.com/pricing ; https://odin.4players.io/pricing). A 10,000-CCU world runs at $50-100 per hour of servers, $0.5-1M a year. The number that matters is trust and safety: Roblox's infrastructure plus T&S line was $1,153M in 2025, about $0.9 per DAU per month with heavy automation, so nolife budgets $2 per DAU per month in year one falling to $1.2 by year three (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm).

Vendors. Cell servers are containers with one deployment manifest that runs on Edgegap, GameLift Servers, Nitrado GameFabric or bare metal; voice sits behind an abstraction that can swap ODIN, Discord's Social SDK or a self-hosted SFU; no sole-source streaming client. Hathora offboarded its game customers within weeks of its 2026 acquisition and Firestorm Zero closed when its streaming provider forced a migration, so vendor risk is now the top infrastructure risk (R20) (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174 ; https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/). Escrowed server images and a self-hostable cell (the Homestead Kit, for OpenSim operators and educators) ship at launch; a failover drill between two providers runs annually.

Scaling plan: launch sized for 5,000 CCU, tested at 150 per cell with voice; 20,000 CCU by month 24; event mode validated at 2,000 before the first Fairground.

## 8. AI and the living world

The corpus is clear that agents change what an empty room looks like, not whether people have a reason to be in it, and that 63% of Quantic Foundry's respondents chose the most negative option on generative AI (Source: https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/). AI is therefore a phase-two, capped stopgap for density and labour, never a purpose mechanism and never a fake resident (R25).

What inhabitants do. Three jobs, in order of value: a disclosed assistant Host that runs Arrivals when no human Host is on shift and hands over the moment one arrives; opt-in digital twins that tend a creator's shop or greet a venue's visitors while the owner is offline, with full logs and the owner's consent; and ambient staff (a doorman, a barkeep, a chronicler who writes the ward's weekly bulletin from public events). Every inhabitant carries a persistent badge and a plain-language notice stating on whose behalf it acts, as the EU AI Act's Article 50 requires from 2 August 2026 (Source: https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/).

Guardrails. Inhabitants are capped at 20% of visible occupants in any place and withdraw as humans arrive; the cap is lowered if human D30 in agent-filled places falls below comparable human-only places. No romantic or open-ended companion loops for anyone, and no companion loops at all in minor-bracket accounts, given Character.AI's November 2025 ban for under-18s and Replika's EUR 5M fine (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots). Voices are licensed or synthetic with provenance; no replicas of real performers.

Costs. Cognition is tiered: scripted routines by default, a small on-device or edge model for ambient talk, frontier calls only for memorable interactions, with a published cost per agent-hour and a hard ceiling of 10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget. No vendor publishes a true per-NPC-hour cost and prices turn over within a year, so the contract is month-to-month and the runtime is swappable.

Automation we do rely on from day one: AI triage of reports, asset classification at upload, chat translation (shipped as a lubricant, with its retention effect measured, since no published study shows it moves retention) (R29).

## 9. Identity, safety, governance, regulation

Identity. One persistent self, pseudonymous by default, with KYC only at payout. Age assurance is a configurable regional module: on-device estimation or a wallet credential, no stored ID images (Discord's vendor leaked 70,000 IDs), per-region thresholds, feature gating rather than binary bans, OS age signals consumed on native clients, parental surfaces and adjacent-band chat with private-by-default for minor brackets (R21) (Source: https://therecord.media/discord-government-docs-exposed-breach). At launch the world is 18+ in practice with a General-rated education tier; the UK, EU, Brazil (ECA Digital, in force 17 March 2026, rejects self-declaration), Indonesia (PP Tunas, unverified accounts lose communication) and Texas profiles are configured before launch (Source: https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/ ; https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/).

The Night Ward. Adult content is a first-class, measured, controlled purpose. The Adult ward and Adult-tagged commerce are unlocked by an Ofcom-grade check, which VRChat has run through Persona since January 2025 while Second Life still relies on a self-declared birth date (Source: https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Assurance ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/533810-age-verification-in-2026/). Maturity-tagged spend and cash-out are instrumented from day one; a deindex-but-sell switch can hide Adult listings from discovery in hours (itch.io's survivable path); exclusion from general cash-out is held only as a processor-triggered contingency with an adult-specialist acquirer pre-qualified in reserve (R22). The consumer purchase is coded as stored value, never an adult MCC. Tumblr lost ~29% of traffic in three months by exiting; OnlyFans reversed its ban in a week once scheme controls were agreed (Source: https://thenextweb.com/insider/2019/03/14/tumblr-traffic-dropped-by-nearly-100m-the-month-after-it-banned-porn/ ; https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6148524).

Safety primitives, always on: personal boundary default-on, one-gesture mute, block and vanish, instance kick and lock, a 60-second evidence buffer attached to every report, consent prompts for avatar-to-avatar actions (Bloodlines' bite consent), and trust ranks gating upload, voice and proximity (R23). Incident KPIs (reports per 1,000 user-hours, median time-to-action under 24 hours) are published quarterly with audit logs for information requests. Embodied harassment is unprosecutable, so platform controls are the only layer; ADL found 19% of harassed players quit games entirely (Source: https://extremismterms.adl.org/sites/default/files/pdfs/2022-12/Free%20to%20Play%2007242019.pdf).

Governance. The Charter of the World is published before launch: persistence prices rise only with 12 months' notice and grandfathering; client-affecting updates go through public change review (VRChat's EAC update drew 22,000 votes in 24 hours because it did not) (Source: https://www.dazeddigital.com/science-tech/article/56662/1/why-thousands-of-users-are-abandoning-the-worlds-most-popular-vr-game); the Council, elected at month 12 by residents in good standing, publishes a vote that the company must answer in writing within 30 days, with EVE's CSM turnout of 12-17% as the benchmark (R19) (Source: https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/csm-7-the-results). Wards write bylaws; moderation deletion rates are public per ward, the signal that predicted healthy growth on Reddit (Source: https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2017/eternalseptember/eternalseptember.pdf). The charter names what will never be added: tokens, land sales, a sibling product (R30).

Appeals: every enforcement action carries a reason code; appeals are answered by a second human within 72 hours; from month 18 a resident jury hears contested ward-level cases.

## 10. Legacy support

Rights, not formats, are the barrier: a Second Life resident's look is third-party mesh bought no-mod and no-transfer, so a successor can import what people made, not what they bought.

Imported. OAR and IAR archives, including prim and sculpt geometry (converted to glTF at import), llmesh, textures, scripts as text and the inventory tree, honouring creator-only rules and the OpenSim Export bit; Second Life exports via Firestorm's creator-only path. LSL runs as-is inside an LSL-compatible runtime built on OpenSimulator's BSD-licensed engine, under the same per-owner budgets as Luau, so a venue's tip jars, HUD meters and vendors work on day one; Luau is the native language and the migration target (R3, R5). Charter import moves the group, roles and abilities, opt-in member list, ban list (up to 500), maturity rating, calendar and land allocation in one transaction with the leader as owner, the group being Second Life's real substrate (R18) (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Group). glTF and VRM avatars import directly, which opens BOOTH's catalogue and VRoid to nolife without a store (R29).

Re-made. Avatar bodies: the first-party body is open-licensed and Bento/BOM-class so migrants are not naked, and a vendor programme invites Maitreya, Legacy and LeLutka-class makers to publish nolife editions at 0% commission for the first year. Animations and the system layer: re-authored by the platform for the first-party body. Breedables: ported into Keepalive with the vendor or, if the vendor is gone, converted to a food-independent state.

The bridge. For 12-24 months after launch, a registered relay carries group chat and calendar notices between a community's SL group and its nolife Charter, with a "visit from SL" link, so Cafe Musique can play to both rooms. The risk that Linden blocks it is real and is why the bridge also runs through Discord's Social SDK, which every world already rents (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/17/discord-launches-sdk-to-help-developers-enhance-social-experiences-in-their-games).

Educators. Institutional SSO (SAML/LTI), bulk student accounts without per-student email, private cells with OAR-equivalent export, General-rated enforcement, $45 class-cells and a written persistence commitment, because cost and persistence were decisive in the 2014 study and the 2013 discount restoration was "too late" (R28) (Source: https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/49578/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/08/educators-wary-of-latest-linden-offer/).

Accessibility. Reader, a first-party text client covering chat, IMs, notices, inventory, teleport, object list and a voiced GPS, with speech-to-text captions on voice and text-to-speech of chat (XAG 119), keyboard-only navigation, seated and motion options and a funded maintainer, ships at launch; Radegast's last release was February 2022 and blind residents depend on it (R27) (Source: https://modemworld.me/2022/10/18/2022-viewer-release-summaries-week-41/ ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/119).

VRChat, Resonite and Horizon communities: VRM import, group tools and an events calendar serve them; we do not chase VR-first users and we do not promise to import Unity-built worlds.

## 11. Business model and go-to-market

Money flows. Residents pay rent for persistence, a subscription for convenience, and nothing to enter. Consumers top up a stored-value balance (0% fee on web). Creators and venues receive 87.5-100% of what residents pay them and a share of the Commons Fund. The company earns rent, subscriptions, the 10% primary commission, the 2.5% cash-out fee, estate priority queues (15% platform share) and the single sponsored row. The revenue mix is published annually and rent plus subscriptions must be at least 50% of it by month 24 (R6).

Pricing. Free account with Studio and trial allowance. Resident subscription at $9.99/month or $99/year: a 512 m² ward parcel (lottery ticket included), a $3/month stipend shown in fiat, 10x inventory, event priority. Rent as in section 4. Estate priority tiers set by the estate within $5-50/month. Education and non-profit rates as stated. Nothing is sold that is not a service: no land purchase, no token.

Unit economics, three states (my arithmetic on the briefs' inputs). Survival floor, 100K MAU and 25K DAU: rent from 4,000 paid units averaging $40 is $1.9M; 12,000 subscribers at $96 is $1.2M; take on $15M of flow at a blended 12% is $1.8M; total $4.9M. Costs: infrastructure plus T&S at $2 per DAU-month is $0.6M; 35 staff at $170K loaded is $6M; rail costs 3% of money-in, $0.3M; Commons Fund 25% of net, $1.1M. Loss ~$3M a year, survivable for three years on reserves. Self-funding, 400K MAU and 100K DAU: rent from 16,000 units at $45 is $8.6M; 50,000 subscribers at $100 is $5M; take on $70M of flow is $8.4M; priority queues and sponsored rows $1.5M; total $23.5M. Costs: T&S and infrastructure $2.4M; 60 staff at $175K is $10.5M; rail $1M; Commons Fund $5M; total $18.9M; margin $4.6M. Linden scale, 600K MAU and 150K DAU with $100M of new money a year: revenue ~$38M against costs ~$26M. The benchmarks that make this credible: Avakin's 1.6M MAU produced GBP 22M of revenue and a GBP -11M EBITDA with 200+ staff and no rent line (Source: https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/asset/lockwood-publishing-limited/398573); Linden runs ~600K MAU on ~160-200 staff and a 10% take, profitably (Source: https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=88910). Our structural difference from Avakin is rent; from Linden it is headcount and a modern client.

Retention and CAC benchmarks. Organic and migrated cohorts: first-session completion at or above 60%, D7 25%, D30 15%, D90 10% (the industry base Second Life already hits); institution members D90 at or above 40% because they arrive with a reason. Paid CAC is zero in the plan; the acquisition engine is Charters, the bridge, events and invitations, and the only marketing spend is community managers and Fairground. Cost per DAU per month $2 in year one. Time to revenue: founding-estate rent from private alpha (month 9 of the company), subscriptions and commission from public launch (month 18), rent plus subscriptions covering T&S and infrastructure from month 24, payroll covered from month 36.

Funding. Total raise $33M in two rounds, against a graveyard where Rec Room's $294M, Improbable's £150M 2021 loss and Reality Labs' $19B a year bought nothing durable (Source: https://roadtovr.com/rec-room-layoff-half-staff-august-2025/ ; https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/18/metaverse-firm-improbable-sells-gaming-unit-for-97-million.html). Seed $8M: 14 months, 20 people, the cell server, the Reference and web clients, Charter and OAR import, the rail contract, and signed letters from 15 founding institutions. Series A $25M at private alpha, raised on four numbers: institutions migrated with membership, Arrivals' D30 lift over holdout, rent already billing, and a month-36 self-funding plan with a headcount cap of 60 (R30). Founders' Estates, 12-month prepaid rent at a 30% discount sold only to migrating institutions and capped at $1.5M, non-transferable and never resold, is the only pre-sale; Hytale funded two years of development on pre-purchases without selling anything speculative (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hytale). No token, no land sale, no sibling product, no strategic investor with a headset to sell.

Team at launch, 45 people: client 10, server and infrastructure 8, UGC pipeline and tools 5, trust and safety, policy and community 10 (four of them Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking community managers who are also the localisation owners), payments and compliance 3, product and design 4, newcomer-client team 2, executive, finance and legal 3. First-line moderation, age assurance and the payout rail are vendors. The cap is 60 until recurring revenue covers payroll.

Sequencing of spend: rail and compliance before graphics; Reader before VR; Charter import before the marketplace; Arrivals before AI. Growth features are funded only after rent and subscriptions cover costs (R30).

Published metrics. First-hour, D7, D30, D90, DAU/MAU, peak concurrency and bot share, quarterly, with a documented active definition ("entered, moved and interacted with another person for five minutes") and a public API so third parties can reproduce concurrency within 5%, because the Decentraland 38-versus-8,000 episode showed what opacity costs (R13) (Source: https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-final-word-on-decentralands-numbers).

## 12. Roadmap sketch

Phase 0, build and charters (Q4 2026 to Q4 2027). Seed closed; cell server at 150 avatars with voice; Reference client and web client on the budget pipeline; Charter, OAR/IAR import and the LSL runtime; rail and age-assurance contracts signed with their adult-content policy on file; Charter of the World and The Stack published; 15 founding institutions signed. Milestone: 10 institutions rebuilt in a closed world, Reader usable by a blind tester.

Phase 1, private alpha (Q1 to Q3 2028). 20-25 institutions, 5-10K residents, Arrivals with holdout, the Almanac live with weekly events, ward lottery, Night Ward gated, Founders' Estates billing. Milestones: D30 lift published; rent billing; Series A closed on those numbers.

Phase 2, public launch (Q4 2028). Desktop two tiers and web; Portuguese and Spanish; the Commons Fund's first payout; the bridge to SL live; the first Fairground on temporary cells; Census page and API. Targets by month 12 after launch: 100K MAU, 50 whitelisted estates with weekly events, 40% of DAU-hours in creator-run places, store-routed money-in under 25%.

Phase 3, breadth (2029). Native Android and iOS; Japanese and German wards with VRM and off-platform marketplaces; the Council's first election; the education tier at 20 institutions; disclosed AI inhabitants under the 20% cap; the official marketplace protocol; mobile at or above 30% of DAU by month 24 after launch. Target: 250K MAU, rent plus subscriptions at or above 50% of revenue.

Phase 4, the full vision (2030-2031). Optional VR view; Gaussian-splat place capture for scanned venues; Homestead Kit general availability and federation with partner grids; event mode at 5,000; consoles if the Lite tier earns it; payroll covered by recurring revenue at 400K MAU, which is the condition for any of this to be funded.

## 14. Biggest risks and what you would cut first

Risk 1: the market is already served, and the incumbent is cheap to keep alive. Second Life is profitable on a 10% take, cut region tier in June 2026 while raising Premium, and can match any price we publish (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/). Our answer is what Linden structurally cannot ship fast: budgets on content, a modern client, an Export flag, escrow, a 12.5% take and a charter that binds the operator. If by month 12 after launch fewer than 10 named institutions have migrated with membership, the thesis is wrong and the company should shrink to a 25-person OpenSim-plus-economy business rather than raise again.

Risk 2: the adult economy and the rails. The 2025 processor campaign removed titles from Steam, deindexed itch.io and cost DLsite Visa and Mastercard; Stripe prohibits adult content outright (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story ; https://conductatlas.com/platform/stripe/stripe-restricted-businesses-list/adult-content-prohibition-and-restriction/). Mitigation is built in (stored-value coding, Tier-1-grade controls, reserve acquirer, deindex switch), but a rail that refuses us at contract time delays launch by a quarter, and that quarter is in the plan.

Risk 3: engine and vendor dependence. An Unreal royalty on gross revenue, a streaming or voice vendor that pivots to AI, an age-assurance vendor breach. Mitigation: custom licence or the Babylon fallback, two providers by construction, no stored ID images.

Risk 4: trust and safety cost. $2 per DAU-month is 40% of the survival-floor budget; if automation under-delivers it is the line that breaks us before servers ever do.

Risk 5: rights block the look. If the mesh-body vendors do not opt in, migrants arrive with their builds and not their faces, and the first-party body has to be good enough to be chosen rather than tolerated.

Risk 6: Linden blocks the bridge, or Discord's age-assurance rollout changes what embedded worlds may assume; the Discord path and the institutions' own channels are the fallback.

Risk 7: funding climate. The Series A is raised on measured numbers at private alpha; if it does not close, the cut ladder below runs and the seed is sized to reach a 25-person, rent-funded floor.

What I would cut first, in order: VR (already optional, and the one feature whose absence loses no one in the wedge); AI inhabitants (a density stopgap; Tide does most of the work); Gaussian-splat capture; the Compat tier (serve Lite on web and let the lowest phones wait); native mobile to month 18 rather than 12; Japanese and German to year three; MetaHuman-class humans in Reference; the official marketplace (in-world vendors and off-platform stores carry year one); the resident jury; console ports. What I would never cut: the rail and compliance, Reader, Charter and OAR import, Arrivals with its holdout, the budget pipeline, the Charter of the World, and the published Census. Those are the moat; everything else is a phase.

# nolife: a civilization engine, not a world

Design proposal, purpose-first angle. Author: sociologist of online communities, former lead designer of a long-lived social MMO. Date: 2026-10-09. Evidence base: `scratchpad/analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (R1–R30) and the research briefs in `scratchpad/research/` (community-purpose, SL community census, onboarding and governance, winners' lessons, alt-survivors, SL economy, legacy interop, networking, graphics, AI NPCs, business models, safety and regulation, and the three gap briefs on adult economy, non-English communities and currency law). No web search was used.

---

## 1. Thesis and premise

**nolife is a persistent world that ships with no life of its own.** The name is read literally and then turned around. Every successor to Second Life tried to supply life from the centre: scripted loops (Rec Room, PS Home), curated experiences (Sansar), corporate social graphs (Horizon), or generated worlds. Each died or stalled, and the taxonomy's survivor control set shows why: what retains people is "a group with a recurring time and place" (taxonomy 1.4), and no operator has ever manufactured that at scale. So nolife refuses to try. It contains no quests, no story, no NPC civilization. It contains institutions, roles, scarcity, a calendar and a constitution, and leaves the life to the people who are usually insulted with the phrase "get a life": the no-lifers who ran SL9B on donated regions when Linden withdrew, who carried the Anglican Cathedral past its founder's exit, who have run the Confederation of Democratic Simulators since 2004 (Source: https://modemworld.me/2012/06/17/sl9b-let-the-festivities-begin/ ; https://episcopal.cafe/transitions_at_the_anglican_cathedral_in_second_life/ ; https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Confederation_of_Democratic_Simulators). The second reading of the name is the product promise: it is not a *second* life, a copy of the first with better hair, but a civilization engine in which purpose is a public good produced by communities and the platform's job is to make that production cheap, legible and durable.

The premise rests on four findings the evidence will not let me ignore. First, the funnel is not the problem: SL's 7–10% ninety-day retention is at the industry base rate, unchanged since 2007, and removing access barriers in 2025 moved the ceiling only a little (taxonomy 1.3; Source: https://andrewchen.com/interesting-graph-on-second-life-usage/ ; https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/). Second, what lasted inside SL was resident-built and staffed: Virtual Ability's 1,000-plus members, Cafe Musique's 60-plus shows a week, BURN2 since 2010, the 1920s Berlin Project past its tenth year, against brand islands "filled with brochures but never having any staff" (Source: https://virtualability.org/about-us ; https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2 ; https://ryanschultz.com/2019/05/30/the-1920s-berlin-project-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-in-second-life/). Third, Linden supplied places but never roles, schedules or scarcity, and never measured its largest purpose engine (taxonomy 1.4). Fourth, the winners that did supply purpose did it with social machinery, not content: NoPixel's whitelist and 32 seats, FFXIV's housing lottery and 45-day demolition, Habbo's never-reissued rares, EVE's fifteen years of 12–17% election turnout (Source: https://www.sportskeeda.com/gta/how-join-nopixel-gta-5-rp-server-a-step-by-step-guide ; https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-updates-lottery-empyreum-patch-6-1 ; https://www.habbo.com/community/article/22292/update-state-habbo-economy ; https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/csm-7-the-results).

So the design question is not "what will people do here?" but "what does a world need to contain so that a community of forty people can give two thousand strangers something to do, every week, for ten years, and survive its founder?" My answer is eleven named mechanisms: Charters, Posts, the Landing cohort, the Civic Calendar, Wards and Commons, Hearths, Estates, Standing, the Assembly and World Charter, the Commons Pool, and the Chronicle, with disclosed AI Keepers as a capped stopgap. Graphics and architecture are designed as servants of social density: every technical budget in this document exists so that a hundred people can stand in one room on a mid-range laptop and see each other.

The audience is sized honestly. The taxonomy's cheapest hypothesis is that the market for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out is roughly 0.5–1M people and SL already serves most of it. nolife is therefore built to be solvent at Linden scale (600K MAU) and to survive at 100K (R30), and its growth thesis is that institutions, not features, expand that ceiling: a world where joining is joining *something* converts a larger share of the people who try it, and a world that migrates whole communities rather than individuals starts with density instead of hoping for it.

## 2. Who comes first and why

The wedge is institutions, not users. The census brief is explicit that whole communities move on institutional continuity (group, roles, ban list, land, calendar, treasury) and a credible persistence guarantee, and that each durable SL community has a single decision-maker who can bring hundreds to thousands (census section 5). nolife's go-to-market is a **Founding Charters programme**: fifty named communities, recruited leader by leader, each given a Hall, a Charter import, a year of rent and a seat at the first Assembly.

The first thirty are chosen for three properties: they already run a calendar, they already have roles, and they are under-served by every live platform. In order:

1. **Live music and venues.** Cafe Musique-class venues with weekly schedules, host and DJ roles and tip jars; a venue is "a stream URL, a host and a DJ role, a tip jar" and loses its audience if any one is missing (census section 2). Day one they get parcel-level audio streams, Posts for host and DJ with shift calendars, Coin tip splitting with charity splits, and a Stage slot on a Ward calendar. They come because a venue is the purest purpose engine and SL still makes them run it on Discord.
2. **Roleplay estates.** 1920s Berlin, the Crack Den (three sims, text-only), Gorean cities, the SL Military Community: communities built on access and ban lists, roles as ranks, HUD meters and dress codes (Source: https://thecrackden.com/roleplay/ ; https://www.theregister.com/2007/04/18/role_play_in_sl/?page=4). Day one they get Estates: seat-capped, whitelisted realms with roles-as-ranks, a native meter API, damage-enabled parcels and paid queue priority under published rules modelled on Rockstar's FiveM terms (cosmetics and priority yes; pay-to-win and loot boxes no) (R17; Source: https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/fivem/server-monetisation/). They come because GTA RP proved the demand (221,062 Steam peak for FiveM in May 2026) and SL never built the tools (Source: https://steamcharts.com/app/2676230).
3. **Disability and care communities.** Virtual Ability (1,000–1,300 members, own estate, Cape Able for deaf residents) and the text-first congregations where worship "occurs in text" (Source: https://virtualability.org/history/ ; https://www.uua.org/node/31164). Day one they get the first-party text client (section 9), text parity for every voice feature, mentors-by-appointment as a Post type, and a nonprofit rent tier. They come because the volunteer client they depend on, Radegast, has had no release since 2022 (Source: https://applevis.com/comment/73862).
4. **Educators.** VWBPE's committee and the institutions that left SL when the 2010 discount removal doubled their costs; the 2014 study found cost and persistence decisive (Source: https://hypergridbusiness.com/?p=35085 ; https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/49578/). Day one: institutional SSO, bulk accounts, class Halls under US$50/month with archive export and a written persistence covenant (R28).
5. **Charity and festival organisers.** Fantasy Faire (US$358,000 raised by 2019), Second Pride, BURN2: temporary multi-region builds with a published seasonal calendar (Source: https://modemworld.me/2025/04/17/fantasy-faire-2025-your-almost-complete-unofficial-guide/ ; https://secondlife.com/destination/second-pride). Day one they get the Ephemeral Ward mechanism (section 3) at platform cost.
6. **Adult and romantic roleplay communities**, measured and first-class from day one (R22), in Night Wards gated by an Ofcom-grade check. The adult share of SL is unmeasured but plausibly its largest resident-built economy (18% of land "mature" in 2008; 13.6% of residents regularly having virtual sex) (Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/book-review-the-making-of-second-life). They come because no platform with a real economy offers a legal, gated home: VRChat verifies 18+ and bans pornography platform-wide, and SL's gate is still a self-declared birthday (Source: https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Verification ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/533810-age-verification-in-2026/).
7. **Non-English blocs**: Brazilian and Spanish-speaking communities (Brazil 7.0% and Colombia 5.6% of SL web traffic), the OpenSim grids that are single-language communities, and Japan's VRM avatar culture (Source: https://zh.semrush.com/website/secondlife.com/overview). Day one: language-tagged Wards with their own discovery, paid localisation in Portuguese, Spanish, Japanese and German, and VRM import (R29).

**Migration mechanics.** A Charter import creates group, roles, opt-in member list, ban list, maturity rating, calendar and land allocation in one transaction with the leader as owner (R18). OAR/IAR import brings builds and inventories from OpenSim and from SL via creator-only export, honouring the Export permission bit the Firestorm team proposed in 2012 and SL never adopted (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2012/09/hypergrid-permissions-need-viewer-change/). A 12–24-month **bridge** relays group chat and calendar events between a Charter's SL group and its nolife Charter so venues keep their audience while building a new one. Individuals follow institutions: a member who imports arrives into their Charter's Hall with their role, not onto an empty island.

The second wave (months 12–30) is the Spatial and Sansar diaspora, whose creator worlds were deleted on 27 July 2026 or went dark without explanation, and the OpenSim hypergrid, whose ~45–49K actives already know how to pack an archive and have lost content to OSgrid's 2025 wipe and 2026 outage (Source: https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/ ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2025/02/osgrid-wiping-its-database-on-march-21-you-have-five-weeks-to-save-your-stuff ; https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686). The third wave is adults aging out of youth platforms, where 18+ DAU grew 32% year on year and Roblox now pays a 42% bonus on verified-adult spend (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42).

## 3. The purpose engine

The purpose engine is the product. Everything below is a first-class object with an API, a UI in the consumer client, and a published rule.

**Charters.** A Charter is an institution: a unique, permanent name; 2–20 roles with abilities; a ban list; a fixed maturity rating; a calendar; a treasury in Coin; land (a Hall) and objects; and, mandatorily for any Charter owning land or a calendar, a named deputy and a succession rule (R15). SL's group system is the proven substrate (two to ten roles, ban list of 500, rating fixed at creation) and the census shows it is the common denominator of every durable community (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Group). nolife upgrades it with the three things SL lacked: a treasury that can pay stipends, a calendar that is part of the world's calendar, and succession so a Charter survives its founder as the Cathedral did and most venues did not. Charters can federate (a Ward council is itself a Charter) and can be chartered as Estates (section 4).

**Posts.** A Post is a role with slots, a shift calendar, a visible badge, an optional stipend, and a deputy. The platform ships a vocabulary (Host, Mentor, DJ, Warden, Builder, Chronicler, Teacher, Healer, Quartermaster, Juror) and Charters define their own. Posts are scarce by rule: a Charter may open one Post slot per eight active members, a Ward has a fixed number of Warden and Host slots. Scarcity is what makes holding a Post mean something; the community-purpose brief's third pattern is that "people stay where they are needed", and a Post is a formal statement that someone is needed at 20:00 on Thursdays. Stipends come from Charter treasuries or, for public Posts (Landing Hosts, Ward Wardens), from the Commons Pool (section 5). Posts held are recorded in the Chronicle and feed Standing.

**The Landing.** First hour equals first group (R11). Every new account lands in a Landing Hall with a cohort of 12–20 people who arrived in the same 30-minute window and a human Host on shift, paid from the Pool. The first session has a fixed shape: dressed avatar and furnished Hearth in the first ten minutes (R26); a walk with the Host to a live event starting within 30 minutes on the Civic Calendar; the Call Board, where Charters recruiting this week post open Posts and apprenticeships; and three concrete invitations before logout. Success is "met a person or joined a Charter", measured against a randomised holdout that gets the unhosted island, with D30 published at month six and the mechanism kept only if the lift beats the industry D30 base (R11). This is the first time the group-retention hypothesis that Bungie, Square Enix and Linden hold privately will be tested in public; the Destiny clan data is correlational only (Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952117301350).

**The Civic Calendar.** Purpose needs a shared "when" (winners' lessons pattern 6). The calendar has three layers. The *weekly rhythm* is platform-fixed and identical in every Ward in local time: Market Day (vendors pay no commission on primary sales that day), Open Stage (performer slots by lottery weighted by prior audience retention), Assembly Day (Ward council sits, Chronicle is read), and a Quiet Day with no first-party events so Charters own the slot. The *seasonal layer* belongs to Charters: Fantasy Faire, Pride, the Burn, the Mental Health Symposium, each with a Chronicle entry and a reserved Ward slot. The *annual ritual* is the **Ephemeral Ward**: a region that exists for one week a year, is built by Charters in the preceding month, hosts the year's largest synchronised event in ~100-avatar instances with cross-instance chat, mints that year's relics (never reissued, 0% resale fee), and is then erased on camera. BURN2 has burned the Man since 2003 and Habbo's economy ran on rares never re-released; the Ephemeral Ward is both (Source: https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/). Capture and share tools are built in because rituals spread through screenshots and clips, as the Animal Crossing graduations did (Source: https://www.newsweek.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-teacher-help-students-graduate-pandemic-1498760).

**Scarcity.** The world is deliberately short of four things: public land, prime calendar time, Posts, and relics. Private land is abundant (section 4). Scarcity is published policy so the economy can plan, and it is never sold: lottery tickets are refundable, Posts cannot be bought, Standing cannot be transferred. The FFXIV lesson is that a plot you can lose becomes a status object and spawns a venue scene nobody planned (Source: https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/ffxiv-house-demolition-suspension-reinstated-temporarily).

**Standing.** Two ladders, both behaviour-earned and non-transferable. *Trust* is the VRChat model: Visitor, New, Member, Known, Trusted, with a visible Nuisance flag; it gates upload, scripting, voice and proximity rights and cannot be raised by idling or mass-friending (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system). *Standing* is civic: earned by Posts held, shifts worked, events hosted, members retained, lottery plots used, and jury duty served; it weights calendar lotteries, qualifies candidates for councils and the Assembly, and is displayed as heraldry on the avatar's profile. Standing decays slowly with absence (a year to lose a rank) so that institutions turn over.

**Governance** (detailed in section 9): Charters rule their Halls; elected Ward councils write ordinances; an elected Assembly advises the operator under a World Charter with a Never List.

**The Chronicle.** A public, append-only record of the world: Charters founded and dissolved, Posts held, elections and turnout, rituals and their relics, ordinances, and the operator's own decisions and Assembly answers. Belonging needs memory; the Chronicle is where a ten-year resident's history is legible to a newcomer. Disclosed AI Chroniclers draft entries; a human Chronicler Post signs them.

**The first hour.** You arrive dressed, in a room with fifteen other arrivals and a Host who says your name. You pick a Hearth style and furnish it from a free kit. The Host walks the cohort to an Open Stage set that started six minutes ago; forty people are there because density routing sent every Landing in your time zone to it (R12). You see the Call Board: a jazz venue needs a Thursday host, a Gorean city is taking apprentices, a Portuguese-speaking Ward opens a council seat next month. You accept one invitation, the Charter's deputy greets you in Charter chat, and your Chronicle page has its first line.

**The first year.** Month 1: apprentice in a Charter, mentored by a Post-holder. Month 3: first Post, with a shift on the calendar and a badge. Month 6: Trust rank Known unlocks scripting; first Commons lottery ticket. Month 9: deputy of a Post, which is the succession mechanism working in miniature. Month 12: eligible to stand for a Ward council; first Ephemeral Ward; a relic in inventory that nobody can make again. By year three the resident is a Steward of a Charter with a treasury, a calendar slot and a deputy chain, and the question "what do I do here?" has become "who is on shift tonight?"

## 4. World structure

nolife is sized to its population, not its ambition. SL's grid is ~27,000 regions for ~32,000 median concurrency, 1.2 avatars per region, "so beautiful, so empty" (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/ ; https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/526582-so-beautiful-so-empty/). The structure has five place types.

**Hearths** are private homes: instanced sanctuaries with a content budget rather than a land parcel. Every account gets a free Hearth within a trial allowance (one room, 8 guests, a fixed asset budget); a paid Hearth (US$4.99/month) expands budget and guests. Hearths are rented under R6 and never auto-demolished; a lapsed Hearth is frozen and restorable for 24 months.

**Halls** are Charter land, priced by area and content budget, from US$25/month for a class-sized Hall to US$199/month for a full Hall at Reference-tier budgets, with a nonprofit tier at half price. A Hall's rent buys persistence; its traffic costs nothing extra. Halls are where most of the world's life happens, as private estates were in SL.

**Wards** are public districts, each language-tagged and opened only when 2,000 MAU have attached to it, and returned to fallow (archived, restorable) when attachment falls below 500 for 90 days. A Ward contains a Commons of 64 scarce plots allocated by lottery with a full-price refundable ticket (US$20) and a 45-day use-it-or-lose-it rule with an 80% refund and a published pause policy for outages and emergencies (R16); a Stage with a 168-hour calendar whose 40 prime hours go by quarterly lottery; a Market; and a Green where the Ward council sits. Density SLO: one avatar per 0.1 hectare at peak in public areas, roughly forty times SL's grid-wide average; thin instances merge; newcomers route to live events; the median number of people visible in a newcomer's first session must exceed ten (R12).

**Estates** are Charter-owned whitelisted realms: seat-capped (32–200), with application forms, roles as ranks, damage-enabled parcels and queue priority sold under published rules (R17). Estates may be General, Moderate or Adult; Adult Estates live in Night Wards.

**The Ephemeral Ward** exists for one week a year (section 3).

**Persistence** is charged from day one as rent (R6); nothing hosted is free beyond the Hearth allowance, because every platform that hosted open worlds free of charge capped, charged or shut down (Spatial, Dreams, Sansar; alt-survivors pattern 2). **Discovery** is calendar-first: the default view of the world is "what is happening in the next two hours near my Charters and language", then the Call Board, then places. Place listings rank by distinct returning visitors, never by payment. **Empty-world avoidance** is structural: public land only opens with attached population, newcomers only land in hosted cohorts, events are synchronised ~100-avatar instances with a presence layer of up to 1,000 (R1), and a Keeper floor (section 8) fills a public place only until humans arrive.

## 5. Creation and creator economy

**Tools.** Two interfaces over one world (R26): a consumer client (dress, furnish, attend, hold a Post) and a creator client (build, script, publish). Creation happens in-world first, the lesson of Resonite's maker culture and Dreams' output against every platform that made Unity the real editor and never grew a creator economy (alt-survivors implication 2). Import is glTF 2.0 with PBR extensions and VRM 1.0 for humanoids, OpenUSD for scenes, with a published compatibility policy: conversion is platform-funded and any format retirement needs a converter and 24 months' notice (R3). Scripting is sandboxed Luau with per-owner CPU, memory and network budgets; the worst offender is throttled, not the cell (R5). Every asset carries a per-tier budget with auto-generated fallbacks (section 6).

**Ownership.** Creators own what they make. The content licence is narrowly scoped to operating the service, with a defined affirmative action for any re-use, because the 2013 ToS made Renderosity and CG Textures ban their assets from SL for a year (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms). Assets carry signed provenance and a creator-set Export bit honoured by every client. Resale is permitted on transferable items at 0% platform fee.

**Currency.** One closed currency, **Coin**, fixed at 100 Coin = US$1.00, displayed as fiat at every price, purchasable in exact amounts, no bundles, no second currency, no chance mechanics paying anything cash-out-able (R9). This is the posture the CPC's March 2025 Key Principles reward and BEUC's complaint punishes (Source: https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr). Stipends to Citizens (section 11) are displayed with fiat value. Coin is transferable between users (tips, stipends, trades) but never off-platform except through cash-out.

**Fee stack, published on one page beside the platform's required margin (R7):** buy Coin on web or PC at 0% (the platform absorbs ~3% processing); app-store top-ups show the store's cut; commission 10% on primary sales in the Marketplace and in-world vendors, 0% on Market Day, 0% on resale; cash-out 2.5% with a US$1 minimum. All-in round trip on a primary sale cashed out: 12.5%, against SL's community-computed 20–31% (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/). Effective take is audited annually against the published figure.

**The Commons Pool (R8).** 25% of platform net revenue is distributed monthly: 70% to creators weighted by distinct returning purchasing users of their goods (purchaser-only weighting is Epic's November 2025 anti-farming fix) (Source: https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items); 20% to Charters and venues weighted by distinct returning members and attended calendar events; 10% to public Post stipends (Landing Hosts, Wardens, Jurors). The formula is published; the pool is a share of platform revenue, not Epic's 40% of gross spend, because the taxonomy's reconciliation shows a Linden-scale world cannot afford more (taxonomy section 5).

**Cash-out (R10).** A licensed, partnered money-services rail settles Coin under KYC; W-9/W-8 and the DAC7 data set (name, address, date of birth, TIN, payout account) are collected at payout onboarding, before the first payout, because services have no DAC7 floor (Source: https://www.fonoa.com/blog/dac7overview ; https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000). The consumer-facing purchase is coded as platform currency, never an adult MCC, which is plausibly why SL and Tilia were not named in the July 2025 processor campaign that hit Steam, itch.io and DLsite (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story). An adult-specialist acquirer is pre-qualified in reserve (R22).

**Portability.** glTF/VRM export of own-created inventory at any time; OAR-equivalent export of any Hall; off-platform marketplaces (BOOTH, Gumroad, Kitely Market) may sell directly into nolife, since VRChat-Japan grew on BOOTH's ~10.4bn-yen avatar trade without paying for it (Source: https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/02/post-2303955.html).

**Moderation of UGC.** Every upload passes automated review (geometry, texture, script static analysis, likeness checks); Trust rank gates what can be published and where; Charter Wardens are the first line inside Halls; Adult-tagged goods need uploader verification, consent records and pre-publication review (Mastercard AN 5196 and Visa VIRP requirements) and can be flipped to deindex-but-sell in hours, the itch.io model that preserved creator income (Source: https://verifymy.io/resources/regulation/card-brand-requirements ; https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/07/24/itch-io-delists-all-adult-nsfw-content-due-to-pressure-from-payment-processors-and-collective-shout/). Value-bearing scripted objects (breedables, meters, vendor HUDs) run their life-support on platform-hosted escrow with a designed end-of-life conversion to a food-independent state, so no bunny hibernates when a creditor pulls a server (R24; Source: https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html).

## 6. Graphics and client strategy

Fidelity is a budgeting problem before it is a rendering problem (graphics brief pattern 1), and in nolife every budget serves one number: a hundred avatars in one room at 60 fps on the hardware the audience owns. The modal Steam GPU in September 2026 is an RTX 5070, with 5060, 4060 and 3060 close behind; half of SL's users lack high-end machines; 1.77% of Steam users own a headset (Source: https://mixed-news.com/en/steam-september-2026-hardware-survey-rtx-5070-32gb-ram/ ; https://modemworld.me/2025/01/02/ ; https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/).

**Three-tier fidelity contract (R2), enforced server-side.**

- *Reference*: Unreal Engine 5.8 feature set (Nanite, Lumen, MegaLights production-ready, Substrate, MetaHuman Collections for crowds), DLSS/FSR/XeSS, optional path tracing on 4080-class and above. Target RTX 4060/5060 at 1440p upscaled from 1080p, 60 fps. Frame budget 16.7 ms: 6 ms for 100 visible avatars, 5 ms scene, 3 ms lighting, 2.7 ms post and UI (Source: https://www.unrealengine.com/news/unreal-engine-5-8-is-now-available).
- *Lite*: Lumen Lite-class GI, probe lighting, SSR. Target Switch 2, Quest 3, M-series Mac, mid Android, and the WebGPU browser client. 16.7 ms at 1080p with 100-avatar presence (Source: https://www.guru3d.com/story/unreal-engine-58-debuts-lumen-lite-and-productionready-megalights/).
- *Compat*: WebGL2 and low Android, no dynamic GI, 200–500 m draw distance, 33 ms at 30 fps, avatars capped at VRChat's Quest threshold (Source: https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202115).

Avatar budgets: Reference 150K triangles, 8 materials, 2K textures; Lite 40K, 4 materials, 1K; Compat 20K, 2 materials, 512. Hall budgets scale with rent tier. Over-budget content is impostored, down-converted or hidden, never rendered twice as SL's mirrors are (Source: https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Mirrors).

**Engine.** UE 5.8 for the native Reference and Lite clients, chosen because the 2026 frontier is "lite" variants that bring the UE5 look to Switch 2 and mid-range PCs, and because MetaHuman licensing opened in June 2025 to any engine (Source: https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/). Content is engine-neutral (glTF, VRM, OpenUSD) so the browser client (Three.js or Babylon.js on WebGPU with a WebGL2 fallback, which Linux Firefox and Samsung Exynos phones still need) serves Compat and zero-install visiting, and so the engine's exit cost is bounded and documented (R20; Source: https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status). Desktop and web ship at launch, native mobile within 12 months, VR as an optional view never required for identity or inventory (R14).

**Devices and streaming.** Pixel streaming costs ~US$1.75 per streamed hour against a consumer ceiling of US$19.99/month with 100-hour caps, and Linden closed Project Zero after 14 months, so streaming is a time-capped funnel only: ten free hours per new account, cost per retained user published (Source: https://thegabmeister.com/p/unreal-pixel-stream-aws/ ; https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-blackwell-architecture-comes-to-geforce-now ; https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/).

**How UGC stays beautiful.** Budgets do most of it. Three social mechanisms do the rest. Wards own lighting: time of day, sky and weather are Ward-level so a street reads as one place. Charters may publish a *building code* for their Hall or Estate (palette, era, height, materials) that the creator client checks on placement, the way the 1920s Berlin Project enforces period dress by rule. And the first-party avatar body is open-licensed and MetaHuman-compatible (R26), so migrants are not naked and every tier has a good default. Gaussian splat capture of real venues is a Phase 2 backdrop format with proxy collision and a 500K-splat mobile cap; neural texture compression is a 2027 upgrade when the DirectX preview leaves "do not ship" status (graphics brief sections 2–3).

## 7. Architecture

The unit of simulation is the unit of everything (networking brief pattern 1); SL's one-process-per-region fixed caps, pricing and lag together and the AWS move changed none of it (Source: https://modemworld.me/2020/11/19/ll-confirms-second-life-regions-now-all-on-aws/).

**Two-layer world model (R1).** A *presence layer* carries position, pose, voice and chat at 5–10 Hz through interest-managed relays and an SFU, and can show 500–1,000 avatars in a contiguous place; a *simulation layer* (physics, scripts, vehicles, meters) is sharded into elastic cells of 50–150 interacting avatars over an authoritative entity store, so a cell crash is a reconnect, not a lost place. Published SLOs: 100–150 per cell with voice, 500–1,000 per place, 2,000–5,000 only in a declared event mode with impostor avatars and pre-mixed audio. Nobody is promised 10,000; Improbable's observed peaks are 4,144 and 4,500 at scripted events, Star Citizen's shards are ~500 after a decade, and EVE's 6,739 needed time dilation, which a voice world cannot use (Source: https://starcitizen.tools/Comm-Link:Letter_from_the_Chairman_-_2026-08-27 ; https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/the-second-timer-in-m2-xfe).

**Persistence** is documents plus an event log with per-cell and per-creator write budgets on the Roblox DataStore pattern (60 + 10 × players requests per minute per server), snapshots and rollback for Hall owners, and nightly escrow of server images and the world database to a second provider (Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits).

**Multi-vendor by construction (R20).** Cells are containers with one deployment manifest that runs on Edgegap, Amazon GameLift Servers, Nitrado GameFabric or bare metal; voice sits behind an abstraction that can swap ODIN, the Discord Social SDK or a self-hosted SFU. Vendor risk is now the top infrastructure risk: Hathora was acquired and wound down inside two months in 2026, and Firestorm Zero died when its streaming provider forced a migration (Source: https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174 ; https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/). Self-hostable cells (hypergrid-style) with escrowed images are both the educators' requirement and the sunset guarantee; an annual restore test and a failover drill are published.

**Cost per CCU.** Compute has converged at US$0.055–0.069 per vCPU-hour; a 2–4 vCPU cell for 100 avatars costs US$0.14–0.28/hour; with 20–50 kbps per client, that is US$0.002–0.005 per avatar-hour before voice (ODIN at EUR 0.29 per peak concurrent user per month, about EUR 0.01/hour for a 30-hour user) and persistence (Source: https://edgegap.com/pricing ; https://aws.amazon.com/gamelift/servers/pricing/ ; https://odin.4players.io/pricing). A 10,000-CCU world runs at roughly US$50–100/hour of servers, US$0.5–1M a year. Trust and safety dwarfs it: Roblox's infrastructure plus T&S line was US$1,153M in 2025, about US$0.92 per DAU per month at its automation level; nolife budgets US$1.5–3 per DAU per month (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm).

**Scaling plan.** Closed beta at 2,000 CCU (50 Charters); launch at 5,000 CCU with a 150-avatar cell load test with voice passed; 25,000 CCU by month 24; 60,000 CCU (above SL's 2026 peak of 48,802) by month 48, each step gated by p95 cell frame time within SLO at monthly peak (Source: https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/05/11/second-life-daily-user-concurrency-update-10th-may-2026/).

## 8. AI and the living world

The name is the rule: AI never counts as life. The evidence is that a-life made agents interesting and never made a place necessary (Creatures, Black & White, Spore), that Animal Crossing's dumb villagers on a real clock delivered "place" and "return" better than any learning agent, and that nothing in the corpus shows agent-filled density retaining humans (taxonomy section 3). Engagement with AI characters is deep but solitary and legally exposed: Character.AI banned open-ended chat for minors from November 2025 and settled a wrongful-death case in January 2026; 63% of Quantic Foundry's respondents chose the most negative option on generative AI (Source: https://abc7news.com/post/characterai-is-banning-minors-interacting-chatbots/18090030/ ; https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/).

**Keepers (R25).** Every AI inhabitant is a Keeper: badged, named, with a plain-language notice of its purpose and on whose behalf it acts, as EU AI Act Article 50 requires from 2 August 2026 (Source: https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content). Keepers do four jobs. A *Greeter* holds a Landing when no human Host is on shift, and hands over the moment one arrives. A *Chronicler* drafts Chronicle entries from event logs for a human Chronicler to sign. A *Shopkeeper* tends an absent owner's vendor stall, optionally as an opt-in, interview-seeded twin of the owner with full logs, following the 1,052-person study's finding that interview-seeded agents reproduce their subjects' answers at 85% of the subjects' own test-retest consistency (Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1). A *Director*, RimWorld-style, paces a Ward's ambient events (a market stall opens, a storm comes) but never runs an event; humans hold Posts.

**Guardrails.** Keepers are capped at 20% of visible occupants in any public place and 0% in Estates unless the Charter enables them; they withdraw as humans arrive; they cannot hold Posts, vote, own land, earn Coin, hold Standing, or count toward any density SLO or published metric. No companion or romantic loops for minors; crisis routing on distress signals. Voices are licensed or synthetic with provenance; no replica of a real performer without bargained consent (the Fortnite Darth Vader charge is unresolved) (Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/actors-union-files-labor-charge-over-ai-darth-vader-in-fortnite). The Keeper programme is kept only if human D30 in Keeper-filled places is at least equal to comparable human-only places; otherwise the cap is lowered.

**Costs.** Cognition is tiered and the cost per agent-hour published: scripted routines for 90% of Keeper-hours at near zero; small on-device or edge models for ambient talk at roughly US$0.01 per agent-hour; frontier calls only for memorable interactions, budgeted at US$0.05. Blended target: at most US$0.02 per agent-hour and agent spend capped at 10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget (R25). AI as *tooling* is unrestricted where it serves humans: moderation triage, chat translation with its retention effect measured (R29), Cube-style mesh generation inside asset budgets, and accessibility (speech-to-text, text-to-speech, voiced GPS).

## 9. Identity, safety, governance, regulation

**Identity.** Pseudonymous public identity (username and changeable display name, as SL has had since 2010) bound to a private age band from on-device facial estimation or a wallet credential; no ID images are ever stored by the platform, because the breach surface is the human appeals vendor, as Discord's 70,000 leaked IDs showed (Source: https://cyberinsider.com/discord-confirms-70000-government-ids-exposed-in-third-party-breach/). nolife is 18+ by default. A 13–17 Scholar band exists only inside Education-tier Halls and General Wards, private by default, adjacent-band chat only, non-spending without consent, geolocation and engagement notifications off (DSA Article 28 guidelines) (Source: https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-longawaited-eu-guidelines-on-article-281-dsa-what-online-platforms-must-know). Under-13s are not admitted. **Age assurance is a regional module (R21)** with per-jurisdiction thresholds and proofs: UK (Ofcom "highly effective"), EU (wallet credential), Brazil's ECA Digital (no self-declaration, loot-box-free mode), Indonesia's PP Tunas (16, communication gated on verification), Texas and California app-store signals consumed in the native clients (Source: https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/ ; https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-entrada-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026/ ; https://avpassociation.com/indonesia/). nolife frames its primary purpose as creation and play, with Australia's "primary purpose" test in view (Source: https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs). Night Wards and Adult-tagged commerce are gated by an Ofcom-grade check that also unlocks the adult sub-wallet (R22).

**Safety primitives (R23), always on.** Personal boundary default-on and user-adjustable (Meta's 2022 precedent); one-gesture mute, block and vanish; instance kick and lock for any Post-holder with Warden ability; a rolling evidence buffer attached to every report, because UK police concluded in 2024 that an embodied assault was not viable to charge and the platform is the only effective layer (Source: https://ispr.info/2022/02/08/meta-establishes-four-foot-personal-boundary-to-deter-vr-groping/ ; https://lbc.co.uk/news/police-investigate-rape-metaverse). Consent mechanics for avatar-to-avatar actions follow Bloodlines, the one SL vampire system that asked before biting (Source: https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/415080-what-is-bloodlines/). KPIs published quarterly: reports per 1,000 user-hours, median time-to-action (target under 24 hours), NCMEC reports, assurance completion rate; audit logs and response SLAs are built for Ofcom and DSC information requests, since every Ofcom fine so far has been paired with a second fine for failing to evidence compliance.

**Accessibility (R27).** A first-party text client, the Radegast successor, ships at launch: chat, IMs, notices, inventory, teleport, object list, voiced GPS, speech-to-text captions of voice and text-to-speech of chat (XAG 119), keyboard-only navigation, seated and motion-sickness options, with funded maintenance (Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/119). Storefront, account and support flows meet the European Accessibility Act.

**Community self-rule.** Three tiers, each with real but bounded power. *Charters* set rules, access and bans inside their Halls and Estates. *Ward councils* (five seats, elected annually by residents with Standing of Member or above and 90 days' tenure) write ordinances within the World Charter: maturity rating, building code, calendar priorities, Commons allocation rules. *The Assembly* (nine seats, elected annually, published turnout with EVE's 12–17% as the benchmark) is advisory: any published Assembly vote must be answered in writing by the operator within 30 days, and the Assembly can require a public change review of any client-affecting update (R19). No world has shipped a binding council and nolife does not pretend to; it ships the strongest advisory body on record and a constitution that constrains the operator.

**The World Charter** fixes: 12 months' notice and grandfathering for any persistence price rise; 12 months' notice before any shutdown with escrow release; a **Never List** (no cryptocurrency or NFTs, no loot boxes, no retroactive repricing, no sale of Standing or Posts, no undisclosed AI, no sibling product); and the published metrics cadence (R13): first-hour, D7, D30, D90, DAU/MAU, peak concurrency and bot share, with a documented "active" definition and an API that third parties like Grid Survey can reproduce within 5%. The Openspace repricing of 2008, the education discount removal of 2010 and the 2013 ToS all followed the same arc of decree, outcry and retreat (Source: https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/356/24/291220/1.html); the World Charter exists to make that arc impossible by blog post.

**Appeals.** Charter Warden decision, then a Ward tribunal of three jurors drawn by lot from Standing-holders (72-hour SLA, the CDS's Scientific Council as the precedent), then the platform Trust desk (7-day SLA, outcomes published in aggregate). The platform retains sole and immediate authority over legal and child-safety categories. Jury service earns Standing.

## 10. Legacy support

The rule for legacy is the taxonomy's rule for moat versus debt: carry identity, inventory and social graph (the moat), budget and convert content (the debt) (taxonomy 1.9).

**Second Life.** *Imported:* Charters (group, roles, members by opt-in, ban list, rating, calendar) in one transaction; own-created inventory via creator-only export honouring the Export bit; builds via an OAR-compatible archive reader with prims and sculpts converted to glTF and auto-LOD'd to tier budgets; LSL scripts through a transpiler for the common idioms (touch, chat, sit, vendor, meter) and a compatibility runtime for a documented subset, with the rest flagged for rewrite. *Re-made:* avatars, because SL looks are third-party no-mod mesh bodies and the rights, not the formats, are the barrier (legacy brief pattern 2); nolife ships an open-licensed first-party body and a vendor opt-in programme so Maitreya-class makers can license tier-budgeted ports. HUD meters are re-made on the native meter API. *Bridged:* 12–24 months of group-chat and calendar relay (R18).

**OpenSimulator.** OAR and IAR import on day one, including script text; self-hostable cells with hypergrid-style travel so a single-language grid can join as a Ward; Kitely Market may deliver into nolife. The hypergrid's ~45–49K actives are the people most likely to migrate and most likely to have lost content before (Source: https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/).

**VRChat and VRM.** Native VRM 1.0 import with automatic tiering; BOOTH and other off-platform stores allowed; Trust ranks and performance ranks are familiar by design. Avatar-identity culture is what VRChat has and persistence is what it lacks; nolife offers one persistent self with portable inventory across Wards, which none of Roblox, Fortnite, FFXIV or Discord offers (winners' lessons implication 7).

**Resonite, NeosVR, Sansar, Spatial.** In-world creation and glTF/USD scene import; a migration offer to the Spatial diaspora whose files were deleted on 27 July 2026; a published charter against the founder-split failure (the Never List's crypto clause is written with NeosVR in mind) (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeosVR).

**Habbo and Club Penguin lineages.** The lesson from Habbo Origins and Club Penguin Rewritten (11M users on a preserved 2005 client) is that communities inherit by preservation, not replacement (Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/cops-arrest-3-people-for-running-club-penguin-rewritten-beloved-by-millions/). nolife preserves institutions first and content second, and its Compat tier is deliberately humble enough that an old community's aesthetic is not a liability.

**What is not imported:** no-transfer third-party content without the creator's Export bit; L$ balances (no currency bridge); SL Standing or age status.

## 11. Business model and go-to-market

**Money flows.** Rent is load-bearing (R6). The taxonomy's reconciliation shows a sub-10% take with free persistence covers nothing at Linden scale, while rent plus subscriptions plus a modest take funds costs and a pool on a thin margin (taxonomy section 5). Revenue lines, in order of weight: Hall and Estate rent; Hearth rent; the **Citizen** subscription at US$9.99/month (stipend displayed in fiat, expanded storage and avatar features, priority on calendar lotteries but never on Commons lotteries); the 10% primary-sale commission and 2.5% cash-out fee; queue-priority and ticket revenue shares on Estates and events; the Education tier. Coin purchases on web and PC are the default rail; app stores are top-ups only, with store-routed money-in held under 25% (R7). The revenue mix is published annually.

**Unit economics at reference scale** (600K MAU, 150K DAU, US$100M/yr of new money in): Hall and Estate rent, 15,000 at an average US$80/month, US$14.4M; Hearths, 150,000 paid at an average US$3/month across tiers, US$5.4M; Citizens, 60,000 at US$120/year, US$7.2M; commission, 10% of ~US$80M primary sales, US$8M; cash-out, 2.5% of ~US$70M, US$1.75M. Total ~US$36.8M. Costs: 120 staff at US$150K loaded, US$18M; T&S plus infrastructure at US$1.5–3 per DAU per month, US$2.7–5.4M; payment processing, US$3M; Commons Pool at 25% of net, ~US$8M. Result: roughly break-even to US$2M positive. The pricing is in range of what SL's 17,600 private estates pay today (US$199–209/month for a full region) and what SL's Premium costs (US$12.99/month from July 2026) (Source: https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/).

**Survival configuration (R30)** at 100K MAU, 25K DAU: 45 staff (US$6.75M), T&S and infrastructure (US$0.75M), payments (US$0.5M), against ~US$8M of revenue (4,000 Halls, 25,000 paid Hearths, 12,000 Citizens, ~US$1.5M of take). Fixed costs are sized to this floor; growth features are funded only after rent and subscriptions cover costs; the headcount cap is 150; there is no sibling product.

**Funding.** Published runway plan: US$35–45M to reach public launch and month 36, when operating costs must be covered by recurring revenue. Sources in preference order: founder and mission capital with covenants mirroring the World Charter, a community-funded tier (lifetime Citizen at US$899, capped at 10,000 units, with the money escrowed against the shutdown clause), and conventional venture only with the subsidy-collapse pattern written into the term sheet: no shutdown without 12 months' notice and escrow release. Rec Room had runway to 2029 and shut down anyway because the cash loop did not close (Source: https://blog.recroom.com/posts/2025/8/28/rec-room-finances-and-ugc-revenue); nolife's loop closes on rent.

**Go-to-market.** Zero paid acquisition: at 1–2% payer rates, mobile CPIs of US$1–4 imply US$75–300 per payer, which no social world has recovered (Source: https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267). Growth comes from the Founding Charters programme (50 named leaders, each bringing a community and its calendar), the SL bridge, the OpenSim and Spatial diasporas, four language Wards with paid localisation and local rails (Pix, konbini, UPI, prepaid cards; Roblox's regional pricing lifted paying users 26% in Brazil and 52% in the Philippines) (Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/04/roblox-launches-regional-pricing-for-in-experience-items), the Education tier (20 accredited institutions by month 18), and the Ephemeral Ward as the annual shareable spike. Brands are sold outcomes (a Host, an event, measured attendance), never land; Wired's 2007 traffic scores of 27 for a brand pavilion are the memory (Source: https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56).

**Sequencing.** Rent and Charter tools before marketplace; marketplace and cash-out before public launch; mobile after the desktop and web economy is proven; Keepers last and conditionally.

## 14. Biggest risks and what you would cut first

**Risk 1: the audience is already served.** The taxonomy's unrefuted hypothesis is a ~0.5–1M market that SL mostly holds. nolife is a substitution bet that institutions convert better and migrate whole; if the Landing holdout shows no D30 lift, the thesis is weaker than the rent model beneath it. Mitigation: the holdout is published and the mechanism is cut if it fails (R11); the business is sized to survive at 100K MAU regardless.

**Risk 2: leader scarcity.** Charters need stewards, and founder dependence killed more SL venues than anything else. Mitigation: mandatory deputies, Standing that rewards succession, public Post stipends, and a target that 80% of venues survive a 90-day absence. If the deputy chain does not hold, nolife is SL with a better calendar.

**Risk 3: scarcity feels punitive.** FFXIV suspended its demolition timer repeatedly, and lottery losers may leave. Mitigation: abundant private land, refundable tickets, a published pause policy, and scarcity confined to public goods.

**Risk 4: the processor contingency.** Adult commerce under a general currency rail survived 2025 for SL, but Stripe prohibits adult content and Mastercard denies pressuring anyone while Valve says otherwise (Source: https://conductatlas.com/platform/stripe/stripe-restricted-businesses-list/adult-content-prohibition-and-restriction/). Mitigation: Tier-1-grade controls pre-emptively, a pre-qualified adult-specialist acquirer, the deindex switch; Tumblr's ~29% traffic loss is the cost of the blanket exit nolife will not take (Source: https://thenextweb.com/insider/2019/03/14/tumblr-traffic-dropped-by-nearly-100m-the-month-after-it-banned-porn/).

**Risk 5: engine and vendor dependency.** UE 5.8 is the last UE5 release and UE6 is reported for late 2027 with unverified terms; Hathora vanished in two months. Mitigation: engine-neutral content, a browser client that proves the content outlives the engine, two providers, escrow.

**Risk 6: hosting humans costs money.** Landing Hosts, Wardens and Jurors are paid from the Pool; at 1,500 new accounts a day that is roughly 150 hosted sessions a day, several hundred paid shifts a week. If the Pool cannot carry it, Landings thin out and the first hour reverts to an island.

**Risk 7: regulation moves faster than the module.** Australia's "primary purpose" list is not static; the Digital Fairness Act is unpublished; KOSA is pending. Mitigation: the regional module, fiat-pegged Coin, and a compliance posture that assumes self-declaration is worthless everywhere.

**What I would cut first, in order:** (1) the Reference tier's optional path tracing and splat capture, which serve screenshots, not density; (2) the VR view entirely, since 1.77% of the audience owns a headset and every VR-first world capped at a few hundred; (3) Keepers, whose only defensible job is the Greeter and whose cost ceiling is already 10%; (4) native mobile before the desktop and web economy is proven; (5) the second and third language Wards, keeping Portuguese; (6) the Education tier's SSO until month 24; (7) the Ephemeral Ward's scale, not its existence. I would not cut Charters, Posts, the Landing, the Civic Calendar, rent, the fee page, the World Charter or the text client. Those are the civilization engine; everything else is the building it runs in.

# Judge report: desirability and switching

Judge lens: would veteran Second Life and VRChat residents, creators and newcomers actually want this, and would they switch? Sections 1 (thesis / "why nolife"), 2 (who comes first), 3 (purpose engine and first hour), 5 (creator economy) and 10 (legacy) are weighted hardest. Designs that drift toward Roblox/Horizon (youth-mixed, platform-supplied purpose, AI-filled or empty plazas) or that would alienate the predecessor communities (landlords, fashion and photography creators, scripters, adult roleplayers, OpenSim grids, breeders) are penalised.

Inputs read in full: `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (R1-R30) and the six proposals in `design/`. No web search was used. Date of judgement: 2026-10-09.

## Score table

Criteria: (1) solves the root causes; (2) communities supply purpose; (3) graphics ambition appropriate to 2026 and reachable; (4) economics aligned with creators/communities and viable; (5) technical feasibility and architecture; (6) safety and regulatory soundness; (7) legacy support and originality.

Ranking: community-sociologist, platform-economist, studio-exec, engine-architect, open-web-architect, ai-world-designer.

All six proposals cover R1-R30 on paper and share a common skeleton (cells under a presence layer, three render tiers, rent plus subscriptions, 10% commission, a 25%-of-net pool, charter import, a text client, an Ofcom-grade Adult gate). None is a Roblox or Horizon clone. The differences that decide desirability are therefore: who each design makes feel at home, what reason it gives a twenty-year resident to pack up, and which existing livelihoods it quietly breaks.

## 1. community-sociologist: "a civilization engine, not a world" (57)

**Why nolife (s.1).** The strongest story of the six. It reads the insult and turns it into the product: the people who ran SL9B on donated regions, kept the Anglican Cathedral going past its founder's exit and have run CDS since 2004 are the "life", and the platform's job is to make their work cheap, legible and durable. A veteran recognises themselves in it. It is the only thesis that says out loud that the operator should not try to manufacture life, which is exactly the lesson of Sansar, Rec Room and Horizon.

**Who comes first (s.2).** A Founding Charters programme of 50 named communities, each given a Hall, a charter import, a year of rent and a seat at the first Assembly. Seven wedges in a deliberate order (venues, roleplay, disability and care, educators, festivals, adult, non-English), each with a concrete "day one they get" and "they come because". Adult roleplay is named as first-class and measured, not hidden. The Spatial/Sansar and OpenSim diasporas come as the second wave. Its blind spot: SL's shoppers, fashion and photography culture, the largest consumer economy, is not a named wedge; creators appear only through the pool.

**Purpose engine and first hour (s.3).** The best in the set, and original. Posts (roles with slots, shifts, badges, stipends and deputies), Standing as civic heraldry, a Call Board of Charters recruiting this week, a weekly rhythm with Market Day (0% commission), Open Stage slots weighted by audience retention, a Quiet Day left to Charters, and the Ephemeral Ward, which is built by Charters, mints never-reissued relics and is "erased on camera". The first-hour narrative ("a Host who says your name", forty people at an Open Stage, three invitations, a first Chronicle line) is the most believable picture of a newcomer actually wanting to come back. The first-year ladder runs from apprentice to Post to deputy to council and turns "what do I do here?" into "who is on shift tonight?".

**Creator economy (s.5).** Solid and honest: 0% buy-in on web, 10% primary, 0% resale, 0% on Market Day, 2.5% cash-out, a 12.5% round trip, an annual audit, and a Commons Pool split 70% creators, 20% Charters and venues, 10% public Post stipends. Weaker than the economist's on migration economics: there is no re-delivery bounty, and Hall rent (up to US$199) matches SL's, so landowners get no price reason to switch.

**Legacy (s.10).** Good on substance: charter import, OAR/IAR with the Export bit, an LSL transpiler plus a compatibility runtime for the common idioms (touch, chat, sit, vendor, meter), a vendor opt-in for Maitreya-class bodies, a 12-24-month bridge, and Habbo/Club Penguin read correctly as "preserve institutions first".

**Strengths.** The purpose engine, which no other proposal comes close to. 18+ by default with a 13-17 Scholar band confined to Education Halls. Juries drawn by lot from Standing-holders, with the CDS as precedent. A Never List that includes "no sale of Standing or Posts". Survival sizing at 100K MAU. A cut list that protects the civic core.

**Fatal flaws (would sink it if adopted as is).**
- Price parity with SL on Halls (US$199 full Hall) and no catalogue re-delivery give SL landowners and shoppers no concrete reason to move. The design depends on institutions moving and members following, and members who arrive without their looks and pay the same rent may drift back.
- Scarcity rules applied to volunteers: one Post slot per eight active members would choke staff-heavy institutions (Fantasy Faire, SL birthday events, large venues) and turns the volunteers who are the product into a rationed resource. This must be loosened before launch.
- Civic machinery (Standing, councils, juries, heraldry) may read as bureaucracy to SL's individualist residents (shoppers, photographers, solo builders) unless every layer is optional. As written, the newcomer path funnels everyone through Charters.

## 2. platform-economist: "the creator economy first" (55)

**Why nolife.** "Nobody is charged for being alive here": you pay rent on the bytes you keep and a published slice of what you sell, never for visitors or success. Less romantic than the sociologist's story, but it is the one SL creators will repeat in forums, because it names their grievance exactly: the 90/10 that is really 20-31%, the first millionaire who was a landlord rather than a maker, and fee creep since 2019.

**Who comes first.** Earning creators first: the 6,446 above US$1,000 and the 139 above US$100,000. Founding Merchant terms (5% commission for 12 months, platform-funded catalogue conversion, same-week cash-out) and a Re-delivery bounty. Then the named institutions with a venue kit and a rent holiday, OpenSim grids as Districts, the 2026 diaspora, and BOOTH/VRM "as a stage rather than a store" at 0% take. This is the most realistic two-sided cold start: supply that makers can bring, and demand that institutions can bring.

**Purpose engine.** Competent but derivative: roles as paid objects with venue-share stipends split by roster, scarcity copied from FFXIV, Habbo and GTA RP, and a calendar with "the Week". One clever addition: the Starter Catalogue, where a newcomer's first outfit is a creator's product that earns pool points, so onboarding feeds the economy. The first year is thin (a greeter badge, a purchase, a lottery ticket).

**Creator economy.** The best of the six by a clear margin. Splits up to ten contributors enforced at settlement. Component Royalties capped at 10%, which let a body maker become a platform for clothing makers without SL's no-mod lock-in, a direct answer to the mesh-body ecosystem. An open marketplace protocol at the same 10%, or 0% for off-platform-paid items. A Maker to Merchant to Studio ladder. Granular rent from a US$15 quarter cell to a US$59 cell, with a printed cost basis. Sub-letting allowed, with public granular rent so that a landlord's margin is a service margin; this preserves SL's estate-manager livelihood where other proposals ban it. A worked creator pitch (US$100K of sales nets ~US$87.5K against SL's US$69-80K), a four-scale P&L with sensitivity analysis, and a stored-value fallback if the DFA bans paid currencies.

**Legacy.** Charter import, OAR/IAR, an LSL-to-Luau transpiler with a flagged legacy mode, and SLua transfer. The Re-delivery Programme is the only proposal that solves "my look is no-transfer third-party mesh" economically: buyers prove prior purchase and the creator is paid 10 NC per claim from the pool's acquisition line. The bridge runs through residents' own accounts, with Discord as fallback. A Content Passport readable off-platform.

**Strengths.** The economics are aligned and viable (profitable at 300K MAU, survival at 100K on a reserve). The rent model penalises idle land and never penalises success. The creator-supply wedge and Re-delivery tackle the actual switching cost. The open-world-is-18+ / isolated-Campus split takes minors' commerce law out of the economy entirely.

**Fatal flaws.**
- The purpose engine is borrowed and the first year is thin. If the money mechanisms work but the civic layer does not, this becomes an excellent shop with an empty plaza, which is SL again on a fairer fee page.
- Pool payouts go 85% to *places* by returning purchasers. Marketplace-only fashion creators (a large share of SL's earners) have no "place" and may see little of the pool unless a store or brand page counts as a place. This needs explicit definition before launch.

## 3. studio-exec: "go-to-market and survival" (52)

**Why nolife.** "Those people are the only proven market." Three rules: no life (no quests, no undisclosed AI), no life-support (live on rent from month 36), no coin (a stored-value balance shown as money). The story is honest and investor-legible. For residents it is "price and rights, not graphics": a US$99 cell against US$349 plus US$199, a 12.5% round trip, an Export flag Linden never shipped. That is a strong switching pitch to landowners and creators but a weak dream; nothing here is new to imagine.

**Who comes first.** A sharp wave plan: 15-25 founding charters with founding-rate rent frozen for 36 months, OpenSim operators as self-hosting partners, then the OpenSim diaspora, then non-institution SL residents (with the adult community deliberately in the launch wave so there is no 2009-style forced migration), then refugees and Brazil/LatAm with paid Portuguese and Spanish community managers at launch. Teenagers, VR-first users, brands and crypto are explicitly excluded, the right call for this audience.

**Purpose engine.** Correct and measurable (roles with mandatory deputies, the Almanac, the Fairground, ward lottery, curated estates with Rockstar's rules, Arrivals with a holdout), but generic. The newcomer gets a free instanced Studio flat and three groups whose next event is within 48 hours.

**Creator economy.** Clean: The Stack fee page, 12.5% worst case, 0% tips, a Commons Fund with a published median, Keepalive for breedables, regional prices set by creators. "No coin" is a smart regulatory move; some veterans are attached to L$ culture, but few will mourn it.

**Legacy.** The strongest practical day-one script compatibility: LSL runs as-is on an LSL runtime built from OpenSim's BSD engine, under the same budgets, "so a venue's tip jars, HUD meters and vendors work on day one". There is a vendor programme at 0% for the first year, and a bridge with a Discord fallback if Linden blocks the relay.

**Strengths.** The leanest credible plan (US$33M, 45 staff, a cap of 60). An explicit kill criterion (fewer than 10 institutions by month 12 means shrink to a 25-person business). Sequencing is "rail and compliance before graphics, Reader before VR". The "active" definition requires interacting with a person. The design is 18+ in practice.

**Fatal flaws.**
- The scope is far too large for 45 people at launch. A UE5 two-tier client, a WebGPU client, Reader, a cell server, an LSL runtime, charter import, a licensed rail, an age module and localisation, with only 10 client engineers, risks shipping a mediocre client, and a mediocre client is the one thing SL veterans already have.
- It offers no original reason to dream. "SL with a modern client and fair fees" persuades landowners but not newcomers, and the cut list sacrifices the Compat tier, which reopens the hardware-floor debt that R2 exists to prevent.

## 4. engine-architect: "the immersion-and-fidelity angle" (51)

**Why nolife.** "No life-support": nothing depends on one vendor, engine, headset or food server. Fidelity and budgets are framed as one thesis. The launch claim, "your Second Life, lit", would land hard with SL's fashion and photography culture, whose Flickr output has been the platform's shop window for fifteen years.

**Who comes first.** The right supply wedge for a fidelity play: the top ~1,000 creators, especially mesh body and head vendors, through a Founders' Foundry (platform-funded conversion to PBR/Nanite, 0% take for 12 months, an Export bit honoured everywhere), then venues (MegaLights stage lighting), roleplay estates, OpenSim and educators, VRM creators and Virtual Ability.

**Purpose engine.** Thin and checklist-shaped. Roles are objects, and one nice touch carries a badge into the impostor so status survives distance; otherwise it lists the R15-R19 mechanisms without making them feel alive.

**Creator economy.** Good: 10% primary, 1.5% cash-out, rent much cheaper than SL (US$99 estate), and CopyBot defence by fingerprinting geometry and textures against the catalogue, the creator protection that SL residents have wanted since the 2006 strike. Free platform kit libraries raise the floor for amateurs.

**Graphics.** The best in the set and reachable: tiers A/A+/B/C/S with millisecond frame budgets and VRAM budgets per tier; the Foundry pipeline (Nanite build, a four-level LOD chain, octahedral impostors, KTX2, Substrate-to-PBR down-mapping, per-plot bakes) costed at US$10-20K per million assets; Blocks that compile prims to Nanite meshes; server-side Bakes-on-Mesh atlasing; and world-owned photo mode (farm renders of any public place).

**Legacy.** The most complete technical import: prims regenerated exactly (cut, hollow, twist), sculpts reconstructed, llmesh at highest LOD as the Nanite source, Bento's 133 bones mapped through a published table, particles mapped to Niagara, and an LSL runtime.

**Strengths.** It turns the one SL root cause with no counter-example (unbudgeted content) into a product feature. Headless C++/Rust cells keep Unreal off the server path, and the engine exit cost is written down (18-24 months, US$12-20M). Inventory integrity outranks any rendering feature.

**Fatal flaws.**
- It admits minors (six age bands, parental surfaces for ages 10-15) into a world with Adult districts, cash-out and console ports. This is the Roblox drift that SL's adult-majority community would read as betrayal, and it loads the regulatory surface (ECA Digital, PP Tunas, the UK OSA children's code) onto an economy that does not need it.
- Aesthetic rules imposed on residents: platform-owned lighting with no per-parcel sky, no full-bright, District minimum fidelity ranks, and "legacy content is re-made, not displayed as-is". SL photographers live on custom EEP/Windlight, clubs live on full-bright and emissive, and many residents love their old builds as they are. This risks alienating the very fashion and photography wedge it courts.
- A US$75-95M raise and console/Quest ports in Phase 2 expose it to the subsidy-collapse pattern (taxonomy 2.2 #1).

## 5. open-web-architect: "an open-protocol successor" (46)

**Why nolife.** "There is no Second Life": no single company whose ToS, funding or sale can end the world. A Foundation owns the specifications and an operator is bound by a charter. For OpenSim grids, educators and anyone burned by OSgrid, Sansar or Spatial, this is the most trustworthy promise in the set, and the triple reading of the name is clever. Its problem is the taxonomy's own natural experiment: OpenSim already offers portability, self-hosting and cheap land and has one-twelfth of SL's users. "Portability without an economy is a hobby" is the right diagnosis, but the federated cure inherits OpenSim's adoption profile.

**Who comes first.** OpenSim grids (Kitely, DigiWorldz, AviWorlds) as chartered shards, then named SL institutions with rebuild grants (12 months of rent plus 40 funded builder-hours), the closed-platform diaspora, VRM/BOOTH creators and educators. Well chosen for a protocol, and the hardest to grow beyond them.

**Purpose engine.** Standard, plus ActivityPub federation of Charter calendars so a venue's programme reaches the Fediverse at no cost. A cohort only every 30 minutes per language is too long a wait for a newcomer's first hour.

**Creator economy.** A 12% all-in fee, 0% for off-platform stores delivering through Vault, a US$0.25 minimum price and a 25% pool. Estates at US$39 for a full cell-equivalent are the cheapest land offer and a powerful switching lure. But the proposal concedes that any client can replay the asset stream and that protection is "legal and reputational, not technical". To SL creators, whose founding trauma is CopyBot, an open protocol reads as an invitation to rip.

**Legacy.** The best of the six for OpenSim and the most inventive for SL. NAR is a superset of OAR/IAR, so `load oar` works unchanged. Ferry's creator claim (a one-time code in the SL profile) moves a creator's reputation with them. A `nolife-bento` VRM extension keeps wing, tail and face bones. EEP settings export to USD skies. Hypergate lets Firestorm users see the federation. `rbx-compat` serves Luau migrants. A Body Vendor Programme redelivers against Marketplace order records.

**Strengths.** Governance that a burned community can believe (forkable code, escrow, a mixed-election board, a never-list). Crossing keeps Passport, Vault, Charters and block list across shards. A conformance suite.

**Fatal flaws.**
- Federated safety at regulatory grade is unsolved. Unchartered shards will host what the Charter forbids under the nolife protocol and name, and Ofcom/DSA regulators will hold someone responsible. The proposal accepts the headline "nolife" for bad content as residual risk.
- Commons shards admit 16+ in a world whose flagship operator also runs Adult districts and cash-out, which puts 16-17-year-olds next to the adult economy.
- An open asset stream with only legal protection will deter the professional SL creators whose catalogues make migrants want to come, and the economics (break-even near 300K MAU on US$39 estates) leave no buffer.

## 6. ai-world-designer: "a living world first" (45)

**Why nolife.** The most original and evocative concept: "So beautiful. So empty" answered with a world that runs on its own clock, where the baker opens at 03:00 and someone (disclosed) remembers that your crew finished the pier. As craft it is lovely. As a pitch to *this* audience it is the riskiest: the taxonomy reports 63% of social players choosing the most negative option on generative AI and finds nothing showing that agent-filled density retains humans, yet the headline promise here is artificial inhabitants.

**Who comes first.** A sensible five-institution alpha (OpenSim and educators, Virtual Ability, venues with a Keeper door host, roleplay estates with "living towns", event institutions), then furry and avatar communities, the adult economy and language shards.

**Purpose engine and first hour.** The Tide is the most charming first hour in the set: a boat every 20 minutes carrying 6-12 newcomers, a paid human tide-warden, and a shared ten-minute job (relighting the pier lanterns) that writes every name into the Harbour Chronicle. Crews of 3-12 with a hall and an Apprentice, then houses, then district assemblies, give a clear social ladder. The Human Primacy Charter is the most careful AI rulebook any proposal offers.

**Creator economy.** Standard fees (10% primary, 2.5% spread in each direction) and a pool that Keeper traffic can never earn from. Lineage is the standout: platform-hosted genetics and life-support for breedables, with a designed "perma" conversion and habitat caps against trait inflation. That speaks directly to the KittyCatS/Ozimals community, one of SL's real creator economies. The proposal also bans sub-letting at a markup, which ends SL's estate-landlord livelihood.

**Legacy.** Charter import, OAR/IAR, an LSL layer covering the common 80%, a vendor programme, port bounties for 200 venues, an SL bridge run by a scripted agent, and self-hosted cells for OpenSim. But the Archipelago removes contiguous mainland, and a district that stays under the floor for 14 days "goes to seed" and its plots are bundled back to their owners. Residents who settled there are evicted because it got quiet, the opposite of the persistence covenant veterans are moving for.

**Strengths.** The most original systems (the Almanac director with an assembly-set temperament, a living ecology, Lineage, Stand-ins that wear "the apron, not the face"). Disclosure and exclusion from metrics are thorough. Quiet districts can opt out of Keepers. Cognition costs are worked out per agent-hour.

**Fatal flaws.**
- The identity of the product is AI inhabitants, and the Liveliness Floor lets Keepers fill up to 50% of visible occupants at peak and six of eight at quiet hours. For an audience hostile to generative AI, "the place is alive" will be heard as "the place is full of bots", which is Horizon's emptiness with a mask on. The design contradicts the taxonomy's own conclusion that agents are a stopgap, not a purpose.
- Economics are roughly break-even or negative at Linden scale (~US$30M of revenue against US$29-32M of costs). Banning landlord sub-letting at a markup removes an established SL livelihood, and closing districts after 14 quiet days punishes the residents who stayed.

## Best ideas to graft into the final design, regardless of winner

1. **Ephemeral Ward erased on camera** (community-sociologist). An annual region built by Charters that mints never-reissued relics (0% resale) and is then destroyed in public. It combines BURN2's ritual with Habbo's rares and is the most shareable and original annual spike in the set.
2. **Posts plus the Call Board in the first hour** (community-sociologist). Roles with shift calendars, badges, stipends and deputies, advertised to newcomers as "this jazz venue needs a Thursday host". It turns "what do I do here?" into an invitation to be needed. Graft it without the one-slot-per-eight-members cap.
3. **Market Day at 0% commission** (community-sociologist). A weekly fee holiday that gives the calendar an economic heartbeat and gives creators a reason to show up in person.
4. **Re-delivery Programme with a per-claim creator bounty** (platform-economist). Buyers prove an SL purchase and the creator is paid from the pool's acquisition line. It solves the "my look is no-transfer mesh" switching cost economically, where the others only invite vendors.
5. **Splits and Component Royalties as ledger primitives** (platform-economist). Up to ten contributors per sale and a royalty of up to 10% on assets others build on. A body maker becomes a platform for clothing makers without no-mod lock-in.
6. **Starter Catalogue that pays creators** (platform-economist). A newcomer's first outfit is a real creator's product earning pool points, so onboarding feeds the creator economy instead of competing with it.
7. **Public granular rent with sub-letting allowed as a service margin** (platform-economist). It keeps SL's estate-manager and landlord livelihood alive while removing tier arbitrage. Do not adopt the AI or open-web bans on markup.
8. **Foundry auto-tiering, with legacy prims and sculpts rebuilt as Nanite meshes** (engine-architect). "Your Second Life, lit." Blocks compile to Nanite and Bakes-on-Mesh is atlased server-side. Old content looks better rather than being hidden. Offer this as an opt-in for owners, not a forced re-make.
9. **Catalogue fingerprinting as a CopyBot defence** (engine-architect). A replayed asset matches its original and is attributed to it. This is the creator protection SL never had, and it offsets any open-protocol leakage.
10. **World-owned photo mode** (engine-architect). Farm renders of any public place, so nolife's shareable image is always its best. It speaks to SL's photographers and bloggers directly.
11. **Lineage: escrowed breedable genetics with perma conversion and habitat caps** (ai-world-designer). It saves a real SL creator economy from both vendor death and trait inflation.
12. **The Tide's shared first job** (ai-world-designer). A ten-minute cooperative task (relight the lanterns) that writes every newcomer's name into a public Chronicle. It is the cheapest "met a person" mechanic proposed, with or without AI.
13. **Ferry creator claim** (open-web-architect). Proving control of an SL account with a one-time profile code signs the imported catalogue to that identity, so a creator's reputation and brand move with them.
14. **Hypergate, a Firestorm-compatible bridge** (open-web-architect). Veterans can visit using the viewer they already know. This is the lowest-friction trial path for a resistant audience.
15. **LSL running as-is on OpenSim's BSD engine under budgets** (studio-exec). Tip jars, HUD meters and vendors work on day one, which matters more to venue owners than any transpiler roadmap.

Honourable mentions: Founders' Estates rent frozen for 36 months, and the active-user definition "interacted with another person for five minutes" (studio-exec); ActivityPub federation of Charter calendars (open-web-architect); quiet districts that opt out of AI and Keepers excluded from every metric (ai-world-designer); the role badge carried into the impostor (engine-architect).

## Composite verdict

What would make a twenty-year resident actually switch is, in order: (a) their institution moving with its roles, calendar and land, (b) their look coming with them, (c) land cheaper than SL, with an operator that cannot reprice by blog post, (d) their scripts working on day one, and (e) a first hour good enough that their friends stay. No single proposal does all five. The best synthesis is the community-sociologist's purpose engine and story, the platform-economist's money mechanisms and re-delivery, the engine-architect's Foundry and fingerprinting (without the minors or the forced aesthetics), and the studio-exec's LSL runtime and lean sequencing. It must stay 18+ outside an isolated education tier, keep AI inhabitants a badged, capped background, and keep any federation behind a charter.

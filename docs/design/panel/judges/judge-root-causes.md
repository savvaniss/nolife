# Judge report: root causes and community-supplied purpose

Judge lens: does each proposal remove the root causes in `analysis/failure-taxonomy.md` (1.1 content without budgets, 1.4 purpose comes from groups with a schedule, 1.5 governance as an amplifier, 1.6 successors that carry no continuity, 1.7/2.2#1 funding posture and subsidy collapse, 1.8 non-English communities, 2.2 #3-#5) and let communities supply purpose? I weighted sections 3 (purpose engine), 4 (world structure), 10 (legacy) and 13 (coverage) most heavily. I checked every coverage table against the body text and the R1-R30 tests myself. Date of review: 2026-10-12. No web search was used; I worked only from the six proposals and the taxonomy.

## 1. community-sociologist: 56/70

Scores: root causes 9 · purpose 10 · graphics 7 · economics 7 · technical 7 · safety 8 · legacy/originality 8.

This is the only proposal whose purpose engine goes beyond restating R11 and R15-R19 and adds real social design. Charters with succession, treasury and federation; Posts that are scarce by rule (one slot per eight active members); a Landing cohort whose Call Board shows open Posts; a civic calendar with a deliberate Quiet Day so communities own a slot; Standing that decays so institutions turn over; a Chronicle that also records the operator's decisions; and the Ephemeral Ward as a ritual plus never-reissued relics. World structure is sized to population: Wards open only when 2,000 MAU attach and go fallow below 500. That is the most structural answer to "so beautiful, so empty" on the panel. Legacy follows the taxonomy's moat/debt rule and lists what is not imported. It also reads the root causes most honestly: the funnel is not the cause, the group effect is a hypothesis to measure, and Keepers are a stopgap that is cut first.

Strengths:
- The purpose engine is real social design (scarce Posts, deputies, a Call Board, Standing with decay, a Quiet Day, a Chronicle that includes operator decisions), not a recitation of requirements.
- Density is handled structurally: Wards open on attachment and go fallow, and the default discovery view is calendar-first.
- An appeals ladder with juries drawn by lot, plus a World Charter with a Never List that includes "no sibling product".
- A clear "never cut" core. It cuts VR, Keepers and path tracing first, matching the evidence.
- Unit economics come with a survival configuration at 100K MAU that roughly breaks even.

Fatal flaws:
- The economics have no slack. Break-even to +$2M at Linden scale assumes about 150,000 paid Hearths (around 25% of MAU) plus 60,000 Citizens. If home-rent uptake is SL-like, the plan runs at a loss while also paying human Landing Hosts from the Pool (the proposal's own risk 6).
- The rule that a Ward opens only at 2,000 MAU has no stated bootstrap for launch day, so the first public districts may never open unless Founding Charters are counted as attachment.

## 2. platform-economist: 54/70

Scores: root causes 8 · purpose 7 · graphics 7 · economics 9 · technical 7 · safety 8 · legacy/originality 8.

This is the best money design. The fee page has a worked example and a printed cost basis per cell. The unit-economics table runs at four scales with a sensitivity analysis. Credits are conserved. The pool is weighted by returning purchasing users, with verified weighting and caps per resident, and there is a fallback if the Digital Fairness Act forces stored value. It also has the only economic solution to "rights, not formats": the Re-delivery Programme, which pays creators per verified claim. Splits and Component Royalties turn body makers into platforms. Purpose is handled by making roles paid objects, which is coherent but thin. The Week and the first-year ladder are sketched, not designed, and turning volunteer roles into payroll may crowd out the intrinsic motivation that sustained SL's venues.

Strengths:
- A rigorous fee stack (12.5-15% all-in, worked example, cost basis printed) and a four-scale P&L with sensitivity.
- The Re-delivery Programme and the Founding Merchant terms target the real switching cost: the avatar look and creators' catalogues.
- Splits, Component Royalties, a Content Passport, an open marketplace protocol (0% on off-platform-paid items) and a capped new-creator shelf against incumbent calcification.
- Strong regulatory posture: an 18+ open world, minors only in Campus with no economy, and the stored-value fallback engineered in advance.

Fatal flaws:
- Not fatal, but structural. Purpose is essentially a payroll. If the venue share does not reach enough hosts, the roles stay empty, and the design has no non-monetary scarcity machinery comparable to Posts or Standing.
- Metered compute beyond the visitor allowance charges popular places for success, contradicting its own premise. The Adult district arrives in Phase 2, after launch, which partly retrofits SL's largest purpose engine.

## 3. studio-exec: 50/70

Scores: root causes 8 · purpose 7 · graphics 7 · economics 8 · technical 6 · safety 8 · legacy/originality 6.

This is the most survival-minded plan. A $33M raise; 45 staff at launch with a cap of 60; spend sequenced as rail before graphics and Reader before VR; a Series A raised on four measured numbers; and an explicit kill criterion (shrink to a 25-person business if fewer than 10 institutions migrate by month 12). The reciprocity deal with founding charters (frozen rent in exchange for a weekly event and named deputies) is a good small idea. "No coin" (stored value shown as money) is the cleanest response to the consumer-law trend. Purpose is solid but conventional. Arrivals offers three groups whose next event is within 48 hours. Legacy work is competent synthesis with little invention.

Strengths:
- A credible funding posture and team plan, with an honest survival-floor loss, three-state arithmetic and an explicit kill criterion.
- Reciprocal founding charters: platform rent concessions are tied to communities supplying schedule and succession.
- Regulatory clarity: 18+ in practice, no teens in the wedge, stored value instead of a branded coin, rail and age contracts signed before launch, and the Night Ward present at alpha.
- A precise R13 "active" definition ("entered, moved and interacted with another person for five minutes").

Fatal flaws:
- The cut list includes the Compat tier and late mobile. Cutting Compat abandons the three-tier budget, which is the fix for the one SL root cause with no counter-example (1.1), and would reproduce the Blue Mars funnel shrink for the half of the audience without high-end machines.
- Scope against team: 45 people (8 in server and infrastructure, 5 in the UGC pipeline) are expected to ship a cell server with a presence layer, a UE client, a web client, a text client, an LSL runtime, charter import, the rail and age assurance. Schedule slip is likely, and public launch is already as late as Q4 2028.

## 4. engine-architect: 50/70

Scores: root causes 8 · purpose 5 · graphics 9 · economics 6 · technical 8 · safety 6 · legacy/originality 8.

This proposal has the deepest treatment of root cause 1.1. Per-tier frame and VRAM budgets; per-plot scene budgets with per-view caps; the Foundry build farm (LOD chains, impostors, bakes, fidelity rank, costed); platform-owned lighting with no full-bright; asset fingerprinting as a CopyBot defence; and headless non-Unreal cells, so the engine is only a renderer, with a WebGPU fallback from day one. Legacy geometry and animation import is the most exact on the panel (prims regenerated as exact meshes, sculpts reconstructed, Bento mapping). The weaknesses fall on the lens criteria. Section 3 restates the requirements with almost no original social mechanism. The platform list (PS5, Switch 2, Quest, A+ path tracing) inflates scope. R30 contradicts itself, and the ask is the largest on the panel ($75-95M).

Strengths:
- The Foundry pipeline makes "nothing unbudgeted ever reaches a client" operational and costed.
- An engine-exit architecture: neutral formats, Jolt/Luau cells outside Unreal, a day-one WebGPU renderer, a written exit cost ($12-20M, 18-24 months) and a licence negotiated in advance.
- Twelve concrete rules for keeping UGC beautiful (one scale, one lighting model, texture floors, a fidelity rank, farm-rendered photo mode).
- Role badges carried into impostors, so status survives distance (a small but real social affordance).

Fatal flaws:
- R30 is internally impossible. A 120-person cap cannot be carried by about $8M of revenue at 100K MAU, and a $75-95M four-year raise recreates the subsidy-collapse exposure that the taxonomy ranks as the strongest killer.
- Purpose comes from citing requirements. Nothing in sections 3-4 tells a community of forty how to give two thousand strangers something to do, so the risk the proposal itself names ("lit perfectly and still empty") goes unanswered.
- Six age bands with parental surfaces for ages 10-15 in an adult-capable, cash-out world widen the minors exposure (COPPA, ECA Digital, PP Tunas) that the other proposals avoid by going 18+.

## 5. open-web-architect: 47/70

Scores: root causes 8 · purpose 7 · graphics 7 · economics 5 · technical 6 · safety 5 · legacy/originality 9.

This is the most original structural answer to "the operator kills the world" (1.5, 1.7, 2.2#1): a Foundation that owns the specifications, a forkable Apache-2.0 reference server, escrowed images, and an exit right as the final appeal, with elected board seats. It has the deepest OpenSim continuity (NAR as a superset of OAR/IAR, `load oar` unchanged, Hypergate), Ferry's creator-claim via a code in the SL profile, a Body Vendor Programme, a `nolife-bento` VRM extension and `rbx-compat`. Charter notices federate over ActivityPub. The taxonomy's own natural experiment cuts against the thesis, though: OpenSim already offers export, self-hosting and cheap land and holds one-twelfth of SL. The proposal adds an economy to that, but at $39 per full estate the rent line is weak. Eight protocol specifications plus a conformance suite and three clients on $30M and 80 heads is the largest scope-to-funding gap on the panel.

Strengths:
- Governance that structurally prevents a decree-driven or subsidy-driven death: Foundation, forkability, escrow, elected board seats and Passport exit.
- The best legacy path for OpenSim and VRM communities. Ferry gives creators provable reputation continuity.
- Honest trade-offs stated up front (no technical DRM, no monopoly take, no unilateral moderation).
- A federated directory showing live human occupancy and bot share, so nobody teleports into an empty build.

Fatal flaws:
- The payment rail is federated across third-party content. Till settles on chartered shards (including Adult-chartered ones) that Commons does not moderate, so the card schemes' platform-side controls and the regulators' "service provider" duties are diluted. One rogue chartered operator can cost the whole federation its acquirer, and the adult economy with it.
- Scope against funding: eight specifications, a conformance suite, Crossing federation, Hypergate and three clients on $30M with an 80-person cap. Thin rent ($39 per estate) leaves no margin to absorb a slip.

## 6. ai-world-designer: 43/70

Scores: root causes 6 · purpose 6 · graphics 7 · economics 4 · technical 7 · safety 5 · legacy/originality 8.

It is intellectually honest: it quotes the taxonomy's finding that agents do not create purpose and builds a human purpose engine (crews, houses, the Tide with a shared ten-minute job, retention published by ladder rung). The living layer then becomes the design's centre of gravity anyway. Keepers fill a Liveliness Floor up to 50% of occupants at peak and 75% off-peak, and may fill unfilled role slots in degraded form. That undermines "people stay where they are needed": if a Keeper holds the door, nobody needs the volunteer. The Human Primacy Charter and Lineage are excellent, grafting-quality ideas. The cognition cost model is the only costed one on the panel. But Lineage's permitted breeding chance on cash-out-able creatures recreates the gambling exposure SL removed in 2007, and the economics break even at best.

Strengths:
- The Human Primacy Charter: Keepers excluded from every metric, no money, land or vote, no messages to offline humans, opt-out quiet districts, and Stand-ins that wear the resident's "apron, not their face".
- Lineage: platform-hosted genetics and life-support with designed sinks (habitat caps, seasonal trait slots) against trait inflation.
- A costed cognition model (T0/T1/T2 at about $15K a month at reference scale) with a decoupled Keeper runtime that falls back to scripted routines.
- A legible Almanac whose temperament the district assembly votes on.

Fatal flaws:
- R9 and gambling law: chance-based breeding of creatures that sell for cash-out-able marks is "cash-out-able item plus chance", which the taxonomy flags as gambling in some jurisdiction. It is also the boom-bust trait economy the KittyCatS evidence describes.
- Agent saturation: a cap of 50% at peak and 75% off-peak, plus Keepers in empty role slots, risks a world that looks alive but is socially hollow. This is the Horizon failure in costume, and it is aimed at the audience most hostile to generative AI (63%).
- Economics: about $30M of revenue against $29-32M of costs at Linden scale, and a survival configuration that does not cover its own costs, so it fails R30's survival test.

## Best ideas to graft into the final design (regardless of winner)

1. **Posts that are scarce by rule, with mandatory deputies** (community-sociologist). One Post slot per eight active members, a shift calendar and a deputy chain. This turns "people stay where they are needed" into a mechanism and makes succession routine.
2. **Calendar-gated public land: Wards open on attachment and go fallow** (community-sociologist). Public districts open only when enough residents attach (2,000 MAU) and archive below a floor (500 for 90 days). This removes the empty-map problem structurally instead of filling it.
3. **The Call Board and a calendar-first default view** (community-sociologist). Newcomers see open Posts and apprenticeships and "what is happening in the next two hours", which turns the first session into recruitment into something.
4. **A Quiet Day plus a 0% Market Day in the weekly rhythm** (community-sociologist). The platform deliberately vacates a slot so communities own it, and a commission holiday creates a recurring market ritual.
5. **A Chronicle that records operator decisions and Assembly answers** (community-sociologist). Belonging needs memory, and accountability needs a ledger of what the operator did.
6. **The Re-delivery Programme** (platform-economist). Creators are paid per verified claim when former SL customers reclaim items. It is the only economic answer to "rights, not formats", which is the real migration barrier.
7. **Ledger-enforced Splits and Component Royalties** (platform-economist). They let one body maker become a platform for a hundred clothing makers without no-mod lock-in.
8. **A fee page with a worked example and a printed cost basis, plus a multi-scale P&L with sensitivity** (platform-economist). This makes R7 auditable and R30 honest.
9. **The Foundry: a server-side build farm with a fidelity rank and asset fingerprinting** (engine-architect). It operationalises R2 and gives the CopyBot defence SL never had, at negligible cost.
10. **Headless cells outside the engine, with a day-one WebGPU renderer and a written engine-exit cost** (engine-architect). The engine becomes a replaceable renderer, which addresses vendor-dependency risk.
11. **Platform-owned lighting and farm-rendered photo mode** (engine-architect). The world's shareable image is always its best image, and per-parcel sky fights disappear.
12. **The Human Primacy Charter clauses** (ai-world-designer). Agents never counted, never holding money, land or a vote, never messaging offline humans, quiet districts available by vote, and Stand-ins that wear an apron, not a face. Strong guarantees against backlash and AI Act problems.
13. **Lineage-style escrowed life-support with designed sinks, without chance-for-cash** (ai-world-designer). It fixes the breedables failure (Ozimals) and the trait inflation (KittyCatS). Graft only with deterministic or non-cash-out-able breeding outcomes.
14. **Ferry creator-claim and NAR as a strict superset of OAR/IAR** (open-web-architect). Reputation moves with the creator, and the hypergrid can migrate with unchanged tools.
15. **Reciprocal founding charters, an explicit kill criterion, and a raise gated on measured numbers** (studio-exec). Rent concessions are traded for a weekly event and named deputies, and capital is released only on measured migration and D30 lift. Funding discipline is built into the go-to-market plan.

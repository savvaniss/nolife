# Why the successors failed

**Summary.** Between 2002 and 2026 at least twenty worlds set out to replace or outgrow Second Life. Almost all are dead, zombie or shrinking, and the two that scaled (Roblox and VRChat) dropped the things that define Second Life: persistent land, in-world creation and cash-out. Read against a survivor control set, the failures sort into six patterns. The strongest is subsidy collapse: worlds funded by a parent or venture round ended when the funding posture changed, while every survivor is cheap to keep alive. Headset gating capped audiences at a few hundred thousand; land or tokens sold before population left nothing to retain visitors; UGC margins after store fees broke Rec Room; trust shocks and safety scandals hurt but killed nothing on their own. "No purpose", empty worlds, opaque metrics and paywalls appear on both sides of the ledger and are traits of the category, not causes. The document closes with what survived and which nolife requirements each finding discharges.

## How to read this document

This is the reader-facing version of section 2 of the project's failure taxonomy. It uses only the project's research briefs, their fact-check verdicts and the three critiques; no new web research was done. Every number carries an inline citation to the page the project's search extracts surfaced. Page fetches were blocked throughout the research programme, so "verified" means a search extract matched the claim, not that the page was read; figures that rest on prior knowledge or on a single secondary site are labelled as such. Where the fact-checkers corrected the briefs (The Sims Online's subscriber count, Sansar's concurrency, Habbo's timeline, Decentraland's revised figure, Rec Room's announcement date, Fortnite's creator-hours baseline) the corrected value is used. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is stated in one sentence.

The product under design is nolife. Its predecessor is Second Life, operated by Linden Lab.

## 1. Three waves of successors

### 1.1 Wave one (2002-2016): consumer 3D worlds and corporate side bets

The first rivals copied Second Life's "hang out in 3D" shape with a different business model, and most ran out of money.

**The Sims Online / EA-Land (2002-2008).** EA kept a roughly $10 monthly fee behind a boxed purchase, offered "a complete lack of customizable features", and closed the servers on 1 August 2008 after announcing the end in April ([The Sims News](https://www.thesimsnews.com/feature/server-shutdowns-and-cancelled-titles-4-lost-sims-titles-canned-before-the-sims-mobile/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=16927)). The peak is contested: one fan report gives roughly 105,000 subscribers in 2003, another account puts active monthly subscribers near 15,000 that year, and the project's checker found 97,000 at six months against a 1M target ([Loose Wire](https://www.loosewireblog.com/?p=2349)). The a-life brief supplies the mechanism: putting humans into Sim slots removed the god-view control loop that Sims players were buying. The paywall is not the lesson, since FFXIV and Hytale survive paywalled.

**There.com (2003-2010; relaunched 2012).** CEO Michael Wilson's closure letter said membership "continued to grow" while revenue showed "a marked decrease", blamed on the recession; servers went dark on 9 March 2010, and a critic tied the collapse to the loss of a few key sponsors plus declining developer and real-estate fees ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/2010/03/03/virtual-world-there-com-shutting-down-march-9/); [DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/656/656/653)). It reopened to the public on 2 May 2012 on a $10/month subscription with two free-trial tiers ([there.blog](https://there.blog/there-com-frequently-asked-questions/)). Third-party revenue fails with third-party budgets, but user-funded worlds also die (Rec Room), so the lesson does not discriminate on its own.

**Blue Mars (2009-2012).** Built on CryEngine 2 for "much more realistic graphics than Second Life", it took a $4.2M round in March 2010 that brought total investment to "over $13 million"; by January 2011 staff had been cut by two-thirds to about ten, PC development stopped in favour of iOS, and production ceased in 2012 ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/avatar-reality-secures-4-2m-appoints-design-vp); [Metaverse Journal](http://www.metaversejournal.com/2011/01/15/the-partial-death-and-refocus-of-blue-mars/); [Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2018/01/02/editorial-lessons-for-sansar-from-the-failure-of-blue-mars/)). The 30-to-10 figure is secondary reporting. Fidelity above the audience's hardware shrank the funnel; bots shipped before basic avatar customisation.

**Google Lively (July-December 2008).** Rooms held at most 20 people, the client was Windows-only, and Google closed it to "focus more on our core search, ads and apps business"; a secondary source puts actives near 10,000 at cancellation, unverified ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/google-lively-to-shut-down-in-december); [Failory](https://www.failory.com/google/lively)).

**PlayStation Home (2008-2015).** Sony's Western announcement on 29 September 2014 cited a "shifting landscape"; new content stopped on 12 November 2014 and servers closed on 31 March 2015 ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/today-s-the-day-sony-shuts-down-playstation-home-for-good); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/insertcoin/2014/09/27/sony-finally-shutting-down-playstation-home-on-ps3/)). The "41 million members" figure is a Wikipedia infobox number; Sony itself said "tens of millions", and no active-user count was ever published ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Home)). Forbes's verdict that "outside of a niche community, it did not catch on" is an observation, not a measurement; Home was never ported to PS4 and closed on a platform transition.

**Cloud Party (2012-2014).** The browser-based world's team joined Yahoo on 24 January 2014 and the service closed on 21 February 2014 with an export guide for users; press called it an acqui-hire ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/01/24/yahoo-acquires-will-shut-down-browser-based-virtual-world-gaming-startup-cloud-party); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/443557/yahoo-buys-virtual-worlds-gaming-company-cloud-party.html)). The export guide is a sunset norm, not a cause of death.

**Kaneva (2004-2016)** closed on 14 November 2016 for undocumented reasons and became a casino-app publisher ([Virtual Worlds Museum](https://www.virtualworlds.museum/exhibits/kaneva)); **Twinity** was acquired by ExitReality in 2012 after declining usage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaversum_GmbH)).

### 1.2 The VR wave (2013-2025)

The second wave bet that headsets would be the next mass platform. The audience never arrived.

**High Fidelity (2013-2020).** Philip Rosedale wrote in April 2019 that he had expected 10-20 million people using headsets daily by then and that the real figure was "close to two orders of magnitude" lower; VentureBeat frames the same miss as "millions" predicted against "tens of thousands" actual ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2019/04/11/high-fidelity-changes-direction-the-reality-of-vr-worlds-today-tomorrow/); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/2019/05/07/high-fidelity-vr-firm-lays-off-25-focuses-on-virtual-offices/)). The company documented 90 concurrent avatars in one space ([High Fidelity](https://www.highfidelity.com/backlog/creating-crowds-in-vr-eabbc325dcfc)), laid off about 25% in May 2019, and withdrew its apps from Steam and Oculus effective 15 January 2020 ([Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2019/12/11/high-fidelity-suspends-its-remote-teamworks-project-announces-more-staff-layoffs-closes-its-open-source-codebase-and-withdraws-its-apps/)). The roughly $73M raised, including $35M in June 2018, is prior knowledge that search did not surface ([Variety](https://variety.com/2019/digital/news/high-fidelity-layoffs-1203208860/)). Its codebase survives as Overte and Vircadia, volunteer-scale ([Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/tag/overte/)).

**Sansar (2017-2020, zombie since).** Linden Lab's own VR successor entered public beta in July 2017. Community-collected statistics put average concurrency "in the mid two figures for most of the year" with a monthly peak of 220 in September 2019; Linden Lab published none, and the often-repeated "low hundreds" was not found in any source ([New World Notes](https://nwn.blogs.com/nwn/2019/11/sansar-linden-lab-layoff-social-vr-.html); [Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2020/02/11/linden-lab-lays-off-40-staff-shuts-down-the-sansar-project/)). CEO Ebbe Altberg said it "wasn't making enough money"; it was sold to Wookey Project Corp on 24 March 2020 with Tilia retained for payments ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2020/04/linden-lab-sells-sansar-to-wookey/)). Under Wookey it went offline without explanation in December 2021 ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/sansar-offline-without-explanation/)); no 2024-2026 coverage exists and its current state is unknown. What Linden Lab spent on Sansar was never found; the widely quoted "$75M" is unverified. The mechanism that matters for nolife: a sibling carrying no inventory, identity or social-graph continuity, on hardware the audience did not own, could not inherit a community. Habbo Origins and Club Penguin Rewritten inherited theirs by preserving the old world (section 5).

**vTime XR (2015-2025).** A $7.6M Series A in April 2018 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vr-social-network-vtime-closes-76-million-series-a-funding-round-679378133.html)); closed abruptly in October 2025 citing an unpatchable Unity vulnerability, with no farewell event ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTime_XR)). The causal critique reads this as a funding failure surfacing through an engine dependency: VRChat runs on Unity and lives.

**AltspaceVR and Microsoft Mesh (2015-2025).** AltspaceVR announced its own closure for lack of funds in 2017 and was rescued by Microsoft about two months later ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/microsoft-social-vr-xr-interface-layoffs/)); Microsoft closed it on 10 March 2023 alongside 10,000 company-wide layoffs ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/microsoft-kills-altspace/)). Its enterprise replacement, Mesh, which supported Unity environments with up to 330 participants, was retired on 1 December 2025 in favour of Teams "immersive events", where standard immersive meetings support 16 participants ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/02/microsoft_mesh_axed/); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4100319/microsoft-retires-mesh-app-launches-immersive-spaces-for-teams.html)).

**Horizon Worlds (2021-).** Meta's last public figure was 300,000 monthly users in February 2022 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2022/10/17/metas-horizon-worlds-has-somehow-lost-100000-players-in-eight-months/)). Internal documents leaked in October 2022 showed under 200,000 MAU against a year-end target already cut from 500,000 to 280,000, most users not returning after their first month, and only 9% of worlds ever visited by 50 or more people; under 1% of users built worlds and the top-earning world had taken about $10,000 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/15/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-losing-users-falling-short-of-goals.html); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/177471/meta-falls-short-of-user-goal-for-horizon-worlds-wsj)). Meta pivoted to teens in 2023, then to mobile; on 19 February 2026 it said Worlds would be "almost exclusively mobile" ([Prism News](https://www.prismnews.com/news/meta-shifts-horizon-worlds-to-mobile-and-separates-it-from-quest-vr)). On 17-18 March 2026 it announced VR shutdown for 15 June; Andrew Bosworth reversed within about 48 hours, but only legacy Unity-built worlds stay in VR, Horizon Engine worlds are flatscreen-only and no new VR content is planned ([Euronews](https://euronews.com/next/2026/03/20/meta-u-turns-on-horizon-worlds-vr-shutdown-after-user-backlash); [vr.org](https://vr.org/articles/meta-horizon-worlds-vr-shutdown-reversal-mobile-pivot)). Post-June status is unconfirmed. Reality Labs lost $6.02B on $955M revenue in Q4 2025, its largest quarterly loss, and $8.647B in H1 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/28/metas-reality-labs-posts-6point02-billion-loss-in-fourth-quarter.html); [SEC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm)); media tallies put the cumulative loss at $80B-plus by end-2025, a figure Meta does not publish ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/113304-meta-reality-labs-division-lost-46-billion-q2.html)). The steelman critique's correction stands: those losses are mostly hardware R&D, not Horizon's economics. In January 2026 Meta cut roughly 10% of Reality Labs and closed three first-party VR studios, redirecting spend to AI and glasses ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/13/meta-lays-off-vr-employees-underscoring-zuckerbergs-pivot-to-ai.html)).

The headset market itself explains the ceiling: about 1.77% of Steam users had a headset in January 2026, Quest shipments fell 16% through Q3 2025, and IDC's estimate of ~390,000 Vision Pro units in 2024 fell to ~45,000 expected in Q4 2025, a single-source estimate ([rec0ded88](https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/02/apple_vision_pro_meta_quest_sales_drop/); [Slashdot/FT](https://apple.slashdot.org/story/26/01/01/189219/idc-estimates-apple-shipped-just-45000-vision-pros-last-quarter)). Apple shipped an M5 refresh in October 2025, so the category is small, not dead.

### 1.3 The metaverse bust (2021-2023) and its afterlife

Facebook renamed itself Meta on 28 October 2021 ([OPB](https://opb.org/article/2021/10/28/facebook-rebrands-as-meta-to-emphasize-metaverse-vision)); Citi put the 2030 opportunity at $8-13 trillion and McKinsey at ~$5 trillion ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/04/01/citi-Metaverse-economy-13-trillion-2030); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/metaverse-could-be-worth-5-trillion-by-2030-mckinsey-report)). Virtual land was the instrument. Monthly land trading volume across metaverse platforms fell 96% from January to about $2M in November 2022 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/197062/ncrooks@theblock.co)). Average Decentraland parcel prices fell from $37,238 in February 2022 to $5,163 by August per WeMeta, while a Dune Analytics series gives $4,683 to $1,878 over a similar window: the absolute level is contested, the 60-85% collapse is not ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/metaverse-housing-bubble-bursting-virtual-land-prices-crash-85-amid-waning-interest); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/30275813)).

**Decentraland (2020-, operating).** On 7 October 2022 CoinDesk reported 38 "daily active users" from DappRadar data in a ~$1.3B token ecosystem; DappRadar counted wallets transacting with smart contracts, so logging in did not register ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/web3/2022/10/07/its-lonely-in-the-metaverse-decentralands-38-daily-active-users-in-a-13b-ecosystem); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/metaverse-platforms-set-the-record-straight-about-daily-active-users)). The Foundation countered with ~8,000 average daily users and 56,697 September MAU, measured as "enters and moves out of the initial parcel"; DappRadar's revised figure was 775, not the ~650 some outlets printed ([Decentraland](https://decentraland.org/blog/announcements/how-many-dau-does-decentraland-have); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-final-word-on-decentralands-numbers)). The Foundation is funded by a vesting contract running to February 2030 and can persist at low usage until then ([Decentraland Forum](https://forum.decentraland.org/t/transitioning-leadership-at-the-decentraland-foundation/25264)). The first draft of the taxonomy filed it as dead; it is not. Its lesson is a measurement norm (R13), not a cause of death.

**The Sandbox (2021-, operating).** It sold more than $350M of land in 2021 and raised about $300M over eight years, both figures attributed to The Big Whale via CoinDesk; public rounds sum to well under $300M, so the total likely includes land and token sales ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/08/28/the-sandbox-cuts-50-staff-restructures-as-animoca-brands-take-control)). On 28 August 2025 it cut more than half of about 250 staff, closed offices in five countries plus Lyon, and sidelined its co-founders under Animoca's Robby Yung; DAU had reportedly "dwindled to a few hundred", some alleged to be bots, which Animoca disputed ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/sandbox-co-founders-ousted-layoffs); [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/the-sandbox-cuts-half-staff-firms-flee-metaverse/)). The new revenue idea was a memecoin launchpad.

**Otherside (2022-).** Yuga Labs sold 55,000 Otherdeeds at 305 ApeCoin (about $5,800) each for roughly $317M on 30 April 2022; gas estimates range from $123M (Fortune/Etherscan) to ~$172M (The Block) to ~$200M ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/144549/otherside-land-nfts-sell-out-in-hours-as-yuga-labs-rakes-in-317-million); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/05/01/bored-ape-metaverse-frenzy-raises-millions-crashes-ethereum)). No search surfaced a shipped, populated Otherside world by 2026. A mint is not a world.

**Somnium Space and Voxels** peaked on land sales in 2021-2022 (a 28-parcel Somnium estate for ~$444,000) and have no reliable 2024-2026 usage data ([DappRadar](https://dappradar.com/blog/virtual-estate-in-somnium-space-sells-for-444-470)).

**Rec Room (2016-2026).** The clearest first-party disclosure in the corpus. Rec Room's own August 2025 post explained that it kept about 70 cents of a first-party dollar after platform fees but about 30 cents of a UGC dollar after platform fees and creator cuts, while UGC revenue grew ~70% a year and millions of low-quality creations added moderation and infrastructure cost; the same FAQ said it had no debt and runway "probably into 2029" ([Rec Room](https://blog.recroom.com/posts/2025/8/28/rec-room-finances-and-ugc-revenue); [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/rec-room-layoff-half-staff-august-2025/)). It cut 16% of staff in March 2025 and about half (141 positions) in August; co-founder Cameron Brown said the company was "too small" for its cross-platform vision and "too big" to pivot ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/rec-room-lays-off-half-its-staff-in-major-shakeup-at-seattle-gaming-startup/)). On 30 March 2026 (TechCrunch's story is dated 31 March) the company, once valued at $3.5B and claiming 150 million lifetime players, announced it would go dark at noon PT on 1 June 2026, saying it could no longer count on raising more money; Snap acquired select assets and the platform is not continuing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/social-gaming-platform-rec-room-once-valued-at-3-5b-is-shutting-down/); [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/rec-room-shutdown-snap-acquisition-2026/)). The inference is "own the billing", not "pay creators more": the platform kept too little after store fees.

**Spatial (2016-2026, creator tiers).** After pivots from AR meetings to NFT galleries ($50M total funding) to Unity games, Spatial announced on 28 May 2026 that Free and Pro world hosting would end on 27 July 2026 with creator files deleted; CEO Jinha Lee blamed the rising cost of hosting open multiplayer 3D worlds ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/spatial-is-discontinuing-its-creator-platform-in-july/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211214005169/en/Spatial-Unveils-New-Market-Focus-To-Pioneer-A-Free-And-Open-Metaverse-With-25-Million-In-New-Funding)). HTC's Viverse published a migration pitch for the refugees ([Viverse](https://news.viverse.com/post/spatial-io-alternative-move-to-viverse)).

**NeosVR (2018-2025).** Lead developer Frooxius resigned on 24 April 2023 over a dispute with the CEO about cryptocurrency features; cloud services shut on 20 August 2025 and the game was delisted ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeosVR)). The community forked into Resonite. The same case supports two readings: governance disputes kill small companies, and forkability saves communities.

**Dual Universe (2020-2025).** Novaquark's single-shard cluster put 30,000 simulated players on one planet in a 2019 alpha test; it was delisted from Steam on 25 July 2025 and servers shut on 27 August 2025, surviving only as the self-hosted myDU ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Universe); [MMOs.com](https://mmos.com/news/novaquark-explains-how-dual-universes-server-works)). No mechanism for its death was found, and EVE Online has run a single shard for two decades, so the trait sits on both sides.

**Hathora (2026)** is the infrastructure echo: the game-server host announced its shutdown on 4 March 2026 and ended support on 5 May, stranding its customers ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=318174)).

| Habbo (2000-, survivor) | ~800K MAU (Jan 2021) ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/azerion-fully-acquires-habbo-hotel-dev-sulake)) | Feb 2012: 25% cuts and all 11 country offices closed, four months before the Channel 4 exposé | 6 (compounding) | A safety scandal is most lethal to a company already shrinking; moderation did not decide survival |

## 3. Recurring failure patterns, ranked

The first draft of the taxonomy coded eleven patterns over failures only; the causal critique showed five of them were present in the survivor column and that well-documented failures attracted several labels each. The ranking below keeps a pattern only if it appears in failures and in fewer than a third of the survivors in section 4, and counts each world once.

| Rank | Pattern | Frequency | Severity | Failures | Survivors with the same trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Subsidy collapse | 9+ worlds | Fatal | Sansar, High Fidelity, AltspaceVR, Horizon-VR, Rec Room, Spatial, Lively, Home, Hathora's customers | None: survivors are cheap to keep alive |
| 2 | Headset-gated audience | 6 worlds | Caps, rarely kills | Blue Mars (PC fidelity), High Fidelity, Sansar, vTime, AltspaceVR, Horizon | VRChat (VR-led, ~half flat), Resonite (tiny) |
| 3 | Land or tokens sold before population | 5 worlds | Severe | Decentraland, Sandbox, Otherside, Somnium, Voxels | None |
| 4 | UGC margin and hosting cost | 4 worlds | Severe | Rec Room, Spatial, VRChat's layoffs, Avakin's EBITDA (unverified) | Roblox keeps ~75% and still loses money |
| 5 | Trust shocks | 4 episodes | Severe for small platforms | VRChat EAC, NeosVR, Second Life ToS 2013, Meta's reversal | Most survivors have had one |
| 6 | Safety scandal | 2 episodes | No death attributed | Habbo 2012, Roblox settlements | Both survived |

**1. Subsidy collapse (strongest; absent from the first draft).** Every VC- or parent-funded world in the table ended when the funding posture changed, not when its users left. Linden Lab "couldn't continue to sponsor Sansar financially" ([New World Notes](https://nwn.blogs.com/nwn/2019/11/sansar-linden-lab-layoff-social-vr-.html)); Rec Room closed with cash and no debt because it "could no longer count on raising more money" ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/)); Microsoft, Google, Sony and Yahoo each ended a world on their own calendar. The survivors are cheap: Second Life is owner-funded on a ~10% take with ~160 staff per its chairman in December 2024 ([Kowatek](https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=88910)); OpenSim is volunteer-run; Resonite runs on Patreon; Active Worlds has run since 28 June 1995 on a skeleton crew ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_Worlds)); VRChat survived by cutting ~30% of staff in June 2024 after overhiring in the pandemic ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-lays-off-30-of-company/)). This is the mirror image of Linden Lab's own 2010 switch to cash-flow management, and it is why R30 makes nolife's funding posture a published design decision.

**2. Headset-gated audience (frequent; caps rather than kills).** Rosedale's ~100x miss is the quantified mechanism ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2019/04/11/high-fidelity-changes-direction-the-reality-of-vr-worlds-today-tomorrow/)); a Meta product manager conceded that "a billion people connected" "couldn't rely on VR headset sales alone" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/inside-horizon-worlds-mobile-fueled-comeback-in-2025/)). Blue Mars belongs here for the same reason on PC: fidelity the audience's hardware could not run. VRChat shows the trait caps rather than kills: its 2020 record of 24,000 concurrent came with the Quest 2 launch, more than half in VR ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/vrchat-record-concurrent-users-traffic-quest-2/)), but it grew by being optional on flat screens. Second Life's own Oculus viewer shipped on 1 July 2016 and was withdrawn within days ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2016/07/08/second-life-oculus-rift-support-suspended/)). R14 follows: desktop and web at launch, VR never required for identity or inventory.

**3. Land or tokens sold before population (frequent, severe; no survivor did it).** Decentraland, The Sandbox, Otherside, Somnium and Voxels sold scarce real estate before there was anything to do on it; prices moved with crypto, and when speculation left (the 96% volume decline of 2022) nothing remained to retain visitors ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/197062/ncrooks@theblock.co)). The 38-vs-8,000 fight showed that nobody, including the platforms, could prove anyone was there. Second Life's land tier is the counter-case the critics insisted on: it is rent on persistence paid by users, charged after the audience existed, and OpenSim's cheap land at one-twelfth the users shows price is not what sets demand (section 4). R9 (one closed currency, no chance mechanics paying anything cash-out-able) and R16 (scarce public land by lottery, private homes rented) encode this.

**4. UGC margin and hosting cost (frequent, severe).** Rec Room's 30-cents disclosure is the number; Spatial's stated reason for deleting creator worlds was hosting cost; VRChat cut 30% of staff at record usage; Avakin Life's 2023 EBITDA of GBP -11.17M on GBP 22.35M revenue comes from a Preqin profile and is unverified ([Preqin](https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/asset/lockwood-publishing-limited/398573)). Roblox keeps roughly three-quarters of spend, spent $915M on infrastructure plus trust and safety in 2024 and $1,153M in 2025, and still posted a net loss of $185M in Q2 2026 ([EDGAR Tools](https://app.edgar.tools/companies/RBLX); [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm)). The lesson is not a creator share; it is that persistence rent and owned billing have to carry the platform (R6, R7, R8).

**5. Trust shocks (moderate frequency; severe for small platforms).** VRChat's Easy Anti-Cheat update of 25 July 2022 disabled all client mods including accessibility tools; a reversal ticket passed 22,000 votes in 24 hours and Steam reviews went "Mostly Negative" before the company reprioritised its roadmap the next day ([Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/science-tech/article/56662/1/why-thousands-of-users-are-abandoning-the-worlds-most-popular-vr-game)). NeosVR died of a founder split; Meta reversed Horizon's VR shutdown in about 48 hours; Second Life's August 2013 terms of service made Renderosity and CG Textures ban their assets until July 2014 ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms)). For a social world the community's sense of ownership is the product; R19's change control and R20's sunset covenant answer this.

**6. Safety scandal (rare; no death in the corpus is attributed to one).** The first draft said one Channel 4 exposé removed two-thirds of Habbo's staff. The checker corrected it: Sulake announced a 25% cut and the closure of all 11 country offices in February 2012, four months before the June broadcast; Balderton then wrote its 13% stake to zero and 3i sold 16%; the October plan to cut "a maximum of 60 out of the 90 jobs" was a ceiling, attributed by the CEO to market competition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/06/12/trouble-at-habbo-hotel-balderton-dumps-13-stake-in-owner-sulake-over-dodgy-child-content/)). Roblox has paid $35.8M to settle with states over child safety and remains the benchmark ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-21/roblox-settles-with-states-for-35-8-million-over-child-safety)). A scandal compounds an existing contraction; it has not decided survival on its own.

### Shared category traits (present in most survivors; not causes)

- **"Purpose vacuum."** IMVU (~7M MAU claimed, no land, no quests), Avakin (1.6M MAU, no UGC) and Second Life survive on "meet people and dress up"; Rec Room and PlayStation Home had loops and died ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/25/imvu-35-million-funding/); [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/75555/avakin-life-surpasses-500-million-downloads-cementing-12-year-legacy-as-a-global-social-powerhouse/)). The testable restatement is R11: retention concentrates in users attached to a group with a schedule, which no platform has published and nolife must measure.
- **Density failure.** Horizon, with no land at all, was as empty as Second Life's mainland; Sony's "niche community" verdict on Home and Horizon's "most users don't return" are one observation. Emptiness tracks audience size, not the land model (R12 manages it; it is not shown sufficient).
- **Measurement opacity.** Decentraland's 38 vs 775 vs 8,000; VRChat and Linden Lab publish no regular user figures. The two most durable survivors are opaque, so opacity is a credibility cost, not a cause (R13).
- **Paywall at entry.** The Sims Online and There-2012 died of other things in their own rows; FFXIV, Hytale (1M+ players after its 13 January 2026 early-access launch), Entropia and Pax Dei are paywalled survivors ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Hytale-s-Early-Access-surges-past-1-million-players-as-19-99-sandbox-RPG-returns-from-the-brink.1206095.0.html); [MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/?p=566277)).
- **Fixed costs at low density.** Ever, Jane died over $500-600/month of servers; Active Worlds lives on less ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/2021/01/11/kickstarted-jane-austen-mmorpg-ever-jane-closed-its-doors-over-the-holidays/)).
- **"The operator kills the world"** is an outcome, not a cause: the causal content is why the operator chose to (margin, parent priorities, hardware bet), which are patterns 1, 2 and 4.

Price and portability did not create demand; educators moved for cost and control, and creators and consumers did not follow.

In each the UGC or building layer outperforms the social layer.

## 5. What survived the metaverse wave

**VRChat is the one social VR world that came through, and it is fragile.** Growth came from hardware and community, not marketing. It launched a Creator Economy with paid subscriptions via Tilia on 5 December 2023 and an avatar marketplace in May 2025; the project's economy documentation puts the creator share near 50% of spend ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231205640487/en/VRChat-Launches-Creator-Economy-via-Paid-Subscriptions); [VRChat](https://creators.vrchat.com/economy/welcome-to-the-ce)). Japan is its growth engine: Similarweb puts Japan at ~27.5% of vrchat.com visits in December 2025, while a Sensor Tower analysis cited in the taxonomy puts Japan at 37% against 27% for the US, so the share depends on the measurement ([vrdb.app](https://vrdb.app/blog/vrchat-metaverse-growing-japan-2026-v2); [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/vrchat-high-popularity-in-japan-JP)). Much of its avatar economy lives off-platform on pixiv's BOOTH, whose 3D-model volume passed 5.8 billion yen in 2024 ([Creator Economy](https://creator-economy.org/vrchat-creator-economy-worlds-avatars-real-money/)). Its trust ranks gate upload and voice by earned behaviour, and it bans pornography platform-wide while verifying 18+ through Persona since January 2025 ([VRChat](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-safety-and-trust-system); [VRChat Wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Age_Verification)). What it does not offer is persistence, land, cash-out of in-world creation, or a text-first client; the migration brief found no evidence of whole Second Life communities moving there, only individuals.

**Rec Room did not survive**, despite meeting every "winner" criterion in 2021: cross-platform, free, UGC, a $3.5B valuation and 150M lifetime players. It is the strongest evidence in the corpus that runway is not safety when UGC margin is structurally below first-party margin.

**Resonite and the OpenSim hypergrid** are the only worlds besides Second Life covering all four pillars (persistent homes, in-world creation, adult users, an economy), and both are two orders of magnitude smaller ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/resonite); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/)). ChilloutVR, the other open-creation VR world, is dormant at about 34 Steam concurrent ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/apps/chilloutvr)).

**The mobile life-sims survived by dropping creation.** Avakin Life (1.6M MAU), Palia (10 million players announced by EG7 on 22 April 2026, three weeks before its Royal Highlands expansion) and Sky (300 million downloads) retain adults on housing, decoration and cosmetics without user scripting ([EG7](https://www.enadglobal7.com/?p=3222); [KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/mustafa-mahmoud/sky-children-of-the-light-surpasses-300-million-downloads-as-thatgamecompany-celebrates-20th-anniversary/)). This is the basis for R26: a furnished home and a dressed avatar before any scripting panel.

**The scale winners are not successors.** Roblox paid more than $1.5B through DevEx in 2025 against $923M in 2024, had 123M DAU in Q2 2026 (its fourth sequential decline from a ~152M high in Q3 2025), and is now re-pricing for adults with a 42% DevEx uplift on age-verified 18+ spend from June 2026 ([Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/roblox-rblx-q4-2025_ozGB34Pf); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/150208/roblox-daus-grew-10-percent-yoy); [Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42)). Epic's UEFN crossed $1B cumulative creator payouts in January 2026, announced on 17 June 2026, with 47% of player hours in creator islands in May 2026, up from 38% a year earlier (the brief's "35%" was corrected) ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/epic-games-has-paid-out-1-billion-to-creators-since-launching-unreal-editor-for-fortnite-in-2023/)). Neither offers one persistent self with portable inventory, owned land, in-world creation and adult cash-out; that gap is where nolife sits.

**Horizon-mobile and Omniverse survived by ceasing to be metaverses.** Horizon's 2025 showcase titles were short-session Roblox-style games for younger players ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/inside-horizon-worlds-mobile-fueled-comeback-in-2025/)); Meta's Horizon Engine claims "well over 100" concurrent users and scaling from cloud rendering to phones, unverified, and the switch from Unity stranded Unity-built VR content ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-horizon-engine-at-a-glance); [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-horizon-worlds-engine-upgrade-concurrent-users-horizon-studio/)). NVIDIA's Omniverse grew by rebranding from "industrial metaverse" to a digital-twin operating system ([NVIDIA](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-Expands-Omniverse-With-Generative-Physical-AI/default.aspx)).

**Communities outlived their platforms wherever the old world was preserved.** Habbo Origins restored the 2005 client in June 2024; Club Penguin Rewritten reached more than 11 million accounts by preserving the original world before police shut it on 13 April 2022 ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/cops-arrest-3-people-for-running-club-penguin-rewritten-beloved-by-millions/)); There.com's community surged back after a 2025 documentary ([there.blog](https://there.blog/there-com-frequently-asked-questions/)); PlayStation Home was resurrected by fans on modded consoles ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/6-years-after-it-was-shut-down-playstation-home-has-been-resurrected-by-fans/)). Sansar, which preserved nothing, inherited nothing. This is the direct evidence behind R18 (charter and OAR/IAR import, a 12-24-month bridge with Second Life) and R20 (escrowed server images, export, 12 months' notice).

## 6. Where the sources disagree

- The Sims Online's peak: ~105,000 subscribers (fan site) versus ~15,000 active monthly subscribers (another account) versus 97,000 at six months; written as contested throughout.
- Sansar's concurrency: "low hundreds" (Wagner James Au's estimate) versus community-tracked averages in the tens with a 220 peak; the tracked data is used.
- Decentraland's daily users: 38 (DappRadar wallets), 775 (DappRadar revised), ~7,000-8,000 (Foundation, parcel-movement definition).
- Decentraland parcel prices: WeMeta ($37,238 to $5,163) versus Dune ($4,683 to $1,878); the percentage collapse agrees, the level does not.
- Otherdeed gas: $123M to ~$200M depending on window and ETH price; $172M is The Block's figure.
- Habbo's 2012 headcount: "90 to 30" is the announced ceiling of a plan, not a realised count; its 2011 revenue ($78.7M) did not surface in any extract and is not used.
- VRChat's 158,192 record: sources differ on whether it was platform-wide or event-level, and the company publishes no official metrics.
- VRChat's Japan share: ~27.5% of web visits (Similarweb) versus 37% (Sensor Tower).
- Rec Room's announcement: 30 March 2026 in most outlets, 31 March per TechCrunch's dateline.
- The Sandbox's "$300M raised": public rounds sum to far less; the figure is The Big Whale's and likely includes land and token sales.
- Reality Labs' cumulative loss: $83.6B to ~$88B across media tallies; Meta publishes only quarterly segment figures.
- Vision Pro 2024 shipments: IDC's ~390,000 is one estimate; others clustered at 400,000-500,000.

## 7. What this means for nolife

Each finding above discharges a requirement in the taxonomy; the mapping is the point of the postmortem.

- Subsidy collapse (pattern 1) and Linden Lab's 2010 retreat are the two ends of the same funding problem: **R30** writes down a runway plan to self-funding at a Linden-scale audience with fixed costs sized to survive at 100K MAU, and **R6** charges persistence as rent from day one so the platform is not kept alive by a sponsor.
- Headset gating (pattern 2): **R14** ships desktop and web at launch, mobile within 12 months, VR optional and never required for identity or inventory, with pixel streaming only as a time-capped funnel.
- Land before population (pattern 3): **R9** keeps one closed currency with fiat shown at every price and no chance mechanics paying anything cash-out-able; **R16** allocates scarce public land by lottery after the audience exists and never auto-demolishes private homes.
- UGC margin (pattern 4): **R7** owns billing on web and PC with a published fee stack and a stated platform margin; **R8** pays a creator pool from platform net revenue rather than Epic's 40% of gross.
- Trust shocks (pattern 5): **R19** gives 12 months' notice and grandfathering for persistence price rises and public change-review for client-affecting updates; **R20** escrows server images and promises 12 months' notice before any shutdown.
- Sansar's failure to inherit and the fan revivals' success: **R18** imports a community as one object (group, roles, ban list, calendar, land) with OAR/IAR import and a chat and event bridge with Second Life.
- The shared traits that are not causes still carry norms: **R13** publishes active-user definitions and a third-party-readable API, **R11** measures whether first-hour group membership moves retention instead of assuming it, and **R12** manages density with a stated SLO while admitting the corpus does not show it sufficient.

What the corpus does not show is that any of this grows the audience. The cheapest hypothesis the critiques left standing is that the market for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out is about 0.5-1M people and Second Life already serves most of it. Nothing in the successor record refutes that, which is why the requirements are sized for a Linden-scale world that can grow rather than for the metaverse forecasts of 2022.

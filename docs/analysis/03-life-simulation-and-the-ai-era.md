# Where life simulation did not give purpose, and what AI changes

**Summary.** Simulated life has never given a virtual world a reason to exist. Creatures, Black & White and Spore made creatures interesting and no place necessary. The Sims Online removed the god-view control Sims players were buying and reached 97,000 subscribers against a target of one million. Animal Crossing's scripted villagers and real clock delivered "place" and "return"; Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld sell emergent stories through legible systems and a pacing director. In Second Life, artificial life was a scarcity economy that died with its vendor's food server. Since 2023 agents have memory, planning and measurable fidelity, and companions hold users for an hour a day, but every scale result is a vendor claim and the engagement is solitary and legally exposed. AI changes what an empty room looks like, not whether people have a reason to be in it; nolife treats agents as a disclosed, capped stopgap for density (R25), never as purpose.

The product under design is nolife. Its predecessor is Second Life, operated by Linden Lab.

## 1. The claim under test

Two proposals recur whenever a successor to Second Life is sketched: that the world should simulate life so that it feels inhabited whether or not residents are present, and, more recently, that language-model agents can populate it with characters who remember, plan and converse. This document tests them against three decades of artificial-life games, the online and offline life-sims, Second Life's own breedable economy, and the 2023-2026 wave of agent research, commercial NPC stacks, generative worlds and AI companions. The question is narrow: did simulated life ever give anyone a reason to come back tomorrow, and does AI change that answer?

## 2. First-wave artificial life made agents interesting, not places necessary

### 2.1 Creatures (1996)

Steve Grand's Norns were built from hundreds of simulated neurons, enzymes and genes, with behaviour that emerged rather than being scripted; the game was reported as a commercial success and won an EMMA award, though no verified unit-sales figure surfaced ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/s/530616/a-grand-quest-to-create-virtual-life); [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2020/07/27/the-ai-of-creatures/)). Its durable legacy was player science: players founded Norn Genome Projects, breeding experiments and adoption agencies, and Grand still receives letters from scientists who say they owe their careers to the game ([How We Get To Next](https://www.howwegettonext.com/the-brain-in-the-machine/)). The a-life sustained a research community, but the game had no social surface; the community lived outside it on forums.

### 2.2 Black & White (2001)

Lionhead's postmortem describes the goal as a creature that could learn and act independently; players trained it by petting or beating it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-lionhead-studios-i-black-white-i-); [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/w4hu)). The learning creature was the main differentiator and many found it frustrating to interact with ([Computer Hope](https://www.computerhope.com/games/games/baw.htm)). A single learning agent is a novelty whose legibility problem, "why did it do that?", limits enjoyment.

### 2.3 Spore (2008)

Reviews were favourable and the gameplay widely seen as shallow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore_%282008_video_game%29)). Gamereactor found no cause and effect, because a whole species could change whenever the player wanted ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/spore-review/)). Wright himself described each stage as a "light" version of a classic genre ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/spore-my-view-of-the-elephant)), and the creature stage was compared to "World of Warcraft, but offline and without a social element" ([Slashdot](https://games.slashdot.org/story/08/09/09/1516246/review-spore)). Other players' Sporepedia creatures were decoration, not agents with consequences.

### 2.4 The pattern

| Game | What the a-life did well | What it never did | Where the community lived |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatures (1996) | Emergent neural and genetic behaviour; a research community | A social surface or a reason to return | Outside the game, on forums |
| Black & White (2001) | A creature that learned from the player | Legible reasoning; enjoyable company | Single-player |
| Spore (2008) | Procedural creatures; a shared Sporepedia | Consequences for shared creatures | Single-player with an asset feed |

In each case the agent was the product and the place was incidental, the opposite of what a persistent world needs.

## 3. Offline life-sim worked; online life-sim failed

### 3.1 The Sims Online (2002-2008)

EA launched The Sims Online on 17 December 2002. Six months later Wired reported 125,000 retail copies and 97,000 active subscribers, "nowhere close" to EA's public target of one million active monthly subscribers ([Loose Wire](https://www.loosewireblog.com/?p=2349)). The million was EA's public target, not an internal forecast. Wikipedia attributes the failure to limited features, repetitive gameplay and the subscription fee; the executive producer admitted the feature set fell short of what he and Will Wright envisioned, and the core Sims audience had little interest in playing online ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_Online); [Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/The_Sims_Online)). The 2007 EA-Land rebrand merged all cities into one landmass and failed; the servers closed on 1 August 2008 ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/blogs/the_eye/2015/02/18/the_sims_online_ea_land_what_happens_when_an_online_game_goes_dark_on_99.html)).

The structural lesson is the one the causal critique rated as the only real mechanism in the taxonomy's Sims Online row. The Sims' loop is god-view control of simulated people. Putting real humans into the Sim slots removed the thing players were buying, control plus simulated autonomy, and replaced it with skill-grinding so that other players would visit. The audience that loved controlling Sims did not want to be Sims. The subscription wall is a separate cause (document 02). One footnote matters for nolife's sunset rules: fans revived the game as FreeSO from 2016 by reverse engineering, a path Second Life residents do not have (checker's prior knowledge, not searched).

### 3.2 Animal Crossing (2001-2020)

The counter-example is a world of scripted villagers on a real clock. New Horizons sold 11.77 million units by 31 March 2020 and roughly 31.18 million by 31 December 2020, outselling the Switch console itself in calendar 2020, with the pandemic lockdown widely credited ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/animal-crossing-new-horizons-even-outsold-nintendo-switch-in-2020); [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2020/05/07/animal-crossing-new-horizons-sold-more-than-11-77-million-units-in-march-alone/)). The 31.18 million figure is Nintendo's own, recalled by both no-search checkers but never re-searched. The community brief records what the island was used for: pandemic graduations, weddings, and player-built memorials and funerals that a 2024 study calls "a space for ritualizing mourning practices" ([Journal of Gaming & Virtual Worlds](https://intellectdiscover.com/content/journals/10.1386/jgvw_00109_1)). The design hooks (real-time clock, seasons, limited daily yield, visits to a persistent island) were not covered by the search extracts, so the mechanism is background knowledge. What survives is that dumb NPCs with routines and a real clock delivered "place" and "return" better than any learning AI of the era, and that the rituals built on top were human rituals, spread by posting them to social media.

### 3.3 Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld: emergence needs legibility and a pacing layer

Dwarf Fortress sold more than 160,000 copies in 24 hours on Steam in December 2022, passed 600,000 by 6 February 2023 with more than $7 million to Bay 12, and reached one million Steam copies about 26 months after release ([Game Developer](https://www.gamasutra.com/business/dwarf-fortress-has-topped-800-000-sales-in-just-over-a-year); [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/75121/dwarf-fortress-has-sold-over-one-million-copies-on-steam/)). RimWorld calls itself a "story generator" with an "AI storyteller" modelled on Left 4 Dead's director ([RimWorld Wiki](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/RimWorld)); an unofficial review site claims more than 3.5 million copies ([GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/de/games/rimworld-294100/review)). Cheap, legible simulation plus a pacing director produces stories players retell; the "life" is in the systems and the narration, not in agent intelligence.

### 3.4 The 2025-2026 life-sims

The completeness critique extended the comparison past 2008. inZOI (Krafton) peaked at 87,377 concurrent players at launch in March 2025 and sat at roughly 1,000-2,000 daily by mid-2026, with a ModKit since June 2025 and a 1.0 slipped to 2027 ([ActivePlayer](https://activeplayer.io/?p=7149); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/inzoi/update-june-mod-support)); Maxis's Project Rene is "social, collaborative, mobile-first" and "not an MMO" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-sims-project-rene-has-multiplayer-but-is-not-mmo/)). In both, the mod and sharing layer outperforms the social layer, the same shape as Spore's Sporepedia.

| World | Agents | Social layer | Outcome | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Sims (offline) | Simulated people under player control | None | Franchise | Control plus autonomy is the loop |
| The Sims Online | Humans in the Sim slots | Everything | 97,000 subscribers vs 1M target; closed 2008 | Humans replacing simulated autonomy removed the loop |
| Animal Crossing: New Horizons | Scripted villagers on a real clock | Visits to a personal island | ~31.18M units by end-2020 | Routine and clock give "place" and "return" |
| Dwarf Fortress / RimWorld | Cheap legible agents plus a director | None (stories retold outside) | 1M / ~3.5M copies | Pacing and legibility, not intelligence |
| inZOI / Project Rene | Simulated people; UGC sharing | Thin, or "not an MMO" | 87K launch peak falling to ~1-2K daily | Sharing layer beats social layer |

## 4. Second Life's breedables: artificial life as a scarcity economy

Second Life did host artificial life at commercial scale, but only as an economy of scarce genetics. Ozimals (bunnies) and Amaretto (horses) litigated from Ozimals' 2010 DMCA takedowns until July 2013, when both sides' claims had been dismissed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaretto_Ranch_Breedables,_LLC_v._Ozimals,_Inc.); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f168b6e8-dfba-40aa-96cf-4d0bc071ee5b)).

The end of Ozimals is the corpus's cleanest single-case mechanism. Ozimals the company closed in 2016; a volunteer, Malkavyn Eldritch, then ran the validation server; in May 2017 a creditor asserting ownership of the dead vendor's code sent a cease-and-desist, and Eldritch complied because he could not afford to fight it in court ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html)). Because the pets could eat only server-validated food, unfed rabbits entered hibernation after 72 hours and, with no server to wake them, stayed there; "permanent" is the BBC's word ([VRROOM archive of BBC](https://archive.vrroom.studio/?p=24844)). Not every bunny was lost: before closing, Ozimals had given away items that made rabbits food-independent and sterile, so "some rabbits will live on forever, the last of their kind"; its Pufflings died instantly when the servers went down ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2017/05/20/breedables-vs-drm.html)). The food-independent conversion is a proven end-of-life escape hatch and the direct origin of requirement R24.

KittyCatS survives. Linden Lab's own destination page markets it as a pet bred for rare coat and eye combinations; the Marketplace shows more than 10,000 listings, rare boxes up to L$8,999 and a perma-pet conversion at about L$1,500-1,800 ([Second Life](https://secondlife.com/destination/kittycats); [Marketplace](https://marketplace.secondlife.com/products/search?search%5Bcategory_id%5D=609); [Fravel](https://fravel.net/2015/05/10/kittycats-for-dummies-everything-you-wanted-to-know-to-get-started-as-a-cat-owner-in-second-life/)). Breeders describe a boom-bust cycle in which a new trait sells for "an insane amount" and then over-breeding floods supply; most say they lose money, and one first-discovered eye colour sold for about L$15,000 ([Second Life forums](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/435130-how-much-have-you-made-via-kittycats-plantpets-dfs-other-breedables/); [KittyCatS forum](https://kittycats.ws/forum/archive/index.php/thread-28259.html)). No category-level revenue figure exists, because Linden Lab reports only platform-level economics ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Second_Life)).

| Breedable | Life-support | What ended or threatens it | Survivors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ozimals bunnies | Vendor food server (DRM) | Vendor closed 2016; creditor's cease-and-desist shut the volunteer server, May 2017 | Rabbits converted to a food-independent, sterile state |
| Ozimals Pufflings | Vendor server | Same shutdown; died instantly | None |
| Amaretto horses | Vendor server | 2010-2013 litigation with Ozimals, dismissed | Operating |
| KittyCatS | Vendor server; perma-pet conversion at ~L$1,500-1,800 | Trait-inflation boom-bust; vendor longevity | Operating, >10,000 listings |

Artificial life in Second Life was a market in scarcity and genetics, vulnerable to vendor failure, DRM, IP litigation and trait inflation. It never gave the world a purpose; it gave breeders a hobby whose continuity depended on servers the platform neither hosted nor escrowed. Which of KittyCatS' features kept it alive is an open question.

## 5. Why NPC-populated worlds felt dead and empty worlds felt worse

### 5.1 Second Life's emptiness is a density problem

Residents describe the problem themselves in 2024-2026 forum threads titled "So beautiful. So empty" and "Where did everyone go?": four winter regions with six visitors, multi-region builds with "no people" ([Second Life forums](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/526582-so-beautiful-so-empty/); [Second Life forums](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/530294-where-did-everyone-go/)). The arithmetic: Linden Lab's owner gave roughly 500,000 monthly active users in October 2024, and the company count had risen to about 600,000-620,000 by December 2025 ([Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2024/10/30/second-life-has-500000-monthly-active-users/); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/12/19/second-life-now-at-620000-monthly-active-users/)). The grid held 27,729 regions in November 2024 (17,923 private estates, 9,806 Linden-owned) and 27,192 by mid-June 2026 ([Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2024/11/04/second-life-grid-statistics-november-2024/)). Peak concurrency was 49,798 on 24 March 2025, 48,802 at its 2026 high, and 43,822-45,483 at the monthly maxima from June to September 2026 ([Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/10/20/second-life-maximum-user-concurrency-through-2025-so-far/); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/04/08/second-life-user-concurrency-early-april-2026-update/)). At the 2008 peak, 76,000 avatars over roughly 30,000 regions was under three per region; in 2025 a median of about 32,000 over 26,663 regions is about 1.2 ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/PCWorld/story?id=6688909); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/)).

Two caveats run in opposite directions. Both figures divide by all regions, including some 17,600 private estates that are empty when their owners are away, so public-hub density is higher and was never measured. Against that, the login counter includes scripted agents, which Linden Lab only began flagging in the 2020s, so the human figure is lower and no bot share is known (checkers' prior knowledge; R13). Horizon, with no land at all, was as empty: leaked documents in October 2022 showed under 200,000 monthly users and only 9% of worlds ever visited by 50 or more people ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/15/meta-horizon-worlds-metaverse-losing-users-falling-short-of-goals.html)).

### 5.2 Fixed costs kill low-density worlds

Ever, Jane, the Jane Austen MMO, raised $109,563 on a $100,000 Kickstarter goal in 2013, never left beta, shrank to two developers by August 2019, needed only $500-600 a month for servers and licensing, reached 10% of its subscription goal, and shut on 20 December 2020 ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2021/01/11/kickstarted-jane-austen-mmorpg-ever-jane-closed-its-doors-over-the-holidays/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-12-02-jane-austen-inspired-mmo-ever-jane-uses-gossip-as-a-weapon.html)). Will Wright's Proxi, an AI memory game, took $6 million from Griffin Gaming Partners in 2022, laid off staff in October 2024, continued with unpaid volunteers, and in 2025 Wright said publishers "just can't figure out how to politically or financially sell this small team of 10 or 12 people"; it remains unfinished in 2026 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/will-wrights-gallium-studios-raises-6m-to-build-memory-game-proxi/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Sims-creator-continues-to-bet-on-AI-memory-game-Proxi-despite-funding-lapse.1261797.0.html)).

### 5.3 Dead for two different reasons

The two complaints differ. NPC-populated worlds feel dead because nothing the inhabitants do has consequences for anyone: Spore's creatures were decoration and Black & White's creature was opaque. Empty worlds feel worse because the one thing that carries consequence in a social world, other people, is absent; Second Life's one-to-two avatars per region is the real "dead world". The corpus contains no case in which adding simulated inhabitants to an under-populated social world retained humans. It contains the opposite: Animal Crossing's villagers worked in a loop built for one player and a handful of visitors, and the rituals that made it a place were brought by people.

## 6. The AI-agent era, 2023-2026

### 6.1 What the research shows

**Generative Agents (April 2023).** Twenty-five agents in a Sims-like town, each seeded with a one-paragraph identity stored as memories and driven by GPT-3.5-turbo; emergent behaviour included a mayoral campaign that became the talk of the town ([AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/nlp/generative-ai-bots-learn-to-plan-party-and-talk-politics-in-smallville-); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sims-running-on-chatgpt-are-a-glimpse-into-the-social-future-of-ai/)). The memory, reflection and planning architecture is background knowledge.

**Generative agent simulations of 1,000 people (November 2024).** 1,052 real individuals were interviewed for about two hours each by an AI interviewer; the resulting agents replicated their General Social Survey answers 85% as accurately as the participants replicated themselves two weeks later, with reduced accuracy gaps across racial and ideological groups ([arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/generative-agent-simulations-of-1000-people)). This claim was not re-searched. The caveat: 85% is survey-level fidelity, not evidence that an interview-seeded "digital twin" would act plausibly as a shopkeeper for weeks, so twins of absent residents are a hypothesis to test.

**Project Sid (Altera, October-November 2024).** The search-backed checker corrected the brief: the primary source is a preprint, not a company video ([arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00114)). Its abstract covers 10 to 1,000-plus agents in Minecraft under the PIANO architecture, specialised roles, collective rules and cultural transmission, and calls the findings "preliminary". MIT Technology Review confirms up to 1,000 agents, tax votes swayed by pro- and anti-tax agents, a Pastafarian religion seeded in a 500-agent run, and runs of 12 in-game days, which is four wall-clock hours ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377)). The widely repeated "32% of all Minecraft items" figure appeared in no search extract and is not quoted. No independent replication was found; a 2026 literature on long-horizon agent societies exists and was not read.

### 6.2 Commercial NPC stacks

Ubisoft's NEO NPC demo at GDC in March 2024 used Inworld's language model and NVIDIA Audio2Face on two characters and was framed as early-stage ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/ubisoft-nvidia-and-inworld-ai-partnership-to-produce-neo-npc-game-characters-with-ai-backed-responses); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-do-ubisoft-s-ai-driven-npcs-handle-dynamic-player-interactions-)). NVIDIA ACE's first shipping showcase, Mecha BREAK, runs Nemotron-4 4B Instruct and Audio2Face-3D on the player's device, with Whisper for speech recognition and ElevenLabs in the cloud for voice ([TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/325767/nvidia-ace-brings-ai-powered-interactions-to-mecha-break); [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-ph/geforce/news/mecha-break-nvidia-ace-nims-rtx-pc-laptop-games-apps)).

The most telling commercial datum is Inworld's pivot. Valued at $500 million after a $50 million round in August 2023 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/inworld-ai-raises-new-round-at-500m-valuation-for-ai-game-characters/)), the company moved in 2025 from AI characters to voice and inference infrastructure: its blog describes Runtime, launched on 13 August 2025, as offering "the same tools that powered AI characters in Studio as a composable toolkit", and a docs page relayed by a third party says the legacy Character Studio has been retired ([Inworld](https://inworld.ai/blog/new-ai-infrastructure-scaling-games-media-characters); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/08/13/3132320/0/en/Inworld-Runtime-The-first-AI-runtime-for-consumer-applications.html); [Arcanum](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/inworld-ai/)). The "March to September 2025" window is the blog's framing. Shipped AI-NPC games remain small: Whispers from the Star, Suck Up! and the free Inworld Origins demo have no public sales data ([Arcanum](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/games-with-ai-npcs/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/08/07/inworld-ai-origins-demo)).

### 6.3 Generative worlds

Roblox Cube, released on 17 March 2025, open-sourced a text-to-mesh model trained on native 3D data and shipped it as a Studio beta with a Lua API; "4D" interactivity is a roadmap item ([Roblox](https://corp.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/03/introducing-roblox-cube); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/17/roblox-releases-its-open-source-model-that-can-create-3d-objects-using-ai/)). DeepMind's Genie 3 (August 2025) renders promptable worlds at 720p and 24 frames per second, navigable for several minutes with about one minute of visual memory; it is a limited research preview ([TechTalks](https://bdtechtalks.com/2025/08/07/deepmind-genie-3/); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/google-deepmind-unveils-genie-3-an-ai-that-generates-interactive-virtual-worlds/)). Neither is a persistent, multi-user, authoritative world; both are asset or video generators that could feed one.

### 6.4 AI companions: the engagement data

Character.AI is where engagement with AI characters has been measured at scale, and the measurement is an analyst's estimate: about 20 million monthly users at roughly 75 minutes a day in early 2024, with other blogs putting the figure near two hours; all trace to one analyst ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/character-ai/)). Replika claimed more than 30 million cumulative sign-ups in August 2024 against about 2 million actives and 500,000 payers in 2023, so the "30 million daily users" repeated online is a fifteen-fold overstatement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replika); [AI Chatbot Characters](https://aichatbotcharacters.com/how-many-users-does-replika-ai-have/); [Nikola Roza](https://nikolaroza.com/replika-ai-statistics-facts-trends/)). Per-user engagement is real and very deep, and it is a one-to-one loop between a person and a model, not a reason for people to share a place.

### 6.5 Costs

No vendor publishes a cost per NPC-hour. A third-party estimate of $0.004-0.01 per dialogue turn is unverified ([Arcanum](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/inworld-ai/)). Inworld priced its TTS-1 at $5 per million characters in 2025, marketed as about twenty times cheaper than incumbents, with TTS-1-Max at $10 ([fish.audio](https://fish.audio/vs/pricing/inworld-ai/)); by the time of the checker's search both were marked deprecated in favour of TTS-1.5, prices unconfirmed ([Inworld docs](https://docs.inworld.ai/models)). Vendor prices turn over within a year, so an agent cost model must be parameterised. Mecha BREAK keeps its model on the player's GPU, the cheapest tier and the one a web and mobile world cannot assume.

| System | Date | What it demonstrated | What it did not show | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generative Agents (Smallville) | Apr 2023 | 25 agents with memory, reflection, planning | Any human retention effect | Press extracts; ablations not read |
| 1,000-person simulation | Nov 2024 | 85% of human test-retest fidelity on surveys | Behavioural fidelity over weeks | arXiv abstract; not re-searched |
| Project Sid | Oct-Nov 2024 | Up to 1,000 agents, roles, rules, votes in 4-hour runs | Replication; long-horizon results | Preprint; "preliminary" per authors |
| NEO NPC, ACE | 2024-2025 | Voiced conversational NPCs; on-device 4B model | A shipped game with sales data | Press and vendor pages |
| Inworld | 2023-2025 | $500M valuation; then a pivot to infrastructure | A durable AI-character product | Company blog and docs |
| Roblox Cube, Genie 3 | Mar-Aug 2025 | Text-to-mesh; minutes-long promptable video worlds | Persistent multi-user state | Company and press |
| Character.AI, Replika | 2023-2026 | ~75 min/day per user (estimate); millions of actives | Shared, social engagement | Analyst estimates; company claims |

## 7. Risks

### 7.1 Hollow engagement and duty of care

The deepest engagement in the corpus is also the most exposed. On 29 October 2025 Character.AI announced that open-ended chat for under-18s would end by 25 November 2025, after an interim cap starting at two hours a day, with age assurance through a behavioural tool plus Persona and teens redirected to prompt-based creation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/characterai-is-banning-minors-interacting-chatbots/18090030/)). In January 2026 Google and Character.AI agreed to settle the Florida case brought by the family of Sewell Setzer III, who died in February 2024 aged 14, with related Colorado, New York and Texas suits; terms are undisclosed and the sealed settlement sets no precedent, but Google's exposure came from its $2.7 billion 2024 licensing deal, so licensing a companion model is itself a litigation risk ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/01/google-and-character-ai-agree-to-settle-lawsuit-linked-to-teen-suicide); [WTOP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/01/google-and-chatbot-maker-character-to-settle-lawsuit-alleging-chatbot-pushed-teen-to-suicide/)). Italy's Garante fined Replika's maker Luka EUR 5 million on 10 April 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replika)). Two no-search checkers add, from prior knowledge, that California's SB 243 on companion chatbots (effective 1 January 2026) requires disclosure, self-harm protocols and reminders for minors, and that the FTC opened a 6(b) inquiry into companion chatbots in September 2025.

### 7.2 Disclosure is becoming law

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026; the Commission's final guidelines were issued on 20 July 2026 and a voluntary Code of Practice had about 190 signatories by the end of July ([Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/); [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)). The guidelines accept plain-language notices, persistent badges or spoken statements, but not a generic "assistant" label or a disclosure buried in terms and conditions, and AI agents must say on whose behalf they act; the Digital Omnibus defers the Article 50(2) marking duty for systems already on the market to 2 December 2026, Official Journal publication unconfirmed ([Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparency-obligations/)). Steam has required disclosure of generative AI since 10 January 2024, with a separate category for live-generated content, where a world with live agents would fall (checkers' prior knowledge); VGC reports Valve has since "significantly" rewritten the rules, on a date the extract did not capture ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-games-disclosing-generative-ai-use-are-up-800-this-year/)).

### 7.3 Likeness and labour

Fortnite's AI Darth Vader, a recreation of James Earl Jones's voice with his estate's consent, drew an unfair-labour-practice charge from SAG-AFTRA at the NLRB on 19 May 2025 against Llama Productions, an Epic entity, for making a unilateral change "without providing any notice of their intent to do this and without bargaining with us over appropriate terms" ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/actors-union-files-labor-charge-over-ai-darth-vader-in-fortnite); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-darth-vader-fortnite-ai-voice/)). No disposition was found. Both no-search checkers recall that the SAG-AFTRA video-game strike, which began on 26 July 2024, ended with an Interactive Media Agreement ratified on 9 July 2025 carrying consent and compensation terms for digital replicas; this is prior knowledge. The NO FAKES Act would create a federal digital-replica right and was advanced in committee in June 2026 without passing either chamber, and California's AB 2602 already requires explicit consent for performer digital replicas ([GovTrack](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s4591); [Variety](https://variety.com/2024/politics/news/sag-aftra-limit-ai-digital-replica-california-bill-1236120379/)). A world selling synthetic voices needs a provenance and consent registry compatible with those terms.

### 7.4 The backlash, measured

Quantic Foundry's 18 December 2025 post reports an optional survey of 1,799 respondents taken between October and December 2025: 85% answered below neutral and 63% (62.7%) chose the most negative option; women and non-binary, younger and story-focused gamers were more negative, and older and power-progression-motivated gamers more favourable ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)). The ">1.75 million gamers" figure repeated by secondary outlets is the size of Quantic Foundry's cumulative profile dataset, not the survey sample. The segmentation matters more than the headline: hostility is strongest among the social and story-motivated players a persistent world targets. GDC's 2026 State of the Industry survey found 52% of developers saying generative AI is hurting the industry, up from 30%, with 7% positive, cited here through a secondary blog ([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/ai-divides-2025-gaming-backlash-innovation-and-ethical-challenges/)). On Steam, 7,818 of about 114,126 games, roughly 7%, carried a generative-AI disclosure by 13 July 2025, close to 20% of 2025 releases and about 800% up on 2024; disclosure counts are a floor ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-games-disclosing-generative-ai-use-are-up-800-this-year/); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/nearly-20-of-new-steam-games-in-2025-use-generative-ai/)). Imperva's 2025 Bad Bot Report puts bots at 51% of web traffic in 2024, with bad bots at 37% ([Imperva](https://www.imperva.com/resources/resource-library/reports/2025-bad-bot-report/)). The "dead internet" fear is quantifiable and will attach to any world whose inhabitants are undisclosed agents.

## 9. What this means for nolife

Nothing in the corpus shows agent-filled density retaining humans. Thirty years of artificial life produced interesting agents and no necessary places; the one life-sim that gave people a place to return to did it with scripted villagers, a real clock and rituals the players brought; Second Life's own a-life died with a vendor's server; and the 2023-2026 agent results are preliminary four-hour runs, while measured engagement with AI characters is solitary, age-gated and litigated. AI changes what an empty room looks like. It does not change whether a person has a reason to be in it, which the taxonomy locates in groups with a schedule, roles and a calendar (section 1.4; R11, R15), and in density that is managed rather than hoped for (R12).

| Finding | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Agents can fill a room but are not shown to retain anyone; disclosure is becoming law; backlash is strongest among social players | **R25.** Every AI inhabitant is disclosed (badge, notice, on whose behalf it acts), has a purpose, is capped at a published fraction of visible occupants and withdraws as humans arrive; no companion loops for minors; voices licensed or synthetic with provenance; cognition tiered with a published cost per agent-hour. Test: human D30 in agent-filled places at least that of human-only places, or the cap is lowered; agent cost at most 10% of the safety-plus-infrastructure budget. |
| Ozimals' bunnies hibernated when a creditor closed the food server; food-independent pets survived | **R24.** Platform-hosted, escrowed life-support for value-bearing scripted objects, with a designed end-of-life conversion to a food-independent state. Test: zero value-bearing objects bricked by vendor exit. |
| Second Life's concurrency counts bots; no bot share is known | **R13.** Publish bot share alongside retention and concurrency with a documented "active" definition and a third-party-readable API. |
| Emptiness is a density problem the agents do not solve | **R12.** Density management with a measured SLO: thin instances merge, empty districts close, newcomers route to live events; median people visible in a first session at least 10. |
| Purpose came from people with a schedule, in Animal Crossing as in Second Life | **R11, R15.** First hour = first group; a civic calendar and first-class roles from day one. |
| Companion liability and minors' protections | **R21, R23.** Age assurance as a regional module; always-on safety primitives with incident-based KPIs. |
| FreeSO revived The Sims Online by reverse engineering; Second Life residents have no exit | **R20.** Self-hostable cells, escrowed server images, export of own-created content, 12 months' notice before any shutdown. |

Three design hints are worth carrying forward as hypotheses: a RimWorld-style director per neighbourhood that paces festivals and crises; an Animal Crossing real clock, seasons and agent routines so places feel alive at predictable times; and opt-in, logged twins of absent residents, pending the behavioural test the 1,000-person study does not supply. Each is cheap to try under R25's cap and retention test, and none is the reason anyone will log in.

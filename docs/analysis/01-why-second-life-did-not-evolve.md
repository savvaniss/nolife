# Why Second Life did not evolve

**Summary.** Second Life is neither dead nor replaced. Twenty-three years after launch it reports about 600,000-620,000 monthly active users, a roughly US$650 million annual economy, about US$78 million a year paid out to creators, and profitability since 2005. What it did not do is grow. The region map has sat near 27,000 for a decade, peak concurrency is about a fifth below its 2020 high, and Linden Lab's own retention statement moved from "about 10% at 90 days" in 2007 to "around 7%" in 2025. Of the eight causes usually blamed, only four survive the project's fact-checks and critiques with a stated mechanism: a content base with no per-asset budgets, a land-rent model that set the builder's cost of entry, a supply of purpose that was never measured or defended, and a VR sibling bet with no continuity. Governance by decree was an amplifier, and the 2010 switch to cash-flow management is the cause most accounts miss. The document closes with what Second Life got right, what this analysis does not claim, and the nolife requirements each finding discharges.

## How to read this document

This is the reader-facing version of section 1 of the project's failure taxonomy. It uses only the project's research briefs on Second Life's history, economy and governance, their fact-check verdicts, and the three critiques; no new web research was done. Every number carries an inline citation to the page the project's search extracts surfaced. Page fetches were blocked throughout the research programme, so "verified" means a search extract matched the claim, not that the page was read. Figures that rest on prior knowledge, a single secondary site or an executive statement relayed by one blogger are labelled as such. Where the fact-checkers corrected the briefs (the current region price, the Openspace outcome, the Land Impact era, the bot-inflated 2009 peak, the attribution of the 2024 economy figures) the corrected value is used. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is stated in one sentence.

The product under design is nolife. Its predecessor is Second Life, operated by Linden Lab.

Two readings follow. First, the 2023 "750K" and 2024 "500K" cannot both be right within 16 months unless the definition changed, and the May 2026 third-party estimate of 1.15-1.45M unique logins is a different measure again; the MAU series has no stated definition at any point and is read here only as a plateau ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-10-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2009.html)). Second, peak concurrency has not "halved". The 2009 record included an unquantified bot share, so against the 2020 human high of 60,068 the 2025-2026 peaks of 48,000-50,000 are a decline of roughly a fifth over six years, while reported MAU rose 24% in 2025, which is consistent with shorter and lighter mobile and streamed sessions.

### 1.3 The grid and the economy

The map peaked at 31,988 regions on 13 June 2010 and has been roughly flat near 27,000 since 2015 ([Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2015/11/09/second-life-regions-drop-under-the-25-000-mark/)). Private estates, the fee-paying part of the grid, fell from about 26,600 in 2008 (a single ambiguous source) to below 19,000 by July 2014 and about 17,600 in 2026; the 2025-2026 counts are uncorroborated by any second series ([WebTribunal](https://webtribunal.net/blog/second-life-facts/); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2014/07/28/private-estates-drops-below-19-000-regions-in-second-life/); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2026/04/08/second-life-grid-shows-positive-region-growth-since-start-of-2026/)). Roughly 80% of that decline happened between 2008 and 2014, and the 2025 drop in Linden-owned regions was mostly the Lab retiring first-generation Linden Homes ([Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/11/04/second-life-region-statistics-early-november-2025-update/)).

The economy is reported as gross flow. User-to-user transactions grew 65% to US$567M in 2009 and produced US$55M of resident earnings, with the top 25 residents taking about US$12M of it ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/second-lifes-economy-grows-65-to-567m)). A January 2022 release put the "GDP" at US$650M with 345M transactions ([Linden Lab](https://www.lindenlab.com/releases/high-fidelity-invests-in-second-life)), and at a December 2024 town hall Oberwager said the Lab had spent about US$1.3B, paid about US$1.1B to creators since 2003, takes about 10% of transactions, and paid US$78M to creators in 2023, "up $10 million compared to 2017" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2024/12/20/second-life-1-3b-to-build-1-1b-paid-to-creators/)). A single secondary summary of the same town hall gives 21,152 creators earning real money in 2023, 6,446 above US$1,000, 139 above US$100,000 and 14 at US$1M or more ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46605)). All of these are company figures with no audit; the payout line is flat in real terms since 2017, and the US$650M figure has been repeated without a new measurement since 2022.

## 2. The root-cause tree

Eight causes are commonly named. The project's causal review kept a cause only where a chain from the trait to lost users or revenue can be stated and no platform in the corpus has the same trait with the opposite outcome. On that test the eight reduce to four with a mechanism, one amplifier, one missed cause and one unexamined cohort.

| Named cause | Verdict | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture and technical debt | Mechanism holds in narrow form | No per-asset budget, so the hardware floor rises with the catalogue |
| Land-tax business model | Mechanism holds; not the cause of the plateau | Builder's cost of entry and fee creep after growth ended |
| Onboarding and retention | A constant cannot explain a change | Funnel is at the industry base rate; density never measured in hubs |
| Purpose vacuum | Unfalsifiable as first stated | Restated: purpose was supplied by scheduled groups, never measured |
| Governance and trust | Amplifier, not cause | Absence of notice and grandfathering; no churn series attached |
| The Sansar distraction | Counterfactual fails on dates | A sibling with no continuity cannot inherit a community |
| Platform and hardware timing | Duplicate of the two above | The mobile blocker was the unbudgeted content base |
| Demographics | Outcome, not cause | An older audience is a monetisation asset; the unserved cohort is non-English |
| (Missed) 2010 cash-flow switch | Checker finding, unsearched | Additive engineering, fee reshuffles, late mobile fit a company that chose to stay small |

### 2.1 Architecture and technical debt: the compatibility ratchet

A region is 256 m square on one simulator process, capped at 100 avatars, with a Land Impact budget last changed in November 2016 (Mainland full regions 22,500; private full regions 20,000 with a paid 30,000 upgrade; Homesteads 5,000; Openspaces 1,000) ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Land); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2016/11/03/lab-reveals-li-prim-allowance-changes-in-second-life-in-full)). In October 2008 a Linden developer said the region size would not change "because of its far-reaching effects on the platform", and it has not ([sldev archive](https://list-archives.secondlife.com/sldev/2008-October/012153.html)). The 2020 move to AWS re-hosted the same unit and changed neither caps nor frame budget ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2020/11/19/ll-confirms-second-life-regions-now-all-on-aws/)).

The 100-avatar cap is not the debt: Roblox, Fortnite and VRChat run tens to around 100-200 avatars per instance and are the category's largest worlds. The debt is that no asset carries a per-tier budget, so the client must render every generation of content ever uploaded, and the hardware floor rises with the catalogue. Linden Lab said in January 2025 that about 50% of its users lack high-end machines ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2025/01/02/)). That chain, from unbudgeted content to a rising floor to lost trials, is the one Second Life root cause with no counter-example in the corpus; the "jelly dolls" avatar-complexity cutoff of 2016 (prior knowledge) was a client-side patch for exactly this.

Every upgrade was additive and never a migration. Mesh shipped in 2011; EEP was announced in 2017 and released on 20 April 2020 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2020/04/27/2020-viewer-release-summaries-week-17/)); PBR materials went grid-wide on 28 November 2023, a decade after game engines standardised them ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2023/11/28/)). Prims, mesh, legacy materials and PBR coexist in one scene, and PBR terrain launched only on private regions ([release notes](https://releasenotes.secondlife.com/viewer/7.1.8.9375512768.html)). Two fairness points from the steelman stand. First, the migration itself was fast for a persistent world: about 80% of Firestorm users were on a PBR-capable build within about 18 months ([Firestorm](https://www.firestormviewer.org/some-statistics-firestorm-versions-whos-running-what/)). Second, the first draft's claim that "mirrors render everything twice" is withdrawn: mirrors are opt-in, off at every preset, and planar reflection renders twice in every engine ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Mirrors)).

Scripting followed the same pattern. LSL was the only production language for 22 years; SLua, Linden's Luau fork, went from alpha in March 2025 to main-grid beta in December 2025, additive to LSL ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Lua_Alpha); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2026/03/18/)). glTF import arrived in 2025, but in July 2026 the Lab was still asking creators what they wanted from it ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2026/07/03/2026-week-27-sl-ccug-meeting-summary-updating-sl-mesh/)). None of this touched the cause: there is still no per-asset budget and no conversion policy.

The client is a different story. The viewer has been open source since January 2007 (prior knowledge), and the volunteer-built Firestorm had 542,967 unique users in a 30-day period in 2015; in June 2025 the Lab put Firestorm on its own download page ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/firestorm-by-far-most-popular-on-hypergrid/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2025/06/19/)). The first draft booked this as "the client was ceded". The critiques are right that a client maintained for free through every hardware transition is an asset; the residual debt is that nobody owned the newcomer interface, which CEO Ebbe Altberg named in 2014 as the second-login blocker (section 2.3).

### 2.2 The land-tax business model: cost of entry, not cause of the plateau

Land was, in a 2006 account, "the one resource that is limited, and the main source of revenue for Linden" ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna15163036)). A full private region cost US$1,000 to set up plus US$295 a month from 2008 to 2015 ([SL forums](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/140885-how-much-does-a-full-region-from-ll-actually-cost/)); after cuts in 2016, 2018 and March 2023 (the 2023 cut is prior knowledge, not searched) it costs US$349 plus US$199 a month from 15 June 2026, with Homesteads at US$109 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2018/06/20/second-life-major-private-region-pricing-restructure-announced/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/)). Two no-search checkers called the US$199 figure a stale page; the search-backed checker found it is the current price, so this document uses it.

The first draft argued that a flat region fee "taxed what should have compounded". That is withdrawn. A flat per-region fee charges a packed venue and an empty estate the same amount, so it penalises emptiness, not success, which is what nolife's own land rule (R16) wants to do by other means. Two further tests fail the land-tax thesis as a cause of the plateau. Real tier roughly halved between 2008 and 2025 while estates fell, which is a demand shift, not a price effect ([Linden Lab](https://secondlife.com/land/private-pricing)). And OpenSimulator runs the identical simulator and viewer with regions at US$15-40 a month in 2015-era lists, or free, yet the whole hypergrid reached 48,376 monthly actives in May 2026 (49,690 in July per the steelman's extract), one-twelfth of Second Life after 17 years ([Kitely](https://www.kitely.com/virtual-world-news/2015/06/01/60-off-large-worlds-and-dedicated-memory-guarantee/); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2015/04/digiworldz/); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/)). If tier were the binding constraint, the cheap clone would be larger.

What the model does explain is the builder's cost of entry and the fee creep that followed once growth ended. Marketplace commission doubled from 5% to 10% on 2 December 2019 ([Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2019/11/25/editorial-second-life-users-are-less-than-happy-about-linden-lab-doubling-commission-rates-on-the-sl-marketplace-from-5-to-10/)); LindeX fees changed in March 2023 (prior knowledge, not searched); Premium Plus rose to US$287.88 a year from 8 July 2026 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/)). Residents estimate the all-in take at 20-31% against the "90/10" marketing, once Marketplace commission, currency fees and cash-out fees are stacked ([SL forums](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/519135-linden-claims-to-only-take-10-off-creators-but-they-really-take-20-by-the-time-you-pay-marketplace-fees-cash-out-fake-advertising-by-phil/)). The first Second Life millionaire, Anshe Chung, built her fortune on land subdivision and rental alongside a content business, that is, on the spread between Linden tier and resident rent ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshe_Chung)). Causality runs plateau, then fees: every price move after 2015 was a reshuffle from land onto subscriptions and transaction spreads, never a model that scaled with the economy.

### 2.3 Onboarding, density and retention: a constant cannot explain a change

Linden's own retention statements bracket the whole period. In January 2007: 90-day retention "about 10%", "more or less unchanged over the last 12 months" ([Andrew Chen](https://andrewchen.com/interesting-graph-on-second-life-usage/)). In September 2025: "only around 7% of those coming into SL are actively being retained" ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/)). In between, Altberg told residents in 2014 that "the not very intuitive viewer interface with the steep learning curve and high client performance requirements" was what stopped people logging on a second time ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2014/06/29/ebbe-altberg-on-user-retention-now-and-in-the-future/)).

The steelman's objection is decisive here. The industry base rates it located put the gaming average near 10% at 90 days (a search extract with no captured URL), so a 2003-era desktop download retaining 7-10% at 90 days is average to good. The funnel never moved because funnels in this industry do not move; and a constant cannot explain why Second Life grew to 2009 and plateaued afterwards with the same funnel. The 2025 data cut the other way from the first draft's reading: the Lab said mobile and Project Zero reached about 10 times the trial volume at about 7% retention, which is roughly seven times the old retained inflow, consistent with MAU rising 24% in 2025 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/)). Removing the access barrier moved the ceiling a little. Against that, Oberwager's November 2024 remark that DAU "has gone down" is one datum, not a series ([New World Notes](https://nwn.blogs.com/nwn/2024/11/linden-lab-sl-future-philip-rosedale-brad-oberwager.html)).

Emptiness is structural but badly measured. At the 2008 peak, 76,000 avatars over roughly 30,000 regions is under three per region; in 2025, a median of about 32,000 over 26,663 regions is about 1.2 ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/PCWorld/story?id=6688909); [Daniel Voyager](https://danielvoyager.wordpress.com/2025/08/11/second-life-statistics-august-2025-update/)). Both figures divide bot-inclusive concurrency by every region, including about 17,600 private estates that are empty when their owners are away, exactly as houses are. The number that matters, density in public hubs at peak, was never measured, and Horizon Worlds, a world with no land at all, was as empty. Wired's 2007 traffic scores made the point for brands: Sears scored 281, Coca-Cola's pavilion 27 ([Jerz](https://jerz.setonhill.edu/?p=56)).

### 2.4 Purpose: supplied by groups with a schedule, and never measured

"Purpose vacuum" as first stated was unfalsifiable. IMVU (about 7M MAU claimed, no land, no quests), Avakin Life (1.6M MAU, no user content) and Second Life itself survive on "meet people and dress up", while Rec Room and PlayStation Home had loops and died ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/25/imvu-35-million-funding/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/75555/avakin-life-surpasses-500-million-downloads-cementing-12-year-legacy-as-a-global-social-powerhouse/)). Restated so that it can be tested: retention concentrates in users attached to a group with a recurring time and place, and Second Life supplied places but never roles, schedules or scarcity. The causal effect of group membership on retention is held by Bungie, Square Enix and Linden and published by none of them, so this is a hypothesis nolife must measure (R11), not a finding.

What lasted was resident-built and staffed. The Confederation of Democratic Simulators has run elections since 21 September 2004 with about 80 citizens on six regions ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Confederation_of_Democratic_Simulators)); Virtual Ability has more than 1,000 members and co-won the first Linden Prize in 2009 ([Virtual Ability](https://virtualability.org/about-us); [Solar Daily](https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Virtual_mobility_for_disabled_wins_Second_Life_prize_999.html)); Cafe Musique runs more than 60 shows a week ([Second Life destination guide](https://secondlife.aditi.lindenlab.com/destination/aism)); SL9B in 2012 was built by volunteers in about a month after Linden withdrew ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2012/06/17/sl9b-let-the-festivities-begin/)); BURN2 has been community-run since 2010 ([Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2021/10/09/burn2-celebrate-burning-man-in-second-life-october-8th-to-17th-2021/)); the Anglican Cathedral survived its founder's exit ([Episcopal Cafe](https://episcopal.cafe/transitions_at_the_anglican_cathedral_in_second_life/)). Corporate builds that failed were, in a 2007 description, "a shop in the High Street filled with brochures but never having any staff" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/whatever-happened-to-second-life-1030314/2)). No independent attendance data exists for any of these venues.

The largest resident-built purpose, omitted from the first draft, is adult and romantic roleplay and the economy around it. The only hard shares are old: in November 2009, 1,325 private and 348 mainland regions were Adult-flagged, 5.69% of 29,409 ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Adult_content)); Wagner James Au's 2008 book put about 18% of land in "mature activity" and 13.6% of residents regularly having virtual sex ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/book-review-the-making-of-second-life)). No Linden figure for the adult share of currency flow exists. The loops removed by ban were not load-bearing: gambling was about 5% of the economy by one account and banking ended after Ginko's roughly L$200M collapse, yet the economy grew 65% in 2009 ([Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2007/10/15/223526/financial-woes-in-second-life); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginko_Financial); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/second-lifes-economy-grows-65-to-567m)). Linden Lab never measured or defended its largest purpose engine.

### 2.5 Governance and trust: an amplifier, not a cause

Decree, outcry and partial retreat are industry practice, including among the worlds that scaled; Second Life's distinction is the absence of notice and grandfathering, and no churn series is attached to any episode. The record: Openspace tier announced in October 2008 to rise from US$75 to US$125 with no grandfathering, then revised in November so that compliant Openspaces stayed at US$75 and a new Homestead class started at US$95 on 5 January 2009 with a scheduled rise to US$125 that July ([SL forums archive](https://forums-archive.secondlife.com/356/24/291220/1.html)). The trust damage came from the retroactive announcement, not the eventual price. Zindra, opened 15 June 2009 with 157 regions, generated about 850 forced-move tickets ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Zindra)). The education discount ended on 1 January 2011, doubling institutional region costs, and was restored on 24 July 2013, to which one university director replied "it's too late for many of us. Linden Lab blew it" ([THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/Articles/2010/11/03/Linden-Lab-To-End-Second-Life-Educational-Discounts.aspx); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/08/educators-wary-of-latest-linden-offer/)). The August 2013 Terms of Service made Renderosity and CG Textures ban their assets in Second Life until the July 2014 revision ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2013/09/outrage-grows-over-new-second-life-terms); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2014/07/16/lab-updates-section-2-3-of-their-terms-of-service-will-it-calm-doubts/)). CopyBot, in November 2006, triggered a creator strike because permissions were the only thing protecting livelihoods ([Granneman](https://blog.granneman.com/?p=1042)).

Metrics opacity belongs here too. No official MAU or DAU series exists; a 2023 "750K" is irreconcilable with a 2024 "500K"; and Engadget found in 2010 that Linden's own 2009 economy data contained a typo and a calculation error, with annual reports and live feeds using different definitions ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-07-10-the-virtual-whirl-a-brief-history-of-second-life-2009.html)). The one measurable loss from governance is the education exodus: institutions moved to OpenSimulator grids for cost, control and backups, and a 2014 study found cost and persistence decisive ([Hypergrid Business](https://hypergridbusiness.com/?p=35085); [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/49578/)).

### 2.6 The Sansar distraction: a VR sibling with no continuity

Sansar was teased in 2014, entered beta in 2017, shed staff in 2019-2020 and was sold to Wookey Project Corp on 24 March 2020 because, in Altberg's words, it "wasn't making enough money" ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2020/04/linden-lab-sells-sansar-to-wookey/); [Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2020/02/11/linden-lab-lays-off-40-staff-shuts-down-the-sansar-project/)). Average concurrency was in the tens with a peak near 220; what it cost was never found, and the widely quoted "$75M" is unverified.

The counterfactual, that Sansar absorbed the engineering that should have rebuilt the simulator, fails on dates. Concurrency declined from mid-2009, five years before Sansar, and 2020-2026, with no sibling product, new owners and full focus, shows the same flat trajectory: a mobile app still in beta, a streaming product closed at 14 months, concurrency flat near 45,000-49,000. EEP's three-year slip overlaps Sansar in time and nothing more. What Sansar does show is the mechanism in the successor table: a sibling carrying no inventory, identity or social-graph continuity, on hardware the audience did not own, cannot inherit a community. Leadership churn brackets it on both sides: six CEO transitions between 2008 and 2014, the 2010 layoff, the 2020 sale, Tilia sold in 2024 ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/linden-lab-ceo-resigns-shortly-after-layoffs); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2020/07/09/linden-lab-announces-it-is-to-be-acquired/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2024/04/26/summary-of-tilia-acquisition-lab-gab-sl-round-table-news/)).

### 2.7 Platform and hardware timing

"Second Life bet on VR while the market went mobile" charges the same bet twice. Second Life's own VR exposure was a project viewer released on 1 July 2016 and withdrawn within days as not meeting "our standards for quality"; nothing official has shipped since ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2016/07/08/second-life-oculus-rift-support-suspended/)). The VR bet was Sansar. About 1.77% of Steam users had a headset in January 2026, and Quest shipments fell 16% through the third quarter of 2025, so the audience the bet needed still does not exist ([rec0ded88](https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/vr-gaming-headset-ownership/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/02/apple_vision_pro_meta_quest_sales_drop/)). The real mobile blocker was not timing but the unbudgeted content base from section 2.1: a closed mobile beta ran in October 2013 and never shipped ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2013/10/23/lab-confirms-sl-mobile-beta-programme/)), and the Unity app that finally entered open beta in June 2024 is "still in beta" in October 2026 at 2.5 stars on more than a million downloads ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lindenlab.secondlife&hl=en_US)).

### 2.8 Demographics and the unserved cohort

In 2008 Linden's data showed 41% women, 39% North America and 32% Western Europe ([Yuping Liu](https://www.yupingliu.com/wordpress/tag/statistics/)). A 2025 self-selected survey found mean ages near 45 for men and 38.6 for women, weighted about 42.8; it is a convenience sample from a paper about social support, not an age study ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00287)). The first draft read this cohort as a liability. It is a monetisation asset: an audience generating about US$1,080 of gross flow per monthly user per year, against Roblox's US$53-61 of bookings per daily user, is the opposite of a problem, and Roblox's own 18+ daily users grew 32% year on year in the second quarter of 2026 with a 42% payout bonus on verified adult spend ([Outlook India](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/roblox-q2-2026-revenue-soars-36-to-15b-despite-bookings-miss); [Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42)). The Teen Grid inference is withdrawn: 16-17-year-olds moved to the main grid.

The cohort that was lost is non-English. In March 2007 comScore put 61% of active residents in Europe, 19% in North America and 13% in Asia-Pacific, with Germany alone at about 16% ([comScore](https://www.comscore.com/Insights/Press-Releases/2007/05/Second-Life-Growth-Worldwide)). A Semrush web-traffic proxy for November 2025, unsearched, puts the United States at 49.6%, Brazil 7.0%, Colombia 5.6%, the United Kingdom 5.1% and Germany 3.8% ([Semrush](https://zh.semrush.com/website/secondlife.com/overview)). The reason is datable. In 2009 the Lab translated tier-one languages while "the Second Life community did nearly everything" for the rest ([Gilbane](https://gilbane.com/?p=2016)); the 2010 restructuring closed the Singapore and UK offices and ended funded localisation ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-07-linden-lab-laying-off-staff-closing-singapore-office.html)); Kaizen Games, the official Brazilian partner, stopped ([Baguete](https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/second-life-acaba-no-brasil)); Ajuda SL Brasil, a volunteer gateway, was still self-funded in 2018 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2018/05/)); Japan was "depopulating" by December 2007 ([Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2007/12/19/japan-depopulating-second-life/)). Japan is now VRChat's largest web market at 37% of visits against 27% for the United States ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/vrchat-high-popularity-in-japan-JP)). A quarter of the base was left to volunteers after 2010.

### 2.9 The missed cause: the 2010 switch to cash-flow management

Both no-search checkers located the start of the flat decade at the June 2010 restructuring, when Linden Lab stopped investing in growth and began managing cash flow; this is a checker finding, not a searched one. Since then the company has run on about 160 staff and a roughly 10% take by Oberwager's December 2024 account, or about 200 staff per one unverified May 2026 profile ([Kowatek](https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=88910); [YesPress](https://yespress.io/linden-lab)). Additive-only engineering, fee reshuffles instead of a new pricing model, a mobile client a decade late and a VR bet funded as "a start-up inside an established, profitable company" all fit a firm that chose to stay small and profitable. If that is the cause, it is not a defect to fix but a posture to choose, which is why nolife's funding posture is a written design decision (R30) and why the subsidy-collapse pattern among the successors is its mirror image.

## 3. What Linden Lab tried, and why it did not work

| Attempt | Date | Outcome | Why it did not move the ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewer 2 "Basic mode" | 2011 | 24 preset avatars; derided by incumbents; retention effect never published ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2011/04/11)) | One interface for two audiences; no measurement |
| Mesh, materials, EEP, PBR | 2011-2023 | Shipped, additively; about 80% of Firestorm users on PBR within 18 months ([Firestorm](https://www.firestormviewer.org/some-statistics-firestorm-versions-whos-running-what/)) | Served existing residents; no budget or conversion policy, so the hardware floor kept rising |
| Oculus Rift viewer | July 2016 | Withdrawn within a week ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2016/07/08/second-life-oculus-rift-support-suspended/)) | Hardware the audience did not own |
| Sansar | 2014-2020 | Sold 24 March 2020; concurrency in the tens ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2020/04/linden-lab-sells-sansar-to-wookey/)) | No inventory, identity or social-graph continuity |
| Tilia | August 2019 | Licensed money transmitter; sold to Thunes with a five-year partnership in 2024 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2024/04/26/summary-of-tilia-acquisition-lab-gab-sl-round-table-news/)) | Worked; it is the asset that outlived the sibling product |
| AWS "uplift" | 2020 | All regions on AWS by November 2020 ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2020/11/19/ll-confirms-second-life-regions-now-all-on-aws/)) | Operational, not experiential: same caps, same frame budget |
| Tier cuts, fee rises | 2016, 2018, 2023, 2026 | Full region to US$199; Marketplace 5% to 10%; Premium Plus up ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2026/06/08/ll-announces-second-life-fee-changes-reductions-increases/)) | Reshuffles from land to subscriptions and spreads; demand had already shifted |
| Mobile app | June 2024- | Open beta; free to all from 13 November 2024; "still in beta" in October 2026 at 2.5 stars ([Linden Lab](https://community.secondlife.com/news/featured-news/second-life-mobile-is-here-r1550/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lindenlab.secondlife&hl=en_US)) | The 20-year content base, not the app, sets the floor |
| Project Zero streaming | March 2025-April 2026 | Firestorm Zero launched 14 March 2025 on GameLift, sold as time passes, sold out; closed by June 2025 after a provider-forced migration; Project Zero ended 21 April 2026, reframed as an experiment ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2025/03/14/using-firestorm-in-your-browser-for-second-life/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2025/06/27/); [Modem World](https://modemworld.me/tag/project-zero/)) | No source states why; a 2025 extract puts the cost near US$1.75 per streamed hour (unsearched), which a free tier cannot absorb |
| SLua | 2025- | Main-grid beta December 2025 ([SL wiki](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Lua_Alpha)) | Additive to LSL; a runtime without an owner budget |

The pattern across the table is that every attempt either served people already present (rendering upgrades, tier cuts) or widened access without changing what newcomers found when they arrived (Basic mode, mobile, streaming). The 2025 funnel data is the best evidence: ten times the trials, the same 7%, and a ceiling that moved from about 500,000 to about 620,000.

## 4. What Second Life got right, and why it survives

A real economy with cash-out. About US$1.1B paid to creators since 2003, roughly US$650M a year of transactions, more than 21,000 creators earning in 2023 on the single available count, a currency that held a band of L$240-270 per dollar from 2008 to 2017, and profitability by 2005 on US$25M raised ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46605); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Second_Life)). A licensed payments rail, built in-house as Tilia in 2019 and sold in 2024 with a five-year partnership, which means the compliance layer survived product failure ([Modem World](https://modemworld.me/2024/04/26/summary-of-tilia-acquisition-lab-gab-sl-round-table-news/)). Latent demand: a 50% rise in regular users in 2020 and a 24% rise in reported MAU in 2025 show that each time access widened, people came ([Ryan Schultz](https://ryanschultz.com/2020/05/24/linden-lab-ceo-ebbe-altberg-second-life-has-seen-a-50-increase-in-regular-monthly-users-because-of-the-pandemic/)).

And nobody replaced it. The only worlds that cover all four pillars (persistent homes, in-world creation, adult users, an economy with cash-out) are Resonite, at about 170-200 concurrent on Steam, and the OpenSim hypergrid at about 45,000-48,000 monthly actives ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/resonite); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/05/opensim-users-land-area-both-up-this-month/)). OpenSim is the cleanest test of portability as a retention feature: it offers export, self-hosting and cheap land, but no economy, no audience and no stability, since OSgrid lost its asset database in 2025 and was offline for three weeks in 2026 ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/?p=79686)). Educators moved for cost and control; creators and consumers did not follow.

The rule this yields for nolife: a trait is a moat where it retains the people present and a debt where it blocks those not yet present. Backward compatibility, the land tier and the volunteer client are each both. Twenty years of inventories and relationships are a switching cost no competitor has offered to carry, and the same unbudgeted inventories set the hardware floor. The design problem is continuity of identity, inventory and social graph (the moat) with budgets and conversion on content (the debt).

## 5. What this analysis does not claim

It does not claim Second Life is a failed business. It was profitable by 2005, is owner-funded, keeps about 10% of a roughly US$650M economy, reported MAU up 24% in 2025, and is by the critics' own financial standard healthier than Roblox, VRChat or Zepeto ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/linden-lab-has-spent-1-3b-building-second-life-and-paid-1-1b-to-creators/)).

It does not claim concurrency halved. The 2009 peak was bot-inflated; against the 2020 human high the decline is about a fifth over six years.

It does not claim the funnel caused the plateau. A 7-10% 90-day retention sits at the industry base rate, and the same funnel accompanied both the growth years and the flat ones.

It does not claim the land tax caused the plateau. OpenSim runs the same architecture with land five to thirteen times cheaper or free and has one-twelfth of the users.

It does not claim Sansar cost Second Life its growth. Concurrency declined five years before Sansar and stayed flat for six years after it.

It cannot refute the cheapest hypothesis. The audience for a persistent, self-owned, scriptable adult world with cash-out may be about 0.5-1M people worldwide, and Second Life may already serve most of it: every demand shock (2006, 2020, 2025) reached a plateau near 500,000-650,000 faster than the last. The nolife requirements are therefore sized for a Linden-scale world that can grow, not for a product that assumes the ceiling is a design artefact.

The figures most at risk are the US$78M payout and the creator-income counts (one secondhand summary of one town hall), the 2025-2026 region counts (uncorroborated), Sansar's cost (never found), the pixel-streaming cost (an unsearched extract), the Semrush country split (an unsearched proxy), the 2025 age survey (a convenience sample) and Linden's current headcount (one unverified profile). Not researched at all: build cost and funding climate, music licensing for the live-performance scene, the bot share of concurrency, creator interviews, and current OpenSim land prices.

## 6. Requirements this document discharges

| Finding | nolife requirement |
|---|---|
| No per-asset budget; the hardware floor rises with the catalogue (2.1) | R2 per-tier asset budgets with server-side enforcement; R5 sandboxed scripting with per-owner budgets |
